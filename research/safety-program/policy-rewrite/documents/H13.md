# Hydrogen sulfide and hazardous atmospheres

Work in areas that may contain hydrogen sulfide or another dangerous atmosphere is planned around exposure prevention, detection and escape. This policy applies to affected production, tank, monitoring and support work.

The supervisor identifies likely release sources and communicates alarms, escape routes and assembly points. Where monitoring is needed, the crew uses a detector suitable for the gases and conditions, checks its readiness and keeps it where it can detect the exposure. Smell is not used to judge whether hydrogen sulfide is present or whether an area is safe.

An alarm or signs of a release prompt withdrawal to a safe location, avoiding the release path and low areas. People notify others who may be exposed and contact the site's emergency response. They do not reenter to investigate or rescue without the protection and response capability appropriate to the atmosphere.

Respirator use follows the respiratory-protection requirements triggered by the task. Unknown or immediately dangerous atmospheres are not treated as ordinary nuisance exposures. Workers report a detector fault or a missing escape route before continuing exposed work.
