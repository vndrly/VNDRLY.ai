# Line of fire and critical safeguards

People are kept clear of paths in which moving equipment, suspended material, pressure or stored energy could injure them. This policy applies wherever a person could be struck, crushed, caught or exposed to a sudden release.

The crew identifies the likely direction of movement or release before positioning people. Equipment is stabilized, loads restrained and guards or barriers used for the hazard involved. Hands and bodies stay out of pinch points. A spotter stands where the operator can see them and where movement cannot trap them. People do not rely on eye contact alone when a physical separation is needed.

The operator confirms that the work area is clear before movement begins. Workers do not enter a guarded or isolated area while hazardous motion or energy remains. If a barrier is moved, a restraint fails or the operator loses sight of someone exposed, the affected movement stops.

The supervisor and crew restore the safeguard or choose another safe method before continuing. Checking a safeguard means confirming the physical condition, not merely checking a box.
