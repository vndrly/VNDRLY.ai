# Control of hazardous energy

Equipment is protected against unexpected startup, movement and release of stored energy during servicing that exposes people to those hazards. The supervisor selects the energy-control method applicable to the equipment and activity.

The worker performing isolation identifies all energy sources, shuts equipment down, isolates the sources and applies the required lockout or tagout devices. Stored pressure, gravity, electrical charge and other residual energy are released, restrained or otherwise controlled. Isolation is verified before exposure begins. A stop button or control selector alone is not an energy-isolating device.

Each person covered by a group isolation receives protection through the applicable group procedure. Shift changes and contractor interfaces maintain continuous protection. Workers do not remove another person's device except through the exceptional-removal procedure permitted by the governing energy-control standard.

Before restoration, equipment is checked, people are clear and affected workers are informed. Where 29 CFR 1910.147 applies, its written procedures, role-specific training and at-least-annual procedure inspections apply, subject to the standard's stated exceptions. Those duties are limited to work within that standard's scope.
