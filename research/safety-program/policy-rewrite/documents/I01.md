# Reporting injuries and hazards

Workers report injuries, illnesses, near misses and hazards promptly so people can receive help and dangerous conditions can be corrected. Reports can be made to the supervisor or another manager. Good-faith reporting does not result in retaliation.

Immediate priorities are medical assistance and protection from further harm. The supervisor obtains the information needed to understand what happened without delaying treatment. Workers describe what they observed and distinguish facts from assumptions. A report can be made verbally;

The company makes government notifications required for the incident. Under federal OSHA's reporting rule, a work-related fatality is reported within eight hours, and a qualifying inpatient hospitalization, amputation or loss of an eye within twenty-four hours, subject to the rule's timing and reporting conditions. Applicable state reporting requirements are followed where they govern.

The responsible manager determines whether an OSHA recordkeeping entry is required and protects personal information. Near misses and ordinary hazard reports are not automatically treated as OSHA-recordable injuries. The affected workers are told how the immediate hazard has been addressed.
