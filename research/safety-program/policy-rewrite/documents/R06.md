# Changes to work and equipment

Changes are considered before they expose people to hazards the existing work method does not address. This policy applies to changes in equipment, materials, staffing, service scope and operating conditions.

The supervisor identifies what will change and which people or systems it may affect. The crew checks whether existing isolation, protection, access, training and emergency arrangements remain suitable. A like-for-like replacement is distinguished from a change in function, capacity or hazard. Workers are told about changes affecting the instructions they use.

If a proposed change creates an uncontrolled exposure, the affected work waits while a safe method is established. Temporary changes are communicated clearly so the next crew does not mistake them for the normal arrangement. The return to normal conditions is also considered when removing temporary equipment or controls.

For a process actually covered by OSHA process safety management, the employer follows the management-of-change provisions of 29 CFR 1910.119(l), including its exception for replacements in kind and its required training and updates.
