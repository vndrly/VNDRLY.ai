# Company and site orientation

People receive understandable information about their work environment before exposure to unfamiliar hazards. This policy applies when joining the company, starting at a different site or taking on materially different duties.

The supervisor explains how to obtain help, report hazards, stop unsafe work and respond to an emergency. Orientation covers the person's actual work area, access routes, alarms, assembly arrangements and relevant restrictions. Site-specific information is obtained from the site contact rather than assumed from a previous location.

Instructions are provided in a language and form the worker can understand. The supervisor invites questions and checks understanding through conversation about the work. A worker can ask for an explanation without being treated as having failed a universal induction test.

Required role training is assigned through the applicable course policies. Changes in routes, alarms or responsibilities are communicated to the people affected. If a worker cannot identify the safe action for an immediate hazard, the supervisor resolves the uncertainty before assigning that exposure.
