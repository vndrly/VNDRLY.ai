# Emergency practice

Emergency practice helps people use the response arrangements relevant to their work. This policy applies when the company practices an alarm, evacuation or specialist response.

The person arranging a practice identifies the response being exercised and keeps the exercise itself safe. Workers understand whether an event is a practice and how a real emergency would be communicated. The exercise can be a discussion or a practical activity suited to the objective; people are not placed in an actual hazardous atmosphere or fall exposure to demonstrate awareness.

After the activity, participants identify difficulties such as an inaudible alarm, blocked route or unclear location information. The supervisor corrects those problems and communicates the change to affected workers. The exercise is used to improve the response, not to create unrelated course-completion conditions.

A legally required practice interval applies only to the activity covered by that rule. For example, the permit-space rescue standard's practice provisions apply to the designated rescue service, with its stated exceptions.
