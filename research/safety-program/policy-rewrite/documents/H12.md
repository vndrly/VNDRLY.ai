# Electrical safety

Electrical work protects people from shock, burns and unexpected equipment operation. This policy applies to electrical equipment and work near exposed electrical hazards.

Workers keep enclosures closed, use equipment suited to its location and report damaged leads, exposed conductors or overheating. Damaged equipment is disconnected or removed from use without exposing the person doing so. Non-electrical workers keep clear of exposed energized parts and overhead lines.

Electrical work is deenergized where required by the governing standard. The worker performing the work isolates the supply, controls stored energy and verifies the deenergized condition using suitable test equipment. Electrical qualification is specific to the task. Where energized work is legally permitted, qualified workers use the work practices, approach protection and protective equipment required for the exposure.

Only people performing the relevant electrical task receive the duties of a qualified electrical worker; an awareness course does not make every employee an electrician. If a person receives an electrical shock, others isolate the source before touching them and summon emergency help. Work does not resume with damaged insulation or an unresolved electrical fault.
