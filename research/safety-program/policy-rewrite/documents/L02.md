# Worker participation and safety communication

Workers have direct knowledge of the work and a voice in how it is performed safely. This policy applies to safety communication within the company, including conversations between workers, supervisors and managers.

Supervisors explain hazards and changes in language workers understand, invite questions, and give workers an opportunity to describe problems with the task. Workers can raise concerns directly with their supervisor or another manager when the supervisor is unavailable or involved in the concern. Reports are considered on their facts; good-faith reporting does not result in retaliation.

Safety discussions address the actual work: what could hurt someone, what prevents that harm, and what has changed. A conversation at the work area can address a simple issue; a wider discussion can address a change affecting several crews.

The supervisor communicates the resolution to the people affected. If an immediate hazard remains, the affected work is paused rather than waiting for a meeting.
