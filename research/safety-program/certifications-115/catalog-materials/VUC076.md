# VUC076 — Enhanced Well Control

## Program definition

An original VNDRLY/U advanced course for experienced drilling personnel with established well-control fundamentals. It addresses difficult pressure-control decisions through technical instruction, calculations, realistic team simulation and incident analysis. It is not IWCF accreditation, a renewal of an external Level 3/4 certificate, or a stand-alone authorization to conduct live-well contingency operations. Assess operator and supervisor responsibilities separately. Record surface or combined surface/subsea simulator scope; combined scope requires the additional observed subsea work.

Outcomes: recognize and protect barriers when normal assumptions fail; reason about pressure and volume in deviated wells; distinguish evidence from hypotheses about flowback; evaluate and simulate volumetric, stripping, lubricate-and-bleed, bullheading and reverse-circulation methods; respond to non-shearable and equipment problems; manage abandonment transitions; communicate, challenge and document decisions. All numerical examples are fictional models with explicit assumptions. Actual field methods require the approved well-specific program, verified equipment capabilities and qualified supervision. Never use a classroom margin or valve sequence as a field prescription.

## Q01 — Experienced judgment and barrier ownership

Experience helps identify patterns but can also normalize deviations. Begin each operation by drawing the actual pressure paths from every exposed formation to the environment. Mark each required barrier, its elements, current verification evidence, owner and limits. A closed symbol on a diagram means intended state; it does not prove sealing, control-energy availability or a usable flow path. Identify changes in barrier status during tripping, stripping, circulation, testing and removal of equipment.

The operator monitors and performs the approved response within assigned authority. The supervisor evaluates the state, selects an approved strategy with the technical team and directs its execution. Both must stop and challenge unsafe or unclear instructions. A specialist may be needed when formation, equipment or method assumptions fail. Neither seniority nor a remote message supplies missing well data.

**Worked case:** a crew says the well is secure because a BOP close command was sent. The supervisor asks for actual pressure/flow observations, control confirmation and any known leak path before declaring the barrier effective. The operator reports what is observed, not what the command was supposed to achieve. **Changed case:** control indications disagree. Maintain the applicable safe condition, escalate the disagreement and verify through approved means; do not open the well merely to see which indication was correct. Record uncertainty explicitly.

## Q02 — Pressure windows, datums and calculation discipline

Hydrostatic pressure for a uniform liquid model in oilfield units is 0.052 × density in ppg × true vertical depth in ft, giving psi. Measured depth determines length and volume, not vertical pressure head. For multiple fluid layers sum the pressure contributions over their vertical intervals. During circulation, friction and surface backpressure alter the pressure profile. A static calculation is not a prediction of every transient.

Fictional S76 has TVD 8,000 ft, MD 12,000 ft and uniform 10 ppg liquid. Static hydrostatic pressure is 4,160 psi. If a communicating, reliable drillpipe reading is 400 psi in the stated static condition, the simplified formation-pressure estimate is 4,560 psi. Balance density is 4,560/(0.052 × 8,000) = 10.9615 ppg before any approved operational margin. A false assumption that MD is TVD would produce 6,240 psi and conceal underbalance.

A simplified shoe at 5,000 ft has assigned verified allowable total pressure 3,200 psi; uniform 10 ppg above it contributes 2,600 psi, leaving 600 psi surface allowance in that specified static model. Do not confuse formation-based allowance with equipment working pressure. Gas distribution, friction, fluid changes and other weak points can change the limiting pressure. **Changed case:** 11 ppg above the shoe contributes 2,860 psi, leaving 340 psi under the same model. A denser fluid does not increase every available pressure margin. Always state reference level, gauge/absolute basis and the location to which a limit applies.

## Q03 — Highly deviated and horizontal wells

A long lateral adds volume, friction and gas-distribution complexity without adding equivalent vertical head. Gas may occupy a long measured interval while displacing relatively little vertical liquid head; the pressure response changes as it reaches the build section. A straight-line pressure schedule derived for a simple vertical geometry may therefore be unsuitable. Use the approved geometry-aware model, fluid properties and pressure schedule. Track the position of fronts using measured segment volumes, while calculating hydrostatic effects with vertical increments.

Original two-segment example: 3,000 ft MD of lateral at 0.04 bbl/ft contains 120 bbl but has zero vertical rise in the ideal horizontal model. A 2,000 ft MD build segment with assigned average capacity 0.05 bbl/ft adds 100 bbl and has separately supplied TVD change. Their combined volume is 220 bbl. At calibrated output 0.1 bbl/stroke, ideal displacement is 2,200 strokes. This does not identify gas position precisely when mixing, slip, dissolution or losses occur.

**Worked case:** surface pressure changes as gas moves out of the lateral. The crew compares expected segment response with measured pressures/volumes instead of treating any deviation as choke error. **Changed case:** a larger washout changes the build-section capacity to 0.06 bbl/ft; volume becomes 120 bbl and combined volume 240 bbl, or 2,400 ideal strokes. Revalidate the schedule and uncertainty. Do not keep the old stroke count because the TVD is unchanged.

## Q04 — Fingerprinting without dismissing an influx

A baseline fingerprint records the well's response to a defined operating transition: pump rate, fluid, rotation, depth, time, flowback rate, cumulative volume and pressures. Formation breathing can return fluid after circulating pressure falls, but a resemblance to earlier flowback does not prove that new formation fluid is absent. Changed permeability, density, losses or gas content can invalidate the baseline. A gain must be evaluated under the approved detection/response plan, not explained away to protect the schedule.

Training baseline F76 returns 1.2, 0.6 and 0.2 bbl in successive one-minute intervals after the same approved transition: cumulative 2.0 bbl and declining rate. A new event returns 1.2, 1.5 and 2.0 bbl: cumulative 4.7 bbl with increasing rate. The new response differs by 2.7 bbl and contradicts the declining baseline. The correct decision is to invoke the approved suspected-influx response and communicate the evidence; do not wait for a gas detector before acting on the gain.

**Changed case:** the new event returns 1.0, 0.5 and 0.2 bbl, but mud density has changed and there was an unrecorded tank transfer. A closer-looking trace is still not proof of benign breathing. Reconcile transfers and state changes while maintaining required well control. Alarm management must preserve meaningful detection in drilling, connections and tripping; nuisance alarms call for a controlled correction, not disabling the entire system.

## Q05 — Gas behavior and the limits of simple models

Gas compressibility makes pressure and volume interact. In a simplified isothermal ideal-gas example, absolute pressure times volume stays constant for a fixed amount of gas. A 5 bbl bubble at 3,000 psia becomes 10 bbl at 1,500 psia. Gauge readings cannot be inserted without the appropriate atmospheric reference. Real well behavior also depends on temperature, gas properties, dissolution, slip, distribution and flow regime.

In a closed well, migrating gas can raise pressures and threaten equipment or formation limits. Holding a surface gauge constant without accounting for lost hydrostatic head can reduce bottomhole support. Conversely, preventing all expansion indefinitely can overpressure vulnerable parts of the system. The response must follow a validated method within the well's envelope, not a desire to keep one number visually steady.

**Worked case:** a trainee assumes the 5 bbl bubble remains 5 bbl as pressure halves; the instructor has them apply the absolute-pressure relationship and explain the extra volume. **Changed case:** temperature or gas dissolution changes significantly. The simple relationship is no longer sufficient; use the approved model and measured trends. Oil-based fluids may conceal gas until conditions change, so a quiet earlier return does not guarantee a gas-free later phase. Treat an unexpected trend as a reason to reassess, not to force data onto a simplified curve.

## Q06 — Volumetric control as a measured contingency

When circulation is unavailable and gas migrates, a qualified plan may manage expansion by coordinating pressure increases and measured liquid removal. Its purpose is to preserve an acceptable bottomhole pressure band while avoiding excessive pressure elsewhere. It is not simply bleeding to the original casing pressure and is not necessarily a complete kill. Method suitability depends on known geometry, fluid behavior, reliable pressure/volume measurement and a safe handling path. Losses or uncertain communication can invalidate the assumed balance.

Original model V76 uses uniform upper-annulus capacity 0.05 bbl/vertical ft and an assigned liquid-minus-gas gradient difference 0.45 psi/ft. One barrel corresponds to 20 vertical ft and 9 psi hydrostatic difference. A 90 psi planned replacement increment therefore corresponds to 10 bbl in this idealized model. These numbers illustrate pressure-volume exchange; they are not universal safety margins, permitted bleed volumes or a field cycle instruction. A real schedule must establish initial margins, working increments, limits, measurement tolerances and transition criteria.

During simulation, log each authorized increment, actual liquid volume and pressure response; verify that the model assumptions remain valid. Stop at the scripted hold if volume or pressure deviates, gas reaches the handling transition, losses appear or equipment limits approach. **Changed case:** capacity becomes 0.06 bbl/ft at the same gradient difference: each barrel represents 7.5 psi, so 90 psi corresponds to 12 bbl. Using 10 bbl would no longer represent the same pressure exchange. Obtain the correct geometry rather than treating tank marks as a universal schedule.

## Q07 — Stripping, displacement and containment transitions

Stripping moves pipe through a pressure-containing system. The equipment arrangement, tool-joint dimensions, sealing capability, pipe forces, internal flow control and measured displaced volume must be addressed together. A joint that cannot pass a closed ram requires the approved alternative containment sequence, never an improvised simultaneous opening. Distinguish pipe-heavy behavior from pipe-light conditions requiring suitable restraint/snubbing equipment. Do not infer it solely from total pipe weight at surface.

For a simplified force illustration with effective sealed area 12 in² and pressure 800 psi, pressure force is 9,600 lbf. If the assigned available downward force is 8,500 lbf before friction, the 1,100 lbf imbalance indicates that gravity alone is insufficient under the model. Actual effective area, buoyancy, friction and restraint calculations require the specific arrangement. No learner physically performs pressurized stripping in this course; the simulator reproduces the verified procedure and state changes.

Training displacement D76 is assigned 0.006 bbl/ft for a stated pipe/flow condition. Moving 90 ft corresponds to 0.54 bbl; 150 ft corresponds to 0.90 bbl. Actual open/closed-end and influx interactions change the applicable displacement model. **Changed case:** the model requires 0.54 bbl compensation but the measured result is 0.20 bbl. Stop at the approved safe state and investigate measurement, communication and fluid balance. Do not hide the discrepancy by changing the trip sheet to the expected value. Maintain the applicable barrier during every transition.

## Q08 — Lubricate and bleed reasoning

Where circulation is unsuitable and gas can be handled through an approved surface path, a qualified plan may replace gas with liquid in controlled stages. Added liquid must establish its intended hydrostatic contribution before the corresponding pressure reduction can be justified. Pumping a volume does not prove that it has fallen to the modeled position. Mixing, trapped gas, losses and restricted communication can invalidate that assumption. This method is different from lubricating a mechanical component or using a wireline lubricator.

Original L76 model uses upper capacity 0.04 bbl/vertical ft and assigned liquid-minus-gas gradient difference 0.48 psi/ft. Four barrels represent 100 vertical ft and 48 psi of hydrostatic replacement if the assumed segregation occurs. Six barrels represent 150 ft and 72 psi. The simulator's approved plan defines the permitted increment, stabilization criteria and pressure band; the learner must not invent a field wait time or bleed target from these illustrations.

**Worked case:** after the modeled addition, pressure/volume behavior indicates that liquid has not established the intended column. Hold the planned reduction and seek the prescribed verification/technical response. **Changed case:** losses begin. The simple closed-system model is invalid; do not continue repeating the last successful stage. Maintain the safest approved condition and escalate. A zero surface gauge at the end is not enough to declare the well killed; verify stable barrier status, fluid condition and required monitoring before a transition or equipment removal.

## Q09 — Bullheading and the destination of injected fluid

Bullheading pumps into a contained well rather than returning the influx through a conventional circulation path. The destination may be uncertain: fluid can enter a weaker zone instead of the intended reservoir, create underground flow or threaten shallow containment. Suitability needs formation/injectivity knowledge, casing/cement integrity, pressure limits, fluid compatibility, rate/volume modeling and a contingency plan. Avoid the false choice that inability to circulate automatically makes bullheading safe.

A simplified pressure balance for teaching is injection-point pressure = surface pressure + hydrostatic contribution − friction loss along the pumping path. With 700 psi surface, 3,600 hydrostatic and 200 friction, the modeled point pressure is 4,100 psi. If the assigned validated injection-point limit is 4,300, the numerical residual is 200 psi; it does not establish containment or acceptable injection destination. When flow stops, friction contribution changes, so the same surface pressure does not represent the same downhole state.

**Worked case:** increasing surface pressure to 1,000 psi with unchanged modeled hydrostatic/friction yields 4,400 psi, above the assigned 4,300 limit. Stop under the prescribed procedure, not raise the limit to fit the result. **Changed case:** pressure unexpectedly falls while injected volume rises. Consider losses or an unintended flow path; do not celebrate the lower pressure as automatic success. Confirm the intended barrier and formation response through qualified analysis. Never adopt an arbitrary over-displacement percentage or universal pressure/rate from this example.

## Q10 — Reverse circulation and arrival risk

Reverse circulation sends fluid down the annulus and brings returns through the inner tubular path. That path may bring formation fluids to surface sooner because its internal volume is smaller. Confirm communication, check-valve direction, internal pressure limits, solids/plugging risks, erosion and surface handling before choosing it. A pressure-rated annulus does not establish that the tubing, return line or separator can handle the planned flow.

Original R76 has a 60 bbl return-path volume and assigned constant liquid rate 2 bbl/min; ideal transit is 30 minutes. At 3 bbl/min it is 20 minutes. These are liquid-volume estimates only; gas slip/expansion and multiphase flow can change actual arrival and handling demands. Prepare the safe receiving path and monitoring before starting, not just before the calculated arrival minute.

**Worked case:** a crew copies a conventional-circulation pressure schedule into reverse operation. The supervisor rejects it until the correct flow-path hydraulic model and equipment limits are confirmed. **Changed case:** pump pressure rises while returns fall. Treat obstruction, losses or other abnormal behavior as a stop/escalation trigger under the approved plan; do not simply increase pump rate to reach the expected return volume. Record pressure, rate, strokes and observed returns together. A single gauge cannot locate every restriction.

## Q11 — Unusual shut-in states and non-shearables

Contingency readiness must account for what is physically across the BOP at the moment action is required. A non-shearable is relative to the installed ram capability and actual tubular/tool condition, not just a generic equipment name. Non-sealable geometry is a separate concern. Keep a verified space-out and tool-position record, identify exposure windows and define the approved response before entering them. If the required closure cannot be achieved, there must be an engineered alternative and escalation path; wishful reliance on a shear button is not a barrier.

The state of pumps and return paths also matters. A shut-in command while pumping can produce rapid pressure consequences if a flow path is closed. The correct response depends on the installed sequence and control logic; do not prescribe a universal valve order from memory. In simulation the operator must recognize the actual state, apply the validated emergency procedure and report achieved conditions.

**Worked case:** the tally places a large tool joint across the only available sealing element, but the supervisor assumes plain pipe is there. The operator challenges the assumption and uses the approved contingency rather than issuing an ineffective command. **Changed case:** a position sensor conflicts with the tally. Maintain control and resolve the discrepancy through approved means. Never move the string blindly to make a displayed symbol look correct. Log the uncertain state and the technical authority consulted.

## Q12 — Advanced equipment and proof of function

Read the actual system as connected functions: pressure containment, actuation energy, control logic, monitoring, choke/kill paths, return handling and emergency capability. Redundant control panels do not necessarily mean independent power or hydraulic supplies. A successful function indication does not prove a pressure seal. Conversely, a pressure test of one component does not prove the entire required flow path or emergency sequence is available.

Verify installed ratings and deratings for pressure, temperature, fluid, geometry and service. Confirm accumulator/control readiness using applicable current design and test criteria; do not import a dated generic bottle count or pressure into a different system. Determine which alarms indicate command, position, pressure, leakage or communication failure. Ensure the team understands how the system behaves after loss of power, communication or a sensor.

**Worked case:** two screens both show closed because they share the same failed transmitter. Treat them as one source, seek independent approved evidence and retain the restriction. **Changed case:** a replacement component has the same nominal working pressure but a different temperature or fluid compatibility envelope. Stop acceptance until the exact service is verified. The simulator includes a command/achieved-state mismatch and a restricted return path so learners must diagnose the system, not memorize panel colors. No real safety device is disabled for training.

## Q13 — Subsea pressure references and degraded capability

A combined surface/subsea learner must distinguish rig-floor, mudline and formation datums, riser fluid head, seawater replacement, choke-line friction and delayed control/pressure response. Removing or changing a riser contribution can reduce hydrostatic support. A surface-derived schedule must not be transferred to subsea geometry without the proper model. Confirm what each gauge measures and how line friction affects interpretation during flow and after shutdown.

Original S76-S illustration uses 2,000 ft of 10 ppg riser fluid and the same height of assigned 8.6 ppg seawater, excluding air gap for this model. Their hydrostatic values are 1,040 and 894.4 psi; the difference is 145.6 psi. This arithmetic is not a disconnect authorization or a complete riser-margin calculation. The actual program must address barriers, equipment, environment, vessel position and emergency procedures.

**Worked case:** an operator sees a pressure change after a rate transition and immediately changes choke position without allowing for the modeled line response. The supervisor requires the approved rate/pressure procedure and data interpretation. **Changed case:** a control pod becomes unavailable or vessel conditions threaten station keeping. Invoke the applicable degraded-mode/emergency decision process and required notifications; do not improvise a disconnect sequence. Combined assessment requires a validated subsea simulator scenario with actual control logic and limitations, not only the 145.6 psi calculation.

## Q14 — Incident study: Pryor Trust and failed detection

The US Chemical Safety Board's investigation of the January 2018 Pryor Trust blowout identified failure of hydrostatic prevention and the detection/BOP-response barrier. Its findings included unplanned underbalanced drilling/tripping, omitted flow checks and ineffective alarm management. Five workers died. Study these findings as a system problem: procedures, data interpretation, alarms, change management and escape arrangements must work together. Do not reduce the lesson to one person's mistake or claim that one added question would guarantee prevention.

Build a timeline of evidence available before the outcome was known. For each warning, identify the expected response, owner and barrier it should have protected. Separate source facts from hypotheses and from original training injects. Ask why a reasonable person in that operating system might dismiss a signal, then design controls that make the safer action easier and verifiable. Preserve challenge and escalation even when the experienced person is confident.

**Original training case, not historical rig data:** expected trip fill is 6.0 bbl, actual fill is 2.5 bbl and an alarm is suppressed as nuisance. The 3.5 bbl discrepancy is unresolved; invoke the approved suspected-influx response rather than accepting a benign story. **Changed case:** a shift handover says only 'same as last night.' The incoming supervisor must obtain actual barrier status, pressure/volume trends, alarm state and unresolved deviations. Simulator decisions are assessed on available evidence, not hindsight or blame.

## Q15 — Plugging, abandonment and barrier transitions

Abandonment planning must identify all potential flow paths, including annuli and communication behind casing, and specify barriers appropriate to the jurisdiction and well condition. Placing material at a planned depth is not proof that isolation exists. Require placement evidence, verification criteria, results and a clear handover of residual risks and future obligations. Temporary suspension and permanent abandonment have different requirements; neither is merely switching off monitoring.

Before removing BOP or other pressure-control equipment, verify the required independent barriers and tests under the applicable approved program. Consider the effects of displacement, cement setting, leaks, trapped pressure and changing differential direction. A positive pressure test may not demonstrate the negative differential that the next phase will impose. Do not invent universal plug lengths or test pressures from a different jurisdiction.

**Worked tabletop:** a plan shows two plugs, but one placement record is missing and an annular path is not covered. The correct decision is to hold the equipment-removal transition, resolve both the physical barrier path and verification evidence, and obtain the required approval. **Changed case:** a test passes but the observed pressure later rebuilds. Reassess the source and barrier state; do not dismiss it because the form already says pass. Record actual test conditions, stabilization, instruments, acceptance criteria, result and independent review. A successful course does not confer abandonment design authority.

## Q16 — Method selection, teamwork and defensible handover

Select methods by the actual state: circulation availability, influx location/behavior, pipe position, barrier capability, formation limits, fluid compatibility, handling capacity and verified data. Compare at least the feasible approved options and their failure modes. If no option is justified within the envelope, secure the safest available condition and escalate to specialist support. Do not make a method fit by suppressing an inconvenient measurement.

Use short closed-loop commands, a designated operational lead and clear roles for pressure control, volume tracking, barrier verification and logging. Read back critical values with units and reference. Encourage a specific challenge: 'The measured volume differs by 3.5 bbl; the influx assumption is unresolved; I recommend the approved stop/secure response.' The lead acknowledges, acts or explains a supported alternative and confirms the achieved state. Fatigue, time pressure and authority gradient are operational hazards, not character flaws.

**Worked handover:** the well is contained, but method execution is paused because losses invalidate the model. Report current barriers, pressures/trends, volumes, equipment state, limits, actions already taken and the unresolved decision. Do not report 'killed' because a surface gauge is low. **Changed case:** a new supervisor proposes resuming the old schedule. Require review of the changed assumptions and a documented approved restart. Final debrief evaluates both technical control and team behavior using evidence from the simulator log, then assigns specific remediation and a changed independent reassessment.

# VUC076 — Instructor workbook and simulation assessment

## Delivery, entry and equipment

Plan 72 hours: 16 instruction, 12 calculation/case laboratories, 28 facilitated simulation practice, 12 independent role evaluation/debrief, and four controlled examination/review hours. Extend for prerequisite remediation; do not compress by deleting a method. This enhanced course assumes established drilling well-control knowledge and current employer-recognized competence appropriate to the learner's role. Entry diagnostic covers pressure/volume units, TVD versus MD, communicating-gauge limitations, barrier diagrams, detection and approved shut-in response. Gaps are remediated using the owned VUC005/VUC007 foundation materials before enhanced practical participation. Those foundations are prerequisites, not an external certification waiver.

Instructor/evaluator must have demonstrated well-control teaching and relevant surface/subsea simulation competence, including the contingency methods being assessed. Use an evaluator independent of coaching where practicable. Provide the coursebook, calculator, original S76 data cards, charts/schematics, action/response logs, private final forms and a validated well-control simulator. Validate the model, initial state, control mapping, expected response and stop criteria before delivery. Never train by inducing an influx, defeating a real BOP or operating a live pressurized well.

The supplied desktop state-board cases below are complete reasoning simulations with stated inputs, action choices, responses and scoring. They can be run without software by an instructor revealing the next state after the learner's decision. They do not model all transient physics. Full VNDRLY practical scope additionally requires observed execution on a validated dynamic well-control simulator with the equipment-specific procedure mapped to its controls. If only state-board work is delivered, record an enhanced decision-study result, not the dynamic operator/supervisor award. Surface/subsea scope and each contingency method actually demonstrated must be recorded.

Use a facilitator, operator, supervisor, volume recorder and independent observer; rotate so every candidate is assessed in the claimed role. Team success cannot hide an individual's unobserved competence. Supply legible schematics, accessible displays and agreed working language. Require readback of pressure, volume and units. Additional reading time is allowed; giving the correct operating decision to a candidate is coaching and invalidates that independent trial.

## Laboratory applications

For each lab, retain calculations, assumptions, comparison to limits and the resulting decision. Explain errors before the matching simulator run. All inputs are fictional; field schedules are not inferred from them.

### W01 — Pressure and geometry

Using S76, calculate 4,160 psi liquid head, 4,560 psi simplified formation estimate and 10.9615 ppg balance density. Show the incorrect MD calculation 6,240 psi and explain the error. At the shoe, calculate 2,600 psi liquid head and 600 psi allowance; changed 11 ppg gives 2,860 and 340. For the lateral/build pair, calculate 120 + 100 = 220 bbl and 2,200 strokes; changed build capacity gives 240 bbl and 2,400 strokes. Expected decision: pressure uses vertical intervals while displacement uses measured segment volumes; neither simple model is a full deviated-well kill schedule.

### W02 — Fingerprint and gas behavior

Plot F76 baseline intervals 1.2/0.6/0.2 bbl and new intervals 1.2/1.5/2.0. Calculate totals 2.0/4.7 and discrepancy 2.7. Explain why an increasing trace cannot be dismissed as the same baseline. For 5 bbl at 3,000 psia falling to 1,500 psia, obtain 10 bbl under the stated isothermal ideal-gas model. Changed 4 bbl at 2,400 psia falling to 1,200 gives 8 bbl. Identify at least three real-well assumptions that may invalidate either simple calculation. No pressure/volume trend alone proves an actual benign event.

### W03 — Volumetric model

V76 capacity 0.05 bbl/vertical ft and gradient difference 0.45 psi/ft give 20 ft/bbl, 9 psi/bbl and 10 bbl per assigned 90 psi exchange. Changed capacity 0.06 gives 16.6667 ft/bbl, 7.5 psi/bbl and 12 bbl. Use the V76 state-board cycle and explain why holding casing pressure forever or bleeding back to the original value is not the learning objective. Inject a 2 bbl unexplained loss; expected response is to hold/escalate under the approved model, not continue a closed-system schedule.

### W04 — Stripping forces and volume

Assigned effective area 12 in² × 800 psi gives 9,600 lbf against 8,500 downward: 1,100 lbf upward imbalance before stated exclusions. At 600 psi, pressure force is 7,200 and downward residual 1,300 before friction/other effects. Explain why neither simplified result alone approves a real transition. For D76, 90 ft × 0.006 = 0.54 bbl and 150 ft gives 0.90. Compare observed 0.20 against expected 0.54: discrepancy 0.34 bbl. Mark the containment state throughout the fictional tool-joint transition; never allow both required barriers open.

### W05 — Lubrication and injection alternatives

L76: 4/0.04 = 100 vertical ft; ×0.48 = 48 psi replacement. Six barrels give 150 ft and 72 psi. Contrast the need for actual segregation evidence with merely counting pump strokes. Bullheading model: 700 + 3,600 − 200 = 4,100 psi; against assigned 4,300 leaves 200. Changed surface 1,000 gives 4,400, exceeding by 100. Explain the risks of unknown destination and losses. Select the appropriate hold rather than applying either method solely because circulation is unavailable.

### W06 — Reverse and subsea paths

R76:60 bbl at 2 bbl/min gives 30 minutes; at 3 gives 20. Draw the annulus-down/tubular-up path and identify return restrictions, ratings and early gas-arrival concerns. For S76-S, calculate riser head 1,040 psi, seawater head 894.4 and difference 145.6. Changed 1,500 ft gives 780,670.8 and 109.2. Explain why these calculations cannot authorize a disconnect and why a surface schedule is not a subsea schedule.

### W07 — Incident and unusual-state case conference

Read the CSB source summary and distinguish its findings from original injects. Original expected trip fill 6.0 versus actual 2.5 gives 3.5 bbl discrepancy. Produce a barrier/event timeline, a specific challenge statement, immediate approved-response decision and corrective controls for alarm management and handover. Add non-shearable tool-position uncertainty and command/achieved-state disagreement. Expected product identifies actual closure capability, independent verification, authority and escalation. Do not invent historical rig data or blame a named person beyond the source evidence.

### W08 — Abandonment and method-selection review

Given two claimed barriers, missing placement evidence for one and an uncovered annular flow path, draw the barrier envelope and hold the equipment-removal transition. Request the applicable verification and technical approval. Then use I76 to compare feasible methods, rejecting any whose assumptions fail. Produce a handover containing current state, uncertainties, limits, actions, responsibilities and restart requirements. Changed case adds pressure rebuild after a nominally passed test; retain the hold and investigate rather than treating the earlier signature as conclusive.

## S76 teaching state board — executable instructor cases

These are original discrete teaching states, not transient well predictions or field procedures. The facilitator keeps the next-state column hidden, asks the learner to state action/reason, records the response and then reveals consequences. Stop a branch immediately for an unsafe decision, explain after scoring, restore the starting state and use changed facts for reassessment. Do not allow a candidate to erase the original outcome. A technical instructor reviews all values and model limitations before use.

Common fictional controls: MONITOR displays/logs state; SECURE invokes the simulator's previously validated pump/containment sequence; HOLD preserves the validated controlled state and calls technical review; RUN-PLAN executes only the named approved simulation schedule; VERIFY requests defined independent evidence. These are educational command labels, not real valves. An actual simulator adapter lists the exact physical controls, interlocks, expected indications and emergency sequence for each command. Learners must demonstrate that adapter procedure for a dynamic award. No generic real-world close/open order is supplied.

| G bullheading: approved candidate plan surface 700, hydrostatic 3600, friction 200, point limit 4300; injection destination evidence initially missing | Calculate 4100, but HOLD until destination/containment and full plan are verified; reveal qualified evidence or a weak-zone conflict based on instructor branch | Numerical margin alone treated as authorization: fail. Bsurface 1000 gives 4400, above limit; reject even if destination is verified |
| H reverse: verified candidate path 60 bbl, rate 2, handling capacity specified in adapter | Identify 30-minute ideal liquid transit, verify full reverse path/limits and begin approved simulation; reveal return restriction and invoke prescribed controlled stop | Wait for 30 minutes despite abnormal return/pressure: critical delay. B rate 3 gives 20; gas arrival may precede simple estimate |
| I unusual state: pumps indicated running, closure command sent, achieved state uncertain | Apply validated emergency adapter sequence, report actual state and request independent confirmation | Repeated blind commands or declaring secure from command only: critical. B non-shearable across a closure element requires approved alternative, not assumed shearing |
| J advanced/subsea: two displays share one failed sensor; one control channel degraded | Identify common cause, retain restriction and obtain independent evidence; combined learner applies validated subsea contingency and datum/line-friction interpretation | Treat displays as independent proof or invent disconnect steps: critical. B changes control availability and riser state; assess applicable response |
| K incident/abandonment: 6.0 expected fill/2.5 actual, alarm suppressed; later two barrier symbols but missing evidence and uncovered annulus | Secure/respond to discrepancy, restore meaningful monitoring through approved process; hold removal until verified complete barrier envelope | Continue on a reassuring narrative or remove equipment because two symbols exist: critical. Bpressure rebuild after test requires new assessment |

For D the discrete model deliberately assumes the specified uniform vertical geometry, gradient difference, no losses and reliable measurement. The pressure numbers represent a pedagogical exchange model, not permitted real-well operations. At gas-arrival transition, changed geometry, loss or approaching limit, the model stops; no further cycle is authorized. For E/F/G/H the adapter must supply validated dynamic schedules and pressure bands. Their absence prevents a dynamic operating award, while the supplied state board still supports complete decision instruction and assessment.

Score five dimensions 0–2: preparation/model validity, technical execution, monitoring/limits, closed-loop communication, evidence/handover. Two=independent correct and reasoned; one=safe but incomplete; zero=wrong/missing/unsafe. Pass each at least 8/10, no zero in safety-related execution/monitoring and every essential outcome met. Critical failures include deliberate barrier bypass, unsupported pressure/volume action, ignoring a positive indication, unauthorized restart, fabricated evidence or unsafe command despite a challenge. Stop the scenario and require remediation plus changed independent reassessment. Technical and team competence must both pass; a polished briefing cannot compensate for lost pressure control.

## I76 integrated individual and team evaluation

Prepare T teaching and independent A/B simulator versions. Supply complete well geometry, fluids, formation/weak-point evidence, equipment/control schematic, verified pressure and handling limits, approved response/kill/contingency schedules, measurement tolerances and acceptance bands. These are configuration inputs for a real simulator, not blanks in the authored lesson. The qualified instructor validates them through a dry run and attaches them to the record before assessment; absent verified inputs prevent that execution, not permission to guess.

Round 1: operate the horizontal detection case, secure under the approved sequence, collect reliable data and report. Round 2: supervisor evaluates the method against actual constraints, briefs the crew and directs a validated circulation or contingency sequence. Across the course every learner completes volumetric, stripping, lubricate/bleed, bullhead and reverse scenarios; a correct rejection of an invalid case is necessary but does not replace a separate valid execution. Round 3: introduce one model-invalidating change and one communication/equipment conflict at safe simulation points. Round 4: confirm achieved controlled condition, hand over remaining limitations and debrief. Combined scope adds a complete subsea run, not just extra arithmetic.

The operator is assessed on recognition, timely approved actions, monitoring, challenge and accurate reporting. The supervisor is assessed on assumptions, method selection, clear instruction, limit management, escalation and verification. Rotate roles for a combined-role award; never credit a role solely from observing another candidate. A desktop-only course records decision-study completion and cannot masquerade as dynamic practical competence.

Expected evidence includes prebrief/barrier map, completed calculations, simulator time series, actions/readbacks, deviations and responses, final achieved state, unresolved items and individual rubric. Pass all claimed-role essential outcomes, all method stations and no critical failure. Timing is evaluated against prevalidated scenario response criteria disclosed in the adapter, not an invented universal number of seconds. Instructor pauses for equipment malfunction are recorded and the affected complete sequence is repeated after restoration.

## Final assessment, remediation and credential

Private Form A/B each has 16 original cases, 48 decisions, 96 points; allow 160 minutes. Pass 77/96 and at least 4/6 in every domain, no unresolved critical error. Decision scoring 2=correct with supported reasoning, 1=safe incomplete, 0=wrong/missing/unsafe. Coursebook/calculator allowed; keys, collaboration and outside assistance prohibited. Reassessment has changed data and retained original context; preserve failures and demonstrate targeted remediation before the new attempt.

Award requires 32 corrected formative checks, 8 labs, 16 stations, I76 in claimed role/scope and final. Issue VNDRLY/U Enhanced Well Control with explicit operator/supervisor, surface/combined and dynamic-versus-decision-study status. It does not renew IWCF/IADC certification or authorize live contingency operations. VNDRLY's one-year review reminder is an administrative follow-up; employer and external qualification rules remain separate. Material authorship is not a learner pass.

# VUC076 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Does a BOP close command prove containment?

**Answer:** No; verify achieved state and relevant pressure/flow evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Command and effective barrier are different facts. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

Who can challenge an unsupported instruction?

**Answer:** Any team member; the lead must acknowledge and resolve it.

**Reason/remediation:** Authority does not replace evidence. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

For 10 ppg and 8,000 ft TVD, what is hydrostatic head?

**Answer:** 4,160 psi.

**Reason/remediation:** Use 0.052 ×density ×TVD, not measured depth. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

Why can denser fluid reduce surface pressure allowance at a shoe?

**Answer:** It consumes more of the fixed allowable total pressure.

**Reason/remediation:** Total pressure at the weak point is the constraint. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

Does a horizontal segment add hydrostatic head equal to its MD?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Volume follows measured length; hydrostatic contribution follows vertical change. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

What changes when build-section capacity rises?

**Answer:** Volume/strokes and schedule assumptions must be revised.

**Reason/remediation:** Unchanged TVD does not mean unchanged displacement. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

What distinguishes the new F76 trace from baseline?

**Answer:** 4.7 versus 2.0 bbl and increasing rather than declining interval returns.

**Reason/remediation:** An unsupported breathing explanation cannot dismiss the anomaly. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L2

Does a matching trace prove benign breathing after fluid changes?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** The baseline conditions and transfer accounting must remain valid. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L1

Which pressure basis belongs in the ideal gas relationship?

**Answer:** Absolute pressure.

**Reason/remediation:** Gauge pressure lacks the required reference. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L2

Why is constant surface pressure not always constant bottomhole pressure?

**Answer:** Hydrostatic contribution changes as gas expands/displaces liquid.

**Reason/remediation:** Control requires a pressure profile, not one fixed display. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L1

What does V76 exchange per barrel?

**Answer:** 9 psi for its stated 0.05 capacity and 0.45 gradient difference.

**Reason/remediation:** One barrel represents 20 vertical ft in that model. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L2

Can the same closed-system schedule continue after losses appear?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Its volume/pressure assumptions no longer hold. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L1

Does 9,600 lbf pressure force versus 8,500 downward show adequate gravity-only insertion?

**Answer:** No, the simplified imbalance is 1,100 lbf upward before other effects.

**Reason/remediation:** Actual force/constraint evaluation is required. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L2

What if expected 0.54 bbl differs from measured 0.20?

**Answer:** Hold and investigate the 0.34 bbl discrepancy.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not falsify a trip record to fit expectation. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L1

Does pumping liquid prove its intended hydrostatic contribution?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Segregation, communication and losses must be verified under the plan. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L2

What is L76 replacement for 4 bbl?

**Answer:** 100 vertical ft and 48 psi under the stated model.

**Reason/remediation:** Capacity and gradient difference set the teaching exchange. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L1

Why does available pumping capacity not establish bullheading suitability?

**Answer:** Destination, containment, formation and equipment limits remain unresolved.

**Reason/remediation:** Injected fluid may enter an unintended weak zone. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L2

What happens to friction contribution when flow stops?

**Answer:** It changes/disappears with the flow state.

**Reason/remediation:** The same surface pressure no longer implies the same pumping pressure balance. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L1

Why can reverse circulation bring fluids to surface sooner?

**Answer:** Its inner return path may have smaller volume.

**Reason/remediation:** Prepare handling before starting rather than waiting for a calculated arrival. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L2

Can a conventional schedule be copied into reverse operation?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Flow path, hydraulics and equipment limits differ. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L1

Is non-shearability a universal property independent of BOP?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Installed shear capability and actual tool condition matter. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L2

What does a conflicting tool-position indication require?

**Answer:** Maintain control and resolve using approved evidence/procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** Blind movement can worsen the closure problem. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L1

Do two displays using one transmitter prove redundancy?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** They share a common failure source. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L2

Does equal nominal working pressure establish replacement compatibility?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Temperature, fluid, geometry and service envelope must match. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L1

What is S76-S head difference?

**Answer:** 145.6 psi under the stated 2,000 ft 10/8.6 ppg model.

Does that difference authorize disconnect?

The course supplies executable discrete state-board cases for teaching. Surface and combined subsea scopes are distinguished, and each claimed method must be demonstrated.
