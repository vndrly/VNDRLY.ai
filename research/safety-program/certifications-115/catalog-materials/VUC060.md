# VNDRLY/U Ultrasonic Examination — Crack Height Sizing

VUC060 · Original materials · 2026-11-18

## Scope and prerequisites

This specialist course addresses conventional ultrasonic sizing of planar features connected to the surface opposite the scanning surface, including weld-root geometry. It preserves the catalog's legacy QUSE scope while distinguishing it from the currently listed API QUTE programs. The available QUSE orientation is dated July 2015; it is historical context, not proof of current external examination rules. [S60-01] VNDRLY completion is not API certification, employer UT qualification or fitness-for-service acceptance.

Require demonstrated conventional UT competence, geometry/arithmetic, safe instrument handling and applicable vision/prerequisite records before independent scored work. The instructor supplies an approved specimen-specific technique and manufacturer instructions. Beginners receive foundation teaching rather than being signed off on advanced sizing. All work uses cool, stable, depressurized training specimens. Actual practical performance is required; course creation is not a claim that any learner has performed it.

## Q01 — Define the quantity before measuring

Crack height, depth to tip, remaining ligament and length along the weld are different quantities. Establish the scanning surface and the connected surface on a section drawing. For a feature connected to the opposite surface of a flat plate of local thickness t, if the tip lies at depth d from the scanning surface, height h=t−d. That equation assumes correct feature identity and the stated geometry. A numerical difference cannot establish those assumptions.

Write the required report quantity and units at the top of the worksheet. If the request says “depth,” clarify whether it means distance from the scanning surface or penetration from the far surface. Do not let an ambiguous column heading propagate into engineering decisions.

**Worked:** local thickness 20 mm, established tip depth 14 mm gives height 6 mm and near-surface ligament 14 mm. **Changed:** the feature is near-surface connected instead. The same depth no longer supports the original far-surface subtraction. Redraw the connection and use the applicable method before reporting. P01 tests the definition, not memorized subtraction.

## Q02 — Procedure, role and safe readiness

Identify the approved sizing technique, material/thickness range, probe/wedge, reference standards, calibration, scanning pattern and reporting limits. The procedure must be suitable for the actual feature and geometry. A technique that detects a response may not resolve the tip accurately enough for sizing. Production acceptance belongs to its authorized decision maker; the examiner reports supported measurements and limitations.

Inspect the bench arrangement, instrument/cable condition, specimen stability, sharp edges and couplant handling. Use compatible materials and safe electrical practices under the equipment manual. Do not test hot, moving, pressurized or energized process equipment on this course's authority. Record interrupted work so a restart does not skip the affected region.

**Worked:** a learner has a conventional detection procedure but no validated sizing method for the specimen. Hold scored sizing and obtain the appropriate technique. **Changed:** the method is supplied but a heavy sample is unsecured. Correct the physical setup before work. P02 requires a completed readiness/authority checklist with actual observations.

## Q03 — Material, weld root and geometry

Review local thickness, surface contour, weld crown/root and reference direction. Geometry can create strong echoes that mask or resemble feature responses. A nominal drawing is a starting point, not proof of the actual specimen profile. Verify accessible dimensions with the approved method and preserve deviations. Do not grind a crown simply to make interpretation easier unless explicitly authorized for that specimen.

Separate known geometric responses from possible crack-related responses through controlled probe movement and corroboration. The strongest echo may be a corner or root, not the crack tip. A complicated root cannot be dismissed as “noise” when it prevents reliable sizing. Report the actual limitation and obtain an appropriate technique or access solution.

**Worked:** nominal thickness 20 mm but verified local thickness 19 mm. With the same established tip depth 14 mm, height is 5 mm, not 6 mm. **Changed:** local thinning varies across the crack length. Pair each tip depth with the corresponding local thickness; do not subtract all depths from a single favorable nominal value.

## Q04 — Sound path and corrected travel time

For a simple pulse-echo material path, S=vt/2 using the correct mode velocity and material-only round-trip transit time. Wedge/electronic delay must be handled by the approved calibration. Use consistent units and name the path. An angle-beam path is longer than its normal-depth component; reporting S as depth can greatly overstate or understate the intended quantity.

Keep raw readings, applied configuration and calculations together. If the velocity or zero changes, determine which prior measurements are affected. Do not correct only the final printed number while leaving the stored setup inconsistent.

**Worked:** corrected transit 10 µs at 3.2 mm/µs gives 16 mm path. **Changed:** raw transit 12 µs contains 2 µs delay; corrected path is still 16 mm, while using raw time gives 19.2 mm. The 3.2 mm error is not a tolerance to ignore. P04 links an actual reference check to this calculation.

## Q05 — Angle, depth and leg interpretation

For a flat first-leg ray at angle θ from the normal, d=S cosθ and offset=S sinθ. Draw the ray before using the formulas. Reflected paths require the proper leg treatment and actual boundary geometry. Curved surfaces require an appropriate model; the flat formula cannot be extended by simply changing the thickness number.

Angle error influences depth. Near a sizing boundary, a small geometric error may materially change reported height. Demonstrate the method on suitable known references over the range used. Do not choose the angle that makes an unknown feature agree with an expected answer.

**Worked:** S=20 mm at 60° gives d=10 mm; in 20 mm plate with a valid far-surface feature, h=10 mm. **Changed:** at 55° the same S gives d=11.4715 mm and h=8.5285 mm. This illustrates sensitivity to angle, not permission to change angles arbitrarily. P05 requires a labeled sketch and verification of the actual configuration.

## Q06 — Probe selection and reference suitability

Select the approved probe/wedge and settings for the expected depth, resolution and geometry. Frequency, pulse characteristics and beam behavior affect whether separate responses can be resolved. A more precise display does not improve physical resolution. Reference blocks must be identified and suitable for the method; a shallow notch alone does not prove deep-tip performance throughout the intended range.

Inspect wear and contact. Record actual system identifiers and software/settings. Any changed probe, wedge or relevant configuration requires the procedure's applicable checks. A previous operator's saved file can assist setup but cannot replace the learner's demonstration.

**Worked:** the packet specifies reference features at three depths, but only the shallow one is checked. Range performance is not established. **Changed:** all references are checked, then the wedge is replaced. Assess and perform required new checks rather than carrying old evidence forward unquestioned. P06 requires actual inventory and reference selection.

## Q07 — Calibration and independent verification

Calibration establishes the measurement relationship; verification tests whether it remains suitable. Use known positions across the relevant range, identify the reference and record actual readings. For each point, signed error is measured minus reference. Evaluate against the announced training criterion and its decision rule. Do not average a high and low error into an apparent pass if each point must comply.

The reference truth must be more reliable than the measurement performance being assessed. Unknown notch depth or unsupported documentation cannot serve as a precise grading standard. If independent truth is inadequate, the specimen cannot be used for scored sizing until resolved.

**Worked:** reference depths 5/15 mm read 5.2/15.8. Errors+0.2/+0.8; against fictional±0.5 mm, the second fails. **Changed:** readings 4.8/15.2 both meet the numerical band, but reference identity is missing. Numeric agreement does not establish traceability. P07 requires actual calibration and a separately recorded verification.

## Q08 — Locating a tip-diffracted response

A feature tip can produce a diffracted response whose arrival helps locate the tip. It may be much weaker than a corner response; mistaking geometry or another mode for the tip creates a false height. Manufacturer teaching illustrates the distinct corner and tip responses, but the actual specimen requires a demonstrated technique. [S60-02]

Follow the approved probe movement, range and evaluation settings to seek a repeatable response consistent with the feature geometry. Corroborate location using permitted positions/directions. Preserve the observed waveform and the reasoning for identification. If a unique tip cannot be established, report sizing as unresolved rather than selecting a convenient small echo.

**Worked:** strong corner echo and earlier weak response are present, but the weak response does not track consistently with probe movement. It is not yet a proven tip. **Changed:** repeated approved observations establish a consistent tip location. Record that evidence and then calculate height; do not claim the initial uncertainty never existed. P08 uses actual known-feature specimens under supervision.

## Q09 — Amplitude sizing and equivalent reflectors

Amplitude-drop and equivalent-reflector approaches describe response behavior under particular assumptions. Beam width, orientation, shape and coupling influence the result. BINDT notes limitations when flaw geometry/orientation is complex. [S60-03] Equivalent reflector size is not automatically the actual physical height of a crack; DGS relates response to a reference model. [S60-04]

Use amplitude information only as the approved sizing technique permits. A half-amplitude point is about−6.02 dB relative to the peak, but finding it does not by itself validate the physical boundary. Document reference, evaluation gain, clipping and beam conditions. Do not switch methods mid-examination solely to obtain a more favorable number.

**Worked:** peak 80% and comparison 40% give ratio 0.5 and−6.0206 dB. **Changed:** the peak was clipped. The ratio is no longer established from that display; restore a valid approved measurement basis and repeat. P09 demonstrates the response limitation on a reference, without awarding crack-height accuracy from amplitude alone.

## Q10 — Scan the full feature and find maximum height

A crack can vary in height along its length. A single favorable section does not establish the maximum. Use the procedure's scan pattern over the entire assigned feature, maintain contact and track position. Record local thickness and identified tip depth at each required location. The maximum supported height is the largest valid local height, not the average.

If contact or interpretation fails at an interval, that interval remains unresolved. A high confidence result elsewhere does not fill the gap. Rescan under valid conditions and preserve the original coverage record. Do not silently exclude a difficult crown or edge.

**Worked:** valid local heights 3.2, 4.1, 3.8 mm have maximum 4.1 mm. **Changed:** an unexamined interval lies between the first two locations. The maximum of measured values remains 4.1, but the whole-feature maximum is not established. P10 requires actual systematic scanning and an explicit coverage map.

## Q11 — Multiple tips, branching and ambiguous boundaries

More than one plausible tip response may occur. Do not select the smallest height by preference or assume every extra response belongs to a separate crack. Compare repeatability, geometry and approved corroborating evidence. Report the greatest supported height within the procedure's definition, while preserving uncertainty about unresolved features.

Separate resolution limits from absence of a feature. If two responses merge, adding decimal places does not identify their true boundaries. An alternative approved technique may be needed; invoking its name without actual qualification and performance does not resolve the result.

**Worked:** far-surface feature in 20 mm plate has established tips at depths 14 and 12 mm; corresponding heights 6 and 8 mm. Maximum supported height 8 mm. **Changed:** the 12 mm response is later proven geometric. Withdraw that interpretation with a traceable revision; 6 mm remains supported only if the remaining feature and full coverage are valid. P11 requires evidence-based reasoning, not guessing a maximum.

## Q12 — Repeatability, bias and uncertainty

Repeat measurements can show consistency without proving correctness. Three readings all displaced by a wrong zero may agree closely. Compare against independent known truth as well as repeatability. Record signed errors to reveal systematic over- or undersizing. Do not treat average zero error as proof that every measurement meets tolerance.

Uncertainty describes limitations in the measurement and reference basis; it is not a discretionary allowance to add or subtract until a result passes. Use the predeclared decision rule. Where the evidence cannot resolve whether a criterion is met, state that rather than rounding away the issue.

**Worked:** errors+1,−1,+1,−1 mm have mean 0 but RMS 1 mm. **Changed:** all four errors+0.8 mm have RMS 0.8 and mean+0.8, showing bias despite smaller RMS. These are training calculations, not API scoring rules. P12 compares actual repeat readings with protected reference truth after the attempt.

## Q13 — Units, conversion and report precision

Keep the instrument, worksheet and final report units consistent. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm. A height expressed as a decimal fraction of an inch is not the same number in millimeters. Retain sufficient intermediate precision and round only according to the reporting instruction. Never use rounding to move a failed measured error inside its limit.

Record the measured quantity explicitly, such as far-surface-connected height, rather than an unlabeled “depth.” Include coordinate convention and local thickness basis. If a conversion reveals an implausible result, revisit the source reading rather than silently altering it.

**Worked:**0.25 in equals 6.35 mm. **Changed:**0.20 in equals 5.08 mm, difference 1.27 mm. A worksheet containing 6.35 under an “inches” heading is not a harmless cosmetic error. P13 requires a checked dual-unit report with a second calculation or independent review.

## Q14 — Drift, changes and recovery

Perform verification at required intervals and after relevant changes. A failed check identifies a potentially affected examination period; assess records since the last valid check under the procedure. A successful later correction does not validate old results automatically. Preserve the failure, affected IDs, corrective action and actual repeat examination.

Changes to probe, wedge, velocity, range or evaluation method may affect prior data differently. Identify the real dependency rather than repeating unrelated work or overlooking affected work. Do not erase original measurements to make the file look clean.

**Worked:** last valid 10:00, failure 12:00; five specimens scanned between. All five require affected-scope assessment. **Changed:** only the fifth is repeated after correction. The first four remain unresolved unless the approved disposition specifically establishes their validity. P14 uses a safe instructor-issued drift condition and actual recovery records.

## Q15 — Reporting to engineering without overclaiming

Provide specimen/component identity, procedure, actual equipment, configuration, references, coverage, measured quantity/units, maximum supported height and limitations. Distinguish the recorded result from an acceptance decision. A sizing examiner does not independently convert a height into remaining life or safe operating pressure without the relevant authority and assessment.

Make revisions traceable. If later evidence changes tip identification or local thickness, show the original value, new basis, affected result and recipient notification through the authorized workflow. Do not bury a meaningful limitation in a generic disclaimer.

**Worked:** report says “crack depth 6 mm, acceptable” without surface convention or acceptance basis. Correct the quantity/coordinate statement and remove unsupported disposition. **Changed:** authorized engineering acceptance arrives for a named condition. Attach it with its scope; do not imply it applies to another component or changed measurement.

## Q16 — Blind performance and continuing competence

Independent assessment requires an unseen specimen set with protected, validated truth and adequate range of feature heights/geometry. The learner sets up, verifies, scans and reports without coaching. Written answers and familiar demonstration specimens cannot replace it. Scoring must be announced before the attempt and must not be altered after seeing results.

Use the outcome to diagnose errors: wrong quantity, calibration bias, tip confusion, coverage gap or reporting error. Teach the cause and reassess on changed specimens. Retain evidence of failed and successful attempts. Course completion records only the actual demonstrated scope; employer qualification and production authorization remain separate.

**Worked:** nine of ten heights meet the announced band, but one specimen was never scanned and its value was copied. This is an integrity failure, not 90% competence. **Changed:** the learner honestly reports an unresolved size. That avoids fabrication but still requires remediation and a valid changed demonstration before the practical can pass. I60 assesses actual performance, not confidence.
