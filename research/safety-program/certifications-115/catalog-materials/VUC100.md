# VNDRLY/U Control Room Operator Emergency Response

VUC100 • Original training and assessment materials • 18 November 2026

Purpose: prepare and assess an experienced, employer-nominated CRO in simulated offshore emergencies. This package is not OPITO approval, an external certificate, or asset authorization. Full proprietary performance criteria were unavailable; public scope and regulator guidance inform this original package. No real plant controls, emergency channels or live alarms are operated during exercises.

Entry: employer-confirmed plant/control-room competence, current emergency-plan familiarization, supported readiness declaration, and fitness for the planned simulation. A new operator completes prerequisite development first. Planned delivery: 40 hours—16 instruction and guided practice, 8 laboratories, 4 familiarization/readiness, 8 individual scenario assessment/debrief, 2 controlled written assessment, 2 remediation planning. Add time when needed; elapsed time alone earns nothing.

Objectives: establish readiness; assess evidence and alarms; execute and verify authorized responses; coordinate communications, responders and people accounting; manage degraded systems and refuge threats; preserve a defensible handover and recovery record.

## Q100-01 — Readiness and the CRO boundary

The control room operator maintains a reliable picture of the installation and performs the actions allocated to that role in the emergency plan. An alarm display is one source of evidence, not the whole emergency. Information also arrives from field personnel, permit records, fire and gas systems, marine operations and the incident commander. Learn which decisions you may make immediately under standing instructions, which require consultation, and how to escalate when the normal decision maker is unavailable. Waiting for permission that the emergency procedure already grants can be as dangerous as inventing authority you do not have.

Before taking a shift, verify current procedure revisions, plant configuration, impairments and compensating controls, simultaneous operations, people on board, weather, communications and relief arrangements. Read back unresolved issues to the outgoing operator. The incoming operator must explicitly accept the handover; departure of the outgoing operator is not evidence of acceptance. Obtain support when fatigue, unfamiliar equipment or missing information undermines readiness. The employer must establish asset competence and readiness for emergency assessment; a classroom certificate cannot establish these facts.

Worked case: the handover sheet says pump B is available, but a later isolation record says it is locked out. Preserve both records, establish the current authorized status and identify the remaining protection. Do not mark the pump available to make the readiness checklist green. Notify the responsible supervisor and use the site's impairment process before continuing affected operations.

## Q100-02 — Building and updating the situation picture

Separate observation, interpretation and uncertainty. A detector reports a signal at a named location and time; it does not by itself establish the extent or cause of a release. Record tag, timestamp, quality flag, trend, related indications and corroborating reports. Distinguish a live value from a frozen value that still looks plausible. A display refreshed at 10:04 may contain a measurement last taken at 09:55. Ask what changed first, what can escalate next, and what evidence would invalidate the current explanation.

Use the current plant schematic and cause-and-effect information to locate affected systems and dependencies. Maintain a brief status board showing confirmed conditions, suspected conditions, completed actions with confirmation, and outstanding actions with owners. Update it after each material change. An earlier safe route or working system can become unavailable; do not let a stale board guide a new team into danger.

Worked case: two gas indications rise, a pressure trend falls and a field caller reports a loud hiss. The operator need not identify the failed component before taking the prescribed protective action. Report a suspected release supported by those observations, initiate the applicable response, and avoid sending someone to confirm it by entering the suspected hazard area.

## Q100-03 — Alarm priorities and attention management

An alarm requires assessment or action according to its defined response. Acknowledge only to show receipt; acknowledgment does not correct the condition or prove the action was completed. Use priority, consequence, time available, first-out information and the response procedure to organize work. Do not process a flood merely in the order rows appear. Identify the initiating event while retaining attention to independent hazards and essential safety-system failures.

Request the assigned support operator when workload exceeds safe capacity. Delegate specific tasks with an owner, expected feedback and deadline; retain oversight of the emergency picture. Record alarm suppression or shelving only through the approved authorization and tracking process. Silencing a nuisance sound must never conceal an unresolved critical alarm. Use text and tags as well as colors, especially when displays or vision make colors unreliable.

Worked case: a compressor trip produces many dependent alarms while a temporary-refuge air intake alarm appears separately. The intake alarm may threaten people even though it is not the first event. Apply its response and inform command while a second operator organizes the compressor sequence. Do not clear all alarms to make the screen readable.

## Q100-04 — Executing and verifying protective actions

For each action, identify the correct equipment, controlling procedure and authority. Read the action and its prerequisites, select the correct control, operate it in the simulator, then verify the resulting state from the designated feedback. A command sent is not a valve closed. A green command icon can coexist with missing position feedback or continued flow. Log the command time, observed response and uncertainty. Escalate failed or ambiguous actions using the defined alternative response.

Understand the difference between normal process control, process shutdown and emergency shutdown on the particular installation. The course does not prescribe a universal shutdown, depressurization or ventilation sequence. These can have interacting consequences. Never bypass an interlock, force an output or reset protection to restore production during an unresolved emergency. Preserve safeguards and obtain the authorized engineering and command decisions required for recovery.

Worked case: simulated isolation command XV-21 is issued at 10:03. The closed indication never arrives and flow remains indicated. Report attempted isolation, not confirmed isolation. Follow the simulated failed-isolation branch and advise the commander that the hazard may continue. A radio statement that someone thinks the valve moved does not replace the required confirmation.

## Q100-05 — Communications that close the loop

Use the emergency channel and message format specified by the installation. Give identity, location, incident, immediate hazards, actions taken, assistance required and the next update. Use exact equipment and route identifiers; ask the recipient to read back safety-critical instructions and correct discrepancies before proceeding. A transmitted instruction without acknowledgment is an outstanding task. Keep a fallback channel and communications-loss procedure available.

Protect channel capacity by separating urgent action messages from routine updates. Assign a recorder or communicator where the emergency organization allows, but ensure the person controlling plant receives material changes. Tell command when a message is unconfirmed. Do not assume an email, paging message or automated alert reached a person. External agencies require the facility identity, location, contact channel, incident and support request; the authorized communicator follows the local plan.

Worked case: CRO requests route R2 and receives a readback of R1. Stop and correct the mismatch rather than accepting a general yes. If primary radio then fails, establish the approved alternate link, verify both parties can communicate, and repeat the current instruction. Do not send an unbriefed runner through an uncertain hazard area.

## Q100-06 — Coordinating responders and control of work

The CRO supports command with plant state, hazards and protection status. The incident commander and response leaders retain their allocated authority over tactical deployment. Before reporting an area safe, determine exactly which evidence is required. A stopped pump does not prove zero pressure, a closed valve does not prove isolation integrity, and a clear detector in one location does not prove an entire area safe.

Identify active permits, contractors and simultaneous operations affected by the event. Communicate the emergency restrictions and obtain acknowledgment through the control-of-work arrangements. Preserve the permit and isolation record so a relief operator can distinguish suspended work from completed work. Track response teams by identity, task, location, communications and next report; do not invent missing information to complete a board.

Worked case: a maintenance team asks to restart an isolated fan because visibility is poor. The fan's relationship to gas spread and refuge protection is not established. Refer the request through the approved emergency procedure and competent authority; do not use a production preference as authorization. Inform the response leader of the unresolved condition and the available verified information.

## Q100-07 — People, muster and missing-person information

People accounting uses verified identities and locations, not just a total. Maintain the baseline roster and authorized changes; distinguish people at muster, assigned response personnel, casualties, transferred people and unconfirmed reports. A person reported by two muster points must count once. Reconcile discrepancies with the muster coordinator rather than overwriting them. Record the time and source of every correction.

Pass missing-person information promptly: name or identifier, last confirmed location and time, likely task, known hazards and source reliability. The CRO does not dispatch an unprotected search solely because a roster is short. The incident commander uses this information to make the response decision. Continue updating accountability during relocation or evacuation; transfer records must preserve who left, destination and confirmation of receipt.

Worked case: the roster contains P01 through P12. A list contains P01 through P10 plus P10 again and P11. Twelve entries represent eleven unique people; P12 remains unaccounted for. Report that discrepancy rather than declaring all present. If P12 is later verified in the medical area, record source and time, and inform the coordinator before closing the discrepancy.

## Q100-08 — Refuge, evacuation and time-dependent decisions

Monitor the condition and availability of temporary refuge, escape routes, evacuation means and associated communications according to the installation plan. Endurance is conditional on the scenario and functioning protective systems. Do not treat a design duration as a guarantee after an impairment. Communicate deteriorating air, temperature, power, access or protection status to command immediately and follow the applicable response.

Time calculations support decisions; they do not authorize waiting until the arithmetic reaches zero. Use a common clock and state assumptions. Compare the latest credible time to loss of a safe option with the time needed to decide, communicate, move people and complete the action. Include uncertainty and the actual plan's safety allowances. A route marked blocked must not be used merely because it is the shortest on the map.

Worked case: a synthetic exercise gives a provisional refuge endpoint of 10:30. At 10:18, twelve minutes remain; preparation and movement are estimated at nine minutes. The nominal margin is three minutes, not permission to delay three minutes. A new HVAC fault invalidates the earlier endurance assumption. Notify command now and reassess available options under the emergency plan.

## Q100-09 — Loss of utilities, displays and control-room habitability

Emergency operation can lose its usual information and control channels. Recognize failures of electrical power, instrument air, network links, sensors, displays, communications and ventilation as possible common-cause problems. A frozen trend can be mistaken for stability. Record the last valid time, use approved independent information where available, and report degraded control capability. Never improvise electrical switching or system restoration beyond your authority.

Know the criteria and process for moving to an alternate control location. Verify its communications, information and control capability; do not assume it mirrors the primary room. Transfer critical tasks and records with explicit acceptance while maintaining the actions required by the emergency procedure. If the primary room is unsafe, life protection and the evacuation plan govern; a neat handover is not a reason to remain in danger.

Worked case: the display still says healthy, but timestamps have stopped and two communications channels fail together. Declare the information unreliable, use the approved degraded-mode response, and tell command what can and cannot be confirmed. An estimated backup duration must be recalculated if its load changes or its condition is unknown.

## Q100-10 — Human performance, challenge and relief

Stress narrows attention and can cause fixation on the first diagnosis. Use concise checkbacks: What do we know? What is uncertain? What is the immediate threat? Who owns the next action? What will trigger reassessment? Invite a second operator to challenge the picture. A junior colleague's observed discrepancy is evidence worth checking, not a challenge to rank.

Avoid giving one person incompatible simultaneous tasks. Prioritize life protection and emergency control, then arrange support for records and communications. State overload early. If you make a wrong selection in the simulator, disclose it immediately and follow the safe correction procedure rather than hiding it. During relief, summarize incident state, confirmed and failed actions, impairments, people status, deadlines and unacknowledged messages. Require the incoming operator to read back the critical items.

Worked case: an operator is answering external calls while monitoring a deteriorating refuge. Assign authorized communications support and confirm who is watching refuge trends. If the relief operator hears isolation complete when the record says command sent/no feedback, correct the discrepancy before transfer. The outgoing operator remains responsible until accepted relief or the emergency plan requires otherwise.

## Q100-11 — Records, recovery and learning

An emergency log is a decision record, not a retrospective story of a perfect response. Use event time and entry time when they differ. Record source, fact, uncertainty, action, owner, acknowledgment and result. Preserve original errors with a visible correction and reason. Retain simulator traces and messages for assessment. Protect personal and medical information according to local requirements while still providing necessary emergency information.

Emergency stabilization does not authorize production restart. Recovery needs the appropriate command decision, verification of safeguards, treatment of isolations and permits, equipment checks and the site's restart process. Record unresolved conditions in the relief and recovery handover. Debrief actions and system conditions: what information was available, why a decision seemed reasonable, where the process failed and what change will prevent recurrence. Assign corrective actions with owners and evidence of closure.

Worked case: the fire indication clears but the detector is faulted and valve feedback remains absent. Record those facts; do not close the incident as equipment healthy. The recovery decision must address the failed evidence and required checks. An earlier optimistic verbal report should be corrected, not deleted.

## Q100-12 — Integrating performance across changed emergencies

Competence requires coherent performance through changing conditions. Start with readiness, recognize the event, act within authority, verify results, coordinate people and responders, manage degraded conditions and give a reliable handover. The assessor must observe the learner operating the simulator and communicating with role players, not merely describing what an ideal operator would do. Two different emergencies prevent success by memorizing one sequence.

Before assessment, study the fictional installation data and practice the interface without exposure to final inject timing. During each assessed scenario, maintain a working status board and log. Explain reasoning when asked after the action; the assessor should not coach decisions during the run. If an unsafe decision occurs, the controller stops the affected simulation, preserves evidence and records the failure. Remediation includes the underlying knowledge and a changed scenario, not repeating the exact sequence until it looks familiar.

Worked case: a learner handles a release well but declares all accounted for from a duplicate muster list in the second exercise. Strong performance in the first exercise does not cancel the critical accounting error. Correct the misconception, practice identity reconciliation and reassess with a different roster and emergency. Completion is recorded only after all required evidence meets the decision rules.

# VUC100 practical workbook and instructor delivery guide

## Facility, personnel and safeguards

Use an isolated training simulator or a clearly labeled inert control-panel mock-up with simulated feedback, alarm/event list, plant diagram, status board, clocks, recorded role-player communications and printable forms below. It must have no connection to production controls, live alarm systems or public emergency numbers. A competent CRO emergency instructor controls injects; a separate assessor observes each learner. Role players cover commander, field leader, muster coordinator, relief operator and shore communicator. One person may combine roles only where timing remains credible and the learner can distinguish each identity.

Provide induction, breaks, accessibility arrangements, fitness screening appropriate to the simulation, a stop word and a real-emergency procedure. Never create actual toxic atmosphere, fire, loss of ventilation, electrical failure or blocked escape. A real emergency ends the exercise. No pressure to continue a distressing scenario overrides safety. Adjust presentation access without coaching assessed decisions. Log accommodations and ensure the assessed functions remain observable.

Before delivery, instructor completes adaptation record A100: installation model/revision___ ERP revision___ role authorities___ alarm response cards___ cause/effect references___ controls/feedback___ impairment rules___ communications/fallback___ muster roles___ refuge assumptions___ evacuation options___ alternate-room capability___ restart authority___ assessor approvals___. For a workplace-specific assessment, use the employer's approved data and validated simulator model. This self-contained fictional model is sufficient for the original VNDRLY simulation package but not proof of competence on an actual asset.

## Fictional installation Meridian — learner reference R100

Training facility only. Modules M1 process, M2 utilities, TR temporary refuge, CCR primary control and ACR alternate control. Routes R1 crosses M1; R2 bypasses M1 to TR; R3 links TR to the training evacuation point. Availability is determined by current injects, not the drawing alone. No learner physically enters a hazard area.

ERP revision 6 applies. CRO may immediately raise the simulated general alarm, notify command, initiate the indicated simulated protective control and report failure under the response cards. The incident commander controls team deployment and evacuation decisions. CRO cannot authorize entry, bypass safeguards or restart production. If commander contact fails, use the named deputy via the verified fallback channel and the ERP standing protective actions; do not wait to raise an alarm or execute an already authorized response. Field entry needs the response leader's authorized plan and its verified protection requirements.

Response card G: two valid gas indications in M1 or a corroborated reported release require general alarm, command notification and simulated M1 isolation command XV-21. Verify closed feedback AND the scenario's flow-zero confirmation. If either is absent, label isolation unconfirmed, report continuing hazard and invoke failure branch F. F requires command escalation, preserving protection and keeping responders informed; no local entry or bypass. Response card T: TR intake threat requires immediate command notification and simulated protective ventilation command TR-P under this model; verify feedback. Missing feedback means protection uncertain, not achieved. These are invented training actions, not instructions for a real installation.

Channel E is primary; channel B is approved fallback. Test two-way communication before relying on B. Callsigns: CRO, Command, Field, Muster, Relief, Shore. Critical instructions require exact readback. Support operator may organize alarm history and logging while CRO retains priority control. ACR has voice and independent status cards but NO remote plant controls unless a later explicit exercise card changes that capability. Shutdown and evacuation are not equivalent actions. Recovery requires command release plus documented resolution of safeguards, permits, isolations and checks in the actual authorized process.

## Scenario A — release, failed isolation and refuge threat

Learner receives shift packet: 12 people P01–P12; pump B isolated; duty commander available on E; R1 unavailable because it crosses M1; R2 initially available; R3 initially available. Detector tags G1/G2 valid. Model instruction G applies. TR provisional endpoint 10:30 is a controller-provided estimate, not guaranteed endurance. Initial alternate power estimate 20 minutes at stated exercise load; no assumption after a load or condition change.

Controller sequence, relative to 10:00: at minute 0, valid G1/G2 rise, process pressure falls and Field reports hiss in M1. Expected: recognize corroborated release, raise alarm, notify Command, execute G and verify. At minute 3, acknowledge XV-21 command but withhold closed feedback and show continuing flow. Expected: F branch and accurate unconfirmed status. At minute 5, deliver 30 dependent alarms plus separate TR intake alarm. Expected: T response, priority management and support delegation. At minute 7, Field asks to enter through R1 because the pump is stopped; require role/route/verification challenge. At minute 9, give muster entries P01–P10, P10, P11. Expected: eleven unique, P12 missing. At minute 10, Command reads R1 back to a requested R2 instruction; break E after the mismatch. Expected: correct, verify B, repeat current instruction. At minute 13, Muster provides verified P12 at medical area, source Medic at 10:13. Expected: reconcile twelve unique and record correction. At minute 18, provide nine-minute movement estimate and endpoint 10:30: twelve remaining, nominal margin three. Immediately inject failed TR protection feedback. Expected: report changed assumption and urgent reassessment; no guaranteed margin. At minute 21, relief arrives and calls XV-21 confirmed. Expected: correct status, transfer outstanding actions explicitly. End when the learner provides a coherent handover, not at an arbitrary successful-looking screen.

Role-player rules: answer requests only from the reference packet and injects. If asked for unspecified information, say not yet confirmed and log the request; never invent a reassuring answer. Command acknowledges timely reports but does not supply the learner's next step. Field does not enter on an unsafe instruction; controller freezes the simulation and records the critical error. Muster provides identities, not a precomputed correct answer. Controller records when each inject was issued and when required actions occurred.

## Scenario B — utility failure and degraded information

Independent changed scenario, not a replay. Roster B01–B14. At 14:00, displays refresh but measurements are timestamped 13:52, quality uncertain; primary power and channel E fail. ACR has voice/status only. R2 is now blocked; R3 status unknown. Initial TR estimate endpoint 14:25 is provisional. Active permit W7 is suspended on the system but its contractor has not acknowledged. Backup-load estimate is 18 minutes at the original load; no operational guarantee or instruction to remain in a threatened room.

At minute 0, learner must recognize eight-minute stale data and apply degraded-mode response, notify command through tested B and disclose ACR limits. At minute 3, Field asks whether W7 work can continue. Expected: emergency restrictions and safe communication/escalation, not reliance on a database flag. At minute 5, issue muster B01–B12, B12, B13: thirteen unique, B14 missing. At minute 7, introduce a colleague's contradictory valid local status report; expected evidence review, not dismissal. At minute 10, give 12-minute movement estimate: fifteen minutes to provisional endpoint, nominal margin three. Then declare R3 unavailable and new TR intake fault: previous estimate invalid. At minute 12, controller confirms B14 at a different verified muster point; reconcile fourteen. At minute 14, Command proposes relocating CCR functions to ACR. Expected: describe its limitations, follow habitability/transfer plan and verify task acceptance; do not imply full remote control. At minute 17, display recovers but one safety feedback remains faulted; production asks for restart. Expected: preserve unresolved status and recovery gate. At minute 20, conduct relief and debrief. Require all essential decisions even if a learner recognizes them earlier than the nominal inject time.

## Changed remediation scenario C

Use only after documented remediation. Roster C01–C16 with C01–C14, C14, C15; C16 initially unconfirmed and later verified by Medic. Start 16:00, valid release information in M2; reference card G-C substitutes XV-31 and requires closed feedback plus flow-zero. Supply closed feedback but omit flow-zero. TR endpoint 16:24, at 16:12 movement estimate ten minutes: twelve remaining, nominal margin two. Introduce blocked previously selected route, wrong readback, support operator overload and a recovered display with unresolved detector fault. Instructor must validate all substituted tags/response cards before use. The learner receives the revised reference, not hidden criteria. Preserve two successful independent scenarios within a maximum three assessed attempts under this package; if the attempt limit is exhausted, require further development and a newly documented assessment plan. Do not count repeated coached runs as successful independent assessments.

## Scoring, remediation and credential decision

Score each of 12 station dimensions 0–4: 0 absent/unsafe; 1 major assistance or missing essentials; 2 safe but incomplete or materially prompted; 3 independently safe, accurate and traceable; 4 independently safe with timely anticipation and clear explanation. Require at least 39/48 and every dimension at least 3, with no critical error. Both integrated scenarios must independently satisfy all relevant criteria; do not average a failure away. Critical errors include unauthorized bypass/restart, false isolation confirmation, knowingly sending a person toward an unverified hazard, false all-accounted status, concealing failed communications or remaining in a simulated unsafe room contrary to the plan. Freeze unsafe simulated action, record the evidence and remediate before reassessment.

Complete all 24 formative checks and six labs with corrected explanations, pass the separate controlled final (72 points, pass 58, at least 4/6 each domain, no critical error), and achieve the observed simulator decision above. Written exam time is 120 minutes. Keys remain in the restricted instructor folder. Remediation identifies the failed reasoning, provides changed guided practice and then uses the changed final or scenario without coaching. Preserve initial and subsequent results. An assessor reviews borderline evidence and any appeal through a second competent reviewer; do not lower criteria to meet a schedule.

Issue only the original VNDRLY/U Control Room Operator Emergency Response — Simulation Assessed certificate. Record learner identity, package hash, scenario versions, assessment dates, assessor, prerequisite evidence and explicit fictional/asset scope. No OPITO certificate, real-emergency experience or employer appointment is implied. Site-specific competence and continuing drills remain employer responsibilities. Review after significant plant/procedure/role changes or performance concerns; plan reassessment at least every three years as the public OPITO recommendation indicates, while following any stricter local requirement. This package's completion is a materials-authoring milestone, not evidence that any person has completed its training.

# VUC100 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q100-01-L1

A readiness sheet and a later isolation record disagree. What is the safe handover?

**Answer:** Resolve the current authorized status, retain the discrepancy and notify the responsible supervisor before accepting affected readiness.

**Reason/remediation:** A ticked checklist cannot override a current impairment. Revisit Q100-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-01-L2

Does course completion authorize independent CRO duty?

**Answer:** No. Employer asset competence, readiness and appointment remain necessary.

**Reason/remediation:** Simulation evidence has a defined scope. Revisit Q100-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-02-L1

A display refreshes now but its measurement is nine minutes old. Which time matters?

**Answer:** The measurement time determines the age of the evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** A refreshed screen is not a fresh measurement. Revisit Q100-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-02-L2

Must you identify the failed flange before responding to corroborated release indications?

**Answer:** No. Perform the prescribed protective response and report the uncertainty.

**Reason/remediation:** Diagnosis must not delay required protection. Revisit Q100-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-03-L1

Does acknowledging a critical alarm close its action?

**Answer:** No; track response and verified result separately.

**Reason/remediation:** Receipt, command and achieved state are different facts. Revisit Q100-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-03-L2

A flood accompanies a separate refuge alarm. What is the priority method?

**Answer:** Use consequence and response requirements; allocate support and address the independent refuge threat.

**Reason/remediation:** Chronological processing can miss a life-threatening independent event. Revisit Q100-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-04-L1

XV-21 receives a close command with no feedback and continuing flow. What is its state?

**Answer:** Isolation attempted, not confirmed; follow the failed-action branch and inform command.

**Reason/remediation:** Command success cannot be inferred from selection. Revisit Q100-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-04-L2

May protection be bypassed to clear nuisance trips during an unresolved emergency?

**Answer:** No. Preserve protection and follow the authorized response.

**Reason/remediation:** Convenience is not bypass authority. Revisit Q100-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-05-L1

R2 is requested but R1 is read back. What next?

**Answer:** Stop and correct the route identifier, obtain correct readback and verify the current route status.

**Reason/remediation:** A general acknowledgment can hide a dangerous mismatch. Revisit Q100-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-05-L2

A primary link fails after a message. Is the instruction received?

**Answer:** Not unless acknowledged; establish and test the approved fallback and repeat the current instruction.

**Reason/remediation:** Transmission is not receipt. Revisit Q100-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-06-L1

Does stopped equipment prove zero stored energy?

**Answer:** No. Report the actual known state and use the approved isolation verification.

**Reason/remediation:** A stopped machine may retain pressure or energy. Revisit Q100-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-06-L2

Who decides response-team tactics?

**Answer:** The allocated incident commander and response leader; CRO supplies verified plant information and performs assigned controls.

**Reason/remediation:** Coordination must preserve role boundaries. Revisit Q100-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-07-L1

P01–P10 plus P10 and P11 are listed against P01–P12. What is the count?

**Answer:** Eleven unique people; P12 is unaccounted for.

**Reason/remediation:** Twelve entries include one duplicate. Revisit Q100-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-07-L2

May an unprotected search be ordered solely to balance the roster?

**Answer:** No. Escalate last-known-location evidence for a command-led risk decision.

**Reason/remediation:** Accounting pressure does not remove exposure hazards. Revisit Q100-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-08-L1

At 10:18 the provisional endpoint is 10:30 and movement needs nine minutes. What is the nominal margin?

**Answer:** Twelve minutes remaining minus nine gives three minutes; report uncertainty and do not delay by that margin.

**Reason/remediation:** The endpoint is conditional and can deteriorate. Revisit Q100-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-08-L2

A new HVAC fault occurs. Does the old endurance remain guaranteed?

**Answer:** No. Notify command and reassess under the plan.

**Reason/remediation:** An impairment can invalidate the calculation assumptions. Revisit Q100-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-09-L1

A steady trend has stopped timestamp updates. What should be reported?

**Answer:** The value is stale and current condition unconfirmed; apply degraded-mode procedures.

**Reason/remediation:** A flat display can be failure rather than stability. Revisit Q100-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-09-L2

Does the alternate room automatically have full control?

**Answer:** No. Verify its actual available controls, information and communications.

**Reason/remediation:** Location change is not capability proof. Revisit Q100-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-10-L1

One operator is overloaded by external calls and refuge monitoring. What changes?

**Answer:** Allocate authorized support with explicit task ownership and retain monitoring coverage.

**Reason/remediation:** Delegation needs confirmation rather than an assumption. Revisit Q100-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-10-L2

A relief readback converts attempted isolation into completed isolation. What next?

**Answer:** Correct it before acceptance and transfer the unresolved action explicitly.

**Reason/remediation:** A mistaken handover can erase a critical hazard. Revisit Q100-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-11-L1

Should an erroneous log entry be deleted?

**Answer:** Preserve it with a traceable correction, time and reason.

**Reason/remediation:** A reliable record explains what was known and when. Revisit Q100-11 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-11-L2

The fire indication clears but the detector is faulted. May production restart?

**Answer:** Not from that indication alone; apply authorized recovery checks and resolve failed evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Alarm clearance is not proof of safe restoration. Revisit Q100-11 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-12-L1

Can excellent first-scenario performance average out a critical second-scenario failure?

**Answer:** No. Remediate and reassess the failed competence in a changed scenario.

**Reason/remediation:** Critical decisions require independent satisfactory evidence. Revisit Q100-12 and perform its changed application.

## Q100-12-L2

Does discussing a response replace observed simulator operation?

**Answer:** No. Observe control use, communication, verification and recordkeeping.

**Reason/remediation:** Knowing a description does not demonstrate integrated performance. Revisit Q100-12 and perform its changed application.
