# VUC107 — Cathodic Protection Tester

Original VNDRLY/U training materials. This is not AMPP certification or permission for electrical, excavation, hot-work or specialist design tasks.

## Q107-01 — Role, preparation and safe boundaries

A tester produces trustworthy measurements and identifies conditions needing a more experienced corrosion practitioner. A reading is not a design decision or proof that a whole asset is protected. Before work, identify the structure, owner, drawing revision, test-point identity, electrolyte, material, applicable procedure and person authorized to interpret results. Review the route, access, traffic, weather, communications, electrical hazards and buried services. A familiar test box may contain foreign bonds or induced voltage: familiarity does not prove it safe.

Use the instrument and leads rated for the actual environment and anticipated voltage. Inspect insulation, fuses, probes, enclosure and battery; check calibration status and function before and after the task. Do not insert a probe into an unknown terminal or open an energized rectifier cabinet merely to complete a worksheet. Electrical work, hazardous-area equipment, excavation, confined spaces and exothermic attachment require separate authorization and controls. Stop for damaged equipment, unexpected touch voltage, gas alarm, lightning, loss of safe access or uncertain asset identity. Report the incomplete test rather than inventing a value.

All hands-on teaching here uses an isolated, current-limited low-voltage simulator with accessible protected terminals. Actual field demonstrations require an approved task plan and competent supervision. Worked decision: the test point is locked and the record is due today. Photograph the identifier from a safe position, record inaccessible/not tested, notify the owner and arrange authorized access. An old reading copied into today's field creates a false maintenance record.

## Q107-02 — Electric circuits and measurement units

Voltage is potential difference, current is charge flow, resistance relates voltage to current, and power is energy per unit time. DC direction and polarity matter; AC requires the appropriate instrument mode and frequency response. Convert millivolts to volts and milliamperes to amperes before substituting into equations. For a resistive DC circuit V = IR and P = VI. Series resistances add; parallel branches share voltage and their currents add. Kirchhoff's current law expresses conservation at a junction; voltage rises and drops balance around a complete loop.

For an isolated 12 V teaching supply across 4 ohms, I = 3 A and P = 36 W. Two resistors, 4 and 8 ohms in series, give 12 ohms and 1 A. The same resistors in parallel give 1/R = 1/4 + 1/8, hence R = 2.667 ohms and total current 4.5 A. Real supplies have limits and leads have resistance; these calculations do not authorize connecting that load to an actual source.

A voltmeter is connected across points; a direct ammeter is placed in series only within a verified safe circuit. An ammeter across a voltage source can create a short. An ohmmeter must not be applied to an energized circuit. Disconnecting a bond can interrupt protection or create an unsafe potential difference, so use approved shunt/clamp methods when the procedure requires them. Work out expected range before connection. A display of 25 mV is 0.025 V, not 25 V.

## Q107-03 — Corrosion cells, chemistry and polarization

A corrosion cell requires anodic and cathodic reactions, an electronic path and an electrolyte path. Metal dissolution occurs at the anode; reduction consumes electrons at the cathode. Electrons travel through the metal while ionic conduction closes the electrolyte path. Different metals, oxygen availability, deposits, temperature and local chemistry can establish unequal surface conditions. A single pipe can contain both anodic and cathodic regions without being attached to a visibly different metal.

Cathodic protection supplies current to shift the protected surface toward cathodic behavior. Polarization is a change in electrode potential associated with current; it is distinct from voltage drop in the surrounding electrolyte. Coatings reduce exposed area but damaged or disbonded coatings can concentrate demand or shield a surface from effective current. Neither coating appearance nor a very negative remote reading proves protection under a shield.

Faraday's relation connects charge to ideal electrochemical mass change: m = ItM/(nF). For an original ideal example, iron dissolving as Fe 2+, I = 0.010 A, t = 86400 seconds, M = 55.85 g/mol, n = 2 and F = 96485 C/mol gives about 0.250 g. This assumes that current is entirely associated with that reaction. It is not a field wall-loss prediction: current distribution, efficiency, reaction chemistry and area are unknown. A small anodic area coupled to a large cathodic area can suffer concentrated attack. Explain the mechanism before proposing any change.

## Q107-04 — Galvanic and impressed-current systems

A galvanic system couples a suitably active anode to the structure through a metallic connection and shared electrolyte. The anode is consumed while providing protection current. An impressed-current system uses a DC power supply, anode circuit, structure connection and electrolyte return path. In the conventional arrangement the rectifier positive terminal goes to the anodes and negative to the protected structure. Reversed connections can drive structure corrosion. Identify actual labels and drawings rather than relying on cable color.

Recognize anode beds, junction boxes, bonds, isolation devices, test leads, coupons, permanent reference cells and protective devices. Isolation limits unintended current demand; bonding may be intentional for continuity or interference control. Do not remove a bond or bypass an isolator to make a reading look better. The owner must evaluate consequences for all connected facilities.

Worked comparison: a small coated component requiring little current may be suited to a galvanic approach, while a larger demand can require controlled impressed current. This is a concept comparison, not a tester design rule. Soil resistance, coating condition, anode life, neighboring structures, power reliability and maintenance all affect selection. If output falls after rain, do not declare anode exhaustion from that observation alone. Verify equipment, record output and potentials, and refer the pattern for diagnosis. Changes to rectifier taps, anode installation or bond resistance require the authorized procedure and responsible practitioner.

## Q107-05 — Meters and reference electrodes

Structure-to-electrolyte potential is measured against a reference electrode with a known, stable electrochemical potential. Copper/copper-sulfate cells are commonly used for soil/fresh-water work; suitable silver/silver-chloride cells are used in marine applications. Different filling solutions and temperatures change reference potential, so the name alone is insufficient for a conversion. Record cell type, identifier, condition and comparison result. Follow the manufacturer's preparation, storage and contamination controls. Do not substitute a random metal rod and call it an equivalent reference.

Compare a field cell against a maintained master in the same suitable electrolyte under the approved method. A large or unstable difference requires investigation, not an undocumented correction. Inspect porous contact, liquid condition, crystals where applicable, leakage and lead continuity. Keep the electrode from contamination; use proper contact with the electrolyte, not dry asphalt. A high-input-impedance voltmeter reduces loading, but poor contact can still make a high-resistance measurement unreliable.

Teaching acceptance card R107 permits a field/master difference no greater than 10 mV under its specified comparison conditions. Cell A differs by 4 mV and is stable; cell B differs by 28 mV and drifts. A passes this fictional check; quarantine B and document it. This is not a universal manufacturer tolerance. If a meter reads -0.91 V with red on structure and black on reference, reversing leads should reverse sign, not change magnitude materially. Preserve the standard connection convention in the report.

## Q107-06 — Structure potentials, interruption and interpretation

Identify the correct structure terminal, establish sound permitted contact, place the reference at the procedure's defined location and record polarity, units, position and current condition. ON readings include potential contributions from current flowing through resistive paths. Synchronized interruption can help distinguish the immediate resistive change from subsequent polarization decay, but only when relevant sources and timing are controlled. A delayed reading is not automatically an instant-OFF reading, and an un-interrupted foreign source can leave residual error.

For the training steel dataset, ON is -1.120 V, valid immediate OFF is -0.910 V, and later fully established depolarized value is -0.770 V against the same verified CSE. ON-to-OFF magnitude change is 210 mV; polarization decay magnitude is 140 mV. Do not use ON minus depolarized, 350 mV, as the polarization decay. Record the actual time series and evidence supporting the baseline rather than selecting a convenient late value.

US gas-pipeline Appendix D contains material-specific protection alternatives, including specified steel potential and polarization-shift approaches, with measurement qualifications. It is not a universal acceptance rule for every tank, ship, alloy or environment. A raw ON reading more negative than -0.85 V CSE does not alone establish compliance; voltage drops and applicability require evaluation. The tester records conditions and flags uncertainty. Coupons approximate selected exposed surfaces; their connection, area, location and interruption method affect interpretation. Never claim a coupon represents all shielded surfaces.

## Q107-07 — Current, shunts and output checks

A calibrated shunt provides a known small voltage drop at a stated current. Use I = measured mV × rated A / rated mV, retaining polarity for direction. Connect a suitable voltmeter across the designated shunt terminals; do not short the shunt or open a live bond. A DC clamp requires appropriate capability, zeroing, conductor placement and range. Clamping both outgoing and returning conductors may cancel the measured field.

A 50 mV/10 A shunt reads +17.5 mV under the drawing's positive convention: current is +3.5 A. The same device at -7.5 mV indicates -1.5 A, meaning reverse direction relative to that convention, not a negative physical magnitude. Record direction with the terminal convention. Check for loose connections and contact errors only by authorized safe methods.

An isolated simulated rectifier has 18 V external output and 3 A verified current, yielding an apparent operating circuit resistance of 6 ohms and DC output power 54 W. These are operating-point values, not a complete model of electrode polarization or rectifier efficiency. Compare external readings with built-in indications and historical conditions. If panel current is 4 A but the validated shunt gives 3 A, record the discrepancy and refer instrument diagnosis. Do not adjust protection output to reconcile a faulty panel meter. Unexpected zero current can arise from power loss, open leads, poor contacts or measurement error; it is not by itself proof of one failed component.

## Q107-08 — Soil resistivity, sampling and pH

Resistance is measured for a particular geometry; resistivity expresses a material property with length units. In the simplified Wenner four-pin arrangement, equal spacing a and sufficiently shallow pins support rho = 2 pi a R. Use the same length units throughout. For a = 2 m and R = 12 ohms, rho is 150.80 ohm-m, or 15080 ohm-cm. Significant pin depth, nearby buried metal, uneven contact and layered soil affect interpretation; use the applicable correction or approved method rather than applying the simple formula blindly.

Before inserting pins, obtain required utility clearance and safe access. Lay out equal spacing, verify terminal assignments, avoid lead coupling per the instrument instructions and record spacing, depth, orientation, moisture and weather. Repeat at specified spacings and directions; do not average away a meaningful difference. Soil-box testing uses the box's verified geometry factor: rho = R A/L. If A = 40 cm 2, L = 10 cm and R = 250 ohms, rho = 1000 ohm-cm. Sampling moisture and compaction must be stated because disturbed samples may differ from in-place conditions.

Single-probe resistivity methods require their instrument-specific geometry and calibration; do not reuse the Wenner multiplier. Soil pH measures acidity/alkalinity, not CP adequacy. Calibrate a suitable pH method with appropriate buffers and retain sample identity, temperature and preparation. Antimony/CSE methods require the approved conversion and limitations; an unexplained voltage is not a valid pH. Worked decision: two samples labelled the same station have different moisture and no depth record. Retain both results as qualified observations and resample; choosing the lower value creates false precision.

## Q107-09 — Continuity, isolation, locating and interference

Electrical continuity means an effective metallic path; isolation intentionally interrupts selected paths. Similar potentials on opposite sides of an isolator can suggest a problem but can also occur for other reasons. Use the authorized combination of potential comparison, controlled source response and suitable isolation instruments. Do not connect an ohmmeter to an energized installation or remove surge protection without approval. AC mitigation and overvoltage devices can provide safety functions that must remain intact.

A locator can couple conductively or inductively to a structure. Verify the intended conductor and avoid signal transfer to parallel utilities. A trace is evidence supporting location, not permission to excavate. Record confidence, depth-estimate limitations and conflicts with drawings; follow the owner's locating and excavation controls.

Stray DC can enter a structure at one location and leave at another, with corrosion risk where current discharges. Time-varying sources can cause fluctuating potentials and bond current. AC exposure introduces both personnel hazards and corrosion considerations; DC protection readings alone do not resolve it. In a training log, structure potential cycles from -0.95 to -0.62 V in step with a nearby switching event while reference and contact remain stable. Preserve synchronized time records, source state and location, then refer to the responsible corrosion specialist. Do not install an improvised drain bond to a neighbor. Coordination, ownership and engineered protection are essential.

## Q107-10 — Installation assistance, marine work and records

A tester assisting installation checks the approved drawing, material identity, anode condition, lead polarity, cable insulation and test-point labels before burial or concealment. Anode backfill and packaging must match the approved product method. Connections must provide continuity without unacceptable structure damage; coating repairs must be compatible and inspected before covering. Exothermic welding, pin brazing and other attachment methods require their own approved procedure, competent personnel and fire/structure controls. A classroom demonstration does not authorize a live pipeline attachment.

For marine drop-cell surveys, identify the approved reference type, deployment depth, position, tide/current conditions and structure connection. Control over-water access, dropped objects, entanglement and vessel operations. Lower and retrieve only by the approved arrangement; record movement and instability rather than selecting a desired reading. Marine reference scales cannot be silently substituted for soil CSE values.

A usable record contains asset/test-point identity, date/time, operator, instrument/cell IDs, procedure revision, raw readings with signs and units, locations, source/interruption states, environmental conditions, anomalies, repeat tests and disposition. Preserve original entries with traceable corrections. Worked closeout: two points fail access, one reference check fails and five readings are valid. Report all eight planned points with distinct outcomes; do not call the route complete or replace the three missing values. Restore authorized temporary test arrangements, verify the required operating state with the owner and hand over unresolved risks. An honest incomplete result enables maintenance; a fabricated complete route hides corrosion risk.

# VUC107 workbook, observed practice and delivery

## Entry, resources and timetable

Entry: basic arithmetic, ability to read signs/units and follow a stop-work instruction. Diagnose unit conversion and circuit understanding before practice; remediate rather than exclude a learner for unfamiliar notation. This original 40-hour schedule comprises 12 hours instruction/worked examples, 16 supervised practical hours, 6 integrated application hours, 2 review/remediation hours, 2 final-exam hours and 2 feedback/changed-demonstration hours. More time is required if safe independent performance is not achieved. Ten domains are assessed; simulator practice precedes any approved field demonstration.

Instructor: experienced corrosion practitioner competent in the particular measurements, supported by authorized electrical/field specialists where needed. Assessor observes performance directly and retains evidence; a written answer cannot stand in for instrument handling. Provide accessible written diagrams, large displays and extra reading time as appropriate without weakening safety or independent calculation requirements.

Resources: isolated current-limited simulator with protected voltage/current terminals and known resistors; rated meter and sound leads; labelled shunts; suitable DC clamp; maintained reference/master cells and comparison vessel; approved soil-resistivity instrument, clear training area, shallow pins and measured spacing; soil box with recorded dimensions; labelled samples and pH buffers/instrument; inert anode/cable/isolator models; locator training loop; marine drop-cell dry mock-up; drawings, safe-work cards and forms. No mains cabinet, live pipe attachment, uncontrolled excavation or over-water deployment is required for this course. If resources are missing, defer the relevant observed station; do not substitute a slide and claim competence.

Training card R107: reference comparison limit 10 mV under common controlled comparison conditions; equality permitted, instability not permitted. This is a fictional exercise criterion. Record actual manufacturer requirements separately in real work. Simulator uncertainty/tolerance: voltage ±0.01 V, current ±0.05 A, resistance ±1%; account for actual instrument specifications when grading physical observations. Instructor verifies safe setup and expected readings before learners enter. Do not create real electrical faults; use fault cards.

## Worked laboratories

### W107-01 — Circuits and current

Set isolated 12 V model with 4 and 8 ohm resistors. Learner draws series and parallel arrangements before touching leads, predicts currents, selects meter modes, measures safely and compares. Series expected 1 A, parallel 4.5 A total, branches 3 and 1.5 A. A separate 50 mV/10 A shunt dataset gives 17.5 and-7.5 mV: 3.5 and-1.5 A. Instructor injects an ammeter-across-source sketch; learner rejects it. Evidence: circuit drawing, calculation, range selection, polarity and discrepancy explanation. Changed practice: 10 V with 5 and 5 ohms; series 1 A, parallel 4 A. Never exceed the simulator's actual ratings; use displayed synthetic data if a requested load is outside rating and label it accordingly.

### W107-02 — Reference and potential chain

Learner inspects cells, performs comparison, records stable 4 mV and drifting 28 mV exercise results and selects the suitable reference under R107. On the low-voltage structure simulator, use the approved red-structure/black-reference convention and record ON -1.120 V, immediate OFF -0.910 V and established depolarized -0.770 V. Calculate 210 mV immediate change and 140 mV decay. Instructor reveals a second current source was not interrupted: learner withdraws an IR-free claim and qualifies the dataset. Changed run controls both sources and supplies -1.05/-0.88/-0.80 V: 170 mV immediate and 80 mV decay. Learner must not choose the ON value to conceal a weak result.

### W107-03 — Electrolyte and location

With clearance for the training area, lay out equal 2 m spacing and shallow pins; record actual depth and conditions. Synthetic resistance 12 ohms yields 150.80 ohm-m. Rotate the array and compare an instructor-provided 18 ohm result: 226.19 ohm-m; preserve both rather than averaging without a plan. Soil box 40 cm 2 area, 10 cm separation, 250 ohms yields 1000 ohm-cm. Learner identifies one sample with missing moisture/depth and requests resampling. Demonstrate pH calibration and sample identity using the instrument's approved method; no pH-to-protection shortcut. Locator mock-up includes a parallel conductor: learner records uncertainty and refuses excavation authority. Changed array 3 m/8 ohms yields 150.80 ohm-m but does not prove homogeneous soil.

### W107-04 — Integrated route and handover

Route A has eight planned points A1–A8. A1–A5 have verified instrument/reference and asset identities; A6 is locked, A7 has a failed reference comparison, A8 has conflicting asset labels. A1 data: ON -1.120/OFF -0.910/depolarized -0.770 V CSE, approved source synchronization. A2 shunt 50 mV/10 A reads 17.5 mV. A3 Wenner 2 m/12 ohms, shallow equal pins. A4 isolator-side potentials are similar but no confirming test is authorized. A5 current oscillates in time with a foreign switching source. Learner issues five valid observation sets, three incomplete/invalid points, with A4/A5 referred rather than declared acceptable. Instructor asks for an all-pass route and unauthorized bond removal; both must be rejected.

Route B changes conditions: B1–B6 planned; B1 potential -1.05/-0.88/-0.80 V, B2 shunt 50 mV/5 A reads 20 mV, B3 soil box 50 cm 2/10 cm/100 ohms, B4 marine cell lacks filling solution, B5 damaged lead, B6 inaccessible. Expect 80 mV decay, 2 A, 500 ohm-cm, three qualified/missing points and safe escalation. Each learner independently completes both routes, including actual safe instrument handling on the simulator. These are synthetic routes, not claims of field experience.

## Observed stations

### P107-01 — Plan and stop
Task: identify asset, procedure and hazards; inspect equipment; respond to cracked-probe and unexpected-voltage cards. Pass evidence: stops unsafe work and records honest limitation.

### P107-02 — Circuit setup
Task: draw and assemble a rated isolated circuit; choose voltage/current modes and convert units. Evidence: correct connection and independent calculation without hazardous shortcut.

### P107-03 — Corrosion model
Task: label anode, cathode, electronic and electrolyte paths; explain ideal mass example and shielding. Evidence: sound mechanism and no wall-loss overclaim. Observed review station.

### P107-04 — Components/polarity
Task: trace inert galvanic and impressed-current models, identify polarity, bonds and isolators. Evidence: identifies reversal card and refuses unauthorized adjustment. Observed review station.

### P107-05 — Reference management
Task: inspect, compare and document cells; reject drifting result. Evidence: traceable cell identity, comparison and disposition.

### P107-06 — Potential survey
Task: connect correctly, place reference, record source states and timed readings. Evidence: separates immediate change from decay and qualifies incomplete interruption.

### P107-07 — Current/output
Task: use shunt and clamp on safe model, retain polarity, compare internal/external indication. Evidence: correct scale, direction and discrepancy handling.

### P107-08 — Electrolyte
Task: lay out cleared shallow array, measure spacing, use soil box and demonstrate pH method. Evidence: geometry, units, sample conditions and limitations retained.

### P107-09 — Isolation/locator
Task: use approved simulator tests and locator loop, preserve protective devices and report interference. Evidence: no energized resistance test, unsafe bypass or unsupported excavation claim.

### P107-10 — Installation/marine/closeout
Task: inspect inert lead/anode model, rehearse dry drop-cell identification and complete route handover. Evidence: polarity/label conflicts held, marine reference/depth requirements stated and all planned points accounted for.

## Assessment and award

Score each of ten observed dimensions 0–4: 0 absent/unsafe, 1 substantial help, 2 safe but incomplete/prompted, 3 independent correct and traceable, 4 independent with well-supported anticipation/error detection. Require 32/40, each at least 3, both integrated routes satisfactory and no unresolved critical error. Eight stations require actual safe practical performance; two are observed technical reviews. Missing equipment or field authorization means defer that demonstration, not award by attendance. Critical errors include dangerous meter connection, defeating safety devices, unauthorized live work, fabricated readings, concealed polarity/identity conflict or unsupported protection compliance claim.

Complete 20 explained checks and 4 labs. Controlled final: 10 cases, 30 decisions, 60 points, 120 minutes, pass 48 with at least 4/6 per domain and no critical error. Changed retake and keys are instructor-only. Retain first attempt, feedback and changed evidence; do not coach during a scored attempt. Remediation identifies the specific cause—unit conversion, instrument use, criterion selection or records—then repeats a changed task. An independent competent reviewer resolves appeals against the same rubric.

Issue VNDRLY/U Cathodic Protection Tester Training only after all original requirements pass. Record learner identity, version/hash, assessor, dates, simulator versus field evidence and limitations. This is not AMPP CP1, statutory operator qualification, employer authorization or proof of six months' experience. External certification requirements must be obtained from AMPP; public pages differ in how experience is described, so course attendance is never represented as external eligibility. Employer determines continuing practice/review needs; recheck after prolonged absence, equipment/procedure change or unsafe performance. Materials completion is distinct from any learner award.
