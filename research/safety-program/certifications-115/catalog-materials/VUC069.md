# VUC069 — Articulating Crane and Loader Operation

## Purpose and declared pathways

This original VNDRLY/U package covers articulating boom crane, winch-equipped articulating crane and permanently attached material-handling fork/loader functions. The common knowledge course includes all three. Actual performance endorsements are earned separately and must identify the equipment, attachments and control mode demonstrated. This is not CCO certification or employer/site authorization. A written pass alone is a knowledge record, not an operating credential.

The public CCO handbook reviewed is ACO CH REV 01/25. CCO has announced changes effective July 2027; those future changes are not treated as already effective in this November 2026 package. The original VNDRLY performance requirements below remain explicit regardless of external designation changes. Recheck external candidate requirements when applying for an external exam.

Use the actual crane manufacturer's instructions, applicable rules, approved site plan and configuration-specific chart. All A69 capacities, support calculations and weather numbers are fictional teaching examples. They are not manufacturer ratings, soil engineering or approval to perform a real lift.

## Q01 — Scope, duties and authorization

An articulating crane's commercial delivery role does not make every operation exempt from construction requirements. Determine what the machine is actually doing: simple unloading, placing packaged supplies under a qualifying delivery provision, staging for construction, holding a member for installation or another task. Limited delivery exclusions have conditions. Handling structural/prefabricated components or holding material for installation cannot be assumed to qualify merely because the truck belongs to a delivery company.

The operator, employer, site controlling entity, lift supervisor, riggers and signal personnel each have responsibilities. Establish authorization, applicable training/evaluation, task, equipment and stop authority before setup. This course does not qualify a person to design supports, modify controls or declare a legal exemption. Escalate unclear scope to the responsible employer/site authority while preserving safe operation requirements.

Worked case: a truck delivers roof trusses and is asked to hold one while workers connect it. Do not apply a generic materials-delivery exemption. Treat the activity according to the applicable construction requirements and qualified plan. Changed: palletized supplies are simply unloaded to the ground. The scope facts differ, but safe setup, capacity and competent operation remain necessary.

## Q02 — Machine geometry and control identity

Identify the base, column/slew system, inner and outer booms, hydraulic extensions, any manual extensions or jib, stabilizers, attachment, controls and power source. An articulating boom can fold and change geometry in several linked motions. Radius is horizontal distance from the specified rotation reference to the load line/center as defined by the actual chart, not total metal length or the distance from a truck bumper. Manual extensions and attachments can introduce separate capacity limits.

Trace controls to actual motion during protected unloaded familiarization. Confirm the correct radio receiver/transmitter and control ownership. A person standing on the opposite side of the truck may misinterpret intuitive left/right directions; use the fixed labeled reference and agreed signals. Keep the operator out of the fold, stabilizer and slew pinch zones. Do not use a remote controller from an unverified machine because the buttons look alike.

Worked: a chart assumes the main boom configuration but a manual extension is installed. The displayed generic maximum capacity cannot be used until the actual configuration and deductions/limits are established. Changed: the extension is removed but controller identity is uncertain. Resolving configuration does not resolve unintended-control risk.

## Q03 — Site access and support information

Assess the route onto and off the site as well as the setup point: transport height/width, overhead wires, slopes, soft shoulders, traffic, pedestrians and room to deploy/stow. Obtain known subsurface information such as tanks, utilities, vaults and disturbed fill. Pavement appearance does not establish bearing capacity. Excavation edges and retaining structures require appropriate assessment; there is no universal distance from an excavation that makes every setup safe.

The supporting system must meet manufacturer level and support requirements throughout the lift. Ground can change after rain, thawing, excavation or repeated loading. Coordinate necessary preparation with the responsible site entity rather than inventing an operator's soil rating. Establish exclusion zones for stabilizers, truck movement, slew and load travel; ensure emergency access and visibility.

Worked: a planned stabilizer position is above a buried service vault shown on the site drawing. A thick mat does not erase the void. Hold that setup and obtain an appropriate alternative or engineered support solution. Changed: the vault is avoided but the new location is sloped loose fill. Reassess that actual support condition independently.

## Q04 — Stabilizers, mats, level and chocks

Follow the exact deployment sequence and permitted stabilizer extension configurations. Verify whether partial/asymmetric extension is allowed and which working areas and capacities apply. Do not select full-extension ratings when a leg is partially deployed. Load distribution changes with boom position; truck weight divided equally among legs is not a valid substitute for the governing maximum reaction. Level must be checked by the specified method and tolerance.

Mats/cribbing must be suitable for the applied reaction, ground and geometry, including their own strength and effective bearing area. A larger board is not automatically a sound mat. Keep hands and feet clear during deployment and do not stand where a stabilizer can trap you. Use wheel chocks, brakes and vehicle condition as instructed; do not guess whether tires should remain loaded or lifted for a particular design.

Worked A69 paper calculation: a supplied maximum stabilizer reaction is 120 kN and a supplied allowable pressure is 200 kPa. Ideal required area is 120/200 = 0.60 m². A 0.8 × 0.8 m mat gives nominal 0.64 m² and average 187.5 kPa. This comparison alone does not prove mat strength, effective distribution, soil suitability or complete setup acceptance. Changed: only 0.50 m² is effective; average rises to 240 kPa and fails the fictional pressure basis.

## Q05 — Inspection and hydraulic hazards

Use the actual inspection procedure to examine visible structure, joints/pins/retainers, cylinders, hoses, fittings, stabilizers, controls, safety functions, hook/attachment and required records. Check the transport restraint state before deployment and the hydraulic/PTO condition. Leaks, damaged hoses, unexpected drift, cracked structure or missing retention require the appropriate hold/reporting. A clean appearance does not establish condition of inaccessible components; report limits rather than climbing unsafely.

Hydraulic fluid under pressure can penetrate skin. Never search for leaks with a hand, loosen a connection under pressure or stand beneath a boom supported only by hydraulics during maintenance. Follow authorized isolation and mechanical support procedures for service work. Treat a suspected injection injury as an urgent medical emergency under the site plan, even if the visible wound is small; give responders accurate fluid/equipment information when available. Operators do not diagnose severity from appearance.

Worked: a hose weeps when the outer boom moves and the operator wants to wrap it with tape. Stop/report, protect exposure and use qualified repair and required verification. Changed: the leak disappears when unloaded. Pressure-dependent leakage is still a defect; do not resume on that basis.

## Q06 — PTO, deployment and stowing

Engage and disengage power take-off only according to the vehicle/crane procedure, with the specified transmission, brake, engine and interlock conditions. An illuminated indicator is not the entire readiness check. Establish a protected area before unfolding; the boom and attachment may sweep close to the operator or truck. Watch clearances and hoses through the complete deployment sequence, not only the final working position.

Stowing is a controlled operation with potential pinch and attachment hazards. Land loads first, follow the specified boom/extension/attachment sequence, secure transport locks and restraints, retract stabilizers as instructed and verify PTO/control state for travel. Do not drive with a partially secured boom because the destination is nearby. A second person may confirm clearances but cannot replace the operator's required checks.

Worked: the boom is folded but its transport latch has not engaged. The truck is not ready to leave. Correct and verify the restraint rather than relying on hydraulic holding pressure. Changed: the latch is secure but one stabilizer remains extended. Complete the entire travel check; one resolved item does not authorize movement.

## Q07 — Load-chart selection and operating areas

Match the chart to crane model, configuration, extension/jib, stabilizer state, working sector and attachment/winch arrangement. Read notes that define included weights and required deductions. Determine the most limiting point of the entire path, not only pickup radius. Moving from over the rear to over a partially supported side can change capacity even without changing radius. Use actual permitted intermediate-radius rules; do not interpolate or extend a chart without authorization.

A69 fictional fully supported rear-sector chart lists gross capacity at 3/5/7/9 m as 4, 000/2, 800/1, 900/1, 300 kg. It requires the next larger listed radius for intermediate radii. Its fictional restricted-side chart at the same radii is 2, 500/1, 800/1, 200/800 kg. Gross includes payload and all listed suspended accessories. These invented numbers illustrate sector/configuration reasoning only. The real crane's instructions may use different conventions.

Worked: at 6 m use the 7 m column. A 1, 300 kg payload plus 250 kg fork and 100 kg other accessories totals 1, 650 kg. Rear capacity 1, 900 gives 250 kg margin; restricted-side capacity 1, 200 fails by 450. A route crossing into that sector is not approved by the pickup calculation. Changed: radius reaches 8 m in the rear sector; 1, 300 kg capacity also fails the same 1, 650 kg load.

## Q08 — Load moment, weights and deductions

Obtain reliable weight for the actual load, attachments, rigging and any specified deductions. Retained material, packaging or moisture can change weight. A load-moment indicator cannot be used as permission to lift an unknown object until an alarm reports its weight. Make units consistent and identify whether a chart states kilograms, pounds, metric tonnes or another unit. Keep actual chart footnotes with the calculation.

For teaching, load moment about a reference is force times perpendicular distance. A 10 kN load at 4 m produces 40 kN·m; at 6 m it produces 60 kN·m. This does not mean a crane's capacity can be extrapolated with a constant-moment rule. Structural, hydraulic, geometric and stability limits can govern differently. Manual extensions may have individual fixed maximum loads even when the main chart appears generous.

Worked A69: chart gross 1, 900 kg, beam 180, slings 70 and specified attachment deduction 200 leave 1, 450 kg for payload under the assigned convention. A proposed 1, 500 kg payload fails by 50. Changed: an extension separately limits payload to 1, 200 kg. The lower applicable limit governs; do not choose the more favorable calculated remainder.

## Q09 — Rigging and suspended-load control

Use manufacturer-approved attachments and a qualified rigging arrangement with identified ratings, compatible connections and edge protection. Position the boom hook vertically over the load center of gravity. A load can tilt or shift as it clears support if lifting points or center of gravity are misunderstood. Keep hands out of tensioning and landing pinch points and do not allow anyone beneath the load.

Sling angles and unequal loading matter. In an ideal symmetric two-leg classroom example, each leg's tension is W/(2 sin theta), theta above horizontal. Real rigging requires the actual component configuration and instructions; the equation does not qualify an unreviewed arrangement. Do not side-pull, drag, shock-load or use a boom to break a snag free. Tag lines are used only where the plan avoids entanglement and other hazards.

Worked: a 2, 000 kg ideal load with two equal legs at 30 degrees produces 2, 000 kg-force equivalent in each leg. A 1, 500 kg leg rating is inadequate even though half the load is 1, 000. Changed: the center of gravity shifts. The equal-leg assumption itself is no longer valid; replan rather than simply select a stronger sling by guesswork.

## Q10 — Permanently attached fork/loader functions

A material-handling fork or loader attachment controls load differently from a suspended hook. Verify approved attachment identity, rated load center, fork engagement, clamping/retention arrangement, load integrity and the crane's configuration. Pallet condition and the integrity of packaged materials matter. Center the load as required, fully engage the supported load under the instructions and establish retention before moving. A clamp that touches the package may not be properly applied.

Keep people clear of the attachment and load. Do not use forks to pry, push or rescue a slipping load outside their designed function. A broken pallet or shifting stack can change center of gravity and retention suddenly. Follow the device-specific response to unstable load; land safely rather than asking someone to hold it by hand. A load center farther from the designed reference can reduce permitted capacity; use the attachment's chart/instructions, not a self-invented linear derating.

Worked A69 case: the supplied fork limit is 1, 600 kg at a specified 0.5 m center. A 1, 400 kg package projects to a 0.8 m center, and no rating is provided there. The 1, 400 figure alone does not establish acceptance. Obtain the actual permitted configuration/rating or reconfigure the load. Changed: center is corrected but pallet boards are broken. Structural support remains unresolved.

## Q11 — Winch, reeving and two-block prevention

Winch operation adds rope, drum, sheave, reeving, downhaul and block-clearance requirements. Verify actual line-pull ratings, parts of line, block/hook capacity, required rope wraps and permissible travel. Inspect rope construction/condition, terminations and spooling under the applicable instructions. Choose required downhaul weight and reeving from the manufacturer; an improvised heavy object is not a substitute.

Two-blocking occurs when the lower block/hook assembly is drawn into the upper sheave/boom arrangement, potentially damaging rope or equipment. Changes in boom extension/articulation can change clearance even if the winch command is unchanged. Understand and test required prevention functions by the authorized method; never bypass them to finish a lift. Avoid slack, crossed wraps and uncontrolled rope payout. Maintenance/replacement requires qualified procedures and compatible rope, not merely matching diameter.

Worked A69 comparison: supplied safe line pull 900 kg with two ideal parts gives 1, 800 kg before other restrictions, while the applicable boom chart permits 1, 500 kg. The lower 1, 500 remains the ceiling before all deductions/other limits. Changed: one part is reeved; the ideal line comparison is now 900 kg. The old two-part lift plan cannot be reused.

## Q12 — Smooth coordinated movement

Articulating, telescoping, slewing and hoisting motions interact. Familiarize unloaded in the protected envelope before handling the approved test load. Make the prescribed low initial check to confirm load/attachment stability and control. Plan a path with sufficient clearance for the boom joints, truck, attachment and load—not merely the boom tip. Avoid abrupt commands that produce swing, impact or sudden load shift.

Maintain a safe control position outside fold and crush zones. Remote operation may improve visibility but can tempt an operator to walk beneath the boom or load; do not do so. Stop if required visibility or communication is lost. If the load begins to rotate or swing beyond the approved envelope, restore safe control and land under the trained procedure. Do not chase movement with increasingly aggressive commands or ask another person to catch the load.

Worked: the load clears a wall, but the folding outer joint approaches a worker at the control side of the truck. Stop; the whole machine envelope matters. Changed: the worker leaves but the load continues to swing into the wall's clearance zone. Restore control and reassess the path independently of the cleared person.

## Q13 — Signals and blind placement

Agree the applicable articulating-crane hand signals or alternate method before movement. Establish the designated signaler, crane identity, direction reference and handoff process. The operator must understand each command; a hand wave is not adequate merely because it is confident. Respond to emergency stop from anyone. Stop safely when required signals conflict, become unclear or are lost.

Blind placement requires an effective view of the load path/landing through the signal system and controlled exposure of others. Plan where the signaler stands so they do not enter a crush zone to see better. Test voice radios and distinguish them from machine-control radios. Resume only after confirming actual load/area conditions, not simply when noise returns to the channel. A camera does not automatically eliminate the need for a qualified signaler or other controls.

Worked: a loader package is placed behind a parapet and radio contact drops. Stop rather than completing the last lowering command from memory. Changed: the radio returns with two voices directing different cranes. Re-establish unique crane identification and one understood directing signal before movement.

## Q14 — Power lines and other site hazards

Identify all overhead/underground services and establish the applicable work-zone assessment and protection before setup or deployment. Treat overhead lines as energized unless the required utility confirmation and protective conditions establish otherwise. Include the unfolded boom, extensions, attachment, load and possible movement in the assessment. Transport clearance is a separate concern from stationary lifting clearance.

Do not prescribe one universal safe distance for every voltage and activity. Use the applicable regulatory method, utility information and site plan, with required barriers, observers or other measures. If a required measure disappears, stop the affected operation. Contact with a conductor requires the emergency plan, warning people away and utility/emergency assistance; do not approach or touch potentially energized equipment to help. Training uses tabletop cases, never a deliberate live approach.

Worked: the truck passes safely beneath a line with the boom stowed, then starts unfolding below it. The travel condition does not establish safe deployment. Stop and assess the full working envelope. Changed: the utility says a nearby different circuit is off. Verify the actual line and required conditions; do not generalize the message.

## Q15 — Weather, dynamic effects and stability changes

Wind, gusts, ice, temperature and visibility affect control and equipment. Follow actual operating/setup/stowing limits and attachment/load restrictions. A broad panel can become uncontrollable below a nominal crane wind limit. Rain may soften stabilizer support, and unloading can alter the vehicle/load state used by a particular stability system. Monitor actual conditions and stop before losing control rather than waiting for an overload alarm.

Abrupt acceleration, snag release and impact can impose loads beyond a static calculation. A69 load-moment arithmetic is not a dynamic design method. Avoid sudden stops and side loading; use approved recovery for instability rather than swinging a load experimentally to the other side. If a stabilizer settles, stop safely, protect the area and obtain appropriate support review. Do not simply extend that leg further with a suspended load as an improvised correction.

Worked A69: a fictional stop limit is 9 m/s and observed gusts reach 10.5. Stop under the exercise plan; average wind of 6 does not remove the exceedance. Changed: wind is 7 but the mat begins to sink. The support failure independently requires a safe stop and review.

## Q16 — Overload protection and fault response

An overload prevention or limiting system depends on correct configuration and functional sensors. It supports, but does not replace, reliable planning and operator judgment. Verify actual permitted modes and working-sector/stabilizer information. Do not use a bypass, service mode or altered setting to obtain a greater lift. The fact that a system allows motion does not prove every attachment or ground condition is safe.

On an alarm or malfunction, follow the actual safe response and permitted recovery motions. Different systems inhibit different functions; guessing which movement reduces demand can make conditions worse. Protect the zone, land safely when the prescribed procedure allows, and obtain qualified assistance. Record fault conditions and restrictions. A restart that clears an alarm is not evidence of repair or correct calibration.

Worked: the display assumes all stabilizers fully extended but one is partial. Hold and resolve actual setup/configuration under the instructions. Changed: the input is corrected and the system stops extension before the desired landing. Replan within capacity; do not override the system to reach the destination.

## Q17 — Emergency, maintenance and transit handover

Plan responses for power/hydraulic failure, unintended movement, fire, electrical contact and medical emergencies before the lift. Keep emergency controls accessible and communication available. Operator response protects people and follows equipment-specific procedures; it does not include unqualified repair under a suspended boom/load. Isolate and support equipment through authorized maintenance procedures, recognizing stored hydraulic energy and gravity.

Before road travel, verify boom/attachments/stabilizers locked and stowed, tools/mats/chocks secured, PTO/control state correct, load restrained and transport dimensions/route legal and safe. This training does not confer a driving license or load-securement qualification beyond the observed crane stowing task. Handover records actual defects, restrictions, inspection/repair/release evidence and final state. Do not erase a restriction because the crane is moving to another site.

Worked: a leak is repaired but a required retention pin is missing from an attachment. The vehicle is not ready for transit or operation until the actual safe restraint/configuration is established. Changed: the pin is installed but no required repair verification is recorded. Repair action and readiness evidence remain separate.

## Q18 — Integrated decision and evidence

Integrate task scope, authorization, site/support, configuration, inspection, load/rigging, path, signals, weather and securing. Do not approve from a single favorable number. The same load may pass at pickup and fail after a sector/radius change. Recheck every material change and preserve the actual plan version used for the lift. If needed information is absent, stop and obtain it instead of copying a nearby crane's chart.

Worked A69: payload 1, 200, attachment 250 and rigging 100 total 1, 550 kg. At 6 m rear-sector radius use the 7 m/1, 900 kg cell: margin 350. If the route crosses the restricted side at the same radius, 1, 200 kg capacity is exceeded by 350. A correct pickup calculation cannot authorize the whole path. Changed: the path stays rear, but support validation is missing. Capacity remains only one of the required conditions.

The full practical has distinct crane, winch and loader components. Observe each claimed component and record omissions on a limited credential or knowledge record. Do not convert a written explanation of winch handling or fork retention into an actual-performance endorsement. This preserves useful complete training materials without inventing external qualification or learner results.

Allow additional practice; attendance does not replace proficiency. Instructor must be competent and authorized on the actual equipment and each claimed crane/winch/loader function. Site/support design, rigging, inspections and maintenance remain with appropriately qualified personnel. Record instructor competence, learner diagnostic, accommodations, actual hours and selected pathways before delivery.

Provide an inspected articulating crane with an approved hook configuration, suitable approved winch configuration and suitable permanently attached fork/loader configuration, using separate machines if necessary. Provide actual manuals/configuration charts, inspected rated rigging, verified stable test loads, approved landing supports and pallets/packages, site/support evidence, appropriate mats/chocks, radios/signals reference, weather monitoring, exclusion barriers, emergency arrangements and record forms. If a pathway's actual equipment is absent, its knowledge can be taught but its performance endorsement remains unearned. Full operating completion requires all pathways below; limited records explicitly identify the omitted pathway.

Validate equipment/site conditions before training, including underground hazards, ground reactions and support, level, permitted stabilizer state, load paths/sectors, all component ratings and emergency plan. Use generous capacity margin and stable nonhazardous loads. No actual electrical exposure, uncontrolled load, hydraulic fault or bypass is introduced. Faults and regulatory-boundary cases are tabletop/simulator exercises. Demonstrate a known safe example, allow supervised changed practice and assess independently on different loads/routes. Final keys remain instructor-only. ## Station scoring

Score identity/basis, preparation/method, accurate result/control, safety/limits and reproducible record 0–2 each. Require 8/10 at every station, no zero and no unresolved critical issue. Zero is absent/wrong/unsafe; one is safe but incomplete; two is complete and supported. Critical issues include unsafe support or capacity violation, bypass, exposure under load/boom, side pull, ignored stop/lost required communication, unverified attachment retention, unsafe hydraulic contact or fabrication. Stop unsafe action and require changed reassessment after remediation. Paper numerical tolerance is 0.01 in stated units; do not round a failed limit into a pass.

## I69 independent practical sets

Prepare distinct teaching T and assessment A/B sets. The instructor validates the actual crane, hook, winch and loader configurations and site/support separately. Each set contains at least two verified stable hook/winch loads and two suitable intact packaged loader loads with known centers and compatible retention. Record weights, ratings, actual chart/footnotes, worst path radii/sectors, support basis, control mode, rigging/attachment identity, weather, exclusion/signals and emergency arrangements. Protect the actual load truths/approved plans from inappropriate coaching while giving learners all information needed to plan safely.

Use lightweight visual markers, spacious routes and supported landing rectangles at least 0.5 m larger than each load footprint on every side. The actual instructor-approved clearance may be greater. No person stands as an obstacle or beneath the boom/load. Set a safe training truck-bed or equivalent supported loader landing with verified capacity/access. Do not copy CCO zigzag geometry, timing or scoring. Schedule about three hours for the integrated components plus safe familiarization; elapsed time is recorded but not used to reward rushing.

Common setup: independently identify scope/authority, inspect, survey, deploy under the validated support plan, confirm level/stabilizers and control identity, calculate actual whole-route capacity and brief signals/exclusion. The operator then performs the components below on actual suitable equipment.

**Crane component:** vertical hook alignment and approved initial check, controlled articulated/slew/telescope transfer to landing A, reverse transfer on a different safe path and a final precise placement. Require three completed transfers with stable support and safe rigging release.

**Winch component:** verify actual reeving, line limits/downhaul and required protection; perform initial check and two controlled transfers combining hoist with permitted boom motion while maintaining safe block/boom clearance. Assess actual rope/spooling observation and stop response. Do not create two-block contact or disable prevention.

**Loader component:** inspect the approved fork/retention arrangement; center/engage/secure the package; perform a ground-to-supported-bed transfer and a return transfer with a changed safe path. Include one validated blind placement with qualified signals for an unrestricted loader practical record. If no safe blind placement exists, retain the explicit limitation and defer that skill award. Both transfers require stable supported landing and controlled release of retention.

All seven transfers must remain within validated support/capacity/envelope, without side pull, uncontrolled swing/rotation, collision, unplanned contact, insecure load, ignored signal or person exposed beneath the load. The operator may pause safely to restore conditions. Assessor intervention to prevent danger fails independent performance; record the exact action and remediate. Observe manual-based stowing/transport restraints, stabilizers/PTO/control state and completed handover at the end.

For changed set B alter verified weights, pickup/landing positions and actual permitted sectors/configurations without adding real hazards. Require fresh calculations/briefing and the complete affected pathway attempt. Record equipment/weather interruptions separately; restore safe conditions and repeat the affected complete transfer, with renewed familiarization. A different machine needs full identity/configuration/familiarization and the full affected pathway assessment. Do not infer performance on one type from another.

# VUC069 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Does a delivery-company label exempt every crane task from construction requirements?

**Answer:** No. Determine actual activity and conditions of any limited exclusion.

**Reason/remediation:** Holding/installing or specified component handling cannot be assumed excluded. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

Does this course issue CCO certification?

**Answer:** No. It records VNDRLY training and actual demonstrated scope.

**Reason/remediation:** External certification and employer authorization are separate. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

Is radius measured from the truck bumper by default?

**Answer:** No. Use the actual chart-defined rotation/load reference.

**Reason/remediation:** A wrong datum can select an unsafe chart cell. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

Can a manual extension be ignored because it is not hydraulically powered?

**Answer:** No. Match configuration, deductions and separate limits.

**Reason/remediation:** Manual extensions affect geometry and permissible loads. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

Does a thick mat make a buried vault safe to support a stabilizer?

**Answer:** No. Obtain appropriate support assessment or relocate safely.

**Reason/remediation:** A mat does not remove a void or establish structural capacity. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

Why assess exit as well as entry?

**Answer:** Stowed dimensions, changed site conditions and traffic may differ.

**Reason/remediation:** Successful arrival does not guarantee safe departure. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

Supplied reaction 100 kN and allowable pressure 200 kPa: ideal area?

**Answer:** 0.50 m².

**Reason/remediation:** Force divided by pressure; this does not verify mat strength or real soil. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L2

Can full-extension ratings be used when a stabilizer is partial?

**Answer:** Only if actual instructions explicitly establish that configuration/rating; do not assume.

**Reason/remediation:** Support geometry can change working-sector capacity. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L1

Should a hydraulic leak be located with a bare hand?

**Answer:** No. Use authorized safe inspection/isolation methods.

**Reason/remediation:** Pressurized fluid can penetrate skin. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L2

What response is required for suspected injection despite a tiny wound?

**Answer:** Immediate professional medical attention through the emergency plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Visible size does not establish injury severity. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L1

Does a PTO indicator alone establish full deployment readiness?

**Answer:** No. Follow all actual vehicle/crane conditions and sequence.

**Reason/remediation:** Interlocks, support and surrounding clearance remain relevant. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L2

Can hydraulic holding pressure substitute for a required transit latch?

**Answer:** No. Establish the specified transport restraint.

**Reason/remediation:** Pressure alone does not provide the required secured travel condition. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L1

A69 at 6 m rear sector uses what capacity?

**Answer:** 7 m column, 1, 900 kg gross.

**Reason/remediation:** The paper rule requires the next larger listed radius. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L2

Why calculate the entire route rather than pickup only?

**Answer:** Radius or sector can become more restrictive later.

**Reason/remediation:** Initial capacity does not authorize every subsequent position. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L1

A 12 kN load at 5 m has what simple moment?

**Answer:** 60 kN·m.

**Reason/remediation:** Force times perpendicular distance; not a crane-chart extrapolation rule. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L2

May a favorable main chart override a lower manual-extension limit?

**Answer:** No. Respect every applicable limit and its actual convention.

**Reason/remediation:** A separate component restriction can govern. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L1

Ideal 1, 800 kg symmetric load, two legs at 30 degrees: demand per leg?

**Answer:** 1, 800 kg-force equivalent.

**Reason/remediation:** 1, 800/(2×0.5); half-load reasoning omits angle. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L2

Can a crane pull a snagged load sideways to free it?

**Answer:** No. Stop and resolve obstruction through an approved plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Side force and sudden release create uncontrolled demands. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L1

Does a fork touching the pallet prove correct engagement and retention?

**Answer:** No. Verify actual supported engagement, centering and retention under its instructions.

**Reason/remediation:** Contact alone does not establish safe load control. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L2

May a load-center rating be extrapolated by guesswork?

**Answer:** No. Obtain the actual permitted configuration/rating.

**Reason/remediation:** Attachment geometry changes capability. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L1

What is two-blocking?

**Answer:** Lower hook/block assembly being drawn into the upper sheave/boom arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** It can damage rope/equipment; boom motion can change clearance too. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L2

Can an improvised weight replace the specified downhaul?

**Answer:** No. Use the manufacturer-approved arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** Weight/reeving affect controlled rope behavior and equipment limits. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L1

Why inspect the entire articulated envelope rather than only the tip?

**Answer:** Folding joints and attachments can strike/crush people or structures elsewhere.

**Reason/remediation:** Tip clearance does not describe all moving parts. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L2

Is walking beneath the boom acceptable with a remote for a better view?

**Answer:** No. Use a protected position and approved signaling/view.

**Reason/remediation:** Remote control does not remove overhead/crush exposure. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L1

Required radio contact is lost during blind placement. What happens?

**Answer:** Stop safely and re-establish confirmed communication/conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not finish a remembered unseen command. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L2

Can a stop from a non-designated person be ignored?

**Answer:** No. Respond appropriately to emergency stop.

**Reason/remediation:** Normal directing authority does not cancel emergency warnings. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L1

Safe stowed transit beneath a line proves safe unfolding there: true or false?

**Answer:** False. Assess the full working/deployment envelope.

**Reason/remediation:** Stowed and deployed geometry differ. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L2

Does a nearby circuit being off prove the actual line is safe?

**Answer:** No. Verify the actual line and required protective conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Electrical identity cannot be assumed. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L1

A69 stop gust 9 m/s; observed 10.5. Exceedance?

**Answer:** 1.5 m/s.

**Reason/remediation:** A favorable average does not erase a gust exceedance. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L2

Can the operator level a settling leg experimentally with a suspended load?

**Answer:** No. Follow safe stop/recovery and obtain proper support review.

**Reason/remediation:** Improvised correction can worsen instability. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L1

Does a permissive overload display prove the ground can support the crane?

**Answer:** No. Support and configuration must be independently established.

**Reason/remediation:** The system has defined sensing/model limitations. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L2

May a service bypass be used to reach a landing outside capacity?

**Answer:** No. Replan within actual permissible conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** A bypass is not operating authorization. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q17-L1

Does a new site remove an unresolved defect restriction?

**Answer:** No. Preserve the restriction and repair/release evidence in handover.

**Reason/remediation:** Relocation does not repair equipment. Revisit Q17 and perform its changed application.

## Q17-L2

Does a repaired hose alone establish transport readiness?

**Answer:** No. Verify required release and all stowed/restraint/PTO/leg conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Repair and complete travel readiness are distinct. Revisit Q17 and perform its changed application.

## Q18-L1

Can a hook-only actual assessment award winch and loader performance too?

**Answer:** No. Observe each claimed pathway separately.

**Reason/remediation:** A written explanation does not establish actual rope/fork control. Revisit Q18 and perform its changed application.

## Q18-L2

What does a correct pickup capacity calculation omit if used alone?

**Answer:** The whole route, support, condition, signals and other readiness requirements.

**Reason/remediation:** Every material condition must be satisfied. Revisit Q18 and perform its changed application.
