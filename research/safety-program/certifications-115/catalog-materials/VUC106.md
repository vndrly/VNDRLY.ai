# VNDRLY/U Plant Manager / Incident Commander — Initial Response

VUC106 • Original initial training •18November 2026

For prospective leaders, incident-team members and support personnel. This is not OPITO approval, employer appointment or a substitute for jurisdiction-specific hazardous-material command qualifications. Full proprietary criteria unavailable; public scope and primary regulator guidance support this original package. Local role development continues after initial training.

Planned 32 hours over consecutive days: 10 instruction/prebrief, 16 scenario practice/debrief, 4 labs, 2 written review/development. Every learner leads two different scenarios and participates in support roles; add time for valid observation. Objectives: prepare, activate, assess, delegate, communicate, account for people, coordinate environmental/external interfaces, manage escalation/stress and hand over/recover.

## Q106-01 — Initial command development and authority

This course develops initial incident-management skills for plant leaders, team members and support personnel. It does not appoint an incident commander or establish every legal qualification that a particular hazardous-material response may require. The employer must define the role, applicable jurisdiction, plant knowledge, training and demonstrated competence before assignment. A senior job title does not automatically transfer command during an incident.

Learn the site emergency organization, succession and interfaces with public responders. Identify who commands site operations, who controls specialist tactics, who communicates with external agencies and how command transfers are announced. Public responders may have statutory responsibilities distinct from the plant organization. Follow the agreed arrangements rather than assuming either party's authority disappears when the other arrives.

Worked example: a plant director arrives after a trained commander has established control. The director provides resources and advice through the structure unless an explicit authorized transfer occurs. Two simultaneous command voices can create incompatible instructions and expose people.

## Q106-02 — Preparing the plant emergency system

Preparation links hazards to practical response capability. Review inventories, process/utility dependencies, containment and drainage, occupied buildings, access routes, contractors, neighboring sites and environmental receptors. Confirm actual response teams, equipment, communications and external-support arrangements. A contact list is not proof that a resource is available now.

Assign roles for operations, safety/technical advice, people accounting, liaison, logistics and records according to the site plan. Check deputies and relief. Identify impaired protection and compensating controls before an event. Plan for failed power, network or communications and inaccessible documents. Involve the people who will use the plan so instructions remain workable under stress.

Worked example: the plan assumes two response teams, but the shift can staff one and the second vehicle is unavailable. Record the real capability, revise response options and escalate restrictions through the responsible authority. Do not count the same crew twice or call a requested mutual-aid team available before confirmation.

## Q106-03 — Activation, priorities and first decisions

A credible report requires prompt assessment and activation of the appropriate plan. Establish location, hazard, people at risk, source/time and immediate protective actions already taken. Announce command and objectives. Protect life and prevent escalation while obtaining the information needed for the next decision. Avoid delaying an already justified alarm or notification until the exact failed component is known.

State objectives as outcomes, then select safe feasible tactics with competent advice. Controlling a leak, accounting for people and preserving safe access may require different task owners. Identify what could change the priorities and when the team will review progress. Production recovery is not an initial objective when people or protection remain uncertain.

Worked example: a transfer release is reported near a contractor work area. Activate the plan, stop exposed work through the authorized process, obtain people status and request the approved source-control response. Sending an unprotected supervisor into the area to confirm the report is not a prerequisite for protective action.

## Q106-04 — Situation assessment and changing strategy

Maintain a shared record of confirmed facts, reports, assumptions and unknowns. Evaluate data age and quality; a refreshed dashboard may contain stale information. Ask for information that changes a decision rather than collecting every possible detail. Record options, constraints, selected action, reason, owner and expected feedback.

Monitor whether actions achieve their intended result. A shutdown command without required feedback leaves source control uncertain. If a tactic fails or conditions change, revisit the strategy. Consider interactions with adjacent units, utilities, occupied buildings, drains and off-site receptors. Do not continue a plan solely because it was publicly announced or effort has been invested in it.

Worked example: the CRO reports isolation attempted but continuing flow. The commander must retain the continuing-release assumption and reassess containment and exposure. A plan based on release stopped is unsupported, even if the control button was correctly pressed.

## Q106-05 — Delegation and responder safety

Delegate to named competent roles with an outcome, authority limit, constraints, reporting requirement and review time. Confirm understanding. The commander maintains the overall picture while specialist leaders control their techniques within the plan. Do not improvise firefighting, chemical treatment, confined-space entry, electrical switching or medical treatment from a management course.

Responder protection includes hazard assessment, competence, equipment, communications, backup and withdrawal criteria under applicable procedures. Track team location/status and missed reports. Stop or change a tactic when its prerequisites fail. A missing person creates urgency but does not remove the need for a defensible rescue plan. Ask specialists what can be done safely and what cannot.

Worked example: a response team reports the planned approach unsafe and requests an alternative route assessment. The commander should obtain that assessment and protect remaining options, not simply order the team to find another way in. A resource that cannot operate safely is not available for that tactic.

## Q106-06 — External responders, neighbors and public information

Establish the designated liaison with fire, medical, police, environmental and other relevant agencies under the site plan. Provide site identity, hazard information, current access, people status, actions and assistance requested. Verify receipt, dispatch and arrival separately. Correct changed gate or route information promptly. Do not assume a requested resource is confirmed.

Off-site protective instructions and public information must follow the applicable authority and agreed arrangements. The plant supplies accurate facts and required notifications; it does not invent a public evacuation zone from an unverified reading. Neighboring operations may be threatened or contribute to escalation, requiring timely liaison. Use a designated spokesperson and distinguish confirmed facts from estimates; avoid releasing personal information unnecessarily.

Worked example: responders are directed toward Gate 1, but a wind/incident update makes that access unsuitable. Notify liaison and gate staff, obtain the authorized safe alternative and confirm the responders received it. A corrected internal map does not automatically update vehicles already approaching.

## Q106-07 — People accounting and welfare

Require unique-identity reconciliation across muster, contractors, visitors, response teams, medical and transfers. Raw totals can match the baseline while hiding a duplicate and a missing person. Ask the coordinator to state confirmed, unconfirmed and moved people with source/time. Preserve last-known-location evidence for missing people and provide it to the authorized response decision.

Medical professionals direct treatment; command coordinates access and resources. Confirm assistance arrangements and receiving destinations for transfers. Monitor welfare during extended incidents and plan relief, rest and communication. Do not call a person evacuated because a seat is allocated or a transfer requested. Departure and receipt are separate events.

Worked example: one worker appears at both muster and medical while another is absent. Reconcile the identity and movement rather than declaring the raw total complete. A later medical confirmation closes only the relevant discrepancy; an unreported response team remains a separate issue.

## Q106-08 — Containment and environmental decisions

A release can migrate through drainage, water, soil or adjacent plant. Obtain approved site drawings and substance-specific information. Determine which containment is actually usable, what is already occupied and what incoming volume is credible. State uncertainty. A nominal bund capacity may not equal usable capacity during the incident.

Use competent spill/environmental advice and approved procedures. Do not improvise neutralization, dilution, mixing or opening drains. Such actions can increase exposure or spread contamination. Coordinate environmental measures with life safety and responder protection. Record notifications and specialist recommendations through the designated roles.

Worked example: a fictional containment system has 30 cubic meters usable, 12 occupied and a potential 23 additional. Remaining capacity is 18 and potential shortfall 5. Report the gap and uncertainty; do not order a hazardous drain operation merely to make the arithmetic fit. The calculation supports the decision but does not replace verified technical advice.

## Q106-09 — Shelter, evacuation and loss of site capability

Shelter or evacuation decisions depend on the actual hazard, protective building capability, routes, timing and responsible authority. Do not assume every building provides protection or every outdoor route is safe. Monitor protection failures and loss of access. A provisional time estimate is conditional and may become invalid when ventilation, power or route availability changes.

Confirm capacity and suitability of transport and receiving arrangements. Distinguish nominal resources from available ones. Do not overload or rely on an unconfirmed arrival estimate. If the command location becomes unsuitable, transfer through the plan while disclosing the alternate location's limitations. Preserve people accountability and communication during movement.

Worked example: thirty-eight people require a movement plan and one of two twenty-four-place vehicles is unavailable. Available capacity 24 leaves 14 without that option. Obtain confirmed alternatives and reassess timing; neither an extra name on a manifest nor an optimistic ETA creates capacity.

## Q106-10 — Stress, team performance and challenge

High workload can create fixation, rushed communication and missed feedback. Use brief structured reviews: facts, threats, objectives, actions, gaps and next decision. Assign support and relief before overload becomes failure. Keep critical monitoring owned when routine calls or media requests compete for attention.

Encourage evidence-based challenge regardless of seniority. Acknowledge bad news, correct the picture and change the plan when needed. Do not defend a failed assumption to avoid embarrassment. During practice, feedback should identify observable behavior and consequences rather than label a person. Record coaching so developmental help is not mistaken for independent performance.

Worked example: a junior liaison identifies that mutual aid was requested but never confirmed. Check the evidence, correct the resource board and reassess options. Dismissing the warning because the resource was already announced can leave the plan dependent on a nonexistent capability.

## Q106-11 — Handover, recovery and individual development

Stabilization is not recovery. Retain restrictions while protection, plant integrity, permits, isolations, people or environmental status remain unresolved. Transfer command with current objectives, confirmed and failed actions, people/responders, external commitments, deadlines and recovery constraints. The incoming commander accepts and affected roles are informed.

Recovery and restart use the authorized technical and management process. Preserve original logs with visible corrections. Debrief decisions against information available at the time, identify both individual and system improvements, assign owners and define evidence of closure. Initial-course completion includes an individual development plan for local familiarization, drills and any additional role qualification.

Worked example: a learner completes two initial scenarios but lacks site chemical and external-agency knowledge. Record successful course learning within its scope and identify the remaining employer development. Do not convert an initial training certificate into a declaration of independent readiness for that plant.

## Resources and delivery safeguards

Use an isolated incident-command room or accessible remote role-play system, fictional site map, status board, named roster, resource cards, training communications, clocks and the forms below. No live alarms, public emergency calls, plant controls, chemicals, physical firefighting or transport operations. ## Fictional site Alder

Units T transfer, U utilities, B occupied building and M muster. Gates G1 near T and G2 alternate. Only controller-confirmed routes are available. Learner is incident commander for the simulation. CRO applies approved simulated control card: credible transfer release requires stop/close commands with stopped, closed and flow-zero confirmation; missing feedback means source control unconfirmed. Response leader owns specialist tactical assessment; Medic owns clinical decisions; Muster reconciles identities; Liaison contacts simulated external services and competent public authority; Gate receives current safe access; Technical supplies containment/product information. No learner gives real public instructions or specialist operating sequences.

Primary channel E, approved fallback B requires two-way test. Initial emergency activation, alarm and role delegation are authorized by the model. Hazardous entry requires a competent plan and safeguards; no physical entry occurs. Recovery requires verified protection, permit/isolation resolution and authorized release. A resource request or estimate is not confirmation of capability, dispatch or arrival.

## Scenario A — controllable transfer incident with environmental gap

Start 09:00, roster A01–A16. One team staffed, despite an old plan listing two. Containment verified usable 30 cubic meters, occupied 12, potential further 23: remaining 18, possible shortfall 5. Drain destination unknown. G1 affected, G2 status unconfirmed.

Minute 0: CRO and Field provide credible release report near contractors. Observe activation, command, objectives and role allocation. Minute 3: source-control command sent but flow continues; require unconfirmed status and revised strategy. Minute 5: Response requests task boundaries and reports no safe entry plan yet. Learner must not order improvised entry. Minute 7: Technical confirms capacity data but not drain destination; require specialist/environmental coordination, no drain opening or chemistry improvisation. Minute 9: Muster reports A01–A14, A14, A15: fifteen unique, A16 unconfirmed. Minute 11: Medic verifies A16 at medical with time/source: sixteen global. Minute 13: Gate confirms G2 suitable at 09:13; Liaison reads G1 back. Correct and confirm responders receive current access. Minute 15: CRO supplies all required source-control feedback; environmental disposition and one protection fault remain. Minute 17: director asks for immediate restart and tries to take command without handover. Preserve authority/recovery boundaries. Minute 20: Relief accepts plant, people, environmental and resource constraints. Debrief observed strengths and specific development gaps.

## Scenario B — escalating utility incident and threatened building

Start 14:00, roster B01–B24. One qualified team; mutual aid requested but unconfirmed. Two fictional 14-place vehicles, initially available. Building B has provisional protective endpoint 14:30 dependent on stated protection; this is a training estimate, not guaranteed safety. G1 unavailable, G2 initially suitable.

Minute 0: credible utility/process incident, source state uncertain. Activate plan and obtain technical picture. Minute 3: one vehicle unavailable: fourteen available, ten-person gap. Minute 5: Response reports planned approach unsafe; obtain options without directing uncontrolled entry. Minute 7: Muster lists B01–B22, B22, B23: twenty-three unique, B24 unconfirmed. Minute 9: coworker says B24 probably at medical; retain as lead, not confirmation. Minute 11: Medic confirms B24: twenty-four global. Minute 13: Liaison says aid may arrive at 14:25 but dispatch not confirmed; learner must not count it available. Minute 15: movement estimate eleven minutes gives fifteen remaining and four nominal margin. Immediately inject ventilation failure and route uncertainty; previous timing no longer guarantees a viable option. Minute 17: G2 becomes unavailable; update responders through liaison and obtain authorized alternative. Minute 19: junior communicator challenges an obsolete aid ETA on board while media calls arrive. Expect evidence-based correction and delegated public communication. Minute 21: controller supplies alternative only after capability request: ten approved places, available 14:24, route G3 confirmed by Field at 14:21, operator retains safe-movement authority. Confirm integration and current conditions rather than assuming the old building estimate is restored. Minute 23: primary E fails; test fallback and give current command/people/access picture. End with a supported protective decision, resource/identity plan and accepted relief. No actual shelter, evacuation or public direction is enacted.

## Decision, remediation and original award

Eleven dimensions scored 0–4: 0 absent/unsafe, 1 major assistance, 2 safe but incomplete/prompted, 3 independent sound traceable, 4 independent with timely anticipation. Require 36/44, each at least 3, no unresolved critical error and two independently satisfactory leader-role scenarios. Coached initial practice remains separate. Critical errors: unauthorized hazardous deployment, overload, false all-accounted/received report, improvised chemical/drain action, unsupported public instruction, concealed failed protection, unauthorized restart or fabricated evidence. Stop unsafe simulated action, record, remediate and observe changed performance. A competent independent reviewer resolves disputed evidence against unchanged criteria.

Complete 22 explained checks and 4 labs. Controlled final 11 cases/33 decisions, 66 points, pass 53, at least 4/6 each, no critical error, 120 minutes. Changed final/keys remain private. Retain failed attempts and coaching. Written pass is not a substitute for observed role practice.

This records initial training, not statutory hazardous-material command qualification, external OPITO approval or employer appointment.
