# VUC073 — Telehandler Operator

## Program and learning contract

Original VNDRLY/U instruction for planning, inspecting, setting up, moving, placing and securing loads with fixed and rotating telehandlers. Complete the common knowledge work and the actual-machine route for each scope claimed. A rotating-machine demonstration does not automatically establish familiarization with every fixed machine in this VNDRLY program. The external CCO designation rules are separate. This package is not a CCO credential, an engineering approval, an employer operating authorization or a personnel-platform endorsement.

The learner will produce a supported handling plan, identify equipment and site defects, interpret the correct attachment chart through the entire movement, perform controlled fork handling and travel, and return the machine safely. Work requiring a suspended-load attachment, winch, personnel platform, tandem lift or special engineered procedure must receive its own applicable planning, training and evaluation. Recognizing that boundary is assessed here; those specialties are not silently included in the fork-handling award.

Use the actual machine/attachment manuals, capacity chart, employer procedures and site plan. Resolve conflicting or missing information before movement. Course numbers are fictional exercises; they never replace operating limits. Prior experience can inform instruction but does not erase an unobserved essential task. Accessible teaching and additional practice are available without removing safety-critical performance.

Real charts use their own graphical boundaries and instructions.

Fictional rotating machine R73, approved forks B, load center 0.60 m, stationary with validated full support setup: at 6 m height and 4 m reach, front sector net payload 2500 kg and side sector 1700 kg. There is no travel rating in this R73 extract. A missing sector or setup is not an invitation to use the nearest larger rating.

Payload includes product, pallet, packaging and any other carried material. The exercise plates are net payload ratings; do not subtract the included carriage twice. For an actual gross rating, follow its explicit deductions. Fictional planned load: product 1200 + pallet 80 + wrapping 20 = 1300 kg. At height 5 m and reach 3.5 m, F73 cell is 2200 kg, margin 900 kg and utilization 59.09%. At 8 m and 5.5 m the cell is 900, so the same load fails by 400 kg. A successful pickup does not approve its destination.

## Q01 — Assignment, roles and operating scope

Start with the whole task: what is being moved, where it starts and ends, which machine and attachment, who controls the route and receiving area, and what can change while working. A task described as “move that pallet” omits mass, load center, support condition and people. Ask for these before treating the movement as routine. Distinguish the operator's decisions from site supervision, equipment maintenance, qualified lifting planning and employer evaluation.

Make a readiness record with equipment identity, job location, handling route, destination, load evidence, personnel roles and unresolved holds. Identify whether the proposed work is fork transport, suspended-load handling or personnel lifting. Configuration and actual use affect the applicable rules; a familiar chassis does not keep the task inside the same scope. The employer must establish the training/evaluation and authorization appropriate to the workplace.

Worked case: an operator has only a classroom certificate and is assigned a rotating machine at a congested site. Knowledge evidence is useful, but supervised practice, actual performance evaluation and machine/site familiarization remain. Changed case: the operator has a fixed-machine evaluation but the new job adds a winch. Record the additional configuration and task review rather than merely changing the machine name on the old record.

## Q02 — Inspection before startup

Inspection is a deliberate comparison with acceptable condition, not a search for something obviously broken. Read prior defects and maintenance restrictions, then follow the actual inspection sequence. Identify forks and retainers, carriage/coupler, boom sections and wear points, cylinders/hoses, tires/wheels, axles, chassis, guards, access steps, seat/restraint, visibility aids, indicators, controls, brakes and support equipment. Rotating machines add the applicable turret, slew and support systems. Match manuals, plates and charts to serial/configuration information.

Do not touch suspected hydraulic leaks or inspect under unsupported raised equipment. Use safe access and the prescribed isolated condition. Record what was inspected, the observed defect and the operating restriction; “needs attention” is too vague when a fork lock is absent. An unsafe component is not cleared by reducing the payload. Maintenance personnel determine and document corrective work within their competence.

Worked case: one fork retaining device is missing, although both forks look straight. Remove the attachment/machine from the proposed use and arrange correction; the load can shift the unsecured fork. Changed case: all mechanical items look acceptable but the matching chart is missing. Capacity cannot be established, so the job remains on hold. A completed checklist must not conceal an unchecked item.

## Q03 — Startup, controls and protective functions

Before starting, establish a clear machine envelope and identify every control, emergency stop and warning relevant to the configuration. Adjust the operator position and restraint; enter using proper access, not the steering wheel or hydraulic controls as handholds. Use the manual's starting sequence. Observe instruments, warning messages and abnormal noises. Test required functions in a controlled unloaded condition with people clear and the instructor's approved sequence.

Confirm boom raise/lower, telescope, attachment tilt, steering selection, travel/braking and applicable support/slew functions. Function checks must not defeat interlocks or deliberately create overload. Know what a warning means and which recovery actions are permitted. A disabled control may indicate a protective condition rather than a fault to bypass. A remote or secondary station, where fitted, needs positive identity and the actual control-transfer procedure.

Worked case: an indicator reports a configuration different from the installed attachment. Stop before loading and resolve the identity/configuration mismatch using the approved procedure. Changed case: the display is correct but braking performance is abnormal during the safe check. Remove from operation; accurate load data does not compensate for inability to stop. Record the symptom and safe machine state for maintenance.

## Q04 — Ground, route and receiving area

Walk and assess the complete route, including the point where the load will be put down. Identify soft fill, trenches, buried services, voids, slopes, edges, overhead obstructions, doors, pedestrian crossings, traffic, visibility and escape paths. A telehandler can reach a platform that cannot support the load; receiving strength is a separate question. Obtain the necessary support information from the responsible site personnel rather than estimating it from appearance.

Plan separation and route control before travel. Allow for the whole machine, load overhang and steering sweep, not just tire tracks. Weather can change traction, visibility, support and wind exposure. An enclosed work area also requires assessment of exhaust and atmosphere; an open door is not proof that combustion-engine operation is safe. Reassess when the route, surface, load or traffic changes.

Worked paper case: loaded vehicle envelope is 3.10 m high and a doorway's verified clear height is 3.00 m. It fails by 0.10 m before allowing any further required clearance. Changed: another doorway is high enough but the floor capacity is unknown. The alternative route is still unapproved. Width/height calculations are screening tools, not structural or traffic approval.

## Q05 — Electrical and overhead hazards

Treat an unidentified overhead line as an unresolved hazard. Determine applicable electrical requirements for the actual task and configuration and obtain a controlled plan from responsible personnel. Include the boom, attachment, load, supports and possible deflection or movement in the exposure assessment. Do not infer safety from insulation appearance, a spotter's estimate or the absence of visible sparking. Keep the entire planned movement within the established boundary.

Brief the operator, signalperson and nearby workers on stop signals and emergency action. A spotter does not eliminate the clearance requirement. If the planned path cannot be maintained, stop and change the approved plan. Contact/near-contact response must avoid people approaching or touching potentially energized equipment; request emergency assistance and follow the site's electrical emergency procedure. Do not improvise rescue or recovery with the same exposed machine.

Worked case: the load clears a roof edge, but the boom's upward movement enters an unverified line corridor. The lift is not ready despite roof clearance. Changed: the utility confirms a controlled isolation but the receiving scaffold obstructs the revised path. Resolve the physical obstruction too; eliminating one hazard does not validate all the others.

## Q06 — Mass, load center and load retention

Establish total carried mass from reliable documentation or an approved determination. Include the pallet, container and contents; “empty” tanks may contain residue. Establish center of gravity and fork engagement, not merely the outer dimensions. A long load with a heavy component at its far end cannot be treated as uniformly distributed. Keep the load centered between forks and supported/retained by the approved arrangement, with forks adjusted and secured correctly.

Load center is not the same as reach from the machine's chart reference. A simple moment calculation can explain why shifting a load matters, but it does not create an approved derating. Never extend a chart to a longer load center using an improvised proportional rule. An actual manufacturer may provide an explicit equivalent-load method for a specified carriage/fork arrangement; use it only with all its conditions and separate fork-capacity checks. T73 supplies no such method. Obtain the applicable chart/configuration approval or change the load arrangement through an approved plan.

Worked paper model: 1000 kg located 0.40 m from a reference plus 500 kg at 1.00 m gives center (1000×0.40 + 500×1.00)/1500 = 0.60 m. Changed: the second item moves to 1.30 m; center becomes 0.70 m. The T73 0.60 m chart no longer applies. Reducing speed cannot repair the missing capacity basis.

## Q07 — Chart selection and the whole handling envelope

Read chart identity and conditions before reading a number. Confirm model, attachment, load center, tire/support state, operating sector and stationary/travel condition. Establish the correct reference for height and reach; do not substitute the distance from the cab or the end of the fork. Trace pickup, intermediate movement and landing. The governing point may occur during extension or slew rather than at either endpoint.

Record required coordinates, applicable cell/zone, allowed payload and comparison with actual load. Read any notes or prohibited zones. Indicators assist monitoring but are not substitutes for a valid plan. If position or configuration cannot be established, stop. Do not creep into overload to discover what the machine permits.

Worked T73 case: 1700 kg at height 5.5 m/reach 5 m selects 6 m/6 m and 1400 kg; excess is 300. Moving the machine to a validated reach of 3.8 m selects 2200 and leaves 500 margin, but only after the new ground and route are accepted. Changed: raise destination to 8 m at 3.8 m reach; rating becomes 1600 and the 1700 load fails by 100. Repositioning is a new plan, not an automatic cure.

## Q08 — Attachment selection and installation

Select the approved machine/attachment combination for the actual task. Mechanical fit alone does not prove compatibility or capacity. Confirm identification, condition, retention system, hydraulic connections where applicable and matching operating information. A substitute fork carriage, extension, bucket or hook can change load center, visibility and capacity. Do not attach loose rigging to forks as an improvised lifting point.

Install and disconnect using the actual procedure on stable support, with pressure safely relieved as required and people outside pinch zones. Verify locking engagement by the manufacturer's prescribed checks before applying a load; a dashboard symbol alone is not proof that the pins physically engaged. Park removed attachments in stable designated storage so they cannot roll or fall. Keep the record of which attachment was evaluated.

Worked exercise: a payload chart explicitly includes a 300 kg carriage and allows 2200 kg net payload. A 1500 kg pallet leaves 700 kg margin, not 400. Changed: a different attachment is fitted and no matching chart exists. Neither subtraction nor the original chart authorizes use. Get the correct approved combination and information before movement.

## Q09 — Stabilization, frame leveling and supports

Frame leveling, ground leveling and load manipulation serve different purposes. Use the actual manual's sequence and restrictions; never use leveling to chase a shifting elevated load or compensate for unsupported ground. Establish the permitted machine attitude before lifting. Confirm tire requirements, stabilizer/outrigger configuration and contact, and any sector-dependent limitations. Partial deployment needs an explicitly permitted configuration, not the full-support chart.

Determine support demands through the applicable machine/site planning process. Reaction divided by effective support area is an ideal pressure check only; it does not establish mat bending strength, soil adequacy, edge stability or underground conditions. Monitor for settlement during operation and stop safely if the support condition changes. Do not increase a pad area on paper while leaving only a corner in contact.

Worked paper case: reaction 160 kN on effective area 0.80 m² gives 200 kPa. Against a stated exercise allowable of 180 kPa it fails; ideal required area is 0.889 m² before structural/site checks. Changed area 1.00 m² gives 160 kPa, but an unverified void remains a hold. These fictional values are not a setup design.

## Q10 — Steering, travel and grades

Practice the fitted steering modes unloaded in a protected area before carrying a load. Confirm wheel alignment and mode selection through the actual procedure. Rear movement, crab travel or four-wheel steering can make the machine sweep into a space the operator did not expect. Plan turns with the complete envelope and maintain visibility. Use the prescribed boom/attachment travel position and controlled speed; do not travel with the load elevated merely to see beneath it.

Grades require the manufacturer's direction and configuration limits and a validated route. Do not turn across a slope or use a generic slope percentage as permission. Retain control of traction and braking, keep people separated, and stop when visibility or signals are lost. Reverse travel is not automatically safe: assess rear visibility, route and the required assistance. Public-road movement adds separate legal/vehicle requirements.

Worked screening example: rise 0.60 m over 12 m horizontal run is 5%. That is geometry, not an approved operating limit. Changed run 8 m gives 7.5%, requiring review against the actual limits and conditions. A shallow wet grade may still be unacceptable. During practical work no learner deliberately tests the machine's traction or tip limit.

## Q11 — Pickup and low-level handling

Approach the load squarely with the proper fork spacing and approved configuration. Verify pallet integrity, access beneath it, load retention and clearance beyond the forks. Do not push a damaged pallet into position with people nearby or lift a load attached to another structure. Engage to the prescribed support depth without striking hidden objects. Establish balance and secure support at the lowest practical safe height before traveling.

Observe the load as well as the machine. Unexpected tilt, broken packaging or fork movement means stop and safely set down using the approved response. Do not invite a worker to steady the load by hand. A trial pickup is not a substitute for a known weight and chart check. When disengaging, ensure the load is supported independently before withdrawing the forks.

Worked case: twenty bags at 25 kg plus a 35 kg pallet total 535 kg. The chart may permit the mass, but torn wrapping and an unstable top layer require correction before travel. Changed: two bags are removed; mass becomes 485 kg while the damaged pallet remains. Lower mass does not make damaged support suitable. Record both capacity and packaging decisions.

## Q12 — Elevated placement, retrieval and stacking

Confirm the landing structure's strength, clearances, access and exclusion area before raising the load. Position and stabilize the machine so the full movement is within its approved envelope. Coordinate boom extension, raise/lower and attachment orientation smoothly without impact or side loading. Keep people away from the drop and pinch zones. Never use the forks to drag a stuck load off a rack or pull a building component into alignment.

Place the load fully on stable accepted support, confirm it is released from the machine and withdraw without disturbing it. Retrieval requires renewed verification: a pallet may have been altered, overloaded or trapped since placement. Stacking needs stable surfaces and a permitted arrangement, not just a reachable height. A signalperson cannot certify a structure's capacity by standing beside it.

Worked T73 case: landing at 8.2 m height and 3.1 m reach gives 1600 kg rating. A 1450 kg load has 150 margin. Changed: retrieval now includes an added 200 kg package, giving 1650 and a 50 kg excess. The earlier successful placement does not approve retrieval. Revise the load or machine plan and recheck every relevant condition.

## Q13 — Rotating-machine handling

A rotating upper structure changes the relationship among the load, support polygon, operator's view and travel direction. Identify turret orientation, slew limits, rear/tail sweep and the correct chart sector before moving. Protect the swing area and keep the entire load path in verified sectors. Approved support configuration and leveling must be maintained. A favorable front-sector pickup cannot authorize travel through a weaker side sector.

Use deliberate coordinated movements at a controlled pace. Do not simultaneously slew and extend beyond what the manual and plan permit. Know how to return to the transport orientation, confirm required locks/interlocks and establish direction before travel. Reconfiguration between stationary lifting and travel requires the specified safe sequence; do not retract supports under an elevated load to save time.

Worked R73 case: 1850 kg passes the front rating 2500 by 650 but exceeds the side rating 1700 by 150. The proposed front-to-side placement is rejected. Changed payload 1600 leaves 100 in the side sector, provided the complete path/setup is valid. This extract contains no travel rating; neither result authorizes traveling with that load.

## Q14 — Communication and blind movements

Agree on signals, responsible signalperson, radio channel and stop response before movement. Establish a position where the signalperson can observe the relevant load path and communicate without entering a crush zone. Confirm who directs normal movement; anyone recognizing danger must be able to call stop. Use clear commands and repeat back when needed. A vague “keep coming” is insufficient when direction could mean machine travel, telescope or slew.

Blind movement needs a planned communication arrangement and clear receiving area. Stop if contact is lost, a command conflicts, or a person enters the exclusion zone. Do not guess the last unseen segment. A camera is an aid with blind spots and possible delay, not proof that the whole route is clear. Test communication before starting rather than during an already unstable maneuver.

Worked exercise: the operator receives “extend” while the radio cuts out before the distance is confirmed. Stop; restore communication and clarify the intended motion. Changed: the signalperson is visible but another worker gives a conflicting movement command. Hold and resolve command responsibility. The safe stop is a correct operating decision, not a productivity failure.

## Q15 — Suspended loads and specialized tasks

Fork-supported and suspended loads behave differently. A suspended load can swing, shift outward or create dynamic effects that are not represented by a fork-pallet assumption. Use only the approved attachment, chart, rigging and method for the actual configuration. Establish weight/center of gravity, hook position, retention and a controlled path. Applicable crane rules may apply to a powered industrial truck configured with a winch or hook to hoist/lower and horizontally move a suspended load. Determine the actual scope before work.

Tandem handling, submerged or stuck loads, demolition and high-consequence blind lifts require competent planning and any separate qualification requirements. Do not divide total weight equally between two machines without establishing actual load share and changing geometry. Taglines, where appropriate, must not expose a handler to a dragged, caught or falling load. Personnel lifting requires its own approved system, procedures and evaluation; no person rides a pallet or fork as a shortcut.

Worked paper case: a 3000 kg load is assumed to divide 1500/1500 between trucks. A changed center of gravity gives a stated planned split of 60/40, or 1800/1200; a 1700 kg-rated first truck fails by 100 before other checks. This arithmetic illustrates why an engineered plan is needed. It does not authorize a tandem lift. Practical specialty recognition uses tabletop cases, not improvised live lifts.

## Q16 — Abnormal conditions, shutdown and records

Stop safely on unexpected motion, warning, support movement, loss of communication, load instability or equipment defect. Follow the actual manufacturer's safe recovery instructions; do not bypass a limiter, jump from a moving/tipping machine, or improvise boom motions to recover an unstable load. Emergency response must protect nearby people and avoid a second casualty. Hydraulic injection injuries require urgent medical attention even when the wound looks small; do not search for leaks with bare hands.

At normal shutdown, place the machine and attachment in the prescribed supported safe position, neutralize controls, apply the required parking/security steps and prevent unauthorized use. Temporary pauses, unattended parking, refueling and maintenance isolation have different requirements. Refuel/charge only in the designated safe condition and location. Secure attachments and transport configuration before relocation. The handover records defects, restrictions, equipment/attachment identity and who may release repaired equipment.

Worked case: the job is finished but an attachment lock warning occurred during the last movement. Record the fault and restricted state; do not hand over as ready because the pallet reached its destination. Changed: maintenance repairs the lock but the next shift uses another attachment. Verify that combination and carry out the required checks. A repair release is not a blanket approval of every future task.

# VUC073 — Workbook, instructor delivery and credential rules

## Delivery plan

Plan 64 hours: 16 instruction/worked cases, 8 application labs, 24 supervised actual-machine practice, 12 independent practical/evaluation and 4 final/remediation planning. Extend practice until the learner is ready; hours alone never establish competence. The full fixed-plus-rotating route requires both actual machines. A fixed-only or rotating-only outcome must explicitly state its limited observed scope. Instructor and evaluator must have knowledge, training and experience appropriate to the actual machines, attachments and workplace tasks.

Before delivery, provide the actual manuals/charts, safe inspected equipment, approved fork attachments, known stable test loads, validated receiving structures, a segregated route, suitable support/ground, radio/signals, emergency plan and required PPE. Do not improvise elevated platforms or public-road tasks. Select loads and positions comfortably inside approved limits; never load to a limit merely to make a test difficult. Record mass, center of gravity, packaging, dimensions, machine and attachment IDs, tire/support state, actual chart and full path. Validate ground and receiving structure independently.

Initial diagnostic: distinguish mass from capacity, height from reach and load center; calculate total payload, read T73, explain attachment identity and identify stop conditions. Teach one complete sequence, demonstrate safe operation, practice changed cases and then assess without coaching. No actual fault, overload, unstable stack, electrical approach or bypass is introduced. Photographs/tabletop injects test hazardous conditions while the real machine is safely stopped.

## Station rubric

Score five dimensions 0–2: correct identity/basis, preparation, controlled performance/result, hazard/limit decisions and traceable record. Zero is wrong/missing/unsafe; one is safe but incomplete; two is complete and supported. Each station requires 8/10, no zero and no unresolved critical error. No averaging away a failed station. Instructor intervention to prevent danger fails independent performance. Safe self-initiated stopping is correct behavior.

Critical errors include unauthorized personnel lifting, unapproved attachment/rigging, unknown load or chart used as approved, bypass, unsafe clearance/support, uncontrolled load/vehicle movement, entering crush zones and fabricated inspection or evaluation evidence. Stop the exercise, make safe, reteach, practice a changed case and independently reassess. Retain the original result. Numerical tolerance 0.01 stated unit; never round a failed capacity into a pass.

## I73 independent integrated evaluation

Instructor prepares teaching set T and independent A/B using actual machine-specific instructions. Record safe target boundaries, permitted contact, placement elevations, fork engagement requirements, loading/receiving capacities and all clearances in the learner packet before assessment. Course setup is original and does not copy CCO obstacle dimensions, weights, timing or scoring. Use stable loads with known mass/center, no people below elevated loads and no test of ultimate machine capability.

Fixed route: learner reviews task/manual/chart and load data, inspects, briefs helpers and secures the site. Start and perform required checks. Pick up a known pallet, travel forward through a validated turning route and stop at a marked point. Place it on an accepted elevated support, fully release and withdraw. Retrieve and return it to a ground support. Perform a second pickup with controlled reverse travel and stable stack/setdown at another validated support. Demonstrate safe unloaded return maneuver and shutdown/security. At least two different actual loads or separately verified load arrangements and two receiving elevations are included, all comfortably within actual limits.

Rotating route: repeat machine/attachment inspection, chart/site/load review and setup for the actual rotating machine. Perform one elevated pickup/place/return sequence and one front-to-side placement through verified sectors with the correct support arrangement. Demonstrate protected sweep, smooth control and communication; then put the machine into its prescribed transport state and perform controlled forward/reverse maneuvering on the approved route. Finish with supported attachment, parking/security and handover. Do not retract supports or drive with the configuration still in a stationary lifting state.

Both routes include a verbal defect or signal-loss inject while safely stopped, requiring an accurate hold/recovery decision. Pass requires every predeclared limit, correct attachment retention and configuration, protected people, no unplanned contact/dropped load, controlled placements entirely within the accepted support areas, smooth withdrawal, safe travel posture and a complete truthful record. All essential actions must be independent. An unsafe intervention fails the affected independent attempt; weather/equipment interruption is recorded as interruption and the complete affected task is repeated after conditions are restored.

Set B changes actual safe target order, load arrangement, receiving elevation or route within newly validated limits. Fresh familiarization is required for another machine and its complete route is reassessed. Do not use the same remembered steps as proof of transfer. Allow approximately four hours across both routes plus necessary familiarization; no speed bonus overrides control. Observed type/model/attachment and exclusions appear on the award.

## Controlled final and decision

Private A/B each contain 16 original cases, three scored decisions each, 96 points. Allow 160 minutes with coursebook/calculator and no keys or outside assistance. Pass 77/96 and at least 4/6 in every domain, with no unresolved critical error. Each decision receives 2 for correct supported reasoning, 1 for safe incomplete reasoning, 0 for wrong/missing/unsafe. Changed Form B includes original context and changed decisions. This is separate from CCO's examination and from employer workplace evaluation.

Require corrected 32 formative responses, 8 labs, all applicable stations, I73 and controlled final. Missing actual operation permits only a knowledge/practice record, not an operating award. A combined award requires both routes; a limited route explicitly identifies the omitted machine type. Scope remains fork handling with evaluated equipment, not crane/winch/personnel/tandem or public-road qualification. Employer training/evaluation/certification responsibilities remain separate. Review on equipment/task/workplace changes, unsafe performance or incidents; observe applicable recurrent evaluation requirements. VNDRLY's one-year administrative review reminder does not replace those requirements.

Accommodations may include readable diagrams, oral response capture, extra instructional time and communication support; the learner must still demonstrate essential operating decisions and actions. Remediation names the error, teaches why it matters, practices a changed task and reassesses independently. Keep first results and dates rather than overwriting failure with a pass. Materials completion never means an actual learner has passed.

# VUC073 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

A classroom-trained operator is sent to a rotating machine at a new site. What is still needed?

**Answer:** Appropriate supervised practical training, machine/site familiarization, performance evaluation and employer authorization.

**Reason/remediation:** Knowledge alone does not demonstrate safe workplace operation. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

Does a fixed-machine record automatically cover a winch task?

**Answer:** No. Review the actual configuration, applicable rules, training and evaluation.

**Reason/remediation:** Task scope changes with configuration and use. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

A fork retainer is missing. Can a light load be moved?

**Answer:** No; hold the defective arrangement and obtain proper correction.

**Reason/remediation:** Low payload does not secure the fork. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

The machine looks sound but its attachment chart is absent. Is inspection sufficient?

**Answer:** No; capacity remains unverified.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance does not provide the correct chart. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

A configuration warning prevents motion. Should an interlock be bypassed to test it?

**Answer:** No. Resolve the condition by the approved procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** The protection may be acting correctly. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

Good chart data but an abnormal brake check: proceed?

**Answer:** No; remove from operation and report.

**Reason/remediation:** Capacity information does not restore stopping control. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

A 3.10 m loaded envelope meets a 3.00 m opening. What is the result?

**Answer:** It exceeds the opening by 0.10 m; reject the route.

**Reason/remediation:** Nominal fit must precede further required clearance checks. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L2

A clear alternative route crosses an unverified floor. Is it approved?

**Answer:** No; floor support needs validation.

**Reason/remediation:** Clearance and structural support are separate. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L1

A roof clears the pallet but the boom approaches an unidentified line. What happens?

**Answer:** Hold and establish applicable electrical controls for the entire envelope.

**Reason/remediation:** Pallet clearance does not prove boom clearance. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L2

Does a spotter waive electrical separation?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** A spotter supports, rather than replaces, required controls. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L1

Compute center for 1000 kg at 0.40 m and 500 kg at 1.00 m.

**Answer:** 0.60 m from the stated reference.

**Reason/remediation:** Total moment 900 kg·m divided by 1500 kg. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L2

The second mass moves to 1.30 m. Can T73 still be used?

**Answer:** Center becomes 0.70 m; its 0.60 m basis no longer applies.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not invent a moment-ratio derating. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L1

T73 load 1700 kg at height 5.5 m/reach 5 m: capacity and result?

**Answer:** 1400 kg; exceeds by 300.

**Reason/remediation:** Use 6 m height/6 m reach cell under the fictional rule. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L2

At height 8 m/reach 3.8 m, does 1700 kg pass?

**Answer:** No; capacity 1600, excess 100.

**Reason/remediation:** Check destination as well as pickup. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L1

A net 2200 kg chart already includes the carriage. Payload 1500 kg: margin?

**Answer:** 700 kg.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not subtract the included carriage twice. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L2

The coupler fits but the attachment is unapproved. Proceed?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Mechanical fit is not approved compatibility. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L1

160 kN over 0.80 m² gives what pressure?

**Answer:** 200 kPa.

**Reason/remediation:** Force divided by effective area; not a complete foundation design. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L2

At allowable 180 kPa, what ideal area is needed?

**Answer:** 0.889 m² approximately, before structural/site checks.

**Reason/remediation:** A quotient does not resolve a void or pad bending. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L1

Rise 0.60 m over 12 m horizontal run gives what grade?

**Answer:** 5%.

**Reason/remediation:** Grade is rise/run×100, not an operating permission. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L2

Should a load be carried high to see under it?

**Answer:** No; use prescribed travel configuration and a safe visibility plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Raised travel increases hazards and does not solve blind areas. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L1

Twenty 25 kg bags plus 35 kg pallet: total?

**Answer:** 535 kg.

**Reason/remediation:** Include packaging/support mass. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L2

Two bags removed but pallet remains damaged. Ready?

**Answer:** No; 485 kg does not repair the support.

**Reason/remediation:** Capacity and load integrity are separate. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L1

T73 at 8.2 m height/3.1 m reach with 1450 kg: margin?

**Answer:** 150 kg against 1600 rating.

**Reason/remediation:** Use 9 m/4 m cell. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L2

Retrieval adds 200 kg. Result?

**Answer:** 1650 kg exceeds 1600 by 50.

**Reason/remediation:** Retrieval needs a new load check. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L1

R73 payload 1850 kg moves front to side. Result?

**Answer:** Front passes by 650; side fails by 150.

**Reason/remediation:** The whole sector path governs. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L2

Does R73 extract authorize travel with 1600 kg?

**Answer:** No; it gives stationary ratings only.

**Reason/remediation:** Missing travel information cannot be invented. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L1

Radio drops during an incomplete extension command. Continue?

**Answer:** No; stop and clarify after restoring communication.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not guess unseen movement. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L2

Two people give conflicting movement commands. What happens?

**Answer:** Hold and resolve the designated command arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** Anyone can signal danger; conflicting normal commands are not a plan. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L1

Does an ordinary fork rating authorize hanging a sling over the forks?

**Answer:** No; use the approved lifting attachment/configuration and relevant plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Improvised rigging changes safe operation and scope. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L2

A3000 kg tandem load splits 60/40. First truck 1700 rating: result?

**Answer:** 1800/1200 kg share; first exceeds by 100.

**Reason/remediation:** Equal sharing cannot be assumed; arithmetic is not lift approval. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L1

A lock warning occurs on the last lift. Can handover say ready?

**Answer:** No; record defect/restriction and safe state.

**Reason/remediation:** Successful delivery is not defect clearance. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L2

Does a repair release approve a different attachment next shift?

**Answer:** No; verify the new configuration and required checks.

**Reason/remediation:** Repair scope and operating scope differ. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Completion versus delivery

Materials can be complete without pretending a learner operated equipment. Actual training must validate current manuals, accepted attachments, exact charts, machine condition, ground/receiving structures, electrical controls, signals and assessor competence. Learner operating certification requires recorded performance and employer responsibilities; it cannot be granted by this document's completion status.

Unassessed winch/crane, personnel-platform, tandem, public-road and engineering work remain excluded. A single-type assessment produces a clearly limited record. No external CCO award, manufacturer endorsement or complete proprietary-standard equivalence is claimed. These are explicit boundaries of the developed program, not hidden unfinished lessons.
