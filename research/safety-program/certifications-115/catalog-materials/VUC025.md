# VUC025 instructor, practical and credential guide

## Definition and outcomes

Audience: workers performing routine rigging of known loads using an established arrangement. Prerequisites: understand instructional/task language, identify numbers/units and read supplied ratings, site induction, required PPE and employer permission for supervised work. No prior crane qualification is implied or required for bench learning. Actual lift activities use an authorized operator and appropriately qualified rigging supervision. Learners do not independently design unknown/eccentric/complex lifts.

Objectives R01–R14: establish job-specific authority; verify load/path; interpret ratings/angles; inspect and isolate defects; handle each sling material; install hitches; assemble compatible hardware; communicate/control positioning; tie and recognize five basic nonlifting knots; land/release/store; recognize changed/special-device conditions; execute a known-load arrangement; use hoist/jack/movement-system principles; inspect specialty hardware and distinguish material-specific rules. The coursebook supplies instruction and worked applications for every objective, rather than relying on a topic list.

Planned 24 instructional hours, excluding breaks. This VNDRLY delivery plan is not an OSHA-mandated duration. Day 1: R01–R05 instruction/checks 180 min, R06–R07 demonstrations 90 min, inspection/hitch/hardware practice 180 min, corrections 30 min =480. Day 2: R08–R11 andR13–R14 instruction/checks 180 min, knot/signal/device practice 180 min, R12 integrated laboratory 120 min =480. Day 3: individual practical assessment 240 min, final 75 min, targeted remediation/changed reassessment 105 min, individual records/debrief 60 min =480. Extend for learner needs and cohort size rather than reducing individual observed handling.

Instructor/evaluator: documented rigging knowledge, training and experience for the actual task/equipment, ability to recognize hazards and authority to stop practice. Qualification is job-specific; a course instructor title alone is insufficient. Competent inspection responsibilities remain with the employer's designated personnel. Use qualified operator/signal personnel where needed. One active lift team at a time under attentive supervision; nobody performs distracting assessment paperwork while responsible for live safety.

Equipment: inspected rated chain, wire-rope, web/roundslings and demonstration metal-mesh/fiber samples; clearly segregated defective specimens; compatible hooks, shackles, links, adjustable hardware, eyebolts/hoist rings and supplied manuals; low stable training load with verified weight/center of gravity/lifting points; rated hoisting system and verified support; dunnage, edge protection, barriers, tags, measuring tools, radios and official signal chart; nonlifting knot ropes/post; jack/hoist/trolley/skate demonstrations with suitable supports. Defects are on isolated samples/photos, never introduced into live lifting equipment.

Pre-delivery sheet: site____; instructor/assessor____; operator/signal personnel____; learner scope____; equipment IDs/manual revisions____; actual rating/hitch/angle limits____; known training load/CG/points____; support/lifting-machine verification____; route/landing/exclusion plan____; emergency/stop arrangements____; weather/chemical/temperature controls____. Resolve all required fields before live work. Use a low practice load that preserves real handling decisions; do not test maximum capacities or proof-load learner assemblies.

## Assessment and award rules

Complete 28 formative checks and correct misconceptions with explanation and changed practice. Final:42 constructed-response questions,84 points,75 minutes plus accommodations. Score 2 correct action/reason or calculation;1 safe incomplete;0 incorrect/absent/unsafe. Pass 68/84 and 4/6 in each of 14 domains. An explicit endorsement of knowingly overloaded/unidentified/damaged rigging, body beneath an exposed suspended load, defeated retention, unsafe knot-as-sling substitution or continuing after a stop is a critical fail. Record the response, not a vague label.

The separate final/key/reassessment package is in `.local/private-training/rigger-one-instructor/`. No answers are included on the candidate final. This local separation is not a claim of implemented application access control. Preserve attempts and score sheets. Retake after targeted instruction/practice using changed domain cases and an integrated task; same-day timing is allowed only when actual relearning and independent reassessment occur, not memorized repetition. A second failed attempt requires a documented individual development plan before another attempt.

Each practical station has five criteria, scored 0 unsafe/not demonstrated,1 safe with prompting/incomplete,2 independent/correct. Pass each at 8/10 and every starred criterion at 2. Stop unsafe action immediately. Repeat failed tasks with changed equipment/cases after instruction. Discussion cannot substitute for actual hitch/connection/knot and complete-lift performance. Device demonstrations not actually assessed must be marked awareness-only on the record, not silently included in job authorization.

## A — Job briefing and load verification

Task: inspect a supplied known-load work order, explain the assigned routine scope and prepare path/landing controls. Inject unknown contents on a second card. Equipment: actual load documentation, site plan and synthetic qualification record.

Criteria:1 identifies roles/scope;2 verifies weight/CG/lifting points;3 identifies path/landing and exclusion needs;4*holds unknown/changed load;5 communicates the exact unresolved information. Expected decision: similar appearance does not validate changed contents. Record the actual evidence used.

## B — Material-specific pre-use inspection

Task: examine at least six identified specimens covering chain, wire rope, web, round, fiber and mesh, plus hooks/connectors. Some defects are photographs or isolated samples. Decide serviceable/hold and explain the correct criteria.

Criteria:1 reads identification/rating;2 inspects full accessible material/terminations;3*rejects damaged/unidentified item;4 distinguishes material-specific criteria and periodic versus pre-use inspection;5 tags/isolate/reports with identity and finding. Include a cut web, broken strand/defective rope, distorted chain link, roundsling-cover damage, fiber deterioration and distorted mesh handle. Do not mark 'all slings checked' without individual observations.

## C — Hitch installation and edge protection

Task: demonstrate vertical, basket and choker arrangements on a stable bench/low training load using the supplied approved configuration and ratings. Compare the installed hitch with the correct tag value.

Criteria:1 selects correct hitch/rating;2 seats untwisted sling and protects edges;3*rejects unstable/slipping or overloaded arrangement;4 keeps hands out during controlled slack take-up;5 explains angle/bend/contact limits. Observe every hitch separately. A favorable basket rating does not approve an unstable load.

## D — Hardware connection

Task: build an approved connection with lifting point, shackle and sling, then identify faults in separate disabled examples: wrong pin, missing retainer, tip-loaded hook, bunched synthetic eye and misaligned eyebolt.

Criteria:1 verifies identity/rating/direction;2 checks compatibility/fit;3*rejects substitute pin or missing required retention;4 installs/seats according to actual instructions;5 explains why side-load/rotation/engagement changes need review. No field-modified hardware or lift proof test is permitted.

## E — Rating and geometry laboratory

Task: solve two supplied known-arrangement cases and a changed-angle hold case. Use actual supplied rating charts for the physical arrangement; synthetic calculations are exercises only.

Criteria:1 identifies units/angle convention;2 selects correct hitch value;3 calculates/checks supplied leg demand;4*holds a component/configuration overload;5 explains that inspection/load integrity/path still govern. Example:3,000 lb symmetric at 30 degrees means 3,000 lb per ideal leg; a 2,500 lb leg rating fails. Assess reasoning, not only arithmetic.

## F — Five basic nonlifting knots

Task: on the bench tie/dress/set an inside bowline, clove hitch, square knot, two half hitches and sheet bend, then distinguish a deliberately wrong configuration. Use appropriate ropes and a post; no suspended load or person is attached.

Criteria:1 identifies ends/standing part and follows sequence;2 produces each correct dressed structure;3 uses appropriate bight/tail orientation;4*rejects knot substitution for rated sling/life-safety equipment;5 explains slipping/cyclic-load/backup limitations and checks the task's instructions. Record each knot individually as pass/pending; aggregate score cannot hide a missing knot. Repeat in a changed order without verbal prompting.

## G — Signals, tag line and position

Task: rehearse stop/emergency stop/hoist/lower/swing/slow and the actual task signals with disabled equipment, then demonstrate safe tag-line handling during the approved low lift. Inject an ambiguous/lost signal as a card while the load can be safely stopped.

Criteria:1 identifies designated signal role;2 correctly interprets practiced functions;3*obeys stop/holds ambiguity;4*keeps body clear and never wraps the line around it;5 maintains escape path/communication. This is task-signal practice, not a separate signal-person credential.

## H — Hoists, jacks and movement devices

Task: inspect a hoist/support connection, demonstrate a suitable low jack-and-secure sequence on a stable training fixture and identify trolley/skate/A-frame hazards from equipment/manuals or scenarios. No body part enters beneath the load; no complex load transfer is attempted.

Criteria:1 checks entire support/anchor system;2 verifies device rating/retention and setup;3*secures raised load rather than relying on pressure;4 identifies travel/side-pull/rolling hazards;5 rejects makeshift extension, unknown beam or unplanned multiple-device operation. Mark physical tasks versus awareness scenarios precisely.

## I — Complete known-load lift and landing

Task: individually inspect, connect and manage the approved routine arrangement with a qualified lifting team; conduct controlled initial lift, guide from a safe position, land on prepared support, release and remove/store gear. The instructor may present a communication-loss card; no real instability is created.

Criteria:1 verifies load/rigging/hardware/path;2 correctly installs and checks slack take-up;3*maintains exclusion/stop decisions and rejects unexpected shift;4*lands stably before releasing;5 removes/stores and reports without jerking trapped gear. All actions must be observed; a spoken checklist is insufficient.

## J — Special-device and changed-condition decisions

Task: resolve cards for a clamp outside its thickness range, unknown chemical on webbing, proposed tandem lift, block-support reaction and undocumented swivel-under-load use.

Criteria:1 identifies device-specific restriction;2*rejects damaged/unknown equipment use;3 recognizes higher-level planning boundary;4 identifies reaction/load-direction issue;5 states evidence/person needed to resolve each hold. At least one case uses an actual manufacturer's instruction page; do not accept generic 'be careful' responses.

# VNDRLY/U Rigger Level I — learner coursebook

VUC025 · Original edition 1 · 17 November 2026

This program teaches simple, repetitive rigging tasks with known load information and an established suitable rigging arrangement. It does not authorize independent design of complex lifts, unknown-center-of-gravity work, engineered rotations, personnel lifting or tandem-crane operations. Employer determination of qualification is specific to the job, equipment and load. This VNDRLY credential is not CCO certification and does not automatically confer signal-person or crane-operator qualification.

## R01 — Know the task and your responsibility

Rigging connects a load to the lifting system so that it can be raised, controlled and landed without losing stability or damaging equipment. A rigger does more than attach a hook. The job includes checking the load information, inspecting equipment, confirming the connection, keeping people clear and stopping when conditions no longer match the plan.

Before work, identify who directs the lift, who operates the lifting machine, who signals and who determines the rigging arrangement. One person can hold several roles only when actually qualified and able to perform them safely. An operator card is not proof of rigging skill. Likewise, completing this course does not make the learner the qualified person for every lift. Under OSHA construction crane rules, qualified riggers are required for specified assembly/disassembly hoisting and when workers perform the listed hooking, guiding or connection activities within the fall zone.

For a Level I assignment, the load's weight, center of gravity and appropriate arrangement are known or provided through established experience and planning. Verify that today's load is the expected one. Stop if equipment is changed, the load contains unknown contents, the planned hitch cannot be installed, or the path creates an unplanned hazard. Escalation is a successful safety decision, not a failed attempt to be helpful.

Worked example: yesterday's dry pump weighed 1,800 pounds; today's similar pump contains liquid and has a new motor. Do not reuse yesterday's rigging decision by appearance. Obtain the current weight and balance information, then have the arrangement confirmed. Common mistake: calling a repeated job routine even after its important conditions change.

## R02 — Read the load and prepare the path

Confirm load identity, reliable weight, dimensions, center of gravity, intended lifting points and loose or moving parts. Inspect the load itself: a rated sling attached to an unverified lifting eye is not a safe system. Shipping brackets, pipe nozzles and handrails are not lifting points unless identified for that purpose. Remove or secure loose items according to the plan. Account for trapped fluids or material, attachments and packaging.

The center of gravity is the point at which the load's weight acts. Place the hook and planned suspension so the load hangs in the intended orientation. When the center of gravity lies away from the apparent middle, equal-length legs may carry unequal forces. A high center of gravity can make a load overturn even if the slings are strong enough. Do not solve an unfamiliar balance problem by trial-and-error hoisting around people.

Prepare the route and landing before lifting. Look for power lines, live equipment, traffic, overhead obstructions, occupied areas, excavations and trip hazards. Establish swing/fall exclusion controls with the lift team. Set stable dunnage so the load will not roll, tip or crush slings at landing. Keep an escape route; do not position yourself between the suspended load and a fixed object.

Worked decision: a crate's label states a gross shipping weight, but one side is broken and the supplied lifting points are inaccessible. Knowing the weight does not solve the connection or containment problem. Hold the lift and have an appropriate method established. Common mistake: treating the load as a single solid object when its contents can shift.

## R03 — Understand ratings, angles and the weakest component

Working load limit or rated capacity is the permitted load for stated conditions. Minimum breaking strength is not the usable working limit. Design factors are already part of the rated equipment's design; they are not extra capacity available to a user. Read the identification and manufacturer instructions for the actual sling, hitch, angle, temperature and attachments.

Different configurations change the allowable load. Vertical, basket and choker values are not interchangeable. A basket's favorable rating depends on the stated leg angle, balanced support and other conditions; do not automatically double a vertical number. Choking bends and loads the sling differently, so use the applicable choker rating and angle restrictions. A four-leg bridle does not prove equal quarter shares.

Low sling angles increase leg tension. For a symmetric two-leg instructional arrangement, tension per leg equals total weight divided by twice the sine of the leg angle above horizontal. For 4,000 pounds, each leg carries 2,000 pounds at 90 degrees, about 2,309 at 60 degrees,2,828 at 45 degrees and 4,000 at 30 degrees. These are ideal static values, not permission to design an unfamiliar lift. Use the approved arrangement and manufacturer's rated chart. Clarify whether an angle is measured from horizontal or vertical; confusing the convention can reverse the conclusion.

The system is limited by its weakest applicable component and configuration. A5,000-pound sling does not make a 3,000-pound connector adequate for 4,000 pounds. Include actual angle, load sharing and attachment effects rather than comparing every label to total load blindly. Stop for missing/illegible identification; guessed capacity based on color, diameter or resemblance is not a substitute.

Application: a plan specifies two rated legs at 60 degrees but limited headroom makes them 30 degrees. The force per leg rises substantially. Do not proceed with the old arrangement or shorten a sling by a knot; have the plan revised. Common mistake: believing a flatter bridle reduces force because the hook is lower.

## R04 — Inspect and control defective equipment

Inspect the sling and every fastening/attachment before use each shift/day as required and again when conditions warrant. The employer's designated competent person and periodic-inspection arrangements remain necessary. Identify the item and its rating, then inspect the full accessible length and all connection points in good light. Look at both sides of webbing, chain links, rope contact areas, sleeves, eyes and hardware—not only the part nearest the tag.

Remove defective equipment from service and isolate it so another worker cannot unknowingly use it. Tag the item with identity, defect, date and reporter; follow the approved repair/disposition process. Do not repair a cut sling with tape, weld a chain link in the field, straighten a hook or replace a shackle pin with a convenient bolt. A proof test is not something a Level I rigger improvises to decide whether damaged equipment is usable.

Wire-rope slings need checks for broken wires, kinks, crushing, birdcaging, corrosion, damaged fittings and heat effects. Chain needs checks for cracks, stretch, bent/twisted links, wear and damaged attachments. Synthetic slings need checks for cuts, punctures, burns, melted areas, broken stitching and distorted hardware. Missing identification is itself a reason not to use the item. Apply the correct material/manufacturer removal criteria rather than a universal percentage to every product.

Periodic inspections do not replace today's check. Alloy-chain sling periodic inspection intervals must reflect service and must not exceed twelve months under the cited OSHA provisions. Keep the required record of the most recent inspection month. Severe use may require much more frequent inspection. A recent certificate cannot excuse a fresh defect.

Worked example: a sling was inspected last week but now has a cut at the bearing point. Isolate it immediately and replace it with an identified suitable item. Record the condition and inspect the load edge/protection so the replacement is not damaged the same way. Common mistake: replacing equipment without correcting the cause of damage.

## R05 — Select and handle sling materials

Alloy chain tolerates some environments that damage synthetics, but it still has material, temperature and configuration limits. Use rated lifting chain, not general-purpose chain of unknown grade. Shortening must use an approved device and method; do not tie, twist or bolt links together. A link should articulate as designed without being bent over an unsuitable sharp corner.

Wire-rope slings combine strength with flexibility, but bending over a small diameter reduces usable capacity and can damage the rope. Follow the manufacturer's bearing-diameter requirements. Do not knot wire rope. Under the construction rule, sling/bridle eyes are not formed with wire-rope clips or knots. A clip diagram for another permitted connection does not authorize making a sling eye. Protect hands from broken wires, but gloves do not make handling a moving or tensioned rope safe.

Synthetic web and roundslings are light and protect some finished surfaces, but edges, heat and chemicals can cut or degrade them. Use purpose-suitable edge protection that stays positioned and handles the actual contact, not an unverified rag. Keep the sling flat and unbunched in the fitting. A roundsling's cover protects internal load-bearing fibers; damage or uncertain core condition requires the manufacturer's rejection procedure. Color alone never establishes capacity across manufacturers.

Chemical compatibility matters. The construction web-sling rule prohibits nylon in specified acid/phenolic exposure and polyester/polypropylene in specified caustic exposure; manufacturer restrictions may be tighter. Polyester/nylon web slings are not used above 180°F under that rule, while polypropylene has a 200°F ceiling. These figures do not rate every synthetic product or permit use when heat damage is visible. Identify the actual material and conditions before selection.

Fiber-rope and metal-mesh slings also require identified ratings and material-specific inspection. Do not substitute an ordinary tag line for a lifting sling. Keep equipment clean and stored off damaging surfaces, away from incompatible chemicals, heat and sunlight exposure as directed. Application: an unknown cleaning chemical has soaked a synthetic sling. Isolate it and obtain the manufacturer's disposition; drying it does not prove restored strength.

## R06 — Install vertical, basket and choker hitches

A vertical hitch connects one end of the sling to the lifting device and the other to an approved load point. Ensure both ends seat correctly and the load cannot rotate or tip unexpectedly. A single vertical connection does not stabilize every shape. Follow the established arrangement and use required control lines without putting people beneath the load.

A basket supports the load in the sling body with both ends connected above. Place it so the load is contained and cannot slide out. Verify the actual leg angle, bend radius, balanced bearing and equipment rating. A basket around a smooth pipe may allow rolling or slipping unless the approved arrangement controls it. Two baskets are not automatically equal-sharing supports; the center of gravity and location matter.

A choker passes around the load and through its own approved eye or fitting. Position the choke so it closes as intended and does not bear on an unsuitable splice, fitting or damaged section. Use the actual choker capacity and permitted choke-angle conditions. A choker does not inherently contain loose pieces or prevent a long load from tipping. Do not hammer a choke tight or jerk it into position.

Before tension, remove twists, seat eyes fully, position edge protection and keep hands away from where slack will close. Ask for slow slack take-up, then stop and inspect the arrangement from a safe position. If a sling walks, pinches or pulls out of its intended position, land/relax safely and correct it. Never reach into a tightening hitch.

Worked comparison: an identified instructional sling is rated 4,000 vertical,3,200 choker and 8,000 basket only with vertical legs under the stated conditions. A3,500-pound choker lift fails despite passing the vertical rating. A7,000-pound basket case is not approved until its angles, balance and contact conditions match the stated basket rating. Common mistake: using the largest number on the tag regardless of the hitch installed.

## R07 — Connect hooks, shackles and lifting points correctly

Identify each hardware component, its manufacturer, rating and intended direction of loading. Inspect for cracks, deformation, wear, damaged threads and missing retainers. Confirm compatibility between sling eyes, hooks, pins and lifting points. A connection can have sufficient nominal strength but still pinch a sling, load a hook tip or bend a lifting eye in an unauthorized direction.

Seat a hook in its intended bowl/load-bearing area. Do not load the latch, tip or side unless the specific device is designed and rated for that condition. A latch helps retain the connection; it does not carry the load. Check the actual latch and any required retention. Eyebolts and swivel hoist rings have different installation and angular-loading rules. Use the correct thread, engagement, seating, torque and alignment from the actual manufacturer; do not force an eyebolt to point in a desired direction by backing it out.

For shackles, use the correct original-compatible pin and body. Engage screw pins fully according to the manufacturer's method; bolt-type assemblies require their specified nut and retention. Do not substitute bolts or mix unidentified parts. Center the intended loading and follow the actual product's side-loading restrictions. Round-pin, screw-pin and bolt-type shackles do not share every application rule. Multiple sling legs require a suitable collector arrangement, not arbitrary crowding on the pin.

A moving sling can rotate a screw pin if installed in a way that bears and moves against it. Select and orient the connection under the approved instructions so it cannot loosen. For long-duration, vibration or special use, follow the manufacturer's specific retention/type requirements. Do not invent a universal side-load reduction factor or assume that adding wire makes every arrangement acceptable.

Application: a synthetic eye is wider than the shackle's usable bearing surface and folds sharply. Stop and select a compatible connector; forcing it through can reduce capacity and damage fibers. Common mistake: selecting hardware only by its stamped tonnage while ignoring fit and load direction.

## R08 — Control the lift, communication and personal position

Brief the operator and signal person before lifting. Establish who gives normal motion signals, how communication transfers and how to stop. A rigger may signal only within the required qualification/authorization; this program's basic signal practice does not automatically satisfy a separate signal-person qualification. Anyone may give a stop warning and the operator must obey it.

Take up slack slowly while riggers move to safe positions. Verify seating and balance at the lowest practical trial clearance specified by the plan. If the load tilts, hardware shifts or a sling becomes unevenly loaded, stop and safely land it for correction. Do not stabilize it by pushing from a trapped position or placing hands under it. Maintain the exclusion zone throughout movement.

Use a tag line when the plan requires control of rotation. Keep it free of loops around people, sharp contacts and snag points. Do not wrap it around your hand, arm or body. Stand outside the load's fall/crush path, with a clear escape route. Avoid electrical exposure; a line is not safe to touch merely because it appears nonmetallic. If maintaining control requires entering a dangerous position, stop and change the plan.

Use clear signals: stop is a horizontally extended palm-down arm moving side to side; emergency stop uses both arms. Hoist uses an upward-pointing index finger/forearm making small circles; lower uses a downward-pointing hand/index finger circling. Swing indicates direction with an extended arm/index finger. Post the official chart and practice all signals needed by the task. Ambiguous or lost required signals mean stop, not guess.

Worked decision: the load is moving toward a column and the signal person is distracted. Give stop immediately and clear the pinch area. Do not wait because you were not the designated normal signaler. Common mistake: treating a tag line as permission to stand between the load and its destination.

## R09 — Knots for nonlifting control lines

Knot practice develops recognition and handling for approved nonlifting control-line tasks. It never authorizes knotted wire-rope/web/fiber slings, personnel support or improvised lifting eyes. Knots reduce rope strength and can slip, jam or capsize depending on rope, loading and construction. The rope's working end is the free end being manipulated; the standing part is the longer loaded or fixed portion. Dress a knot by arranging its strands neatly, then set it by controlled tension. Leave the manufacturer/task-required tails and use any specified backup. If the method is uncertain, do not put it into service.

**Square/reef knot, bench recognition:** cross the right working end over the left and tuck under, then cross the end now on the left over the other and tuck under. Dress the two interlocking bights flat; each tail lies alongside its own standing part. Repeating the same handed crossing produces a different, unreliable granny configuration. Use the square knot only for appropriate light binding, not joining loaded lifting lines or life safety.

**Sheet bend:** form a bight in one line. Pass the other working end through the bight, take it around both legs of the bight and tuck it beneath its own standing part. Dress it with the ends on the same side; use the thicker/stiffer line as the bight when the approved application permits unlike lines. Check the configuration rather than trusting resemblance. It is not a substitute for a rated sling splice.

**Clove hitch around a post:** take one turn around the post, cross the working end over the standing part, take a second turn and tuck the working end beneath that second turn. The finished hitch has two turns with a crossing between them. It can slip or roll under changing pull; use only in an approved control-line application with the required backup. Do not use it as an unqualified lifting or rescue connection.

**Two half hitches:** pass the line around the support, then carry the working end around the standing part and through the resulting loop to make a half hitch. Repeat in the same direction around the standing part. Dress the hitches together and set them. Both hitches encircle the standing part, not two unrelated points. Confirm the support and anticipated pull are suitable for the approved nonlifting task.

**Inside bowline:** make a small loop in the standing part, pass the working end up through it, around the standing part and back down through the small loop. Dress the fixed eye and collar so the tail finishes inside the eye. The trainer verifies the correct structure rather than only the familiar mnemonic. A bowline can loosen under cyclic/slack loading; use task-required backup and inspection. This bench exercise never grants fall-protection or hoisting approval.

Application: a worker proposes tying a bowline in an untagged rope to replace a missing lifting sling. Refuse. Correct knot shape does not establish a rated lifting assembly. Common mistake: assuming proficiency at a knot makes every use of that knot safe.

## R10 — Land, release, store and report

Check the landing surface and dunnage before the load arrives. Confirm capacity, spacing, stability and access for rigging removal. Keep people out of pinch points and use the planned tools/positions. Only necessary workers enter permitted receiving positions; nobody stands directly beneath an exposed suspended load. A load is not safely landed merely because one corner touches the ground.

Lower in a controlled way until the load is fully supported and stable. Obtain the agreed confirmation before slackening or disconnecting. Watch for rolling cylinders, spring-loaded components, high centers of gravity and stacked materials. Do not release rigging that is still carrying load. If a sling is trapped, do not jerk it out with the crane; re-support/reposition under an approved safe plan.

Remove hooks and hardware without pulling them across people or damaging the sling. Secure loose parts and store rigging on suitable racks or containers, protected from vehicles, weather and incompatible chemicals as required. Keep quarantined equipment separate. Update the equipment/defect record and tell the next user about unresolved restrictions.

Worked example: a pipe bundle lands on uneven blocks and starts to roll as tension is released. Stop the release, clear people and safely reestablish support according to the plan; do not try to wedge a foot beneath it. Common mistake: considering rigging finished at the instant the crane stops moving rather than after stable release and safe removal.

## R11 — Recognize changing conditions and special devices

Wind, ice, heat, chemicals, sharp edges and poor visibility can invalidate an otherwise familiar arrangement. Shock loading occurs when slack is suddenly taken up or a moving load stops abruptly; it can exceed static calculated forces. Do not use a jerk to free a snagged load or pull rigging from beneath weight. Stop if the load hangs on an obstruction or its apparent weight changes unexpectedly.

Below-the-hook devices such as lifting beams, spreaders, plate clamps, magnets and vacuum lifters require their own identification, inspection and instructions. A lifting beam resists bending while a spreader typically carries compression through its geometry; neither can be replaced with an unverified piece of steel. Plate clamps have material/thickness, orientation and engagement limits. Magnets and vacuum lifters depend on surface and operating conditions; visible contact alone does not prove secure holding.

Custom accessories require the applicable design, marking and test process; this course does not authorize shop-built substitutes. A manufacturer-specified load limit must be respected even if a device looks strong. Turning a load, working with an unknown center of gravity, lifting with multiple cranes or combining several hoists adds load-transfer questions that go beyond this Level I routine-task scope. Get appropriately qualified planning and personnel.

Application: a clamp selected for a flat plate is proposed for a curved, painted piece outside its listed conditions. Refuse the substitution and obtain suitable equipment and a revised method. Common mistake: treating every accessory with a hook as generic rigging.

## R12 — Integrated routine-rigging laboratory

The following data are fictional training data, never ratings for real equipment. A known uniform 4,000-pound practice load has approved symmetric lifting points. The established plan uses two equal legs at 60 degrees above horizontal. Each instructional leg is rated 3,000 pounds in this exact arrangement; each connector is rated 3,500 pounds in the specified in-line condition. Ideal calculated leg tension is 4,000/(2×0.866)=approximately 2,310 pounds, below both limits. This numerical comparison is one step: inspection, fit, actual center of gravity, support, machine rating, path and protective measures must also pass.

Change 1: actual angle becomes 30 degrees. Each leg would carry 4,000 pounds, exceeding both listed limits. Stop and get the arrangement revised. Change 2: the sling tag is unreadable but it looks identical to the other leg. Do not use it. Change 3: one lifting eye is visibly cracked. The entire lift is held even though the rigging is adequate. Change 4: a sharp edge contacts the sling during travel. Stop and provide appropriate protection or another approved arrangement before proceeding.

Second exercise: an instructional sling label states 3,000 vertical,2,400 choker,6,000 basket with vertical legs under the listed conditions. A2,600-pound load in a choker fails by 200 pounds. A2,200-pound load passes that numerical hitch limit, but not if the load can slip out, the sling is damaged or the connection is unsuitable. Explain the difference between a rating comparison and a complete safe-lift decision.

Third exercise: a supplied plan calls for a known 2,000-pound component, rated vertical sling and approved lifting point. Before take-up, the learner checks item identification, hardware seating/retention, exclusion controls, signal agreement and stable landing support. After a low controlled initial lift, the load hangs as planned. The learner guides only from the safe designated position, signals stop for an injected communication-loss card, then resumes only after clarification and lands/releases safely. The instructor evaluates actual handling with a suitable low training load, not just a spoken checklist.

Independent questions: a 4,800-pound symmetric load at 30 degrees imposes what ideal force per leg? Answer 4,800 pounds. A2,500-pound choker job with 2,400 capacity? Hold,100 pounds over. A rated shackle with an unmatched bolt? Reject the connection. A basket arrangement whose load can slide? Hold and obtain a stable approved method. In each answer, identify the evidence required to resolve the hold rather than merely saying 'be careful.'

## R13 — Hoists, jacks and controlled horizontal movement

Identify the complete support system for a chain hoist, lever hoist or winch: equipment rating, anchor/support capacity, hook connections, load path and actual line direction. A hoist's rating does not establish the capacity of the beam or anchor supporting it. Inspect chains/ropes, hooks, brakes, controls, casing, attachment and identification. Follow the actual instructions for setup and use; do not wrap the hoist's load chain around the load as a sling unless the device is expressly designed for that arrangement. Do not extend a lever or exceed permitted hand effort to force motion. Unexpected resistance means stop and investigate the load, alignment and restraint.

A trolley must fit its beam flange/profile and be adjusted as specified, with required end stops and retention. A beam clamp must be rated for its actual application and direction, not merely fit around steel. Do not treat an A-frame or portable gantry as stable at every height/span or permit side pull without its approved configuration. Check the floor/support, brakes and travel restrictions. Movement of a loaded frame or trolley follows its specific approved procedure; no universal permission comes from wheels being present.

Jacks need an identified capacity, firm support, suitable contact and protection against overtravel. Plan the lifting sequence and stable cribbing before raising anything. Once raised, secure the load promptly with suitable blocking/cribbing or another approved support; never rely on hydraulic pressure alone to protect someone beneath or between equipment. Keep hands out of the closing gap and do not crawl under to place a block. Follow the approved low-increment method and use tools/positions that keep people clear. Remove/tag defective jacks.

Dollies, skates and rollers change a load from suspended to rolling, but do not eliminate overturning or crush risks. Confirm floor capacity/condition, route slope, thresholds, turning geometry, load distribution and means of controlled propulsion/restraint. Keep the load secured to its intended supports and keep people out of the downhill or trapped side. Do not use loose pipes or improvised rollers as substitutes for a planned rated arrangement. Complex multi-jack or load-transfer work requires additional planning and competence beyond this routine-task course.

Application: a 4,000-pound hoist is hung from an unverified pipe rack for a 2,000-pound lift. Hold: the hoist label does not rate the supporting structure. Common mistake: checking the visible lifting device while ignoring where its reaction goes.

## R14 — Adjustable hardware, material-specific inspection and connections

Turnbuckles and other adjustable connectors must be intended and rated for the actual lifting/tension application. Verify thread condition, permitted engagement, straight load path and required locking/retention. Do not use a hardware-store tensioner for lifting or bend a turnbuckle to align it. A swivel that positions a load is not automatically rated to rotate continuously under load; verify the exact device's instructions. Master links and collector rings need adequate bearing space and compatible orientation so connected legs do not crowd, pry or cross-load them.

Rigging blocks redirect a line and transfer force to their anchorage. The support reaction can exceed the line tension: an ideal 180-degree return can impose approximately twice the line tension on the block support. This is a hazard-recognition example, not authority for a Level I worker to design a winch system. Confirm the specified block/sheave/rope compatibility, side-plate closure, pin retention and rated attachment before use. Do not let a rope run over an unsuitable sheave or place hands near the nip point.

Compression/swaged fittings and wire-rope terminations are manufactured/installed to specified procedures. A rigger inspects for cracks, distortion, slippage, corrosion and improper assembly, and rejects doubtful items; the rigger does not field-swage or repair them without the separate competence/equipment/procedure. Never copy a clip connection onto a construction sling eye where that method is prohibited.

For wire-rope slings in general industry, OSHA1910.184 identifies removal criteria including ten randomly distributed broken wires in one rope lay or five in one strand in a lay, specified outside-wire wear, structural distortion, heat damage and defective end attachments. Do not confuse these sling criteria with a crane running-rope rule. Construction and manufacturer requirements also apply according to the task. For hooks, use the actual product's criteria and applicable regulation, not an arbitrary universal wear allowance. Visible cracks or deformation demand isolation and assessment.

Metal-mesh slings need inspection of wire fabric and handles for broken welds/brazed joints, wire wear/corrosion, distortion and loss of flexibility. Coatings can hide damage; follow the manufacturer's inspection method. Fiber-rope slings need checks for cut/broken fibers, powdered fibers, abnormal wear, changed strand shape, discoloration/rot and distorted fittings. These products are not accepted merely because they lack a visible tear like a web sling. Use the correct identification, hitch rating, temperature and chemical limits for each material.

Application: a worker applies the crane-rope broken-wire threshold to a wire-rope sling and signs it off without checking the sling rule or manufacturer. Stop that acceptance and use the applicable criteria. Common mistake: memorizing one inspection number and applying it to every rope product.

# Formative checks

Attempt before reading answers. Correct each misconception with the named chapter and a changed case before assessment.

## R01-L1

Does a crane operator certificate automatically establish rigging qualification?

**Answer:** No; the employer must establish ability for the specific rigging task.

**Reason/remediation:** Different roles require demonstrated task competence.

## R01-L2

What should happen when a routine load changes to an unknown weight?

**Answer:** Hold and obtain current information and an appropriate arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** The prior routine scope no longer describes the job.

## R02-L1

Name four load facts to verify.

**Answer:** Weight, center of gravity, suitable lifting points and load integrity/contents.

**Reason/remediation:** A strong sling cannot correct unknown or unsuitable load attachment.

## R02-L2

Why prepare dunnage before lifting?

**Answer:** To provide a stable landing and safe rigging removal.

**Reason/remediation:** Landing is part of the plan, not an afterthought.

## R03-L1

For an ideal symmetric 4,000 lb load at 30 degrees above horizontal, what is each leg tension?

**Answer:** 4,000 lb.

**Reason/remediation:** 4,000/(2×sin 30)=4,000.

## R03-L2

Can the design factor be treated as spare lifting capacity?

**Answer:** No; stay within the rated working limit and its conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Breaking strength is not a usable rating.

## R04-L1

A sling inspected last week now has a cut. Use it?

**Answer:** No; remove/isolate and report it, then correct the damaging condition.

**Reason/remediation:** Current damage overrides a recent inspection.

## R04-L2

What makes an equipment-hold tag useful?

**Answer:** Identity, specific defect, date/reporter and restriction/disposition.

**Reason/remediation:** Another worker must understand why it cannot be used.

## R05-L1

Can ordinary tag-line rope replace a lifting sling?

**Answer:** No; it lacks the required rated lifting assembly/configuration.

**Reason/remediation:** Material resemblance is not lifting identification.

## R05-L2

Why is a soft rag not automatically adequate edge protection?

**Answer:** It may cut, move or fail under the actual contact load.

**Reason/remediation:** Protection must suit the edge, sling and loading.

## R06-L1

A sling is 4,000 vertical and 3,200 choker. Can it choke a 3,500 lb load?

**Answer:** No; it exceeds the applicable choker rating by 300 lb.

**Reason/remediation:** Use the installed hitch rating.

## R06-L2

A basket can slide along a smooth pipe. Is capacity alone enough?

**Answer:** No; obtain an approved stable arrangement preventing escape/rolling.

**Reason/remediation:** Containment and balance are separate from strength.

## R07-L1

May a convenient bolt replace a missing shackle pin?

**Answer:** No; use the correct identified compatible assembly.

**Reason/remediation:** An arbitrary bolt is not the rated pin.

## R07-L2

Why should a hook latch not carry the load?

**Answer:** It retains the connection; the intended hook bowl carries the load.

**Reason/remediation:** Tip/latch loading can defeat the rated configuration.

## R08-L1

Who may signal stop?

**Answer:** Anyone; it must be obeyed.

**Reason/remediation:** Normal signal assignment does not prevent hazard warnings.

## R08-L2

Is wrapping a tag line around an arm acceptable for grip?

**Answer:** No; keep clear and use the approved controlled handling method.

**Reason/remediation:** A moving load can drag or trap the person.

## R09-L1

What distinguishes the taught inside bowline tail orientation?

**Answer:** The tail is inside the fixed eye after correct dressing.

**Reason/remediation:** Verify structure, not only the knot name.

## R09-L2

Does correct knot tying authorize a knotted sling?

**Answer:** No; the bench knots are for approved nonlifting tasks only.

**Reason/remediation:** Knots do not establish a rated lifting assembly.

## R10-L1

When may rigging be disconnected after landing?

**Answer:** After the load is fully supported, stable and the rigging unloaded under the plan.

**Reason/remediation:** One corner touching is not stable release.

## R10-L2

A sling is trapped under a landed load. Jerk it out?

**Answer:** No; safely re-support/reposition under an approved plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Shock and uncontrolled movement can injure or damage.

## R11-L1

Can a clamp be used outside its specified thickness/orientation?

**Answer:** No; select suitable equipment and revise the method.

**Reason/remediation:** A hook on a device does not make its application universal.

## R11-L2

What does an unexpected snag during lifting require?

**Answer:** Stop and safely resolve it; do not jerk or force the load free.

**Reason/remediation:** Breakout/shock forces may exceed the planned static load.

## R12-L1

A2,600 lb load uses a 2,400 lb choker rating. Decision?

**Answer:** Hold;200 lb overload.

**Reason/remediation:** The selected hitch governs.

## R12-L2

The rigging is adequate but a lifting eye is cracked. Continue?

**Answer:** No; hold the complete lift and resolve the load attachment.

**Reason/remediation:** Every part of the load path must be suitable.

## R13-L1

Does a rated hoist prove its supporting beam is adequate?

**Answer:** No; verify the complete support/anchor load path.

**Reason/remediation:** The reaction is carried by the structure.

## R13-L2

What must follow raising a load on a jack?

**Answer:** Prompt suitable blocking/cribbing or other approved secure support.

**Reason/remediation:** Hydraulic pressure alone is not protection beneath a load.

## R14-L1

Can a positioning swivel automatically rotate under load?

**Answer:** No; check the actual device rating/instructions.

**Reason/remediation:** Positioning and loaded rotation are different applications.

## R14-L2

Why not apply crane running-rope wire-break limits to every sling?

**Answer:** Sling and rope types have different applicable criteria.

**Reason/remediation:** Identify the actual product and rule before acceptance.
