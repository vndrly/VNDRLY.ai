# VNDRLY/U Major Emergency Management — Initial Response

VUC101 • Original initial-development program • 18 November 2026

Audience: prospective emergency leaders, team members and support personnel. No prior command appointment is required for this initial course. Participants must be able to engage safely with the simulation and understand the supplied fictional plan. This is not OPITO certification or employer command authorization. Full proprietary criteria were unavailable; public product scope and regulator guidance support this original package.

Planned delivery: 32 hours over consecutive training days: 10 instruction/prebrief, 16 scenario practice and debrief, 4 application laboratories, 2 written assessment/development planning. Add time to give every learner the required manager-role practice and feedback. This is the VNDRLY schedule, not a claim to reproduce an external course duration.

Outcomes: prepare the emergency organization; establish command and objectives; evaluate uncertain evidence; delegate safely; coordinate communications, people and resources; recognize escalation; manage stress; hand over and plan development.

## Q101-01 — Initial training and emergency leadership

Major emergency management brings together decisions about people, plant, responders and outside support. The manager must establish priorities and a workable organization while events are still uncertain. This is initial development, not proof of readiness to command a particular offshore installation. Employer appointment, asset knowledge, supervised practice and any required formal competence assessment remain separate. A participant may enter as a prospective leader, team member or support person; everyone practices the manager role so that the demands of command become visible.

Start by understanding the installation emergency plan, authority hierarchy, deputy arrangements and interfaces with marine, aviation, shore and public agencies. State who is in command and how others can contact that person. If command changes, communicate the transfer, time, current objectives and outstanding decisions. Avoid two people issuing incompatible instructions under an ambiguous title. An external specialist can advise without automatically taking local command.

Worked example: a shore manager calls while the installation commander is managing a release. The call provides resources and advice but does not silently transfer incident command. The team records the request, confirms authority and resolves conflicting instructions through the plan. A missing commander triggers the designated succession arrangement, not a competition for the loudest voice.

## Q101-02 — Preparing a usable emergency organization

A plan becomes useful when people can find the right information and carry out their roles under stress. Preparation includes current hazards, plant configuration, impairment controls, available response teams, communications, people accounting, refuge and evacuation arrangements. Check the effect of contractors, maintenance, weather and simultaneous operations. A resource list is only credible if the people, equipment and access are actually available.

Brief the team on roles, immediate responsibilities and reporting intervals. Assign operations, people/muster, communications and logging functions clearly. The leader retains the overall picture while specialists manage assigned work. Use role cards to avoid duplication and gaps. Identify backup personnel and what must happen if a key role is lost. Test communications and make essential documents available if the normal computer system fails.

Worked example: the plan lists two response teams, but the current shift has enough qualified people for one. Record one available team and revise the response options with the responsible authority. Do not divide the same personnel into two simultaneous tasks on a planning board. Preparation should reveal limitations before the alarm, not conceal them.

## Q101-03 — Recognizing the incident and setting objectives

When the first report arrives, establish location, time, people at risk, nature of the hazard and reliability of the information. Activate the relevant alarm and emergency organization under the plan. Do not wait for a complete causal explanation before taking protective steps already justified by the evidence. State a small number of immediate objectives in operational terms: protect people from the threatened area, establish accountability, control the source through authorized actions and preserve a safe evacuation option.

Distinguish objectives from tactics. Protect people is an objective; sending a team through a particular route is a tactic that requires a hazard and capability assessment. Ask what could make the incident worse, what decision is time-critical and what would change the current priorities. Review objectives when new evidence arrives. A response that was reasonable five minutes earlier can become inappropriate after wind, protection or route changes.

Worked example: smoke and a missing worker are reported in a utility module. The manager activates the plan, obtains last-known-location information and asks the response leader for a safe assessment. Ordering immediate entry without knowing conditions confuses urgency with a viable rescue plan. The missing worker remains a priority while the team determines a defensible response.

## Q101-04 — Situation assessment and decision discipline

Maintain a shared picture with confirmed facts, credible reports, assumptions and unknowns clearly distinguished. Use timestamps and sources; an old measurement on a newly refreshed display is still old. Ask for the information that affects decisions instead of collecting every detail. Make an action log that identifies decision, reason, authority, owner, deadline and result. Explain uncertainty rather than replacing it with false precision.

Consider more than one option and identify constraints: people exposed, responder capability, route availability, system status, time to escalation and reversibility. Choose an option within authority, communicate it and monitor whether it achieves the objective. Set triggers for reassessment. An unsuccessful action is new evidence, not a reason to repeat it indefinitely. Invite challenge from team members who see a discrepancy.

Worked example: a control room reports isolation commanded but no closed feedback. The decision board must show unconfirmed isolation. A plan that assumes the release has stopped is unsupported. The manager asks for the approved failed-action response, maintains restrictions and reassesses whether refuge or evacuation options are threatened.

## Q101-05 — Command, delegation and responder protection

Give tasks to named roles with a clear outcome, constraints and reporting requirement. Ask for a readback of critical instructions. Delegation does not remove the manager's responsibility to monitor progress and interactions. Avoid micromanaging specialist techniques that belong to a competent response leader, but require the information needed to judge whether the plan remains feasible.

Responder deployment must account for hazard, competence, equipment, communications, backup and withdrawal arrangements under the local plan. No course scenario authorizes improvised entry into smoke, gas, confined space, unstable structure or energized equipment. Track teams by location, task and status; record a missed report as a problem requiring action. Stop or change a tactic when conditions exceed its assumptions. Protecting a rescue capability is part of protecting casualties.

Worked example: a team reports its planned route blocked and asks to use an unassessed route. The manager does not simply say continue. Obtain the response leader's assessment of the alternative and the required safeguards. If no safe approach exists, preserve the missing-person information and escalate alternatives rather than treating the roster deficit as permission for uncontrolled exposure.

## Q101-06 — Communications and information flow

Use concise structured messages that identify sender, incident, location, hazards, actions, needs and next update. Confirm receipt of instructions that affect safety. A message can be transmitted, received, understood and acted on at different times; the log should show which stage is established. Correct wrong identifiers immediately. Keep urgent action channels clear and route routine requests to the assigned support role.

Coordinate internal, shore, vessel, aviation and public-agency communication through the plan. Provide accurate location, incident, people status, support requested and safe contact arrangements. Do not promise a landing, rescue route or arrival time that the relevant operator has not confirmed. If a primary link fails, test the approved alternate and re-establish the current instruction. Maintain an update rhythm for people waiting at muster so uncertainty does not become rumor.

Worked example: shore says a rescue asset may be available in twenty minutes. Record a provisional estimate, not confirmed rescue. Ask who will confirm dispatch and arrival. If conditions worsen before confirmation, reassess local options rather than treating the estimate as guaranteed protection.

## Q101-07 — Accountability, casualties and welfare

Establish a reliable people baseline and track identities through muster, response duties, medical treatment and transfer. Totals support reconciliation but do not replace it. A duplicate entry can make a count appear complete while one person is missing. Require the muster coordinator to report confirmed, unconfirmed and transferred people separately, with times and sources. Preserve last-known-location information for missing people.

Medical priorities belong to appropriately trained medical personnel. The manager coordinates access, resources and evacuation requests without inventing diagnoses or treatment. Protect personal details while ensuring responders receive relevant information. Consider the welfare of people at refuge, distressed witnesses and relief personnel. Extended incidents need rest, food, communications and replacement arrangements; these should not distract from immediate life protection but must have an owner.

Worked example: a person is counted at muster and again in the medical area. Reconcile the identity and movement before declaring a second casualty or a complete roster. If a transfer is made to a vessel, record who left, destination, time and confirmation of receipt. A planned transfer is not a completed one.

## Q101-08 — Escalation, refuge and evacuation

A manager must recognize when the incident is outgrowing the current response. Look for loss of source control, threatened refuge, deteriorating weather, unavailable routes, failed protection or insufficient response capability. Use the plan's escalation criteria and communicate decisions early enough for the required actions to be completed. Avoid fixation on saving equipment when life-protection options are closing.

Refuge endurance and evacuation capacity depend on conditions. Compare the latest credible time available with decision, communication, preparation and movement time, including the actual plan's allowances and uncertainty. A nominal arithmetic margin is not a safe waiting period. Confirm the capacity and suitability of the available means; subtract unavailable capacity rather than counting it because it appears on a drawing. Do not send more people than an approved means can carry.

Worked example: forty people need evacuation. Two fictional means have twenty-four approved places each, but one is unavailable. Forty-eight nominal places become twenty-four available places, leaving sixteen without that option. This is a capability gap requiring urgent command action, not permission to overload. Obtain confirmed alternatives and reassess timing.

## Q101-09 — Stress, teamwork and challenge

Stress can cause tunnel vision, rushed speech, missed messages, indecision or fixation. Recognize these patterns in yourself and the team. Use short pauses for a structured situation review when the event permits: current threats, objectives, actions, gaps and next decision. Keep language direct. Assign one owner per task and ask for a clear response. Seek relief or support when workload exceeds reliable performance.

Create conditions where a colleague can challenge an assumption without being dismissed. A challenge should identify the observed problem and consequence; the leader should acknowledge it and resolve the evidence. Do not confuse confidence with certainty. Debrief errors without humiliation so the team reports them early in a real emergency. Simulation should be demanding enough to expose decisions, not psychologically punitive.

Worked example: a communicator says the evacuation count is inconsistent while the leader is announcing all accounted for. Stop the announcement, check identities and issue a correction. Defending the earlier statement to avoid embarrassment turns a recoverable error into a dangerous shared belief.

## Q101-10 — Stabilization, handover and recovery

Stabilization means the situation has stopped deteriorating under defined conditions; it does not mean every hazard is gone. Confirm what remains uncertain and which controls must stay in place. Formally hand over command or shift responsibility with a readback of the current state, people status, response teams, outstanding decisions, resource commitments and next review time. Inform affected parties of the transfer.

Recovery and restart follow authorized technical and management processes. Preserve isolations, permits and evidence until their disposition is approved. Do not reset an incident merely because alarms disappear. Establish welfare and communication arrangements for people affected by the event, and preserve a factual record for review. A debrief should identify both individual development and system improvements, assign owners and define how completion will be checked.

Worked example: the apparent leak has stopped after shutdown, but one valve position remains unverified and a response team has not reported back. The incoming manager must receive those unresolved facts. A handover labeled incident closed would conceal both a plant uncertainty and a people-accounting problem.

## Q101-11 — Integrated initial practice and development

Initial training deliberately combines instruction, practice, feedback and repeated application. Each participant leads at least two different emergency scenarios and also experiences a support role. A written answer alone cannot demonstrate command communication, delegation or adaptation to new evidence. The instructor observes decisions and their reasoning, then gives specific feedback tied to the scenario evidence.

Use the first developmental run to identify gaps, practice changed situations and then perform an independently observed run. Separate coached practice from unprompted evidence in the record. Produce an individual development plan that states the gap, its consequence, the required practice or workplace exposure, supervisor and review point. A package certificate should describe initial training completed; it must not imply readiness for appointment as an emergency commander.

Worked example: a learner can state the escalation rule but waits too long to apply it when the simulation changes. The development plan should require practice recognizing and acting on the trigger, not simply rereading a definition. Reassess with a different event and timing so success demonstrates transfer of learning rather than recall of the script.

## Delivery resources and safety

Provide an isolated emergency-management room or accessible remote simulation environment with shared situation board, timestamped message cards, fictional installation plan, roster, resource cards, radio/voice role play, decision log and printed fallback documents. Never call real emergency services, trigger live alarms, operate plant or create physical hazards. Instructor/controller, independent observer and role players for CRO, response leader, muster, shore and relief are required. Combine roles only when identity and timing remain clear. Every learner leads A and B and supports another run; rotate assignments without reusing an already revealed assessment sequence as independent evidence.

Explain stop word, real-emergency response, privacy and recording, breaks and fitness demands. Obtain relevant fitness declaration and accommodations without asking the class to disclose private medical details. Pause unsafe/distressing simulation. Use realistic decision pressure, not intimidation. Provide interface familiarization before scoring. Additional time is required if the cohort cannot receive individual observation within the planned hours.

## Fictional plan Juniper J101

Modules P process, U utilities, TR refuge and E evacuation point. R1 crosses P; R2 connects U to TR; R3 connects TR to E. Route status changes only through controller evidence; unknown is not safe. The learner is assigned emergency manager for the simulation. CRO operates prescribed simulated protective controls; response leader proposes tactics and confirms team capability; muster reconciles people; communicator handles outside support; recorder keeps the action board. Shore advises and provides resources but does not silently take command. Deputy succession requires announced transfer and accepted handover.

For a credible release/fire report, activate simulated emergency organization and general alarm, direct CRO to follow the relevant protective response card, establish people status and restrict affected work. A control command without required feedback stays unconfirmed. No team enters a hazard area without response-leader assessment, plan safeguards and command authorization. No person is sent physically anywhere during this exercise. Roster correction requires identity/source/time. Primary channel E, approved alternate B; confirm two-way contact and critical readback. Only the controller can supply new plant/route/resource facts.

Escalate whenever source control, refuge, route, team capability or evacuation options deteriorate. Early outside support is permitted through the communicator. Capacity cards are hard fictional approved limits; overloading is prohibited. Provisional endurance/arrival times are estimates, not promises. Recovery requires technical safeguard verification, permit/isolation resolution and authorized release; initial management training does not teach plant restart sequences.

## Scenario A — initially controllable utility incident

Start 09:00, roster A01–A12. One qualified response team available; a second appears on an old plan but cannot be staffed. R1 unavailable, R2/R3 initially available. TR not initially threatened. One simulated medical responder available. Primary channel E functional; B can be tested. No real firefighting or treatment occurs.

At 0 minutes CRO reports smoke in U and one worker A12 last seen there. The learner must activate, establish command/objectives, obtain evidence and delegate roles. At 3 minutes CRO reports protective command sent with no feedback; learner must retain unconfirmed status and request the prescribed failure response. At 5 minutes response leader reports route R2 currently usable but requests clarity on objectives and withdrawal conditions. Learner must provide constraints without prescribing specialist entry technique. At 7 minutes muster lists A01–A10, A10, A11: eleven unique; A12 unaccounted. At 9 minutes shore asks to restart a utility to reduce production loss. Learner must reject unsupported restart and preserve command boundary. At 11 minutes communicator reads back R1 instead of R2; learner corrects it. At 13 minutes CRO supplies confirmed protective state and response leader reports incident controlled under the fictional plan, no entry needed. At 15 minutes Medic verifies A12 at the medical point with source/time; count becomes twelve. At 18 minutes controller reports no continuing smoke but one detector fault remains. Learner must distinguish stable from cleared for restart. At 20 minutes relief requests handover. Require accepted transfer, unresolved fault, people status, resource status and recovery owner. Debrief evidence and specific development needs.

Controller supplies confirmation only after the relevant request/action; do not reward assumed results. If learner orders unassessed entry, unauthorized restart or false all-accounted announcement, stop that branch and record the critical error. During developmental practice explain and rehearse the correction; a later changed unprompted run is required for independent evidence.

## Scenario B — escalating release and loss of options

Start 14:00, roster B01–B20. Process P release reports are credible. One team available. R1 crosses P and is unavailable; R2 initially available; R3 initially available. Two fictional evacuation means each have 12 approved places; initially both listed available. TR provisional endpoint 14:30 depends on current protection. Shore rescue initially estimated 20 minutes but not dispatched. Actual simulation carries no people or equipment.

At 0 minutes activate organization and set protective objectives. At 3 minutes CRO reports source isolation attempted/unconfirmed and a continuing release indication. At 5 minutes response leader reports the planned approach unavailable; learner must reassess rather than order any-route entry. At 7 minutes muster provides B01–B18, B18, B19: nineteen unique, B20 missing. At 9 minutes one evacuation means becomes unavailable: available capacity falls from 24 to 12, gap eight. At 11 minutes shore says rescue availability is uncertain and has no confirmed arrival; learner must not build a plan around the old estimate. At 13 minutes B20 is verified at another muster point: twenty confirmed. At 15 minutes preparation/movement estimate is 11 minutes; provisional endpoint 14:30 leaves 15 minutes, nominal margin four. Immediately introduce TR intake protection failure and R3 status uncertain, invalidating the earlier planning assumptions. Learner must escalate, request confirmed alternatives and protect remaining options without overloading. At 17 minutes E fails; require tested B and a current situation update. At 19 minutes a junior role player challenges an obsolete all-clear statement on the board. Learner corrects it and redistributes workload. At 22 minutes controller provides a confirmed alternative resource under a separate approved fictional plan; the learner must request its capacity, timing, route and handover conditions rather than inventing them. Provide card ALT only on request: 20 places, ready at 14:25, access route R4 independently confirmed available by Field at 14:22, specialized operator retains tactical movement control. These facts do not restore the old TR guarantee; leader must coordinate under the updated plan and continue monitoring. End after an explicit decision, team briefing, accountability transfer plan and accepted relief, not after a cosmetic declaration of success.

## Changed practice C

Start 16:00 with 18 people C01–C18. Lists show C01–C16, C16, C17; C18 later verified by Medic. Two 10-place means, one unavailable: ten available, gap eight. Provisional refuge endpoint 16:24; at 16:12 estimated movement nine minutes gives nominal margin three, then a route-loss card invalidates it. Cause is loss of utilities with uncertain display quality rather than a repeat release. Add a missing team report, correctable channel mismatch and a shore request conflicting with local restrictions. Instructor validates tags and plan changes, gives the learner revised reference data, and observes changed decisions. Do not reuse an exact exposed sequence as a final independent run.

## Observed stations and rubric

### P101-01 — Command boundary

Task: Establish the assigned emergency-manager role and resolve a conflicting shore instruction.

Required evidence: Authority confirmed; single current command announced; transfer recorded. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-02 — Readiness and roles

Task: Review available teams and allocate operations, muster, communications and logging.

Required evidence: No double-booking; capability gaps visible; each task has an owner. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-03 — Objectives

Task: Respond to first report and state immediate protective objectives.

Required evidence: Activation timely; objectives distinct from unsafe predetermined tactics. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-04 — Decision picture

Task: Maintain fact/assumption/unknown board through failed isolation.

Required evidence: Unconfirmed state retained; options and review triggers documented. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-05 — Delegation

Task: Assign response tasks, handle missed report and blocked route.

Required evidence: Constraints/readback/feedback clear; responder protection preserved. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-06 — Communications

Task: Correct wrong readback, test fallback and request outside support.

Required evidence: Receipt established; provisional resource timing not treated as guaranteed. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-07 — People

Task: Reconcile duplicate muster and later medical report.

Required evidence: Unique identity count correct; missing status and corrections traceable. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-08 — Escalation

Task: Recalculate options after evacuation-capacity or refuge loss.

Required evidence: Capacity/time arithmetic correct; uncertainty and early decision explicit. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-09 — Team performance

Task: Respond to challenge and overload without losing monitoring.

Required evidence: Challenge checked; workload redistributed with confirmed ownership. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-10 — Recovery

Task: Transfer command and identify unresolved recovery constraints.

Required evidence: Acceptance and communication explicit; no premature restart/closure. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

### P101-11 — Integrated development

Task: Lead both different scenarios and produce individual development plan.

Required evidence: Observed actions support result; coaching distinguished; next development specific. Record source, action/time, feedback and observer rationale.

Use 0–4 per dimension: 0 absent/unsafe; 1 major help; 2 safe but incomplete/prompted; 3 independent, sound and traceable; 4 independent with well-timed anticipation and clear reasoning. Require 36/44, every dimension at least 3, no unresolved critical error, and two manager-role scenarios satisfactory independently. Initial coached practice is developmental and separately labeled. The VNDRLY decision rule is not claimed as an external assessment rule. Critical errors: unsupported hazardous deployment, deliberate overload, false all-accounted declaration, unauthorized restart/bypass, concealment of loss of protection, or abandoning command without required transfer. Freeze unsafe simulated action, record and remediate; use a changed run to demonstrate correction.

Second competent reviewer handles disputed or borderline decisions using the recorded evidence, not a lowered threshold.

Issue VNDRLY/U Major Emergency Management — Initial Response Training only after all required learning, written and observed practice decisions pass. # VUC101 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q101-01-L1

Does initial training appoint the emergency commander?

**Answer:** No. Appointment and asset competence remain employer responsibilities.

**Reason/remediation:** Training evidence has a defined scope. Revisit Q101-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-01-L2

Does a shore adviser automatically take command?

**Answer:** No. Confirm the plan authority and explicitly communicate any transfer.

**Reason/remediation:** Ambiguous command can produce conflicting instructions. Revisit Q101-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-02-L1

Two teams are listed but only one can be staffed. What is available?

**Answer:** One team; record the gap and revise the response options.

**Reason/remediation:** A list does not create personnel. Revisit Q101-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-02-L2

What must a delegated task include?

**Answer:** Named owner, outcome, constraints, feedback and review time.

**Reason/remediation:** Unowned tasks can disappear during escalation. Revisit Q101-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-03-L1

Must the exact failed component be known before raising the prescribed alarm?

**Answer:** No. Act on sufficient hazard evidence under the plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Perfect diagnosis can delay protection. Revisit Q101-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-03-L2

Is send a team through R1 an objective or tactic?

**Answer:** A tactic requiring route and capability assessment.

**Reason/remediation:** The objective is the protective outcome, not a predetermined method. Revisit Q101-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-04-L1

Isolation is commanded without feedback. What goes on the board?

**Answer:** Attempted/unconfirmed isolation, source/time and follow-up owner.

**Reason/remediation:** Command is not result. Revisit Q101-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-04-L2

When should an option be reviewed?

**Answer:** At its stated trigger, failed result or material new evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Continuing an obsolete option can worsen exposure. Revisit Q101-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-05-L1

A team route becomes blocked. May it use any alternative?

**Answer:** No. Obtain the competent response assessment and required safeguards.

**Reason/remediation:** Urgency does not establish route safety. Revisit Q101-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-05-L2

Does delegation remove command oversight?

**Answer:** No. Track progress, constraints and interactions.

**Reason/remediation:** Tasks can conflict or fail after assignment. Revisit Q101-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-06-L1

A rescue asset may arrive in twenty minutes. Is that confirmed protection?

**Answer:** No. Record provisional status and obtain dispatch/arrival confirmation.

**Reason/remediation:** Estimates can change. Revisit Q101-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-06-L2

A critical message has no readback. What is its state?

**Answer:** Unconfirmed; re-establish receipt and understanding through the approved channel.

**Reason/remediation:** Transmission alone is insufficient. Revisit Q101-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-07-L1

A person appears at muster and medical. Count twice?

**Answer:** No. Reconcile identity and movement.

**Reason/remediation:** Duplicate records can conceal a missing person. Revisit Q101-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-07-L2

Who directs medical treatment?

**Answer:** Appropriately qualified medical personnel.

**Reason/remediation:** Management coordinates resources without inventing treatment. Revisit Q101-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-08-L1

Forty people, two 24-place means, one unavailable: what is the gap?

**Answer:** Available capacity is 24; gap is 16.

**Reason/remediation:** Nominal capacity is not available capacity. Revisit Q101-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-08-L2

Does a three-minute nominal margin permit three minutes waiting?

**Answer:** No. Account for uncertainty and changing conditions under the plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Endurance assumptions may fail. Revisit Q101-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-09-L1

A colleague challenges all accounted. What next?

**Answer:** Pause/correct the announcement and reconcile evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Preserving face is less important than an accurate shared picture. Revisit Q101-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-09-L2

What is an early sign of overload?

**Answer:** Missed messages, fixation or incompatible simultaneous tasks.

**Reason/remediation:** Ask for support and redistribute work explicitly. Revisit Q101-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-10-L1

Alarms clear but valve state is unknown. Is recovery complete?

**Answer:** No. Retain uncertainty and apply authorized recovery checks.

**Reason/remediation:** Alarm disappearance is not verification. Revisit Q101-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-10-L2

What makes a command handover complete?

**Answer:** Explicit acceptance of current state, people, tasks, limits and next decisions, communicated to affected roles.

**Reason/remediation:** Departure alone does not transfer responsibility. Revisit Q101-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-11-L1

Does a coached scenario count as independent performance?

**Answer:** No. Record coaching and obtain changed unprompted evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Assessment must distinguish assistance from capability. Revisit Q101-11 and perform its changed application.

## Q101-11-L2

What should a development plan contain?

**Answer:** Specific gap, consequence, practice, supervisor and review evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** A generic promise to improve is not actionable. Revisit Q101-11 and perform its changed application.

| Primary source | Basis used | Coverage and limitation |
|---|---|---|

Each domain has two explained formative questions and separate final/changed-retake three-decision cases. Stations 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11 require observed role-play performance; 2, 7, 10 include observed record/review tasks. All 11 dimensions are assessed; neither a reading check nor a certificate substitutes for employer appointment.

Source limitations are explicit: no inaccessible full specification is represented as reviewed, no external credential awarded, and no actual learner performance claimed by authoring this package. Local data, competent delivery and documented learner evidence are required when the materials are used.
