# VNDRLY/U Well Servicing Well Control

VUC006 — learner coursebook. No completed learner credential is implied by this file. All wells, figures and people in exercises are fictional. Site procedures and equipment limits govern field actions; a classroom calculation is not a well intervention program.

## S01 — Read the well before choosing the intervention

A producing well can retain pressure after production stops. Closing a production valve changes the flow path; it does not remove the reservoir, compressed gas, trapped liquid or stored mechanical energy. Servicing therefore begins with an accurate model of the well and its possible pressure paths. A crew that understands only the tubular it is moving can miss pressure outside that tubular or behind a failed completion component.

Draw a vertical schematic from reservoir to surface. Identify open perforations, tubing, casing strings, packer, subsurface safety valve, side-pocket equipment, plugs and surface tree valves. Mark each annulus separately. Record measured depth for equipment locations and true vertical depth for hydrostatic calculations. Add side outlets, pump paths, return paths and connections to production equipment. A schematic is a working control document: its revision, evidence and discrepancies matter as much as its neatness.

Trace a possible influx along the tubing bore. Next trace one outside the tubing. Ask what prevents crossflow between zones and what a failed packer would connect. A tubing pressure gauge does not establish the pressure in an isolated annulus. Similarly, a closed subsurface valve below a tubing leak may not isolate every surface path. A barrier must be evaluated against the actual path and the operation about to occur.

The work program should identify why the intervention is needed, the well's pressure and temperature envelope, expected fluids and gases, corrosion or scale concerns, known integrity problems and how these were established. Old production pressure is historical evidence, not a substitute for current verification. Changes in fluid level, gas injection, temperature and formation communication can invalidate previous assumptions.

**Worked case.** A workover crew receives a diagram showing a packer at 6,100 ft measured depth. The latest report mentions a possible tubing leak, but no depth or pressure investigation is attached. The proposed task is to open the tubing hanger. The missing information is consequential: the diagram does not demonstrate which pressure paths the proposed isolation controls. The correct readiness decision is to hold the opening, obtain the integrity information and revise the barrier plan. Adding more people or heavier PPE does not establish isolation.

**Practice.** Sketch two parallel paths labelled tubing and annulus. Place an uncertain packer between them and a closed tubing valve at surface. Explain why the closed valve alone does not justify opening an annulus outlet. **Explanation:** its sealing function belongs to a different path; the uncertain packer may allow pressure communication. Each exposed path needs its own supported isolation decision.

**Evidence to carry forward:** current well schematic; pressure observations with units and times; known uncertainty; named intervention supervisor; approved scope and stop conditions. An unexplained inconsistency is a stop condition, not an invitation to select the most convenient drawing.

## S02 — Pressure, fluid head and the limits of calculation

Hydrostatic pressure depends on density and vertical height. In field units, a uniform liquid column produces approximately **P = 0.052 × density in ppg × vertical height in ft**, in psi. Measured depth follows the well path and determines many volumes; it is not the vertical height. In a deviated well, substituting measured depth overstates liquid head and can make an underbalanced well appear controlled. This relationship and distinction are described in the [SLB hydrostatic-pressure reference](https://glossary.slb.com/en/terms/h/hydrostatic_pressure).

Calculate each liquid layer separately when density changes with depth. Add the pressure contributions; do not average densities unless the averaging method preserves each layer's height. Where a column is partly gas or the liquid level is below surface, a full-to-surface liquid assumption is invalid. Gas pressure, temperature and compressibility require the applicable model. A simple liquid-column exercise must not be used to dismiss those effects.

**Worked example.** A synthetic well has 6,000 ft TVD, 7,500 ft measured depth and a verified full column of 9.5 ppg fluid. Its liquid head is 0.052 × 9.5 × 6,000 = **2,964 psi**. Using 7,500 ft would give 3,705 psi, an overstatement of 741 psi. If a valid stabilized surface pressure communicating with the same zone is 312 psi, the simplified static estimate at that depth is 3,276 psi. The density that balances that estimate is 3,276 ÷ (0.052 × 6,000) = **10.5 ppg**. This is a balance calculation, not a specified operational kill-fluid density or authorization to pump.

Extra density is not automatically safer. It may exceed a weak formation's pressure tolerance, cause losses, lower the fluid level and undermine the primary barrier. A program must account for temperature, fluid compatibility, losses, flow friction and surface pressures. During circulation, pressure losses and route selection change the pressure profile. A pump gauge includes resistance along the injection path; it is not simply reservoir pressure.

Consider trapped liquid between closed valves. Heating or pumping into that volume can increase pressure even when the reservoir is isolated. Conversely, a gauge indicating zero may be isolated from the volume being opened. Determine what the gauge actually communicates with and how that communication is verified. Equalization means controlling pressure between specified volumes through an approved path; it is not merely observing two gauges with similar readings.

**Practice.** A 6,000-ft vertical interval contains 2,000 ft of 8.5 ppg liquid over 4,000 ft of 10 ppg liquid. Find the head. **Explanation:** 0.052 × [(8.5 × 2,000) + (10 × 4,000)] = **2,964 psi**. The same answer as the previous example is coincidental; each profile must still be evaluated independently.

## S03 — Volumes, displacement and changing fluid level

Volume control connects pipe movement to well control. Pulling a tubular can require replacement fluid; running it can displace fluid out. What volume applies depends on whether the tubular is open or closed, whether it drains, its internal fluid and the relevant displacement definition. Use the approved tally and equipment dimensions, not an assumed standard joint.

For a cylindrical bore, capacity in barrels per foot is approximately inside diameter squared in inches divided by 1,029.4. Annular capacity uses the difference of the enclosing inside diameter squared and tubular outside diameter squared, divided by 1,029.4. Multiply capacity by length along the wellbore to obtain volume. For field work, confirm dimensions, unit conventions and the approved calculation method. Nominal pipe size does not necessarily equal the diameter used in the calculation.

**Worked example.** A fictional 2.441-inch tubing ID has internal capacity 2.441² ÷ 1,029.4 = approximately **0.005788 bbl/ft**. Across 7,500 ft, its nominal internal volume is **43.41 bbl**. This is a geometric estimate before deductions or additions for tools, tolerances and actual configuration. It does not tell the crew how much fluid a pulled stand should take; that requires the relevant displacement and drainage condition.

In a training trip, the approved displacement is 0.006 bbl/ft and 500 ft is removed. Expected replacement is 3.0 bbl. Only 1.2 bbl is measured. The **1.8-bbl discrepancy** requires prompt reporting and the prescribed response. It could reflect influx, measurement error, a wrong flow route or a wrong displacement assumption. Continuing while debating the cause increases uncertainty and potentially the hazard. First protect the well using the approved response; then investigate with reliable data.

Swabbing reduces pressure below moving equipment; surging increases it. Speed, clearances, fluid properties and restrictions matter. An operation that is statically overbalanced can still experience a transient influx during movement. A tight spot or changing returns requires attention even if the previous stand behaved normally. Do not use an absence of surface pressure as proof that movement cannot induce an influx.

**Practice.** A pump's verified output is 0.08 bbl/stroke. How many ideal strokes move 12 bbl? **Explanation:** 12 ÷ 0.08 = **150 strokes**. Actual delivery must be reconciled with measured volume, pump condition and routing. Stroke count does not prove fluid reached the intended destination.

Record starting and ending levels, calculated expectation, measured movement, instrument limitations and actions. Keep timestamps so another person can reconstruct the event. A supervisor needs the unexplained difference, not merely the reassuring statement that the tank is “about right.”

## S04 — Barrier envelopes and barrier changes

A barrier envelope is the connected set of elements that prevents unwanted flow along a specified path. Individual elements may include verified fluid columns, plugs, valves, seals and pressure-containing bodies. Calling a component a barrier does not establish the condition of the whole envelope. A leaking connector can bypass a sound valve; an unverified fluid level can invalidate a density calculation.

For each operational stage, draw the active envelope and identify its verification. State what happens when a tool passes a valve, a connection is opened, a packer is released or a fluid column is displaced. These transitions often carry more risk than the steady operating phase. The handover from one barrier arrangement to another must be explicit: what remains effective, what is being removed and what evidence is required before proceeding.

Independence matters. Two barriers that share the same vulnerable component, control supply or flow path may fail together. A redundant-looking diagram does not prove functional independence. Evaluate common causes, including hydraulic supply loss, incompatible seals, debris and a tool that prevents both proposed closing elements from sealing.

A pressure test is evidence within its stated conditions. Record the boundary, medium, test pressure, duration, stabilization criteria, instrument identity and acceptance standard. A function test demonstrates movement or response, not necessarily leak-tight sealing under differential pressure. A pressure test in one direction may not demonstrate sealing in the opposite direction. The equipment documentation and approved program determine what is required.

**Worked case.** A plug was tested before a subsequent operation that could disturb it. The crew proposes to rely on the earlier chart without evaluating the intervening event. The chart is genuine, but its continued relevance is unproven. The responsible person must establish whether verification remains valid and what additional check is required. Never “repair” evidence by changing the time or copying a previous result onto a new form.

**Practice.** Two isolation valves are shown in series, but a bypass line around both remains open. Are there two effective barriers to the downstream opening? **Explanation:** no. The diagram must include the bypass; the stated valves do not isolate that path. Identify and control all communicating routes before declaring the opening safe.

The IADC introductory public curriculum identifies barriers and servicing equipment as distinct learning areas; it is a dated scope benchmark, not evidence that this VNDRLY program is IADC-accredited. [Public 2019 introductory scope](https://iadc.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/WSP-02-WS-I_Introduction_rev0.pdf).

## S05 — Recognize an influx and communicate a useful report

Unexpected flow, unexplained tank gain, a trip-fill discrepancy or abnormal pressure can indicate loss of control. Some are also consistent with equipment faults, transfers or thermal effects. Ambiguity does not justify waiting for certainty. A useful response couples prompt recognition with the site's approved securing and notification procedure.

Know the normal condition before starting: which tanks and meters are active, intended pump rates, expected returns, planned transfers, current pressure and instrument resolution. If a tank is isolated from the well, its stable level says little about well behavior. Check that the sensor being watched measures the route actually in use. A disabled alarm should be treated as a changed control requiring formal disposition, not quietly accepted because it was noisy.

Communicate the observation, time, operating state, magnitude, uncertainty and action. For example: “At 10:14 while pulling the fifth stand, measured replacement is 1.8 barrels below the calculated total. No tank transfer is logged. Movement stopped under the trip-response procedure; the supervisor has been called.” This supports a decision. “It might be nothing” does not.

Once the well is secured according to the approved configuration-specific procedure, continue monitoring the required pressures and volumes. Do not interpret the first needle position as a stabilized reading. Identify a blocked line, float/check-valve effect, trapped pressure, continuing flow or temperature change before using pressure to estimate formation conditions. Do not manipulate equipment outside the assigned role to obtain a preferred reading.

**Worked case.** A return meter increases while an assistant is transferring fluid between tanks. The operator cannot establish whether the meter sees that transfer. The correct response is to declare the discrepancy and use the prescribed response, not subtract an estimated transfer and continue. Reconciliation requires confirmed routing, measured transfer and reliable time alignment.

**Practice.** Write a radio message for a pressure rise from 500 to 650 psi over two minutes with pumps off. **Model:** identify the well and pressure point, give both times/readings, state pumps off and any other verified changes, state the action taken under the response procedure, and request the responsible controller. Do not diagnose gas migration solely from this observation.

## S06 — Workover: changing a completion changes the risk

Workover changes a well's completion or restores its function. Examples include tubing replacement, isolation repair and recompletion. It is not simply drilling with a smaller rig. The crew may be working with worn equipment, uncertain integrity, multiple pressure compartments and fluids introduced during years of production. OSHA's [workover overview](https://www.osha.gov/etools/oil-and-gas/servicing/workover) describes remedial operations and associated servicing hazards.

Before moving tubing or releasing a packer, identify the pressures above and below it and in each annulus. Determine how releasing the component changes communication. A packer may be both a mechanical restraint and part of a pressure barrier. Its release can change loading as well as flow paths. The approved program must address both, including personnel position and a contingency if the component does not behave as expected.

Trip control includes tally accuracy, fill measurements, tubing drainage and the condition of any internal check device. Wet tubing, gas-cut fluid or a restricted string can invalidate a routine expectation. Record what actually emerges and where it goes. Do not allow spills, mixed returns or unrecorded tank transfers to destroy the volume accounting on which the well-control decision relies.

If the job changes from a killed-well operation to a live-well operation, stop at that change. Pressure-control equipment, personnel competence, pipe restraint and emergency arrangements must support the new condition. A workover rig's ability to pull pipe does not establish its ability to restrain upward pressure force or perform a snubbing sequence.

**Worked case.** A tubing string was planned to drain while pulling. Several stands remain full, and the fill tank no longer reconciles with the original calculation. Recalculate only after establishing the actual drainage state and protecting the well. Do not erase the discrepancy by changing expected volume after the fact without a documented explanation.

**Practice.** The operator wants to release a packer because tubing pressure is zero; annulus pressure has not been measured. Name the missing decisions. **Explanation:** establish pressure communication and differential across the packer, identify barrier changes and mechanical loading, verify the approved release method and contingency, and confirm the required control equipment. Zero at one measurement point is insufficient.

## S07 — Wireline: control pressure while passing a small line

Wireline operations use a small line to convey tools. Solid slickline and stranded electric line do not seal identically. The pressure-control arrangement must suit the actual line, tool string, well fluids, pressure and temperature. A sealing device suitable for one line type is not automatically suitable for another.

A lubricator provides a pressure-containing space for the tool assembly so it can be positioned clear of the well isolation valve. Its usable length must accommodate the actual assembly and required clearances, not only the nominal tool length. The operator must know where the tool is before a valve or ram is commanded. Closing across an unsuitable tool can damage equipment and fail to isolate the well.

Pressure-control heads seal around the moving line; wireline valves provide specified isolation functions; traps and catchers address tool movement. These functions are different. A tool-catching function alone is not proof of pressure isolation. BSEE's [wireline equipment description](https://www.bsee.gov/sites/bsee.gov/files/2024-05/EStB_Drilling_BID_Wireline_PRs.pdf) illustrates component roles and emphasizes operation within the equipment's verified performance envelope.

Before a run, the crew verifies the assembled configuration, connection compatibility, applicable tests, control supply and the planned response to line break, seal leakage, stuck tools or loss of power. Mark components that cannot be sealed or sheared at particular positions. Tool location uncertainty is itself a control problem. A depth counter must be reconciled with known reference points and stretch or movement effects where relevant.

At recovery, an isolated lubricator can still contain pressure or hazardous fluid. Follow the approved isolation, controlled depressurization and verification procedure before opening. A blocked bleed path can leave pressure trapped despite a zero indication elsewhere. No person should loosen a connection to “see if pressure remains.”

**Worked case.** The recovered assembly includes an added fishing tool, but the original lubricator-space check was not revised. The tool cannot be assumed clear of the valve. Stop the transition, establish actual position and geometry, and obtain an approved revised arrangement. Forcing the valve closed is not a space-management technique.

**Practice.** Explain why grease supply failure on an applicable electric-line pressure-control head requires the specified contingency even if line movement is smooth. **Explanation:** smooth movement does not demonstrate pressure containment; the seal-support system may be losing its required function. The response depends on the installed system and approved procedure.

### Wireline context: open hole, cased hole and changing work scope

Open-hole work encounters an uncased interval; cased-hole work occurs within casing or completion equipment. That difference affects access, sticking risks and the information needed for the pressure/barrier plan. Neither label establishes that the well is unpressurized. A cased well may communicate with producing formations, and an open-hole run may depend on a fluid barrier whose condition changes. Identify the actual exposed interval and current barriers rather than transferring a previous job's assumptions.

**Original scenario:** a logging team receives a drawing for a cased, isolated interval. The new instruction adds a run below the casing shoe into an open interval. The tool may physically fit, but the previous readiness review did not cover this location. The learner identifies the changed interval, obtains the updated well/fluid/barrier information and reviews access and contingency with the responsible supervisor before the changed run. The teaching point is the scope change, not a claim that every open-hole run requires the same pressure-control arrangement.

**Original scenario:** a fishing operation follows a line failure. The recovered length does not reconcile with the deployed line tally, and the tool position is unknown. Do not treat a returning line end as proof that the entire assembly is clear. Preserve the uncertain position in the drawing and follow the approved recovery plan. Closure capability and containment must be assessed for the actual obstruction, not for an assumed empty bore.

**Practice:** the drilling crew proposes changing fluid routing while logging continues. What must be reconciled? **Explained answer:** ownership of well control, the current tool/line position, the effect on fluid level and pressure, isolation functions, monitoring and the agreed response. A logging work order is not permission for another operation to change the controlling conditions without coordination.

The provider's [March 2025 wireline outline](https://fmtcsafety.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Course-Outline-Wireline-3-14-2025.pdf) includes both open-hole and cased-hole context. It is a topic-level crosscheck, not the full external competency specification or authority to issue its credential.

## S08 — Coiled tubing: continuous pipe, multiple control functions

Coiled tubing is continuous small-diameter pipe conveyed through an injector. The system must control both the annulus around the tubing and flow inside it, while managing axial forces and tubing integrity. A moving seal, a ram, a check valve and an injector each serve different purposes. They must be understood as a system rather than as interchangeable emergency devices.

The stripper supports pressure containment around moving tubing within its specified envelope. Other pressure-control components may seal, grip or shear under defined conditions. Their exact order, capability and operating sequence are equipment-specific. Manufacturer offerings include several BOP configurations; a generic “quad” label does not identify what each installed ram can actually do. [Manufacturer equipment overview](https://f-e-t.com/intervention/coiled-tubing-pressure-control/).

Verify tubing size, wall condition, fatigue history, connector and bottom-hole assembly against the approved pressure, tension, compression and bending limits. Pressure containment does not prove structural integrity. Repeated bending over the reel and guide creates fatigue exposure; localized damage or corrosion further changes the risk. Track the actual string and its history rather than assigning a generic capability to all tubing of the same outside diameter.

Internal flow control includes the approved check devices and means of controlling an unexpected reverse flow. Their verification and limitations must be known. A surface valve may be remote from a leak or separation point. Plan the response for a tubing failure, stripper leak, loss of injector control and a tool positioned where an emergency function cannot act as intended.

Pumping pressure includes friction through a long, relatively small bore and the bottom-hole assembly. High pump pressure alone does not establish the pressure applied to the formation. The return route and its limits are equally important. Solids or debris can restrict returns, change pressure and compromise surface processing. The supervisor must distinguish a planned pressure response from evidence of obstruction or loss of containment.

**Worked case.** A replacement bottom-hole assembly is longer and has a section outside the verified shear capability. The prior emergency plan is no longer sufficient. Revise the location tracking, barrier arrangement and response before deployment; do not assume an emergency shear command will solve every position.

**Practice.** A crew reports that the BOP functioned during a control check, so no further verification is needed. **Explanation:** movement is only one part of readiness. Required sealing tests, tubing compatibility, control energy, independent functions, configuration and emergency procedures still require their specified evidence.

## S09 — Snubbing: pressure force and restraint

Snubbing moves tubulars against well pressure when the upward pressure force can exceed the downward effective string weight. The transition between pipe-light and pipe-heavy conditions changes the restraint problem; it does not eliminate the need for positive control. The work uses specialized equipment and a verified sequence. The general principle is described by [SLB's intervention definition](https://glossary.slb.com/en/terms/s/snubbing).

For a simplified closed-end training model, pressure force is pressure multiplied by effective area. Effective area is determined by the pressure boundaries and geometry; it is not always the full outside cross-section. Friction, buoyancy, pressure inside the pipe, connection geometry and dynamic loads affect the real result. An engineering model and equipment-specific limits are required for operations.

**Worked example.** A simulator states an effective area of 4.0 square inches and differential pressure of 2,000 psi. Upward force is **8,000 lbf**. If the stated downward effective load is 5,000 lbf, the simplified net is **3,000 lbf upward** before other effects. The correct conclusion is that upward restraint is required; the number is not a selected working capacity or a complete equipment design load.

The crew must know which slip set is carrying load, which pressure element is containing the well and where the connection is before each transition. A tool joint may not pass a closed ram intended to seal on pipe body. The approved sequence preserves pressure containment and load control through that passage. Do not improvise a generic alternating-open sequence from a classroom diagram.

Identify components that cannot seal or shear, the available internal pipe barriers and the response to hydraulic failure. Account for stored hydraulic energy and unexpected pipe movement. The operator must be able to state the safe condition and the evidence confirming it, not merely recite lever names. A failed indication requires verification through the approved contingency, not assumed completion of the command.

**Worked case.** The planned transition assumes a known string weight, but an unexpected change in fluid inside the tubing alters effective load. Reassess the force condition and approved operating envelope before proceeding. Treat the pipe-light/heavy transition as a controlled stage, not an approximate point where restraint can be relaxed.

**Practice.** Why is a successful pressure test insufficient evidence that the unit can perform the job? **Explanation:** pressure containment and axial load control are separate requirements. Grip capacity, structural limits, geometry, control supply, operating sequence and personnel competence also require verification.

## S10 — Fluids, losses, gas and chemical compatibility

Completion and workover fluids must provide the required pressure function without introducing unacceptable formation damage, corrosion, instability or handling risk. Density is only one property. Temperature can change density and volume; contamination can change chemistry; solids can affect valves and seals. Read the approved fluid specification and safety data sheet, and confirm the sampled fluid represents what is actually in the active system.

Losses can reduce fluid height even when the remaining fluid has the correct density. Adding heavier fluid without understanding the loss mechanism may worsen the condition. A level-based calculation must use a supported fluid level. If the level is unknown, document that uncertainty rather than silently assuming a full column.

Gas can expand as pressure falls. A small downhole volume can create a much larger surface handling demand. Gas dissolved in liquid may emerge as conditions change. A liquid-only volume estimate cannot establish separator, vent or flare capacity. Toxic or flammable gases also change personnel protection and ignition-control requirements. Follow the site alarm, escape and emergency plan; unprotected rescue is not an acceptable response to a suspected toxic atmosphere.

Fluid mixing can produce heat, precipitates or incompatible properties. Do not combine returned fluids simply because both are described as brine. Confirm composition, compatibility and disposal routing. Surface tanks, hoses and processing equipment must support the expected fluids and conditions. A well-control plan that ends at the choke omits the hazards of the material leaving it.

**Worked case.** A return stream changes from clear brine to gas-cut fluid while the tank level rises. The measured surface density is no longer a reliable description of the original downhole liquid column. Report the change, apply the response procedure and reassess with the responsible technical authority. Do not treat the lower reading as a routine mixing correction while continuing unchanged.

**Practice.** A known 500-ft loss of liquid height occurs in a uniform 10 ppg column. What liquid-head reduction does the simplified model predict? **Explanation:** 0.052 × 10 × 500 = **260 psi**. The calculation illustrates why fluid level matters; it does not establish the actual reservoir response or authorize a treatment.

## S11 — Plan a well kill without confusing a method with permission

Killing a well means establishing the intended controlled condition under a specific engineered program. The selected method depends on access, communication, pressures, fluid compatibility, weak points, available circulation paths and equipment capability. A method name does not resolve those constraints.

Forward circulation and reverse circulation use different routes and pressure distributions. The route changes which equipment sees pressure and where friction is applied. A plan must identify both injection and return paths, valve alignment, ratings, measurement points and the response to a blocked route. Verify the plan against the actual completion rather than assuming an open path from a schematic symbol.

Bullheading pushes fluids toward an accepting formation rather than relying on a return path to surface. It requires a technical assessment of injection acceptance, formation and equipment limits, fluid compatibility and unwanted migration. It is not the default answer to an uncertain obstruction. Similarly, noncirculating pressure-management techniques require a specific model, competent supervision and defined monitoring; this course does not supply a universal field sequence.

**Worked example.** In the simplified S02 well, the calculated balance density is 10.5 ppg. A proposed program cannot be accepted solely on that result. It must also establish the actual fluid profile, pressure communication, circulation route, loss tolerance, surface handling, verification criteria and contingencies. A well can appear quiet while pressure remains isolated elsewhere.

Completion verification is an evidence-based decision. Confirm the required stable observations, pressure and volume reconciliation, barrier status and approved checks. Do not declare the well dead merely because a surface gauge reads zero immediately after pumping stops. A change in temperature, trapped compartment or blocked line may explain that reading.

**Practice.** A pump reaches an unexpected pressure limit before the planned volume is delivered. **Explanation:** follow the stop condition and protect the well; report actual pressure, rate, volume and route. Do not override the limit to satisfy the planned volume, nor assume the entire volume went into the intended zone.

## S12 — Organize the operation and hand over a truthful state

The operator representative coordinates the well program and contractor interfaces; equipment operators remain responsible for their assigned controls and limits. The pre-job meeting must resolve who commands the response, who operates each control, who records data and who communicates with the wider site. Two people issuing conflicting commands can turn a manageable deviation into a loss of control.

Use closed-loop communication for critical instructions: identify the receiver, state the action and condition, have it repeated, then confirm completion through the specified evidence. Silence is not acknowledgement. An alarm drill should test recognition and communication as well as equipment familiarity. Stop-work authority must be usable by the person who first sees the deviation.

Manage simultaneous work. Lifting, electrical work, pressure testing, production routing or nearby operations may affect the intervention's exclusion zones, ignition controls, communications or escape routes. A permit for one activity does not automatically coordinate it with another. Changes require reassessment before the changed work begins.

After an abnormal event, preserve the timeline and evidence. Record the original observation, actions, pressure/volume changes, equipment condition and unresolved questions. A restart needs defined acceptance criteria, corrected causes and the authorized decision. Finishing a shift or replacing a crew is not a technical basis for restart.

**Worked handover.** “Well W-6 is secured under response procedure revision 3. Tubing and annulus readings at 18:10 are recorded separately. The suspected return obstruction is unresolved. No opening or movement is authorized. The isolation drawing and test records are attached; the supervisor owns the next assessment.” This conveys restrictions. “Well okay, continue tomorrow” conceals the unresolved state.

**Practice.** A relieved operator asks whether the previous crew's alarm was real. **Explanation:** give observations and supported findings, not an unsupported yes/no. State what remains unknown and the resulting restriction. Do not convert a suspected instrumentation fault into a confirmed diagnosis because the shift has ended.

## S13 — Completion accessories and hidden communication

The tree directs and isolates surface flow, while downhole completion components control paths that may not be visible from the work platform. A sliding sleeve can connect tubing and annulus when opened. A gas-lift valve controls gas entering the tubing. A subsurface safety valve serves its specified isolation function within the completion. These names describe functions, not evidence that a particular installed component is closed, leak-tight or appropriate to the current intervention. Manufacturer examples illustrate [sliding-sleeve arrangements](https://www.slb.com/products-and-services/innovating-in-oil-and-gas/completions/well-completions/completion-accessories/sliding-sleeves) and [gas-lift valve function](https://glossary.slb.com/en/terms/g/gas-lift_valve).

Suppose a well has a gas-injection connection and a tubing isolation at surface. Closing the tubing outlet does not, by itself, isolate the injection source. The intervention plan must identify the source, its energy isolation and the possibility of pressure remaining in connected volumes. Artificial-lift equipment also introduces electrical or mechanical energy. Well pressure isolation and machinery energy isolation are complementary tasks; neither substitutes for the other.

Plugs and landing profiles must match the actual completion. A tool set at a depth is not necessarily a verified barrier. Record placement evidence, applicable test results and the direction/load under which it is credited. Debris, wear, the wrong profile or an uncertain setting operation can undermine the assumption. Retrieval changes the barrier state again, so the retrieval plan must identify what takes over the function.

**Worked case.** A sleeve is scheduled to be shifted to restore communication with a zone. The work package's barrier drawing still assumes the sleeve is closed. The drawing must be revised for the intended open state before execution, including the newly communicating zone and the effect on pressure, returns and isolation. A successful shift is not a successful barrier plan.

**Practice.** List the questions before retrieving a plug. **Explanation:** which path and pressure it isolates; verified pressures above and below; what replaces its function; load and tool compatibility; how release is controlled; the response if it does not release or pressure behavior changes. Do not infer those answers from the plug's depth alone.

## S14 — Obstructions, hydrates and pressure trapped behind a blockage

Sand, scale, wax, debris and hydrates can obstruct a path. The restriction can conceal a pressure difference and prevent a gauge or bleed point from representing the volume beyond it. Removing the obstruction can suddenly reconnect pressure compartments. A cleanout therefore changes the pressure model as well as the physical access.

A hydrate is an ice-like structure involving water and gas under suitable conditions. It is not safely managed by assuming it is ordinary ice. A pressure difference across a plug can accelerate it when it releases. BSEE's [Safety Alert 484](https://www.bsee.gov/sites/bsee.gov/files/2024-04/BSEE%20Safety%20Alert%20-%20De-pressurizing%20Piping%20to%20Remove%20Hydrate%20Results%20in%20an%20Injury_Final%2016APR24.pdf) documents injury associated with improper depressurization during hydrate removal. Use the approved specialist procedure and exclusion arrangements; this course does not prescribe heating, chemical treatment or depressurization sequences.

In a fictional cleanout, pressure above a sand bridge is low while pressure below is unknown. Milling through the bridge may expose the surface system to a different condition. Plan the transition before reaching it. Identify the maximum credible pressure, required containment, fluid handling and the response to a sudden change. Do not approach the obstruction as merely a mechanical delay.

**Worked case.** A crew proposes disconnecting a hose because a blocked bleed valve shows no discharge. No discharge is not proof of zero pressure. The blockage could be preventing pressure release. The correct decision is to retain containment and use an approved means of establishing the actual state, with the responsible technical authority resolving the obstruction plan.

**Practice.** What should be written on the barrier diagram for an unverified blockage? **Explanation:** mark it as an obstruction with uncertain pressure communication, not as an accepted tested barrier. Its persistence, sealing behavior and response to disturbance are unproven.

## S15 — Task-specific changes: perforating, stimulation and abandonment

The intervention's purpose determines which conditions can change. Perforating can create communication with a formation; stimulation changes pressure and introduces treatment fluids; plugging changes the intended isolation arrangement. These operations require specialist personnel and approved programs. Well-control knowledge supports coordination but does not confer explosives, acid-handling or abandonment-design qualification. OSHA's [special-services overview](https://www.osha.gov/etools/oil-and-gas/servicing/special-services) identifies these activities and the importance of coordination.

Before a communication-changing operation, establish the expected pressure state afterward, not just beforehand. A quiet well before perforating is not evidence of its post-perforating condition. Account for the actual exposed interval, fluid head, containment and surface processing. The crew needs a response ready before the transition occurs.

Stimulation introduces high-pressure pumping and potentially hazardous chemicals. Confirm the connected equipment's permissible operating envelope, isolation from unintended paths, pressure monitoring and the handling of returned fluid. Treatment completion does not mean pressure has dissipated. Flowback can contain gas, solids and chemicals that require the planned collection and processing route. Do not use an uncontrolled discharge as a substitute for pressure verification.

Abandonment requires a designed and verified isolation arrangement for the relevant zones and paths. Placing material or setting a plug is an action; proving it meets the required barrier objective is a separate evidence step. Retain location, volume, installation and test records as specified by the approved program and applicable jurisdiction. This course does not supply a universal plug depth or cement volume.

**Worked case.** A stimulation contractor's pump can deliver more pressure than a temporary return-side component can withstand if an isolation leaks. The combined system review must consider that credible communication. Comparing only the pump and injection-line ratings misses the weaker exposed component.

**Practice.** A supervisor says an abandonment barrier is accepted because the planned amount was pumped. **Explanation:** pumped quantity alone does not prove placement, isolation or integrity. The approved verification and acceptance criteria remain necessary.

## S16 — Controlled displacement, margins and equipment response

Moving pipe into a closed, liquid-filled training volume displaces liquid. If the system cannot accommodate the volume, pressure can rise. If fluid is released without the correct model and control, pressure support can be lost. The required management depends on the well state, gas behavior and approved method. A calculated displacement is an input to a controlled procedure, not an instruction to open a bleed valve.

**Worked displacement exercise.** The simulator supplies an effective displacement of 0.004 bbl/ft and planned movement of 250 ft. Geometric displaced volume is **1.0 bbl**. If the simulated accounting shows 1.4 bbl removed from the controlled system, the 0.4-bbl difference must be reconciled. It cannot be dismissed as a rounding error. Actual field pressure management must include the approved pressure/volume model and gas effects.

Gas pressure-volume behavior must use absolute pressure in a simple constant-temperature ideal-gas exercise. If 0.5 bbl of gas changes from 1,000 psia to 500 psia, the idealized volume doubles to 1.0 bbl. Real well fluids may depart substantially from that model because of temperature, compressibility and dissolution. The example demonstrates sensitivity; it is not a field bleed schedule.

Operating margins must account for the combined credible effects. A training limit of 1,000 psi with a planned 900-psi state leaves 100 psi nominal margin. If the approved analysis assigns 60 psi to a transient and 50 psi to measurement uncertainty in the same limiting direction, the plan exceeds the available margin by 10 psi. Considering each effect separately against 100 psi would miss the conflict. The responsible engineer defines which effects combine and how; an operator should flag the discrepancy rather than invent a new limit.

Control readiness includes the required energy supply, indicated state and actual response. A command can be issued without reaching the intended valve position. Identify loss-of-power behavior and which functions retain stored energy, using the actual equipment documentation. Do not defeat an interlock or emergency shutdown merely to keep the job moving.

**Practice.** A simulator's emergency control is accessible, but nobody is assigned to operate it and its effect on the current tool position is unknown. **Explanation:** physical access alone is insufficient. Assign responsibility, verify the configuration-specific effect and contingency, and demonstrate the response before declaring readiness.

# Completion and subsea intervention — original VNDRLY instruction

Read these six chapters after the embedded S01–S16 foundation. All exercise data are fictional. Model arithmetic teaches reasoning; it is not an approved intervention procedure. The functional drawings must be replaced by controlled equipment drawings for actual equipment-specific assessment.

## I17 — Annulus surveillance and integrity decisions

The tubing bore and the spaces outside it are different pressure compartments. A tubing reading therefore cannot be used as an annulus reading. Name each annulus on the current drawing and identify its observation point, fluid, isolation, expected operating envelope and potential communications. The term annular pressure describes pressure in these surrounding spaces; it does not identify its cause. [SLB definition](https://glossary.slb.com/terms/a/annular_pressure).

Begin a surveillance record with time, instrument identity, units, measurement location, temperature or operating condition, recent changes and known instrument limitations. Preserve the unaltered observations. Record whether a gauge is communicating with the intended compartment. A beautifully plotted trend from the wrong compartment is not evidence about the intended barrier.

Annulus pressure may be deliberately applied, associated with temperature changes, or related to an unintended pressure source. Pressure that rebuilds after authorized bleeding and is not explained solely by imposed pressure or thermal effects is a sustained-pressure concern. This is a diagnostic distinction, not permission for an unplanned bleed. [IADC terminology with dated source attribution](https://iadclexicon.org/sustained-casing-pressure/). The responsible integrity specialist determines a controlled investigation, operating limits and acceptance. Do not open an annulus merely to categorize its behavior for a worksheet.

**Worked trend:** At 08:00 the fictional A annulus reads 120 psi and the B annulus 0 psi. At 08:30 A reads 210 psi after a known temperature change; at 09:00 it reads 230 psi with a stable reported temperature. A crew member writes “thermal, safe.” That conclusion exceeds the data. The trend establishes increasing observed pressure; it does not prove a thermal-only cause or the integrity of a barrier. Check communication, instrument condition, operating history, approved limits and the responsible person's assessment. Retain the uncertainty. The B reading cannot cancel the A observation.

An intervention changes the pressure system. Removing gas-lift equipment, changing liquid level, displacing a fluid or releasing a packer can change pressure across tubing and casing. A pre-intervention trend and a post-change trend cannot be compared as if the configuration were unchanged. At each planned transition, record which compartment may become exposed, which element contains it and what new observation is needed.

**Practice:** A verified A-annulus gauge records 180,240 and 270 psi at ten-minute intervals. The job plan provides a training stop threshold of 250 psi. What should the assistant report, and what should the supervisor decide? **Explained answer:** report the actual locations, times and values, including the 250 psi threshold exceedance. Follow the prebriefed stop/escalation response; the supervisor holds the affected operation and obtains the approved integrity assessment. Do not rewrite 270 as 250, average the values below the limit, or invent a bleed procedure. The threshold is supplied for this model only, not a universal field limit.

**Common error:** a stable pressure is described as “safe” without reference to a permitted envelope. Stability is a trend property. Acceptability also depends on containment, loads, fluid, configuration and approved limits. A stable excessive pressure remains unacceptable.

## I18 — Proving the barrier for the intended pressure direction

Testing starts with a question: which element must contain which pressure, in which direction, during the next operation? Draw the tested volume and all possible routes around it. Label test source, observed side, drains, gauges and isolated sections. Define the initial state, test medium, stabilization method, allowable behavior, duration, recording method and stopping conditions before the test. Those parameters come from the approved test program and equipment limits; do not improvise them from this course.

A function test can establish that an actuator responds. It does not alone establish pressure sealing. A pressure test evaluates containment under its stated loading. An inflow or negative-pressure test deliberately evaluates performance with the pressure relationship relevant to potential inflow. An outward pressure test and an inward test are not interchangeable simply because their magnitudes are equal. Some equipment is direction-sensitive; the whole pressure path and the element's documented capability matter. Testing must not remove the independent controls required to conduct the test safely.

**Worked model:** A plug separates a lower chamber at 2,400 psi from an upper chamber. The next operation would leave the upper chamber at 400 psi. The model differential toward the upper chamber is 2,000 psi. A chart showing pressure applied from above does not establish performance under that opposite loading. Nor does a closed gauge branch show that the upper chamber stays at 400. The plan needs a supported direction-specific verification method with independent controls. This example identifies the missing evidence; it is not an instruction to lower live-well pressure.

Interpret records against the actual criteria. A falling pressure may reflect temperature, leakage, redistribution or an instrument issue. The assessor must not award a pass by selecting the least concerning explanation. A replenishment pump can hide leakage if its additions are not measured. A stable isolated gauge can hide movement in the test volume. Record fluid additions, temperature observations, instrument changes and interruptions. Restart or disposition follows the controlled plan, not a retrospective adjustment to the acceptance line.

**Training trace:** The supplied mock plan requires stabilization before a five-minute observation with no replenishment, no unexplained flow, and pressure remaining within the specified band. The operator pumps during minute 3 but records only the final pressure. That trace is invalid for the stated acceptance test even if the final number falls inside the band. Restore the simulation state, document the invalid attempt, review the reason and perform a fresh valid attempt. Do not erase the first record.

**Practice:** Test chamber pressure is recorded at 1,000 psi for five minutes, but the gauge valve was closed before the observation. Is the test passed? **Explained answer:** no; the observation may characterize the isolated gauge rather than the chamber. The necessary measurement communication was not established. Identify the affected evidence and repeat under a verified plan. An instruction to crack a live fitting to check communication is not acceptable.

**Transfer to work:** before opening, compare the verified barrier state with the state that will exist after opening. A test can be real, correctly performed and still irrelevant after equipment movement or a configuration change. The handover must identify its continuing applicability and any limitations.

## I19 — Completion components as connected functions

A completion schematic should show the flow and load functions, not just component names. From the reservoir, trace the tubing bore through any downhole valves and into the tree. Separately trace annular paths around the tubing, packer seals, side-pocket equipment and surface outlets. Tree valves control their specified paths; a tubing hanger supports and seals particular interfaces; a packer separates specified regions. An element's symbol is not evidence of its actual condition.

A sliding sleeve can establish communication between tubing and annulus. A side-pocket mandrel provides a location for a retrievable device; removing or changing that device may alter communication. A landing nipple provides a specified profile for a compatible tool. A plug needs the correct locking and sealing arrangement, not merely a similar outside diameter. A wireline entry guide helps guide tools into the tubing; it is not a substitute for a verified pressure barrier. Read the completion drawing, tool data and planned operation together. See the embedded S13 instruction and its manufacturer references for sleeve and gas-lift functions.

Seal receptacles allow a specified seal assembly and relative movement within its designed range. Pulling beyond that range can alter pressure separation even though the tubing remains physically in the well. Differential pressure and temperature can also alter tubular loading and movement. Verify the permitted movement from the actual equipment and engineering program. Do not assume every polished-bore or extended seal arrangement has the same stroke allowance.

**Functional exercise drawing:**

```text
Production outlet <-- tree tubing path <-- tubing bore <-- reservoir
                                      |
                         sleeve (planned to open)
                                      |
Annulus outlet <------------------ A annulus
                                      |
                            packer separation
Tubing bore also contains: safety valve, nipple/plug profile,
seal assembly inside specified receptacle, entry guide.
An external gas-injection source may connect through separate equipment.
```

**Worked transition:** Before sleeve movement, the approved drawing treats tubing and A annulus as separate. The sleeve is then opened. Crediting the previous annulus isolation without redrawing the new connection misses a potential bypass of the tubing-only barrier. Mark the proposed connection first, then determine the controls and tests needed before permission to move. The same discipline applies when removing a gas-lift device or unseating a plug.

**Geometry practice:** A fictional seal assembly has 1.8 m of verified allowable movement remaining in the planned direction. The proposed movement is 1.5 m, but an unrecorded 0.5 m shift has already occurred in that direction. Is the planned movement supported? **Explained answer:** remaining allowance under this simplified model is 1.3 m;1.5 m would exceed it by 0.2 m. Hold and reconcile the actual position and program. Do not authorize movement because the nominal total assembly length is larger.

**Compatibility practice:** A replacement plug fits the nominal tubing diameter but has a different locking profile. It cannot be treated as an equivalent barrier. Obtain the specified compatible tool and acceptance evidence; a physical fit without the required locking and sealing functions does not meet the objective.

## I20 — Service transitions, operator response and supervision

Wireline, coiled tubing and snubbing share pressure-control principles but move different objects through different sealing and restraint arrangements. A common briefing does not authorize exchanging their response sequences. Use the embedded service model cards to identify the moving boundary, internal flow path, external flow path, retention/restraint function and available backup for the selected service. The assistant reports and supports the prescribed response; the equipment operator executes the configured procedure; the supervisor evaluates changes, interfaces and restart evidence.

For wireline, identify whether the line is slick, braided or an electrical cable and use the appropriate sealing system. Check the actual tool assembly length and position, chamber usable length and component geometry. A tool trap's retention role and a pressure seal's containment role are different. A loss of sealing support can require a controlled response even without a visible leak. Do not continue merely because the last cable type worked with the same equipment.

For coiled tubing, the inside bore and the annular path require separate consideration. Check valves, moving seals, rams and the injector are not interchangeable. Include tubing fatigue/condition, axial loading and the actual shear/seal capability for the tubing and contents. A successful command indication does not prove the intended element closed and sealed. For snubbing, evaluate pressure force and changing pipe weight as well as containment. The transition between pipe-light and pipe-heavy behavior changes restraint needs. Keep the defined restraint through the approved transfer; never infer that one grip is effective merely because the other was released without immediate movement.

**Worked interface case:** A CT operator reports loss of stripper support. The supervisor proposes continuing slowly because a ram is available. That proposal changes the active barrier and movement conditions. The decision requires the configured contingency, verification of what can close on the present geometry, control of internal flow, mechanical restraint and a supported safe state. A generic statement that “the BOP will handle it” is insufficient. The operator follows the approved immediate response; the supervisor does not improvise a replacement seal arrangement while moving.

**Original model:** Pressure is 2,400 psi over an effective 3.5in² area. The upward model force is 8,400 lbf. Downward effective pipe weight is 6,900 lbf, leaving 1,500 lbf upward before other loads. After an additional 2,000 lbf downward weight, the simplified balance becomes 500 lbf downward. This arithmetic illustrates the transition, not the design capacity or operating load. Friction, dynamics, buoyancy assumptions and equipment ratings remain necessary. Never equate “500downward” with permission to release restraint.

**Practice:** A wireline tool was lengthened after the original clearance check; CT check-valve verification is missing; a snubbing console loses one feedback signal. Give one correct common decision and one service-specific check for each. **Explained answer:** hold the affected transition and follow the configured contingency. Recheck wireline usable containment space/tool location; establish CT internal-flow control evidence; verify snubbing restraint and function under the approved lost-feedback response. A blanket “shut everything” is not an adequate demonstrated equipment sequence.

During handover, state which functions are verified, unavailable or uncertain. A supervisor who accepts “all good” without identifying the selected service and current transition cannot make a defensible restart decision. Use a read-back for commands and separate commanded, indicated and verified states in the log.

## I21 — Subsea systems, datums and environmental constraints

A subsea intervention places pressure-control and connection functions where the operator cannot inspect them directly from the work platform. Distinguish three functional arrangements: a subsea lubricator used for riserless wireline work; an open-water intervention riser joining a vessel to an intervention system; and a landing/intervention string running through a drilling BOP arrangement. They differ in where containment resides, what crosses the disconnect boundary, how loads are transmitted and which trapped volumes remain after separation. Their emergency sequences are not interchangeable.

Draw the seabed tree/interface, lower pressure-control functions, connector/disconnect boundary, upper containment, control lines, return routes and tool positions. Then mark what stays on the well and what moves with the vessel. For riserless work, containment of the tool and well fluids remains central even without a full intervention riser. For an open-water riser, include riser contents and vessel movement. For a through-BOP string, include the relationship between the string equipment and the drilling BOP; one system's presence does not prove the other's capability.

Pressure calculations must declare a datum. **Worked model:** formation is 7,500 ft below the surface reference; wellhead is 1,500 ft below it. A continuous 10 ppg liquid column produces 3,900 psi head from surface to formation and 3,120 psi between wellhead and formation. The omitted upper contribution is 780 psi. A wellhead measurement can be combined only with the pressure column below that same reference and the stated communication assumptions. Adding a surface-referenced reading to a seabed-only column without reconciling the reference is wrong.

External seawater pressure affects equipment differential loads. For a model seawater gradient of 0.445 psi/ft at 1,500 ft, external hydrostatic pressure is 667.5 psi relative to surface atmospheric pressure. A component at that depth with 3,000 psi internal pressure on the same reference sees 2,332.5 psi simple differential. This does not establish its rated capacity, collapse resistance or suitability. Real design also requires the manufacturer's reference conventions, pressure/temperature envelope, end loads, fatigue and service compatibility.

**Practice:** At 2,000 ft, use the model 0.445 psi/ft gradient and an internal pressure of 4,000 psi on the same reference. **Explained answer:** external 890 psi, simple differential 3,110 psi. A4,000 psi surface gauge located elsewhere cannot be substituted without knowing the connected column. A smaller differential alone does not justify a component if absolute pressure, temperature, connectors or other constraints are exceeded.

Cold conditions and pressure changes can create hydrate-related obstruction concerns in suitable fluids. Treat an apparent blockage as a pressure-boundary uncertainty, not a solid plug guaranteed to remain. The embedded S14 and pressure-casebook teach the danger of trapped pressure and uncontrolled release. Identify the approved prevention/remediation plan, compatible chemicals, injection paths, environmental controls and monitoring. Never supply heat or depressurize an unverified trapped volume by an improvised method.

**Scenario:** A remotely operated valve reports closed, return pressure falls, and the vessel position begins drifting. The pressure fall could reflect a changed route or loss, not successful isolation. The team follows its prebriefed response, verifies available feedback and escalates the combined well-control and marine condition. Continue monitoring the well functions rather than concentrating exclusively on the vessel display.

## I22 — Subsea readiness, emergency capability and restoration

An emergency system is a set of functions, power sources, communications and verified physical capabilities. A labeled button is not the capability itself. Identify what must seal, what may need cutting, what must remain latched, what can disconnect and what feedback confirms each required function. Define command authority and the relation between vessel, intervention and well teams before exposure. An old BSEE technical model separates sealing, shearing and disconnect functions; it is useful engineering context, not a universal intervention sequence. [BSEE functional-barrier research](https://www.bsee.gov/sites/bsee.gov/files/752ac.pdf).

The emergency plan must address tool geometry at the relevant planes. A device capable of cutting a specified tubing section may not cut a connector, cable combination or reinforced tool at that location. Shearing and sealing are separate capabilities; one cannot be inferred from the other. Identify no-go positions, position verification and the approved contingency if the planned sequence cannot achieve containment. Do not solve an emergency model by declaring an unsuitable tool “probably shearable.”

**Original readiness card:** Tool section K is verified cuttable by the configured device; connection J is not. The planned run places J at the cut plane for 40 seconds during a transition. The team has no documented contingency for loss of position during that interval. The readiness decision is not to accept the gap because it is brief. Revise the operation under the responsible engineering/operational authority, establish a supported contingency and brief the actual limits before work. A classroom assessor cannot write the missing field engineering by substituting a different diagram.

Loss of primary power or communications can affect command, feedback or both. Trace independent backups rather than assuming they are independent because they have different labels. Confirm their supplies, interfaces and tested availability from the controlled configuration. A lost status indication must be recorded as uncertainty, not as confirmation that the last command succeeded. Use actual time-stamped event records in exercises; distinguish demand time, response time and verified condition.

**Worked drill:** At 00:00 the marine team calls the predefined position alarm. At 00:04 the operator gives the required acknowledgment. At 00:08 the primary command is issued. At 00:12 the expected confirmation is absent. The mock procedure requires escalation at that point to the specified backup decision, not repeated blind commands. The learner must identify the missing confirmation, communicate it accurately and follow the supplied configured path. No universal four-second or twelve-second field threshold is created by these fictional times.

Restoration is a new controlled transition. After an emergency or disconnect, establish the well's retained barrier state, the disconnected equipment state, trapped contents, environmental consequences and outstanding failure investigation. Reconnection is not permission to resume intervention. Restore verified equipment, conduct required tests, reassess operating conditions, obtain the designated authorization and hand over restrictions. Preserve the original failure record.

**Practice:** The connector has relatched after a simulated disconnect, but one lower isolation function has no current test evidence. Is “connected” sufficient for restart? **Explained answer:** no. Connection is only one function. The required well-control envelope and its verification must be re-established for the proposed operation; the unresolved isolation evidence remains a hold condition.

**Supervisor exercise:** Write a five-line briefing for the card with nonshearable J. Include geometry limitation, affected time/position, immediate response ownership, the need for an approved contingency, and restart authority. **Model outcome:** no authorizing language until the contingency is supported; explicit hold at the unsafe transition; named communications and truthful status; no claim of external certification from completing this exercise.

## S01-L1

Why must tubing and annulus be traced separately?

**Explained answer:** They may have different pressures and barriers; a tubing isolation does not prove annulus isolation.

A drawing conflicts with an integrity report. What happens before opening the well?

Calculate head for 6,000 ft TVD of 9.5 ppg liquid.

**Explained answer:** 2964 psi.

## S02-L4

Does a zero gauge prove a connection can be opened?

Expected fill is 3.0 bbl and measured fill is 1.2 bbl. Report the discrepancy.

Why can a statically balanced well take an influx during movement?

## S04-L7

Does a function test demonstrate pressure sealing?

**Explained answer:** No; movement and sealing are different properties requiring their specified verification.

Two closed valves have an open bypass. Is the downstream opening isolated?

**Explained answer:** No; the bypass preserves a communicating path.

What belongs in an abnormal-pressure report?

**Explained answer:** Well, pressure point, units, time/trend, operating state, verified changes, action and uncertainty.

A useful report distinguishes observation from diagnosis.

## S05-L10

May a tank transfer be subtracted by guess to explain a gain?

**Explained answer:** No; confirm routing, measured transfer and times while following the required response.

A plausible explanation is not a verified reconciliation.

## S06-L11

Why is releasing a packer a barrier transition?

**Explained answer:** It can connect pressure compartments and change mechanical loading.

Identify the state before, during and after release.

## S06-L12

Can a routine workover continue as live-well work without review?

**Explained answer:** No; reassess equipment, restraint, barriers, competence and the approved program.

A rig that can pull pipe is not automatically a snubbing system.

## S07-L13

Is a tool trap itself proof of pressure isolation?

**Explained answer:** No; tool restraint and pressure isolation are different functions.

Identify the verified pressure-containing element.

## S07-L14

Why recheck lubricator space after adding a tool?

**Explained answer:** The assembly may no longer clear the isolation valve within the available space.

Use actual geometry and verified position.

## S08-L15

Which two flow paths require coiled-tubing controls?

**Explained answer:** Inside the tubing and the annulus around it.

Draw both paths and the relevant barrier elements.

## S08-L16

Why record a nonshearable tool section?

**Explained answer:** An emergency shear function may not work at that position; the plan must address it.

A command label is not proof of capability for every object.

## S09-L17

Effective area is 4 in² and differential is 2000 psi. Find pressure force.

**Explained answer:** 8000 lbf.

Force equals pressure times the stated effective area.

## S09-L18

Why does pipe-heavy still require control?

**Explained answer:** Downward load can cause uncontrolled movement; restraint and pressure containment remain necessary.

The direction of net force changes the control problem rather than removing it.

## S10-L19

What does loss of 500 ft of 10 ppg liquid height do in the simplified model?

**Explained answer:** Reduces liquid head by 260 psi.

0.052 × 10 × 500; density alone does not preserve a barrier if level falls.

## S10-L20

Why is a gas-cut sample not a reliable description of the original liquid column?

**Explained answer:** Gas changes the measured mixture and can expand; the downhole profile must be reassessed.

Do not apply a routine liquid mixing correction without understanding the state.

## S11-L21

Does a calculated balancing density constitute a kill plan?

**Explained answer:** No; routes, limits, fluid state, equipment, monitoring, contingencies and verification are also required.

A number does not authorize a pumping operation.

## S11-L22

An unexpected limit is reached before planned volume. What governs?

**Explained answer:** The approved stop condition; protect the well and report actual state rather than exceed the limit.

Planned volume does not override equipment or well limits.

## S12-L23

What makes a handover useful after an unresolved event?

**Explained answer:** Verified state, observations, uncertainty, restrictions, evidence, next action and named owner.

Do not relabel uncertainty as safe because the shift changes.

## S12-L24

What is closed-loop communication?

**Explained answer:** Address a receiver, give the instruction/condition, receive a read-back and confirm execution by required evidence.

Silence is not confirmation.

## S13-L25

Why does opening a sliding sleeve require a revised pressure-path drawing?

**Explained answer:** It can connect previously separated volumes or zones and change the barrier arrangement.

Evaluate the intended post-change state.

## S13-L26

Does plug depth alone demonstrate isolation?

**Explained answer:** No; compatibility, placement, verification and the credited pressure direction matter.

A location is not an integrity test.

## S14-L27

Can a sand bridge be assumed to be a verified barrier?

**Explained answer:** No; its pressure communication and response to disturbance are uncertain.

Mark uncertainty rather than crediting isolation.

## S14-L28

Why is no discharge from a blocked bleed not proof of depressurization?

**Explained answer:** The blockage may prevent release while pressure remains trapped.

Retain containment and use an approved verification route.

## S15-L29

Does a quiet well before perforating establish the post-perforating condition?

**Explained answer:** No; new formation communication can change pressure and flow.

Plan for the changed state before the operation.

## S15-L30

Why is pumped abandonment volume insufficient as sole acceptance evidence?

**Explained answer:** It does not alone prove placement or barrier integrity.

Apply the approved verification criteria.

## S16-L31

**Explained answer:** 1.0 bbl.

It is a geometric input, not a field bleed instruction.

## S16-L32

**Explained answer:** No; combined effects total 110 psi, exceeding margin by 10 psi.

Use the defined combined-effect model.

# VUC006 — functional simulator workbook

This workbook teaches verification and sequencing on a **fictional, nonpressurized teaching model**. It is not a procedure for a live well or an actual manufacturer's unit. Buttons represent states, not real hydraulic valves. The model intentionally refuses unsafe transitions so the instructor can expose reasoning errors without applying pressure or moving machinery.

## Model board and starting conditions

Draw five boxes: SOURCE, LOWER ISOLATION, TOOL CHAMBER, UPPER CONTAINMENT and CONTROL PANEL. Connect SOURCE to LOWER ISOLATION, then TOOL CHAMBER, then UPPER CONTAINMENT. Add a separate, controlled chamber equalization/recovery route to a labelled closed collection box. The instructor assigns all pressure indications. Use cards for VERIFIED/UNVERIFIED, TOOL CLEAR/TOOL CROSSING, RESTRAINT CONFIRMED/UNKNOWN and CONTROL ENERGY AVAILABLE/LOST.

The source pressure is an arbitrary classroom indication of 100 units; no physical pressure is applied. A second card identifies the pressure in the chamber. The fictitious lower isolation is permitted to close only when TOOL CLEAR is independently confirmed. The chamber may be declared accessible only when lower isolation is confirmed effective, its pressure communication is verified, chamber pressure is confirmed zero and no external pressure source communicates with it. UPPER CONTAINMENT may be declared open only in that accessible state. These are model rules, not real valve instructions.

Use tokens to represent command, indication and verification separately. The learner issues a command; the instructor supplies an indication; the learner must request the relevant verification. This prevents a common misconception: assuming a requested state has been achieved. A pending or contradictory indication leaves the transition incomplete.

### Exercise 1: prepare an entry

Starting state: lower isolation verified effective; chamber pressure zero with communication verified; tool completely in chamber; upper containment not yet verified. The learner must identify that source access cannot be accepted until the specified containment and configuration checks are complete. Have the learner label the containment boundary, confirm tool fit and identify the pressure recovery destination. Only then does the instructor issue the VERIFIED upper-containment card.

Next, the instructor states that the model's approved equalization has made chamber pressure equal to source pressure. The learner records both indications and verifies the intended communication. The instructor then permits the lower-isolation OPEN state, after which the learner moves the tool card to TOOL CROSSING. Ask which element can no longer be assumed closable on clear bore. The answer is the lower isolation; the tool is crossing its boundary. Continue only under the model's verified upper containment.

**Failure injection:** give a zero chamber gauge while a pressure-source card remains connected. The learner must reject the contradictory state, not proceed because the gauge looks favorable. Debrief how isolated gauges and wrong reference points can mislead.

### Exercise 2: recover without trapping an assumption

Starting state: tool crossing lower isolation; upper containment verified; chamber communicates with source. The learner must establish complete tool recovery into the chamber and independent clearance before requesting lower isolation closure. The instructor supplies the closure indication but withholds verification until requested. After effective isolation is confirmed, the learner identifies the approved controlled recovery route and records the instructor's simulated depressurization result.

The learner must verify pressure communication and absence of another source before declaring the chamber accessible. Only then may the upper-containment card be changed to OPEN. Score each distinction: tool position, isolation, recovery route, pressure state and communication. A learner who jumps directly from “closed” to “safe to open” repeats the exercise with a blocked-gauge injection.

# VUC006 — equipment selection and condition workbook

Use fictional data below only for classroom decisions. These tables are not manufacturer ratings. Do not select field equipment from them. Actual operating limits, materials compatibility, inspection criteria and procedures must come from the controlled documentation for the installed equipment.

## 1. Wireline sealing: understand what creates the seal

A grease-injection head uses a close-clearance passage around the cable and supplied grease to sustain a pressure drop. Fit, grease properties, supply condition and movement therefore matter. A line wiper removes excess material from the cable; that cleaning function must not be mistaken for the whole pressure barrier. The manufacturer's [flow-tube description](https://huntingplc.com/products-services/perforating-logging-systems/wireline-hardware-and-accessories/flow-tubes/) explains the basic mechanism. The [NOV Enviro description](https://www.nov.com/products/enviro-wireline-grease-injection-control-head) distinguishes its moving-line sealing system from its stationary-line pack-off function. Product-specific details must not be generalized to every system.

**Selection case.** The fictional job uses cable C-23. Approved model documentation calls for tube set FT-23, grease G-A at the expected temperature and a verified supply/return arrangement. The warehouse offers FT-24 because it physically fits the housing. The learner must reject fit-to-housing as evidence of correct fit-to-cable. Obtain the specified or technically approved equivalent parts and verify the assembled system. Do not compensate for a wrong component by guessing a higher supply pressure.

**Condition case.** During a simulator run, grease consumption rises while cable movement and well pressure are unchanged. Possible explanations include wear, a supply/return leak, altered temperature or a measurement problem. The operator follows the prescribed containment response and reports the trend. The learner must not continue indefinitely by increasing consumption without understanding whether the barrier remains within its approved envelope.

**Questions and explained answers:**

1. Why is “it fits” insufficient? The required sealing clearance and compatible component identity are functional requirements, not just assembly requirements.
2. Why distinguish wiper and pack-off? They perform different functions; a clean cable does not prove containment, and a stationary-line sealing device may not permit movement.
3. What evidence belongs on the readiness record? Line identity/dimensions, selected components, applicable pressure/temperature/fluid limits, required inspection/tests, supply status, response procedure and responsible operator.

## 2. Connections: the weakest exposed part governs

Mechanical connection compatibility includes type, size, sealing arrangement, material, pressure class, condition and correct assembly. Similar appearance or the ability to engage threads does not establish suitability. Trace the pressure boundary through every adapter, hose, valve and body. Include parts that could become exposed through a credible isolation failure or planned lineup change.

**Fictional selection table:**

| Part | Classroom permitted pressure | Temperature envelope | Documented service |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Body A | 5,000 psi | 0–120°C | Fluid F |
| Adapter B | 3,000 psi | 0–100°C | Fluid F |
| Hose C | 4,000 psi | 0–80°C | Fluid F |
| Seal D | 5,000 psi | 0–70°C | Fluid F only |

The proposed state is 3,200 psi and 75°C with Fluid F. The assembly is unacceptable for two independent reasons: Adapter B's pressure limit is exceeded, and Seal D's temperature limit is exceeded. Replacing only the adapter does not fix the seal. If Fluid G is substituted, compatibility must be established again; none of the table's Fluid F entries proves it.

A pressure test at ambient temperature is not evidence that the seal is approved at 75°C. Likewise, a new hose's pressure marking is not evidence that a damaged coupling is acceptable. Inspection and envelope review must both pass.

**Exercise:** revise the proposed state to 2,800 psi and 65°C, still Fluid F. Does the table alone authorize operation? **Answer:** the listed numerical limits no longer conflict, but actual compatibility, assembly, condition, test evidence and approved operating procedure remain necessary. The table is only part of readiness.

## 3. Coiled-tubing differential pressure and damage

Internal and external pressures produce different structural demands. When internal pressure exceeds external pressure, the tube sees an outward differential; when external exceeds internal, it sees an inward differential. Burst and collapse capacities are not interchangeable. Loads, wear, ovality, corrosion and the approved operating model can reduce allowable capability. Never calculate an operational limit from nominal dimensions alone.

**Fictional engineering card:** at the stated axial load and condition, the approved classroom model permits 2,000 psi internal-over-external differential or 1,000 psi external-over-internal differential. These are exercise values only. At 2,800 psi inside and 1,200 psi outside, the differential is 1,600 psi outward. At 500 psi inside and 1,800 psi outside, it is 1,300 psi inward. The second condition exceeds the card's inward limit by 300 psi. A high internal pressure rating does not rescue that condition.

Now the instructor adds an unassessed damaged segment. Neither numerical comparison establishes readiness because the card assumed the previous condition. The learner must request an integrity disposition and preserve the operating restriction. A record that silently resets fatigue history when a reel changes location is not acceptable traceability.

**Practice:** explain why a successful seal test is not a tubing integrity assessment. **Answer:** sealing at the tested boundary and structural condition along the entire string are different properties. The test does not automatically establish remaining fatigue life or suitability for a changed load state.

## 4. Snubbing interfaces and seal changes

A stand-alone unit and a rig-assisted arrangement distribute control functions and loads differently. Before a combined operation, identify which organization and control system owns movement, restraint, pressure containment and emergency response. A shared work platform does not create shared understanding. Interface failure can occur when each operator assumes the other holds the pipe or controls the barrier.

**Case:** contractor A controls one restraint function; contractor B controls movement. The procedure requires a confirmed hold before movement changes. Radio traffic becomes unclear. The correct result is a hold pending acknowledged state verification, not movement based on an expected response. In the classroom model, have both operators point to the verified restraint card and repeat the agreed condition before the instructor permits the next simulated action.

Replacing a worn pressure-containing seal is a barrier transition. The replacement task needs a verified alternative barrier and a controlled accessible work volume. “Maintenance” is not permission to open a live pressure boundary. Identify all trapped volumes and pressure sources, then follow the actual isolation and verification procedure. A spare seal on site improves repair readiness but does not prove the system can safely reach a repair state.

**Practice:** an instructor marks the alternate barrier UNVERIFIED during the planned seal change. **Answer:** do not begin the opening; preserve the current safe state and obtain the required verification or revised contingency. Schedule pressure does not supply missing isolation evidence.

## 5. Pressure testing without creating a new exposure

A test introduces stored energy and must have a controlled boundary, suitable medium, rated equipment, exclusion arrangements, monitoring and controlled depressurization. Personnel must not handle a connection or unsecured hose as though it were harmless because the test is short. BSEE's [Safety Alert 442](https://www.bsee.gov/sites/bsee.gov/files/safety-alerts/bsee-ssafety-alert-442-unsecured-pressurized-hose-result-in-hand-injuries.pdf) describes hand injuries involving pressurized hoses during coiled-tubing work.

**Case:** a fictional test chart shows a pressure hold, but the hose identity and boundary sketch are missing. The evidence cannot establish whether the intended assembled boundary was tested. Recover the actual records or perform the required verified test under the approved procedure. Do not retroactively fill in a remembered equipment identity as if it had been recorded at the time.

**Practice:** what must be known before a connection is inspected by hand after a test? **Answer:** the required isolation/depressurization and communication verification must be complete, with remaining energy controlled. A test-ended announcement or a remote zero gauge alone is insufficient.

# VUC006 — pressure and program casebook

These worked problems extend S02, S03, S10, S11 and S16. Every stated limit is fictional. Use them for calculation and decision practice, never as field limits. Each answer must include its assumptions and the decision the calculation supports or does not support.

## Case 1 — circulating and static states are different

An exercise well has 5,000 ft TVD and a full 10 ppg liquid column. The approved training model assigns 130 psi of annular friction above the point of interest while circulating at the stated rate. No additional surface backpressure is present. The static liquid head is 2,600 psi. The circulating pressure contribution is 2,600 + 130 = **2,730 psi**. Expressed as equivalent density, 2,730 ÷ (0.052 × 5,000) = **10.5 ppg**.

The [SLB ECD definition](https://glossary.slb.com/en/terms/e/equivalent_circulating_density) relates effective circulating density to fluid density and annular pressure loss. The critical interpretation is that the additional contribution depends on circulation. If that contribution disappears when pumps stop, a well balanced only in the circulating condition may become underbalanced. Conversely, the higher circulating pressure may threaten a weak interval that is acceptable under static conditions.

**Worked decision:** the fictional formation pressure is 2,700 psi. The static model is 100 psi below it, while the circulating model is 30 psi above it. The calculation demonstrates a transition risk. It does not authorize a rate, backpressure or density change. The program must manage the transition within the actual well/equipment limits.

**Practice:** at 4,000 ft TVD, 9 ppg fluid and 104 psi annular friction, find ECD. **Answer:** 9 + 104 ÷ (0.052 × 4,000) = **9.5 ppg**. Explain why pump pressure cannot simply replace annular friction in the equation: it includes other parts of the flow path.

## Case 2 — the weak point changes the available surface margin

An engineering exercise supplies a verified permitted pressure of 2,400 psi at a weak point 4,000 ft below the reference surface. A full 10 ppg liquid column produces 2,080 psi there. Under this deliberately simplified static model, only **320 psi** additional transmitted surface pressure fits within the supplied constraint. This is not a universal field MAASP calculation; actual limits depend on the approved model and all applicable constraints.

If density becomes 10.5 ppg, liquid head at that point becomes 2,184 psi and the remaining simplified allowance becomes **216 psi**. Increasing density has reduced the allowance by 104 psi. It may improve one pressure balance while worsening another limit. Any program change must evaluate both effects.

Now add a fictional 150 psi annular friction contribution at that weak point and 80 psi of imposed surface pressure. Total becomes 2,184 + 150 + 80 = **2,414 psi**, above the supplied constraint by 14 psi. Evaluating 150 and 80 separately against 216 would miss their combined effect.

**Practice:** the engineering model instead gives 2,300 psi permissible at the same point with 10 ppg static fluid. Find the remaining allowance. **Answer:** 220 psi. This is a calculation from supplied engineering data, not an independent determination that 2,300 psi is a valid formation limit.

## Case 3 — changing fluid density without losing volume accounting

For a classroom blend with additive volumes and no reaction or temperature change, the mass-balance average density is the sum of each volume times its density divided by total volume. Mixing 40 bbl at 9 ppg and 60 bbl at 11 ppg gives (40 × 9 + 60 × 11) ÷ 100 = **10.2 ppg**. The arithmetic alone says nothing about chemical compatibility.

Suppose the task is to obtain 10 ppg by mixing 9 ppg and 11 ppg fluids. Equal volumes produce that average under the same assumptions. A mixture calculation is not permission to mix fluids whose chemistry is unknown, nor proof that the material downhole now has that density. The active system must be identified and sampled appropriately; fluid may remain in dead legs or isolated volumes.

**Worked error:** a learner averages 9 and 11 to obtain 10 ppg even though the volumes are 40 and 60 bbl. That ignores unequal weighting. Ask the learner to compute the total density-volume contributions and show why the larger volume pulls the result toward 11 ppg.

**Practice:** 25 bbl at 8 ppg plus 75 bbl at 10 ppg. **Answer:** 9.5 ppg, subject to compatibility and additive-volume assumptions. If the mixture contains gas, a simple liquid mass-balance treatment may not represent the relevant state.

## Case 4 — anticipate surface pressure after a fluid change

A fictional well's supported static formation estimate is 3,000 psi at 5,000 ft TVD. With a full 10 ppg liquid column, the simplified remaining pressure at surface is 3,000 − 2,600 = **400 psi**. With a full 8 ppg column, head is 2,080 psi and the simplified remaining surface pressure is **920 psi**. Replacing heavier fluid with lighter fluid can increase surface containment demand.

This is one modeled condition, not the maximum anticipated surface pressure for every possible state. Determining a credible maximum requires the well's actual gas/fluid profiles, temperature and operational scenarios. A fully gas-filled path cannot be represented by simply reusing the liquid calculation. The instructor must reject a learner's attempt to label 920 psi as the universal maximum without those assessments.

**Practice:** use 9 ppg for the same 5,000-ft column. **Answer:** 2,340 psi head and 660 psi simplified surface pressure. State what changed and what did not: formation estimate stayed fixed in the exercise; the liquid contribution changed.

## Case 5 — pressure on a plug is a difference, not one reading

The model supplies 1,800 psi immediately below a plug and 600 psi immediately above it, both at the plug's location and on the same pressure reference. Differential is **1,200 psi upward**. If the upper pressure later becomes 2,200 psi, differential is **400 psi downward**. A plug qualified for one direction and differential cannot automatically be credited for the reversed condition.

Surface readings may need hydrostatic correction before comparison at the plug. Comparing a surface tubing gauge directly with a downhole pressure at the plug mixes reference points. The exercise deliberately supplies both pressures at one location to isolate the concept.

**Practice:** 1,500 psi below and 1,500 psi above gives zero modeled differential. Does this prove safe retrieval? **Answer:** no. The apparent equality must be valid and stable, the replacement barrier and retrieval method must be approved, and other energy/load conditions remain relevant.

## Case 6 — decide what a kill-program review actually demonstrates

The representative receives three pages: a calculation, a pressure chart and a signed contractor checklist. The calculation uses a full liquid column, while the operations log records recent losses and an uncertain fluid level. The pressure chart has no location label. The checklist identifies a different component serial number than the equipment drawing.

None of the documents is necessarily fraudulent, but together they do not establish readiness. The calculation's assumption is unsupported, the chart's relevance cannot be determined and the equipment identity conflicts. The representative records those exact gaps and holds the affected operation. This is more useful than rejecting the packet as “bad paperwork”: it tells the responsible parties what evidence must be corrected.

**Model request for resolution:** “Confirm current fluid level/profile and revise the pressure calculation; identify the chart's measurement point and tested boundary; reconcile the installed component identity with the inspection/test evidence. Retain the current hold until the approved well program and barrier arrangement reflect the verified state.” No new operating instruction is invented.

**Practice:** write acceptance criteria for the resubmission. **Answer:** current supported assumptions; traceable equipment and test boundaries; calculations within approved well and equipment limits; verified routes and surface capacity; configured response; named authorization. A new signature alone does not resolve the technical gaps.

## Case 7 — connected columns can move after the pump stops

Two open-to-atmosphere vertical legs of a fictional model connect at 2,000 ft below their initial liquid surfaces. One contains 10 ppg liquid; the other contains 9 ppg liquid. Before any redistribution, their pressures at the connection are 1,040 psi and 936 psi. The 104 psi difference drives movement from the heavier-column leg through the connection toward the lighter-column leg. A stopped pump does not eliminate that imbalance. This is the mechanism described by the [SLB U-tube reference](https://glossary.slb.com/en/terms/u/u-tube_effect).

For this deliberately idealized exercise, let both legs have equal cross-sectional area, keep each liquid separate, and ignore friction, mixing and gas. If the heavy side falls by x feet, the light side rises by x feet. Equilibrium requires 10(2,000 − x) = 9(2,000 + x). Thus 2,000 = 19x and x = **105.26 ft**. The resulting heights are 1,894.74 ft and 2,105.26 ft; both produce approximately 985.26 psi at the connection. Different areas would require a volume balance as well as a pressure balance.

In a well, restrictions, trapped gas, closed valves and different flow areas change this behavior. The teaching model predicts neither a real flow rate nor the time to stop. Its value is explaining why a changing return after stopping pumps requires interpretation against the actual fluid profile and barrier arrangement. Do not label an unexplained gain as harmless U-tubing without evidence; an influx can coexist with redistribution.

**Independent practice:** at equal initial heights of 1,000 ft, compare 11 ppg and 9 ppg legs under the same ideal assumptions. Which way is the initial driving force and how far does each surface move? **Explained answer:** heavy toward light; 11(1,000 − x) = 9(1,000 + x), so x = 100 ft. Both final connection pressures are 514.8 psi. Identify at least three assumptions that must be checked before relating the model to an actual observation.

## Case 8 — separate buoyancy from pressure acting on a closed end

Start with Archimedes' principle: an immersed body displaces fluid, whose weight supplies the buoyant force. For a fully submerged solid of uniform material density, the ideal apparent weight is its weight in air multiplied by (1 − fluid density/material density), using identical density units. This is a teaching model for buoyancy, not a complete snubbing-force calculation.

The supplied fictional material density is 65.5 ppg, fluid density is 10 ppg, and air weight is 20,000 lbf. The factor is 1 − 10/65.5 = 0.84733. Apparent weight is approximately **16,946.6 lbf**. The buoyant reduction is 3,053.4 lbf. Increasing fluid density to 12 ppg reduces apparent weight to approximately **16,335.9 lbf** under these assumptions.

A tubular with different internal and external fluid columns, closed ends, imposed pressure, contact forces or partial immersion requires the correct free-body model. Do not apply the solid-body shortcut and then add another buoyancy correction for the same displaced fluid. Do not substitute nominal pipe area for the pressure-effective area supplied by the equipment/well model.

**Worked force distinction:** a separate fictional engineering card already gives the applicable submerged downward force as 16,000 lbf and an upward pressure force as 18,000 lbf. Their net, before other specified forces, is 2,000 lbf upward. Applying the buoyancy factor again to the 16,000 lbf would double-correct a value already adjusted. The operator must recognize the upward tendency and use the approved restraint/movement system; the arithmetic does not specify a safe slip sequence.

**Independent practice:** use supplied air weight 13,100 lbf, material density 65.5 ppg and liquid density 10 ppg. **Answer:** apparent weight 11,100 lbf in the ideal solid model. Explain why an externally applied well-pressure force needs separate treatment in a real equipment model, and why that model's definitions must be understood before summing forces.

## Case 9 — the density measurement has a temperature reference

A fluid sample's surface density is not automatically its density throughout the well. Temperature and pressure affect the fluid state; composition and crystallization behavior also matter. The responsible fluids specialist supplies the applicable property model and measurement method. Do not invent a universal correction per degree or use an unverified chart for a different brine.

This original exercise supplies the corrections rather than asking the learner to design them. A fictional laboratory card gives 10.2 ppg at sampling conditions. The approved exercise model supplies average densities of 10.1 ppg for the upper 2,000 vertical feet and 9.9 ppg for the lower 3,000 vertical feet. The head is 0.052 × [(10.1 × 2,000) + (9.9 × 3,000)] = **2,594.8 psi**. Treating the whole column as 10.2 ppg would produce 2,652 psi, overstating head by 57.2 psi.

The numerical difference does not itself choose a replacement fluid. The program must consider the complete pressure window, compatibility, actual fluid level and changes through the operation. If the supplied corrections have the wrong composition or temperature range, stop using them and obtain applicable data.

**Independent practice:** the validated exercise model changes to 10 ppg in the upper 2,000 ft and 9.8 ppg in the lower 3,000 ft. **Answer:** 2,568.8 psi, 26 psi less than the preceding modeled state. State the evidence needed before using either model: fluid identity and composition, supported temperature/pressure profile, property-model applicability, fluid-level/profile evidence and approved calculation review.

# VUC006 — kill-method reasoning and simulator lab

## Purpose and operating boundary

This lab teaches how pressure, volume, rate and a defined control objective fit together. All values are fictional and all actions occur on a paper or software model with **no physical pressure, flow or machinery**. It is not a field kill sheet. Field method selection, fluid properties, equipment lineup, allowable pressures and startup/shutdown sequence require the approved well-specific program and competent personnel.

Students must distinguish three questions: Can the chosen path physically communicate? Can the system tolerate the resulting pressure and fluid? Does the observed response match the model sufficiently to continue? A method is not acceptable merely because its arithmetic can be completed.

## Lab A — forward circulation and a two-stage fluid change

The synthetic well has 5,000 ft TVD, a verified full 10 ppg initial liquid column and a supported formation-pressure estimate of 2,860 psi. The verified static communicating tubing pressure is 260 psi. At the model's fixed training rate, original-fluid string friction is 300 psi. The string volume is 50 bbl and verified ideal pump output is 0.1 bbl/stroke. The model excludes temperature changes, gas solubility, tool restrictions and changing friction except where stated.

The first learning objective is to distinguish restoring a fluid barrier from simply moving an influx. In a conventional two-stage concept, the original fluid can circulate an influx out while controlled surface pressure maintains the pressure objective; a later stage replaces it with the calculated liquid density. The details of actual implementation remain well-specific. A quiet surface return after one stage is not proof that the original liquid alone balances the well.

### Calculate the model targets

Original liquid head = 0.052 × 10 × 5,000 = **2,600 psi**. Balancing density = 2,860 ÷ (0.052 × 5,000) = **11 ppg**. Initial circulating pressure in this simplified string model = 260 + 300 = **560 psi**. Final string-friction pressure at the same rate, with friction assumed proportional to density for this exercise, is 300 × 11/10 = **330 psi**. String displacement requires 50 ÷ 0.1 = **500 strokes**.

The training model explicitly specifies a linear transition between initial and final pump-pressure targets while heavier liquid fills the string. This is not a universal field relationship. Actual friction and geometry may require a different schedule.

| Strokes after weighted-fluid entry | Fraction of string displaced | Model target pump pressure |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0% | 560 psi |
| 100 | 20% | 514 psi |
| 200 | 40% | 468 psi |
| 300 | 60% | 422 psi |
| 400 | 80% | 376 psi |
| 500 | 100% | 330 psi |

The learner calculates the table, labels its assumptions and records the evidence required before using any corresponding real schedule. Stroke zero is the defined entry of new fluid into the modeled string, not an arbitrary pump-start time. Fluid delivery and routing must match that reference.

### Operate the classroom model

The instructor acts as the model and provides pressure/volume readings. The learner states the target, compares the indication and explains the next permitted model action. No actual choke or pump sequence is supplied here. The model allows the learner to request HOLD, VERIFY or CONTINUE according to the exercise's acceptance conditions.

At 200 strokes the instructor supplies 468 psi, the correct density and reconciled volume. CONTINUE is supported. At 300 strokes the instructor instead supplies 500 psi with no explanation. The learner must request HOLD/VERIFY under the training rule: any unexplained target deviation requires assessment before continuation. Possible causes include obstruction, rate change, wrong fluid, a measurement error or an invalid model; no single cause is established by one pressure reading.

A response is not correct merely because it says “adjust the choke.” The learner must identify the control objective and the evidence needed to avoid moving pressure in the wrong direction. Repeated adjustments before the system responds can overshoot. The instructor introduces a two-observation response lag, and the learner records the pending response rather than issuing contradictory changes to chase every reading.

### Verification at the end

At 500 strokes, the model's string has been displaced; that does not establish that the entire well and annulus contain the intended fluid. Complete the approved model's remaining volume accounting and independent pressure-state verification. In a real well, subsequent stable observations, pressure communication, fluid profile, barrier status and approved checks are necessary before declaring the intended controlled condition.

**Formative task:** repeat with a 40 bbl string and 0.08 bbl/stroke. **Answer:** still 500 ideal strokes. The identical count does not make the two systems physically identical.

## Lab B — startup and shutdown change friction support

This model represents bottom pressure as liquid head plus modeled annular friction plus imposed return-side pressure. Liquid head is 2,600 psi and the required model bottom pressure is 2,860 psi. The instructor provides friction at each training rate; the learner calculates the corresponding return-side pressure needed to preserve the objective.

| Training state | Supplied annular friction | Required model return-side pressure |
|---|---:|---:|
| Static | 0 psi | 260 psi |
| Intermediate rate | 60 psi | 200 psi |
| Full model rate | 130 psi | 130 psi |

The sum remains 2,860 psi. The lesson is that friction support changes during startup and shutdown. A fixed pressure chosen for one state may not preserve the objective in another. Actual control methods and reference gauges depend on the circulation route, equipment and approved procedure.

The instructor asks for a shutdown but delays the pressure observation. The learner must communicate the intended transition, seek acknowledgement and track the actual state. They must not declare the well controlled solely because the pump-stop command was issued. This exercise grades reasoning and communication; it does not qualify an actual console sequence.

**Formative task:** at another modeled state, friction is 90 psi. **Answer:** required model return-side pressure is 170 psi. If an equipment limit were below that requirement, the model is not operable as proposed; the learner must not override the limit.

## Lab C — reverse circulation changes where resistance acts

Forward and reverse circulation exchange the injection and return routes. If a narrow tubing string becomes the return route, its pressure loss contributes differently to the pressure profile than when it is the injection route. The location of the controlling restriction and the interpretation of surface gauges change. One must recompute the profile rather than reuse a forward-circulation schedule with the arrows reversed.

The fictional reverse-flow model supplies 2,600 psi liquid head, 350 psi pressure loss above the bottom point along the return route and 100 psi imposed return pressure. Bottom pressure is **3,050 psi** in this simplified relationship. The supplied fictional formation limit is 3,000 psi. The proposed state is unacceptable by **50 psi** even though neither surface number looks large in isolation.

**Learner decision:** reject the proposed state and request an approved revised rate/route/program. Do not choose a new field setting yourself. Trace which components see the pressure and whether the pathway is actually open and compatible with the fluid. The instructor then supplies a revised model with 250 psi return loss and 100 psi imposed pressure; the result is 2,950 psi. It fits that one constraint, but all other required limits and control objectives still need checking.

**Formative task:** name two reasons the revised numerical result alone is insufficient. **Answer:** actual path integrity/communication and other equipment/well limits remain unverified; the assumed friction and fluid profile must also be valid.

## Lab D — bullheading: injection is not proved by rising pressure

Bullheading uses an accepting downhole path rather than bringing the displaced material to surface through a circulation return. Its feasibility depends on formation acceptance and pressure constraints as well as fluid and mechanical considerations. Rising pump pressure can represent compressing a closed system or overcoming resistance; it does not prove that the intended formation is taking fluid.

For this classroom model, an engineer supplies an acceptable injection relationship and a simplified upward gas-movement estimate. The modeled flow-path capacity is 0.006 bbl/ft and the upward movement estimate is 80 ft/min. Their product is **0.48 bbl/min**. The model's permitted operating rate must exceed that displacement-rate equivalent while remaining inside its separately supplied injection and pressure limits. This calculation is deliberately limited; real gas-slip behavior and compressibility cannot be reduced to this fixed value without a valid model.

The engineer's exercise cards permit 0.6–0.8 bbl/min under the stated condition. The learner must identify that 0.4 bbl/min does not satisfy the exercise's movement requirement and that 1.0 bbl/min exceeds the supplied permitted range. The learner does not invent a “safety factor” that violates the other constraint.

### Interpret the log

| Time | Model rate | Pressure observation | Volume/accounting observation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 09:00 | 0.6 bbl/min | On approved model trend | Reconciled |
| 09:02 | 0.6 bbl/min | Rising faster than model | Destination not confirmed |
| 09:03 | Instructor hold | Unresolved | Actual delivered volume retained in log |

Correct response: hold under the model rule, report the divergence and request technical verification. Do not declare injection merely from the pressure rise or keep pumping to achieve a planned total. Over-displacement can expose a different zone or exceed the intended treatment envelope. A theoretical geometric volume is not an unlimited pumping authorization.

**Formative task:** capacity 0.005 bbl/ft and supplied movement estimate 60 ft/min. **Answer:** 0.30 bbl/min displacement-rate equivalent. State the same limitations and need for an approved operating range.

## Lab E — liquid addition and gas management concepts

Noncirculating techniques may manage gas/pressure when ordinary circulation is unavailable. They require a validated pressure-volume model, controlled handling and competent supervision. This section teaches the relationship between liquid height and pressure; it intentionally does not provide a live-well bleed sequence.

The model chamber has a constant capacity of 0.01 bbl/ft and a liquid gradient of 0.52 psi/ft. One barrel of added liquid occupies 100 ft and contributes **52 psi** of hydrostatic head. Two barrels correspond to 200 ft and 104 psi. This relationship explains why a defined liquid addition can change the pressure balance; it does not mean that exactly 52 psi can safely be released at surface in any real situation.

The instructor adds gas-migration, temperature-change or unknown-capacity cards. Each card invalidates an assumption of the simple model. The learner must identify the missing information and stop using the simplified result as a complete pressure-management plan. Separate an engineering safety allowance from a working pressure change used within a defined model; neither can be guessed from the size of a gauge division.

**Formative task:** capacity 0.02 bbl/ft and gradient 0.468 psi/ft. What is the liquid-head contribution per barrel? **Answer:** 50 ft/bbl × 0.468 = **23.4 psi/bbl**. It remains only a geometric/hydrostatic relationship.

## Lab F — final integrated observation

The learner receives a fresh version of one model, a diagram, the assumed pressure/volume relationships and its explicit acceptance envelope. They must:

1. Check assumptions and calculate the schedule or state values.
2. Trace the pressure path and identify every relevant supplied limit.
3. Communicate the intended transition and acknowledge the simulated response.
4. Detect an injected mismatch and stop the model under its stated rule.
5. Preserve the log, explain remaining uncertainty and state what verification is needed before completion or restart.

Score 0–2 per criterion, require 8/10 and independent correctness on limits, mismatch response and completion verification. Unsafe extrapolation from the classroom model to a live-well instruction is a critical failure. Keep the learner's completed schedule and event log with the result. This supplements station G and the operator-representative case; it is not a substitute for actual equipment-specific qualification.

# VUC006 — control systems and site coordination

This instruction extends S04, S08, S10, S12 and S16. It teaches the supporting systems that make the pressure-control plan executable. All equipment figures are fictional. No worksheet supplies a real hydraulic setting, precharge value or equipment operating sequence.

## Hydraulic readiness is more than a running pump

A hydraulic control system supplies fluid pressure and usable fluid volume to perform assigned functions. Accumulators store energy so specified functions may remain available under defined supply conditions. Gauge pressure, usable volume, routing and the required actuation conditions must be evaluated together. A running pump alone does not demonstrate emergency capability. NOV's [BOP control-unit description](https://www.nov.com/-/media/nov/files/products/rig/rig-equipment/pressure-control-systems/bop-control-unit-flyer.pdf) identifies accumulator monitoring and low-pressure alarms among control-system features.

Before a simulation, locate the supply, energy storage, regulators, distribution paths, controls and feedback on the actual controlled schematic. Identify what a loss of the normal power source changes. Do not assume that every function fails closed or that every local and remote control has identical authority. Required stored-energy verification and function tests come from the applicable design and procedure.

**Worked exercise:** a fictional validated test card states that 12 L of usable hydraulic fluid remain within the specified pressure range. The approved model requires 4 L for function A and 6 L for function B, plus a 3 L reserve. The demand is 13 L, exceeding the available amount by 1 L. Both functions moving during a previous test does not resolve the present shortfall. The learner records the failed readiness condition and requests correction and re-verification under the controlled procedure. These values are a supplied arithmetic model, not an accumulator-sizing method.

**Practice:** a new card gives 15 L available for the same requirement. **Answer:** the volume comparison has 2 L margin, but pressure, condition, routing and the rest of the approved readiness checks still need evidence. A numerical pass cannot cure a disconnected control line.

## Loads, connections and the assembled system

Pressure rating addresses a pressure envelope; it does not establish that the wellhead, supports or connectors can carry every external load. A tall assembly adds weight and bending demand. Suspended equipment, movement, wind or an unsupported hose can change the load path. Use the approved support and connection arrangement, including the specified inspection and assembly requirements. Do not improvise a tie-down onto a convenient component without verifying that load path.

**Original case:** the rig-up drawing specifies a support at location X. The location is unavailable, so the crew proposes attaching the support to an instrument bracket. The bracket's load capacity is undocumented. The pressure test record remains valid for its tested boundary, but it does not authorize the new support arrangement. Hold the affected installation and obtain a reviewed support plan. The responsible engineer must assess the changed geometry, reaction loads and component limits.

**Practice:** list the distinct evidence categories. **Answer:** pressure/temperature/material compatibility; mechanical support and connection suitability; assembled identity and inspection; applicable pressure/function testing; approved configuration and response. This prevents one favorable document from standing in for the whole review.

## Gas detection, escape and equipment compatibility

Hydrogen sulfide can rapidly disable a worker. Smell is not a reliable detector. Use the specified, serviceable detection equipment and the site's alarm/escape plan; do not remain in a suspected atmosphere to finish a worksheet or obtain a better reading. OSHA's [H2S quick card](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/HYDROGEN_SULFIDE.pdf) explains the hazard, the unreliability of smell and the need for protective planning. This program does not qualify entry or rescue in a hazardous atmosphere.

**Original case:** during a model well-control event, a gas alarm activates while the operator is recording a pressure trend. The instructor supplies the site's simulated emergency card: notify the controller, use the assigned safe shutdown action only if it can be performed safely, evacuate by the designated route, muster and account for people. The learner must follow the emergency card instead of staying to complete the pressure log. A missing coworker triggers the trained rescue response; it does not authorize unprotected re-entry.

H2S service also requires appropriate equipment/material compatibility. The OSHA [oil-and-gas H2S guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/oil-and-gas/general-safety/h2s-monitoring) identifies sulfide-related material damage concerns. A component's pressure marking alone does not establish suitability for the expected chemical environment. Carbon dioxide and water can likewise affect corrosion conditions; the job's materials assessment must address the actual service rather than merely the equipment's age.

**Practice:** a detector's battery is depleted and a worker says the gas has no smell. **Answer:** do not credit that as monitoring. Apply the site's loss-of-monitoring procedure and restore the required verified protection before the affected work resumes. The absence of smell is not evidence of an acceptable atmosphere.

## Return handling and electrical equipment

The return path continues beyond the pressure-control device. The plan must identify how gas, liquids and solids are handled, what capacity/pressure constraints apply, how the route is monitored and what happens when processing becomes unavailable. A separator's nameplate is not proof that its actual configuration can handle the current mixture and rate. Do not route an uncertain stream to an ordinary open tank merely because a hose reaches it.

Electrical equipment must suit its installed location and the applicable hazardous-area requirements. A battery-powered device is not automatically approved for every classified area. Verify the actual device, rating, location and site restrictions; do not infer approval from appearance.

**Original case:** a temporary electrically driven pump is moved beside the return manifold. Its area suitability is unknown and the emergency route now crosses its cables. The correct coordination outcome is to hold the change, resolve the equipment/location and access conflicts, and update the work plan. An intervention permit does not automatically approve all temporary equipment introduced later.

## Shift fatigue, handover and simultaneous operations

Long-duration operations need a staffing and relief plan that preserves control responsibility and attention. A checklist signed at the beginning of a shift cannot show that a later fatigued or distracted operator remains ready for a complex transition. Before a critical transition, confirm roles, communication and the current state. Use relief arrangements when needed; do not create an unannounced transfer of control.

**Original case:** one operator is monitoring returns while another contractor requests a pressure-test change. The relief operator arrives during the conversation. The team must first establish who has control, acknowledge the hold on the proposed change, and transfer the current pressures, routes, barrier status and restrictions. The handover must distinguish a requested change from an approved and completed one. Neither shift change nor a permit signature erases unresolved conditions.

**Practice:** prepare a four-line handover for that case. **Model answer:** name the current controller; state the verified well and return-system condition; identify the requested but unapproved pressure-test change; name the decision owner and retain the restriction until the change is reviewed and acknowledged.

## Instructor exercise and evidence

After teaching, issue a fresh version of one numerical and one coordination case. Require the learner to identify the controlling function, separate facts from assumptions, interpret the supplied constraints, communicate a justified decision and record the unresolved issue. Score five criteria 0–2; require 8/10 with no unsafe continuation, opening or re-entry. Keep this observed exercise separate from the controlled final. Apply remediation by revisiting the function or assumption that was missed, then use a changed case.

# VUC006 — calculation reference

Use only with established assumptions and approved units. These relationships support classroom reasoning; they do not specify a field operating sequence.

| Quantity | Relationship | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Uniform liquid head, psi | 0.052 × ppg × vertical liquid height, ft | TVD/head, not MD; full liquid assumption must be verified |
| Layered liquid head | Sum each layer's head | Use each density and vertical interval |
| Simplified static bottom pressure | Communicating surface pressure + liquid head | Valid communication and stabilized state required |
| Balance density, ppg | Target static pressure ÷ (0.052 × TVD) | Not an operational fluid specification |

| Annular capacity, bbl/ft | (enclosing ID² − tubular OD²) ÷ 1029.4 | Correct geometry and units |

| Displacement, bbl | Approved displacement per foot × movement | Open/closed/draining condition matters |
| Ideal pump strokes | Volume ÷ verified output per stroke | Confirm actual volume and routing |
| Simplified pressure force, lbf | Differential psi × specified effective area, in² | Real force model includes other effects |
| Ideal gas exercise | P1 × V1 = P2 × V2 | Absolute pressure, constant temperature, ideal gas only |

Record intermediate values, final units and rounding. Carry adequate precision through calculations; do not round a value toward a more permissive operating limit. If assumptions are missing, state that the result cannot yet support the decision rather than inventing them.
