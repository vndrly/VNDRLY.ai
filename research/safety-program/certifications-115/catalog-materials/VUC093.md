# VNDRLY/U Helicopter Administrator — Initial Training

VUC093. Original available-source materials, 18November 2026. Supports HLO/operator/crew administration; does not authorize flight dispatch, loading approval, DG acceptance outside trained function or live helideck control. Entry requires current function-appropriate dangerous-goods and personnel-system preparation verified by employer/operator. Full OPITO specification unavailable; no external approval/exact parity claimed.

## Q93-1 — Administrator role and operating boundaries

The helicopter administrator supports the HLO, aircraft operator and flight crew with accurate movement, passenger and freight information. Administrative preparation does not give authority to dispatch an aircraft, approve flight performance, accept dangerous goods outside one's function or direct passengers around rotating aircraft. Know the operator's procedures, your assigned permissions and the escalation route. Flight safety decisions remain with the authorized operational personnel.

Before training, verify current function-appropriate dangerous-goods instruction and personnel-tracking-system familiarity. An old category label on a certificate does not by itself prove current task competence; the employer/operator must map training to actual functions. This initial course develops administrative skills and leads to supervised workplace development, not automatic independent workplace competence. Identify where the administrator must stop and seek approval rather than fill a gap by assumption.

Worked example: the schedule says flight ready but the HLO reports a deck restriction. Preserve the restriction, notify the operator and do not announce boarding based on the schedule. Changed example: a colleague asks you to sign a dangerous-goods acceptance outside your training. Hold and refer to the authorized person; being able to enter data does not confer acceptance authority.

## Q93-2 — Flight schedule, sectors and movement information

Treat each flight sector as a distinct movement with origin, destination, date, time basis, flight identifier and aircraft details as required by the operator. A multi-stop route changes passengers, baggage and freight at each stop. A single unchecked list for the whole day can misstate who is actually on a particular sector. Confirm the current schedule and communicate amendments to the responsible parties with revision and time.

Separate planned, estimated and actual times. Use the agreed time zone or UTC notation explicitly; do not silently mix local and UTC. Record delays, diversions and cancellations without turning an estimate into an actual departure. Weather, deck status and aircraft acceptance information must come from authorized sources. The administrator relays verified information and identifies missing data; the administrator does not invent weather observations or flight clearance.

Worked example: H93-A is Shore→Alpha then Alpha→Bravo. P1 and P2 leave at Alpha while P3 continues. The second-sector manifest must reflect P3 and any newly boarded people, not all first-sector names. Changed example: destination changes from Bravo to Shore. Amend the sector, passenger/bag routing and communication together; changing only the screen title leaves unsafe inconsistent records.

## Q93-3 — Passenger identity, eligibility and check-in

Check each person's identity against the authorized booking and destination. Similar names are not interchangeable. Verify the operator/employer-required travel eligibility through approved records and refer discrepancies to the responsible person. Do not invent an expiry grace period or bypass a missing prerequisite because a seat is available. Respect privacy while ensuring the operational team receives necessary information.

Record check-in status separately from boarded status. A checked-in person may fail to board or be removed from the flight. Confirm the final movement through the designated process before changing personnel-on-board or site-presence records. Collect assistance or seating-related information through the operator's arrangements without making unsupported medical judgments. Keep passengers in the designated waiting/control area until the HLO/crew process authorizes movement.

Worked example: booking lists Alex Reed, ID X2, while the arriving person is Alex Read, ID X9. Hold the mismatch and resolve identity/booking; do not edit the name to make it look matched. Changed example: P2 checks in then is removed before boarding. Remove P2 and associated baggage from the current flight through the approved reconciliation, revise the manifest and keep the person at the departure location in the tracking record.

## Q93-4 — Weights, units and reliable source data

Use the operator-approved weighing and recording method, verified scales and required inclusion basis. Passenger weight may require specified clothing/equipment treatment; do not add or omit allowances from memory. Baggage and freight must be identified and recorded as required. A guessed weight or yesterday's number is not a current verified measurement unless a specific approved method permits its use. If a scale fails or its status is uncertain, hold affected data and obtain the approved alternative.

Label units on every input and total. Never treat pounds as kilograms or convert twice. For classroom arithmetic, 1 pound=0.45359237 kilogram. A 44 pound item is 19.95806428 kilograms, about 19.96; retain the operator's required precision and rounding method. Do not round a value downward to satisfy a limit. The administrator provides accurate inputs; aircraft mass, balance and performance approval remain with authorized personnel.

Worked example: P1=82 kg, P2=76 kg, P3=91 kg total 249 kg. Bags 8+7+10=25 kg and freight 24 kg give 298 kg recorded payload inputs. This is not an aircraft load-sheet approval and excludes aircraft/fuel figures. Changed example: P2 and 7 kg bag removed: 298−83=215 kg. Update identities and bag count as well as the total; arithmetic alone does not reconcile the movement.

## Q93-5 — Manifest construction, revision and cross-check

Create a legible manifest for the correct sector using the operator-required fields. Preserve passenger names/identities, company, destination, weights, baggage counts/weights and freight details, plus aircraft/flight/date/revision information as applicable. Check totals independently and reconcile actual people/items with the document. A template's green indicator is not evidence that wrong source data became correct.

Control revisions. Late additions, removals, weight corrections or routing changes require a revised manifest and communication/acceptance through the approved process. Mark superseded copies and ensure the responsible ground record and flight crew have the current version. Do not allow an old printed copy to remain the boarding reference after a screen change. Record who prepared, checked, transmitted and acknowledged the revision.

Worked example: manifest v 1 contains P1, P2 and P3. P2 is removed and P4 added. Version v 2 must replace the relevant identity, weight, baggage and totals, and be reconciled before movement. Changed example: printer fails after v 2 is saved. Use the approved manual/backup process with the same current data and revision; do not distribute stale v 1 because it is the only printed page. A saved draft is not proof that the crew received or accepted it.

## Q93-6 — Baggage, freight and routing controls

Associate bags and freight with the correct person or consignment, sector and destination. Identify item counts, weights, contents/description and special handling requirements under the operator's procedure. Secure control after weighing so additions cannot silently change the recorded load. Bulky, heavy, fragile or unusual items need the specified review; do not force them into a normal baggage category to avoid approval.

Aircraft compartment, floor-loading, stowage and restraint constraints are specific. The administrator passes accurate details and obtains required acceptance; the authorized crew/loading personnel decide actual loading. A total below a general allowance does not prove each item or compartment is acceptable. Unexpected freight on arrival must be reconciled with the destination record and reported, not simply left for another flight.

Worked example: two bags tagged Alpha and one tagged Bravo are loaded for a multi-sector route. At Alpha, the records and actual offload must agree so Bravo's bag continues with its passenger. Changed example: a toolbox is added after weighing and sealing. Reweigh/reconcile and revise before acceptance; the original weight is no longer a reliable input.

## Q93-7 — Dangerous goods recognition and escalation

The administrator must recognize possible undeclared dangerous goods and stay within current function-specific training. Batteries, power banks, aerosols, gas cartridges, chemicals, tools containing fuel and other everyday items can require restrictions or special handling. Ask the approved questions and use current operator instructions. A passenger saying it is ordinary equipment does not settle classification or carriage permission.

Do not use a generic list from this course as dangerous-goods acceptance authority. Hold uncertain items in the operator-approved safe process and refer to the competent authorized person. Record identification, decision and required communication without improvising packaging or opening a suspicious leaking item. Passenger baggage provisions and cargo dangerous-goods requirements differ; moving an item between categories does not evade the rules.

Worked example: a passenger presents an unmarked power bank and cannot provide required identification. Hold/escalate under the operator's process; do not estimate capacity from size. Changed example: a damaged battery is discovered. Keep people safe and activate the operator's specific damaged-battery/emergency procedure rather than simply moving it to another bag. Current rules and operator restrictions may be stricter than general passenger guidance.

## Q93-8 — Passenger briefing and helideck interface

Confirm that the required operator-approved briefing has been delivered and understood, including the applicable travel equipment and movement arrangements. Administrative staff do not invent aircraft emergency instructions. Record the briefing status and unresolved questions. People unfamiliar with the site or needing assistance may need an appropriate explanation or arrangement before movement; do not equate attendance with understanding.

Coordinate final identity/headcount and baggage/freight reconciliation with the HLO and flight crew. Pass current manifest and special information through the approved interface. Passengers move only when the responsible team/crew authorizes and controls them. The administrator does not wave people onto a live helideck to save time. A discrepancy at the final check is a hold and correction, not a reason to change the count without checking identities.

Worked example: manifest says four people but only three are present at the controlled departure point. Resolve the missing person and associated bag before boarding; do not assume the fourth is already aboard. Changed example: four people are present, but one is a replacement not on the list. Count matches while identity does not; revise and obtain acceptance through the actual process.

## Q93-9 — Tracking system, manual fallback and data integrity

Personnel tracking supports accountability only when status changes are tied to actual verified events. Distinguish booked, checked in, boarded, departed, arrived and received at site according to the system's defined states. Do not mark someone arrived because a flight was expected. Use unique identities and preserve correction history. Permissions should match the role and personal data should be available only to authorized users.

Know the approved outage process before it is needed. Keep controlled manual records with date, sector, revision, source, preparer/checker and acknowledgments. When service returns, reconcile against actual events and prevent duplicate entries. A successful upload does not prove the values are correct. Resolve conflicts with the responsible operational owner and retain the manual evidence according to policy.

Worked example: the system is unavailable after flight departure. Record the verified departure and manifest revision manually; do not guess arrival. Changed example: the system later shows the same person on two sites because an earlier update was retried. Compare actual movement evidence, correct through the authorized process and retain the audit trail rather than delete whichever record is inconvenient.

## Q93-10 — Disruption, emergency information and handover

Delays, diversions, returned flights and emergencies require accurate status and disciplined communication. Report the latest verified flight/sector, people/items, revision, times and known destination/status. Do not announce an unconfirmed safe arrival or casualty information. Follow the operator/site emergency notification plan and provide authorized responders with current records promptly. An administrator supports response information, not air traffic control or rescue command.

At shift handover, state active flights, latest manifest versions, pending approvals, unresolved discrepancies, manual records and required follow-up. Confirm receipt. Preserve records after a disruption so investigators and accountable managers can reconstruct events. Do not overwrite estimates with fabricated actual times to make the system close the flight.

Worked example: flight is diverted and arrival at planned Alpha is not confirmed. Keep Alpha arrival unconfirmed and update the actual diversion information through authorized channels. Changed example: replacement administrator receives v 1 while v 2 is current. Correct the handover with v 2 and acknowledgment before the next action; the existence of a handover note does not make its contents current.

## Q93-11 — Integrated initial performance and workplace development

The learner must complete a full administrative cycle with realistic changes: booking review, identity and eligibility checks, weights, manifest, freight/DG query, briefing interface, late amendment, manual fallback and final tracking/handover. Each decision must remain traceable to a source and authorized acceptance. A correct total cannot offset an unmanifested person or unsupported dangerous-goods release.

Initial training uses synthetic records and controlled simulations. Workplace competence requires later supervised practice and assessment in the actual system/operator context. Record what the learner performed independently, what required coaching and what remains outside scope. Do not issue an external qualification or independent workplace authorization from this original course alone.

Worked example: learner calculates all weights correctly but uses obsolete v 1 after a late change. Remediate revision distribution and assess a changed case. Changed example: learner holds the flight paperwork because a DG question is unresolved despite schedule pressure. That is correct administrative control, not failure to keep the helicopter moving. The final award records initial training and its limits; employer appointment remains separate.

## I93 integrated initial assessment

Candidate completes cycleA through both sectors and cycle B through diversion/recovery. Controller releases changes only after learner records the current state; do not supply corrected totals. Mock crew/HLO must require current revision and resolve holds before acknowledging. Candidate prepares identity checks, weight worksheet, manifest revisions, change/distribution log, DGquery record, briefing/headcount interface, tracking events and shift handover. No actual software implementation is required; paper ledger records the same control transitions.

Eleven dimensions correspond to P93-1 through P93-11. Score 0 = absent or unsafe; 1 = major coaching; 2 = safe but incomplete or prompted; 3 = independently acceptable; 4 = accurate, independent and explained. Require at least 36/44, at least 3 in every dimension, both cycles and every station passed, and no critical error. Critical errors include knowingly accepting an unmanifested or wrong person, passing guessed weight as verified, unsupported dangerous-goods release, knowingly using an obsolete manifest, unauthorized boarding or dispatch, a false arrival, or fabricated evidence. Stop and retain the failed result; remediate and assess a changed case. A numeric pass never waives a mandatory control.

## Coverage and assessment trace

Each Q93 chapter has two explained formative checks and one separate three-decision final case plus a changed retake. P93 stations match all eleven chapter domains. W93-1 covers sectors/identity, W93-2 weight inputs, W93-3 revisions/fallback, W93-4 freight/DG/boarding and W93-5 disruption/recovery. Integrated I93 requires two complete changed cycles. F93 records operator adaptation, manifest, revisions, events, handover and remediation. Assessment banks and answer keys stay in the protected instructor directory.

## Limits and maintenance

All people, identifiers, masses, timings and scenarios are synthetic original teaching material. No source text is reproduced as a substitute for licensed specifications. Recheck current operator procedures, approved forms, function-specific DG prerequisites, tracking-system instruction and applicable aviation rules before delivery. Record the revision used. Initial instruction does not establish workplace competence, dispatch authority, flight loading approval or live-deck authority. Revisions that change required controls trigger instructor and assessment review; preserve earlier learner evidence and assess material gaps.
