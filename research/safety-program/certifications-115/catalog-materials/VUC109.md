# VUC109 — Cathodic Protection Technologist

Original VNDRLY/U applied analysis and design-calculation training. External CP3 certification and engineering/task authority remain separate.

## Q109-01 — Basis, competence and design control

A technologist must know why a design input is credible, which calculation applies and where the result stops being valid. Start a basis document with asset geometry, materials, coating, environment, continuity, design life, operating constraints, applicable standards and owner acceptance requirements. Identify existing and foreign CP systems, electrical safety interfaces and future connections. Separate measured facts, owner-supplied assumptions and unresolved information. An attractive calculation cannot compensate for the wrong structure boundary.

This course develops original basic-to-intermediate calculation and investigation skills. Complex, critical, mixed-material, high-risk or unfamiliar structures require specialist oversight and any legally required engineering authority. Learning an impressed-current calculation does not authorize independently approving every impressed-current design. Technical supervision includes reviewing procedures and evidence from testers/technicians, not merely assigning their shifts.

Worked decision: an owner requests a single galvanic design for a coated pipe but the drawing shows a bonded tank and an unknown foreign connection. Hold the single-structure assumption. Obtain verified continuity/topology and revise the demand boundary before selecting anodes. Record who may approve assumptions and release drawings. Maintain revision control through procurement, installation and commissioning; a change in coating, material or bond can invalidate the approved basis.

## Q109-02 — Polarization, corrosion and environmental effects

Use electrochemical theory to explain field response, not to decorate a report. Activation-controlled reaction behavior can produce a linear potential-versus-log-current region; concentration limits, passive films, mixed reactions and uncompensated resistance can invalidate a simple fit. On a supplied ideal Tafel branch with slope 0.120 V per decade, a tenfold current change corresponds to 0.120 V; doubling corresponds to 0.03612 V because log 10(2)=0.3010. Do not extrapolate across a transport-limited plateau or different reaction regime.

Nernst equilibrium shifts depend on reaction quotient and temperature; Faraday mass estimates depend on reaction, current, time and efficiency. Neither alone predicts localized damage in a complex field structure. Changes in oxygen, temperature, flow, deposits and chemistry alter polarization and current demand. Amphoteric metals and high-strength materials may be harmed by excessive polarization or local chemistry; more negative is not always better.

Worked evaluation: a current increase produces little additional qualified polarization but greater ON voltage shift. Investigate resistive drop, distribution, shielding and reaction limitations instead of repeatedly increasing output. Distinguish a protected accessible surface from a shielded interface. For reinforced concrete and prestressed systems, seek the applicable specialist criteria and structural constraints. A steel pipeline criterion cannot be transplanted without justification.

## Q109-03 — Data qualification and criteria

A design survey should establish valid potential, resistivity, current, coating and continuity evidence over representative conditions. Check reference chemistry, comparison, temperature, contact, meter loading, sampling time, source synchronization and location. Quantify uncertainty where practical and explain missing coverage. Retain raw data and transformations. A plotted smooth line can hide skipped points or reference changes; review the acquisition record before interpreting the graph.

Select criteria from the applicable asset standard and owner requirements with qualified review. ON values may contain significant IR contributions; interruption must address influential sources and sampling behavior. Polarization decay needs a justified depolarized baseline. A coupon or probe provides local evidence with defined geometry and limitations, not a guarantee for every surface.

Fictional criterion C109 accepts qualified steel OFF values from-0.85 through-1.10 V on its stated reference scale. Values-0.90,-0.88,-0.82,-0.92 V contain a local failure even though the average is-0.88 V. If the-0.82 point has uncertain timing, first qualify/repeat it; do not claim either definitive acceptance or a unique cause from invalid data. Report criteria separately from assumptions and measurement limitations. The original training window is not a universal standard.

## Q109-04 — Geometry, coating breakdown and current demand

Calculate exposed surface from the actual geometry and include fittings, ends or attached structures when relevant. For a cylindrical lateral surface A=pi D L. Multiply area by a justified exposed fraction and current density to obtain current demand, ensuring density is expressed per exposed area when that is its definition. Some specifications express demand per total coated area; mixing those conventions applies coating reduction twice.

Original model: D=0.30 m, L=1000 m, lateral A=942.48 m 2. End-of-life exposed fraction 0.02 and exposed-area density 0.020 A/m 2 give 0.37699 A. A supplied planning factor 1.25 yields 0.47124 A. This excludes ends/fittings by the exercise definition. If the exposed fraction becomes 0.05, the same calculation gives 1.17810 A after factor. This sensitivity is more informative than reporting excessive decimals on an uncertain coating assumption.

Define initial, mean and final demand separately. Mean demand can govern capacity consumption while final demand can govern output; using mean demand for both can under-size the system. Document evidence for coating degradation, environmental variation and maintenance assumptions. Do not silently multiply unrelated conservatism factors: explain what each covers, prevent double-counting and seek review when uncertainty dominates the result.

## Q109-05 — Galvanic output and anode capacity

A galvanic anode must satisfy both instantaneous output and useful life. A simplified output model is I=driving voltage/total circuit resistance. Resistance includes the anode environment and relevant circuit paths; polarization changes the effective driving voltage. Capacity is charge available per mass under specified alloy/environment conditions. If capacity is already an effective rated value, do not apply current efficiency again unless the definition requires it.

For an original supplied effective capacity 1100 Ah/kg, usable fraction 0.80, mean demand 0.40 A and 10 years at 8760 h/year, required mass is 0.40×8760×10/(1100×0.80)=39.82 kg. A20 kg unit provides 17600 Ah usable; two meet the mean-life requirement in this ideal model. But each unit at 0.70 V driving difference and 3.0 ohms supplies only 0.2333 A under the supplied independent-anode approximation. For final demand 0.80 A, four are needed by output. Four 20 kg units have capacity life 20.09 years at 0.40 A, but that does not prove distribution, utilization or actual environment.

Never assume closely spaced anodes act independently: mutual resistance and geometry affect output. Check installation constraints, spacing, lead resistance, product performance, utilization and end-of-life geometry. A mass-only answer is incomplete; a current-only answer may exhaust early. Procurement must match the approved alloy and performance basis, not simply nominal weight.

## Q109-06 — Resistance, distribution and impressed-current calculations

Resistance models are approximations tied to geometry, boundary conditions and soil assumptions. For a supplied long vertical-electrode model in homogeneous soil, R = rho/(2 pi L) × [ln(8L/d)-1]. It assumes appropriate slender geometry and isolated placement; use the actual approved design method for real installations. With rho 50 ohm-m, L3 m and d 0.10 m, the model gives 11.89 ohms. Layering, backfill, nearby metal and multiple anodes can change actual performance substantially.

A simplified impressed-current voltage requirement sums current times circuit resistance, back voltage and other specified drops, then applies the stated design allowance. For I5 A, R4 ohms, back voltage 2 V and lead drop 1 V, the base is 23 V; a supplied 20% voltage allowance gives 27.6 V. Select equipment only after ratings, environment, control range, current capacity, safety and design approval are checked. Do not treat this arithmetic as a procurement specification.

Distribution matters as much as total current. A nearby surface can be overpolarized while a remote point remains weak. Investigate anode placement, coating variation, structure resistance, current paths and shielding. Raising output may worsen the imbalance. A long-line attenuation model can describe how potential/current response decays with distance, but its constants require evidence and its boundary conditions matter. A uniform-soil model fitted to one point is not a validated whole-system prediction.

## Q109-07 — Interference, isolation and mitigation evaluation

Build a circuit-level explanation of suspected interference using synchronized source states, structure potentials, current directions and physical topology. Include foreign owners, AC exposure, safety grounding and protective devices. Static and dynamic DC effects require different observation periods; short sampling can miss the worst state. Distinguish induction, earth conduction and capacitive coupling when evaluating AC concerns.

Mitigation choices may include source configuration, isolation correction, coordinated bonds, distributed anodes or specialist AC measures. Each changes current paths and can create a new problem. A controlled trial needs approved limits, monitoring points, authority, rollback and post-change verification across all affected systems. Never use an unapproved bond as a diagnostic convenience.

Original trial: source-on shifts foreign pointA by-0.20 V and pointB by+0.15 V. A proposed bond improvesB but drives another pointC beyond the supplied negative limit. The trial has not solved the system problem; report the tradeoff and restore or revise only under the approved plan. A satisfactory DC potential does not establish touch safety or AC corrosion control. For a high-consequence crossing with uncertain topology, specialist review is part of competent practice rather than a failure to finish arithmetic.

## Q109-08 — Design alternatives and lifecycle decisions

Compare alternatives on technical feasibility before cost. Each option must meet demand, distribution, life, access, safety, environment and maintenance requirements. Document power availability, outage behavior, monitoring, anode replacement access and ownership responsibilities. A cheaper installation that cannot be safely inspected is not equivalent to a maintainable design.

For an original economic example at 5% discount rate over 10 years, present-worth factor for an annual cost is [1-(1.05)^-10]/0.05=7.7217. OptionG costs 12000 initially plus 500 annually: present worth 15860.87. OptionI costs 8000 initially plus 1100 annually: 16493.91. G is lower by 633.04 in this narrow model. These values exclude replacements, energy escalation, downtime, risk and residual value, so they do not determine a real procurement decision. Show sensitivity rather than hiding omitted costs.

Use a requirements matrix to compare initial/final output, capacity, monitoring, interference, access and environmental constraints. Reject alternatives that fail a mandatory condition even if their cost is attractive. Record assumptions and approval owner. If both options need unresolved specialist analysis, the correct deliverable is a conditional comparison with specific missing evidence, not a false final selection.

## Q109-09 — Installation, commissioning and acceptance

Translate the approved basis into drawings, schedules, material specifications, inspection hold points and commissioning tests. Identify polarity, lead labels, bonds, isolators, anode locations and reference points. Before concealment, verify the installed configuration, connections, coating repairs and as-built deviations. Unapproved substitutions require engineering disposition; visual similarity is not equivalence.

Commissioning should verify continuity/isolation, equipment calibration, polarity, output control, representative potentials, interference and restoration of safety functions. Record baseline conditions and the final approved settings. Allow appropriate stabilization and document timing. Check initial and anticipated operating states; a single favorable measurement at one source setting is not enough.

Worked release: all near points meet C109, the far point is-0.80 V and one foreign line has no owner response. Do not issue unconditional acceptance. Identify the unresolved performance and coordination items, maintain the approved safe state, and assign corrective action and retest. A punch list can distinguish documentation defects from performance defects, but it cannot waive a mandatory protection criterion. Handover must include operating limits, inspection schedule, alarm response, drawings and who has authority to change settings.

## Q109-10 — Troubleshooting, supervision and technical report

Use hypotheses and discriminating tests. A current decrease may reflect an open connection, higher ground resistance, coating change, power fault or instrument error. Determine which observations distinguish those possibilities before ordering repair. Review trends against weather, maintenance, topology and operating state. Avoid changing multiple variables at once without a plan, since the cause of improvement then becomes unclear.

Technical oversight includes checking survey procedure suitability, observing a sample of field work, reviewing anomalous results and verifying calculations independently. A spreadsheet should expose formulas, units, source inputs, assumptions and revision history. Test it with known examples and edge cases; a correct total with incorrect units is not acceptable. Separate learner calculations from controlled design approval.

A defensible report contains purpose, scope, basis, field methods, data quality, calculations, alternatives, uncertainty, conclusions, recommendations and unresolved actions. Include enough raw data and references for another competent person to reproduce key results. State what is outside scope. Where a high-risk design exceeds competence, provide a precise specialist referral with the work already completed. Do not fabricate experience or claim an external credential from this training. Final authoring completion means these learning resources are ready; actual learners must still meet all assessment requirements.

# VUC109 applied workbook and delivery

## Entry and resources

Require demonstrated technician foundations: safe measurements, reference conversion, survey validity, circuit calculations and interference interpretation. Diagnose algebra, logarithms, geometry and units before advanced work. Original 56-hour plan: 16 instruction, 16 calculation workshops, 12 integrated design/investigation, 6 observed technical defense, 3 controlled final and 3 feedback/remediation. Additional remediation is not waived to meet the schedule.

Instructor/assessor must be competent in CP analysis and the supplied design models, with specialist/engineering support for questions outside scope. All designs are synthetic and must never be issued for construction. Use calculators or transparent spreadsheets, plotted datasets, inert installation model, safe low-voltage survey demonstration, drawings and forms. Four practical stations involve data acquisition/setup or model inspection; six are observed calculation/technical-review stations. Verify learner input/formula independence rather than accepting a copied spreadsheet. Record accommodations without changing technical decision criteria.

C109 fictional qualified OFF window:-1.10 to-0.85 V inclusive on a single specified reference. R109 calculations use 8760 hours/year, stated effective capacity and utilization, independent anodes only where explicitly assumed. No coefficient is a universal design value. Real geometry, soil, product, owner criteria and specialist authority govern field work.

## Worked applications

### W109-01 — Basis and data validity

Drawing A shows 0.30 m pipe 1000 m long; exercise excludes ends/fittings. The owner initially calls it isolated. Inject at 10 minutes: a bonded vessel appears but area/condition unknown. Learner must hold the original boundary and list required topology/area verification. Dataset OFF -0.90/-0.88/-0.82/-0.92 V; average-0.88 does not pass every point. Inject uncertain timing at the weak point, then a validated repeat-0.81 V. Expect qualification followed by a valid local failure and investigation, not arbitrary averaging. Demonstrate safe acquisition/reference checks on a simulator before analyzing the dataset.

### W109-02 — Demand and sensitivity

After owner clarification, use pipe lateral area only: pi×0.30×1000=942.48 m 2. Exposed fraction 0.02, density 0.020 A/m 2 exposed area, factor 1.25: 0.47124 A. Changed fraction 0.05 yields 1.17810 A. Learner labels input source, area convention, factor purpose and difference 0.70686 A. Instructor introduces a spreadsheet that multiplies fraction twice; learner repairs and retains revision history. Changed geometry 0.20 m×800 m, fraction 0.04, density 0.020, factor 1.25 yields 0.50265 A. Do not round assumptions into certainty.

### W109-03 — Anode output and life

Effective capacity 1100 Ah/kg, utilization 0.80, mean 0.40 A, 10 years, 20 kg units. Required 39.82 kg; two units meet ideal capacity. Driving difference 0.70 V and 3 ohm per-unit model gives 0.2333 A each; final 0.80 A needs four. Selected 80 kg gives 20.09 years ideal mean-load capacity. Inject anode crowding: independent-output assumption no longer established; learner cannot approve four solely from arithmetic. Changed case mean 0.30 A, 10 years, 1000 Ah/kg, 0.80 use, 20 kg units, 0.20 A/unit, final 0.70 A: 32.85 kg and four units by output. Learner documents both limiting checks and required interaction/distribution verification.

### W109-04 — Resistance, supply and alternatives

Supplied electrode model rho 50 ohm-m, L3 m, d 0.10 m gives 11.89 ohms. Learner states homogeneous/slender/isolated assumptions. Separate supply example 5 A, 4 ohms, 2 V back, 1 V lead, 20% allowance gives 27.6 V. These are unrelated teaching submodels; do not combine the 11.89 ohm electrode with the 4 ohm circuit without a justified network. Economic example 10 years/5%, factor 7.721735: G12000+500/year=15860.87; I8000+1100/year=16493.91. G lower 633.04 under stated omissions. Instructor adds inaccessible replacement location toG: learner returns to feasibility rather than selecting by cost alone.

### W109-05 — Integrated design defense and commissioning

Case A: independently produce basis, demand, anode count/life, alternatives and commissioning plan using W02/W03. At minute 15 unknown bond appears; at 30 owner supplies verified isolated scope; at 45 anode-spacing constraint invalidates independent-output assumption; at 60 commissioning dataset near-0.94/far-0.80 V and foreign shift+0.15 V. Expected: successive holds/revisions, no construction release from model alone, local/foreign investigation and explicit final unresolved actions.

Case B: pipe 0.40 m×500 m, fraction 0.03, density 0.015, factor 1.20; demand 0.33929 A. Separate approved exercise mean 0.50 A, life 15 years, capacity 1000, use 0.85, unit 25 kg, output 0.30 A/unit, final 1.10 A yields 77.29 kg and four units. Qualified commissioning near-0.91/far-0.86 V initially meetsC109; inject unapproved anode substitution and lost safety-grounding record. Learner must withhold unconditional acceptance despite favorable potentials. Changed defense uses near-1.12/far-0.86 and a resolved substitution: performance still fails. Assessor asks learner to explain every formula, assumption and authority boundary without coaching.
