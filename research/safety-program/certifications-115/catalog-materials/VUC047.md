# VNDRLY/U Corrosion Protection — Basic Coating Inspection

VUC047 · Original learning materials · 2026-11-18

## Program purpose

Prepare an entry-level inspector to observe, measure, document and escalate coating work under an approved inspection plan and appropriate supervision. The learner follows a steel coating job from document review through surface preparation, application, cure, inspection and handover. Supplied fictional specification S47 makes every classroom decision answerable without pretending to reproduce a proprietary standard. Actual production work requires its own applicable specifications, current product instructions and authorized personnel.

This program awards VNDRLY/U Basic Coating Inspection Preparation after the knowledge and observed practical requirements are met. It does not issue AMPP certification, qualify a person to approve coating-system design or replace employer safety training. AMPP has a separate certification route involving its course, theory and practical examinations, ethics and conduct requirements. Its current public guide describes supervised entry-level inspection.[S01,S02] No proprietary course manual or full current ASTM/ISO/SSPC equivalence is claimed.

## Q01 — Inspection responsibility and the protection system

A coating is part of a system: substrate, preparation, primer, subsequent layers, application conditions and service exposure. An attractive topcoat can conceal contaminated steel or an incompatible underlying layer. Inspection therefore records the work while evidence is accessible, rather than relying only on the finished appearance.

Identify who applies the coating, who performs contractor quality control, who witnesses or audits, and who can authorize departures. The basic inspector reports against the assigned plan; the owner or designated authority makes decisions outside that delegation. Quality control checks the work. Quality assurance examines whether the system of controls is suitable and followed. Neither permits an inspector to invent acceptance criteria.

Begin each shift by confirming component identity, work stage, governing revisions and hold points. End by recording what remains uninspected. Do not mark an entire tank complete because accessible shell measurements passed.

**Worked:** the contractor requests signature for internal lining based on external paint readings. Decline the unsupported scope and identify the required internal inspection. **Changed:** internal inspection was performed, but on a different tank; traceability still prevents release.

## Q02 — Corrosion and what coatings can control

Electrochemical corrosion involves anodic metal loss, a cathodic reaction, an electrical path and an electrolyte. Conditions that alter wetness, oxygen access, chemistry or temperature can change attack. General thinning and localized attack can have very different consequences even when total metal loss is similar. A small pit is not harmless because the surrounding surface looks sound.

Coatings can isolate the surface from the environment; some systems also inhibit reactions or provide sacrificial protection. A defect can concentrate attack. Galvanic interaction requires attention to the connected materials, electrolyte and exposed areas; coating only one member without design review can change the risk. Mill scale is not interchangeable with a specified prepared substrate and can detach or create problematic local conditions.

Inspection cannot restore lost structural capacity. Record corrosion exposed during preparation and refer significant or unexpected metal loss to the responsible authority before coating hides it. Coating selection must consider actual immersion, chemical, temperature and atmospheric exposure.

**Worked:** localized deep pits become visible after cleaning. Record location and hold for assessment rather than filling them invisibly with paint. **Changed:** only discoloration is observed; document the observation without inventing a depth measurement or mechanism.

## Q03 — Safety before measurement

Blasting and coating work can expose inspectors to dust, hazardous old coatings, noise, moving abrasive, high pressure, solvents and restricted access. Review the task risk assessment and SDS before entering the work area. Determine the actual controls and boundaries, not just the PPE worn by nearby workers. Inspection does not authorize confined-space entry, respirator use without its required program, energized work or access beneath suspended loads.[S03]

Keep clear of blasting and spraying exclusion areas. Do not inspect an operating nozzle or use a hand to investigate an airless injection injury. A suspected injection injury requires urgent medical response under the site emergency plan even when the external wound appears small. Check access, lighting, ventilation and emergency communications again when the work location changes.

For this course, practical work uses clean prepared panels, safe water-based surrogate liquids and approved low-energy instruments. No live blasting, production spraying, solvent exposure or high-voltage holiday operation is required. A learner who stops an unsafe station has made the correct decision; the instructor fixes the setup before resuming.

**Worked:** a requested salt test is inside an unassessed tank. Hold outside and obtain the required safe-entry arrangements. **Changed:** the tank is open but adjacent spraying creates an exposure; access is still not automatically safe.

## Q04 — Documents, pre-job meeting and hold points

Read the coating specification together with the inspection and test plan, drawings, current product data/application sheets and SDS. The specification defines project requirements; product instructions constrain safe, effective use. Resolve conflicts through the authorized route before work. A purchase order saying “epoxy” does not define a coating system, preparation grade, thickness or service-cure requirement.

At the pre-job meeting identify zones, access, sequence, inspection frequency, notification lead times, hold/witness points, sample locations, repair controls and reporting contacts. A hold point requires its defined release before the next stage. A witness point follows the contract's actual notification and attendance provisions; it is not automatically equivalent to a hold.

Use a revision register. When an approved change arrives, identify affected work and instruments, brief the team and preserve the prior records. Do not quietly replace an old limit on yesterday's signed report. The practical S47 specification is a training document only and must never be issued as a production coating instruction.

**Worked:** blasting is finished, but the required preparation hold has not been released. Painting waits even if the applicator is ready. **Changed:** a witness did not attend; consult the actual witness clause instead of assuming permission or prohibition.

## Q05 — Measurement integrity and units

Calibration establishes an instrument's relationship to reference values under defined conditions. Verification checks whether its performance remains suitable. Adjustment changes its response. These are different actions. A calibration certificate does not eliminate field checks after damage, a probe change or changed measurement conditions. Use the actual manufacturer's method and a reference appropriate to the range and substrate.[S04]

Record instrument/probe ID, reference ID/value, before-and-after results, units and relevant configuration. If a post-check fails, identify the readings since the last valid check and evaluate them; simply adjusting the device does not repair historical evidence. Avoid repeated readings until a favorable value appears.

One mil is 25.4 micrometres. A thickness of 4 mil is 101.6 µm, not 4 mm. Preserve enough resolution to make the decision; do not round an out-of-limit result into acceptance. When an uncertainty interval crosses a specified decision boundary, use the governing decision rule or seek disposition rather than claiming certainty.

**Worked:** reference 100 µm reads 107 with allowed training error ±3. The error is +7, outside the check. **Changed:** it reads 102; the check passes, but it proves neither every location's thickness nor coating adhesion.

## Q06 — Environmental conditions

Measure air and substrate temperatures, relative humidity and dew point with suitable instruments. The substrate can differ from the surrounding air, especially in shade, at dawn or beside a heat source. Condensation risk depends on the substrate relative to dew point. Record the actual spread, Ts−Td, as well as the individual readings.[S05]

Use the project and product limits, including application and curing conditions. A familiar dew-point margin is not a universal permission to paint. Check the coldest relevant locations, not only the warm convenient plate. Recheck at the planned frequency and when weather, enclosure, ventilation or work location changes. Instrument stabilization and sensor cleanliness matter.

S47 requires a spread of at least 3°C and RH no more than 85%, plus its temperature range. At Ts 18°C and Td 16°C the spread is 2°C and application is held. A dry-looking surface does not override the failed requirement. Conditions acceptable during application do not prove acceptable conditions through cure.

**Changed:** Ts 22°C, Td 18°C and RH 88% gives a passing spread but failing humidity. Evaluate every required variable independently.

## Q07 — Precleaning and substrate defects

Examine substrate identity and condition before preparation. Oil, grease, salts, weld spatter, sharp edges, laminations and fabrication defects require different responses. Blasting alone is not a reliable substitute for specified degreasing; contamination can be spread or embedded. Use the approved cleaning process and control dirty rags, recycled liquid and subsequent handling.

Separate fabrication correction from cleaning. An inspector may identify a sharp edge needing treatment under the specification, but cannot authorize grinding away structurally significant material. Record weld and edge conditions before coating. Compare the condition to actual project documents rather than an imagined universal edge radius.

After cleaning, prevent recontamination from hands, oily compressed air, nearby work or weather. A previously accepted surface may require reinspection after exposure. Masking must protect required surfaces without concealing areas needing inspection.

**Worked:** a clean-looking panel has visible oily fingerprints. Return it for approved cleaning and reinspection. **Changed:** the fingerprints are gone but the solvent-wipe record is missing; obtain real evidence, not a backdated signature.

## Q08 — Preparation methods and visual cleanliness

Hand tools, power tools, abrasive blasting and water cleaning remove material in different ways. Method choice affects remaining contamination, profile and production hazards. Visual cleanliness describes the surface condition achieved; anchor profile describes texture. Neither automatically proves the other. Use the specified written standard and suitable visual reference for the actual starting condition; a photograph alone may not resolve all acceptance requirements.

Abrasives differ in hardness, shape, size and contamination. Recycled media require process control. Nozzle wear can change air demand and performance. Straight-bore and venturi arrangements influence the stream; the inspector observes equipment condition and the approved setup rather than redesigning compressor capacity on site. Moisture or oil in compressed air can spoil an otherwise adequate preparation process.

Water cleaning can expose an existing profile without creating a new abrasive profile. Flash rust may appear afterwards; allowable condition is system-specific. Stop and obtain the specified assessment rather than painting over it because work is behind schedule.

**Worked:** the visual grade is acceptable but measured profile is below the required range. Preparation is not fully accepted. **Changed:** profile passes but residual contamination remains; the result is still incomplete.

## Q09 — Surface profile measurement

Select an appropriate profile method for the surface and required parameter. Comparators, depth gauges and replica tape do not necessarily report identical quantities. Follow the instrument and consumable instructions, including range, preparation, seating and number of readings. Do not combine unlike measurements as if they were interchangeable.

Replica tape captures an impression. Correct interpretation accounts for its backing and instrument mode; some readers apply corrections automatically. Subtracting a backing twice produces a false low result. Select the actual tape grade and current range rather than a remembered range from an older product.[S06] Preserve the impression and location when required.

Spread readings across the defined inspection area and include difficult geometry. A single favorable profile does not represent a whole component. If readings fall outside the tape's valid range, change the method/grade as instructed rather than extrapolating.

**Worked:** in a fictional manual-reading exercise, total impression thickness is 112 µm and the supplied backing is 50 µm; profile is 62 µm. **Changed:** the electronic display already says corrected profile 62 µm; report 62, not 12. These exercises do not override a real reader's processing instructions.

## Q10 — Salt, dust and other contamination

Visual examination cannot establish that soluble salts are absent. Extraction testing measures the material recovered from a defined area using a defined solution and method. Control the blank, extraction volume, area, cleanliness, recovery and instrument units. Conductivity is not a species-specific chloride result unless the method validly supports that claim.[S07]

Compare with the project limit for the stated method and units. A result in mg/m² cannot be compared directly to µg/cm² without conversion: 1 µg/cm² equals 10 mg/m². A leaking patch or incorrect volume can invalidate the measurement. Do not average an invalid test into a passing batch.

Dust assessment uses its specified method and rating basis, distinct from a salt extraction. Oil detection also requires the prescribed method; a water-break observation is not a complete chemical analysis. Following remedial cleaning, retest affected areas under the plan and document the new result alongside the original failure.

**Worked:** S47 limit 20 mg/m², valid result 26: hold and remediate. **Changed:** result 18 but patch leaked; the numerical value cannot establish acceptance.

## Q11 — Coating families and adhesion

Coatings include binder, pigments and other constituents that produce the intended film; liquid products may contain solvents or water. Organic systems include familiar polymer binders; inorganic systems use a different binder chemistry. Generic names such as epoxy, polyurethane, alkyd, acrylic or zinc silicate describe families, not interchangeable approved products.

Adhesion involves surface interactions and mechanical effects. A rough surface is not sufficient if contamination prevents effective contact. A coating may be a barrier, inhibitive system or sacrificial zinc-rich system; the mechanism depends on the actual formulation and installation. Primer, intermediate and finish layers have different roles and compatibility constraints.

Curing may rely predominantly on physical loss of volatile material or chemical reaction, including reactions between components or with the environment. Dry-to-touch is not proof of service cure. Follow the product's actual temperature, humidity, thickness and time requirements. A coating suitable outdoors is not automatically suitable for immersion or high-temperature duty.

**Worked:** two products are both called epoxy but only one is approved for the specified lining service. Substitution needs authorized technical approval. **Changed:** the same trade name has a different curing agent; identity and compatibility still require checking.

## Q12 — Storage, mixing and thinning

Verify product, component, batch, shelf-life and storage history before mixing. Check the approved ratio and whether it is by mass or volume. Ratios cannot be exchanged without valid density information. Inspect for damaged containers or abnormal material and obtain disposition rather than guessing that stirring will restore it.

Record component amounts, mixing start, method, induction requirement, pot-life clock and actual temperature. Induction is a required waiting period when specified; pot life limits usable time after mixing under stated conditions. Adding thinner does not reset a reaction clock. Use only permitted thinner and limits. Manufacturer application guidance illustrates why product-specific instructions are essential.[S08]

For fictional S47 ratio 4:1 by volume, a 5 L unthinned mix uses 4 L base and 1 L curing component. Adding 0.25 L permitted thinner to 5 L at 60% volume solids gives 3 L solids in 5.25 L, or 57.14% applied volume solids, assuming the supplied simple volume-addition model.

**Changed:** a batch mixed at 09:00 with two-hour pot life cannot be used at 11:15 merely because it still flows.

## Q13 — Application controls and edge coverage

Brush, roller and spray produce different access and film-build patterns. Inspect the approved application method, operator records, material condition and environmental controls. Airless spray uses pressurized material; conventional and plural-component arrangements have different equipment controls. Observing a pump ratio does not establish correct atomization, proportioning or delivered film.

Edges, welds, bolts and recesses can receive inadequate coverage while broad surfaces look uniform. Where specified, stripe coating addresses these difficult geometries; record its sequence and condition before it is hidden. Avoid treating a stripe coat as permission to exceed maximum local thickness or ignore intercoat cleanliness.

Check application while correction is possible. Runs, dry spray and missed regions suggest a need to review technique or conditions. The inspector reports the evidence and nonconformance; changes to material, pressure or thinning follow the authorized process. Do not reach into operating equipment to demonstrate a defect.

**Worked:** flat-panel WFT passes but the specified edge stripe is omitted. Hold the next stage until the requirement is resolved. **Changed:** stripe is present but contaminated before overcoating; presence alone is not sufficient.

## Q14 — Wet film and material quantity

Wet-film measurement provides timely application feedback. Use a clean suitable comb or wheel on the uncured film following its instructions. With a comb, report the interval bounded by the last wetted and next dry tooth; a coarse interval is not an exact value. Repair the disturbed spot as required. Curvature, roughness and technique can affect interpretation.[S09]

The simple estimate DFT=WFT×applied volume-solids fraction assumes the specified product model and no unusual losses or changes. To target 150 µm dry at 60% solids, estimated WFT is 250 µm. Thinning changes the applied fraction. Actual final DFT still needs measurement; the estimate is not acceptance evidence.

For planning, theoretical spreading rate in m²/L is 1000×solids fraction/DFT in µm. At 0.60 and 150 µm it is 4 m²/L. An 80 m² area needs 20 L theoretical mixed coating; at 80% utilization it needs 25 L. These calculations do not authorize extra thinning or replace the actual application allowance.

**Changed:** a 225–250 µm comb interval at 60% predicts 135–150 µm, not a definite 150 everywhere.

## Q15 — Dry-film instruments and substrate effects

Choose a thickness method suited to the coating and substrate. Magnetic methods on ferrous substrates and eddy-current methods on suitable conductive nonferrous substrates rely on different physical responses. A probe used outside its substrate or thickness range can provide misleading results. Nonmetallic substrates may require other approved methods.

Verify the instrument with suitable references and account for substrate geometry and roughness through the prescribed procedure. A smooth reference plate does not reproduce every blast-cleaned surface. Use the actual zero/adjustment or base-metal reading method required; do not invent a universal subtraction. Keep the probe perpendicular and stable where instructed, lift between readings and retain invalid-reading reasons.[S04]

Distinguish a single reading, a defined spot and an area evaluation. The governing sampling standard determines their treatment. S47 uses a fictional explicit rule for learning, not a claim to reproduce SSPC-PA 2 or ISO 19840.

**Worked:** total system thickness is 240 µm, but no primer record exists. It cannot prove the primer met its own thickness. **Changed:** substrate adjustment changes halfway through a batch; segment and investigate the affected records.

## Q16 — Sampling, uncertainty and thickness disposition

Plan measurement locations before selecting favorable readings. Represent the defined area and difficult features under the actual plan. Preserve raw readings, aggregation and location. A passing average can coexist with unacceptable local thin or thick areas. Averages do not fill inaccessible regions or erase outliers automatically.

S47 defines three readings per spot, each within 120–180 µm, and each spot mean within 135–165 µm. A spot 120,150,180 has mean 150 and passes these supplied conditions. A spot 110,155,185 also has mean 150 but fails two individual limits. This deliberate example teaches why an attractive average is insufficient.

If a decision rule includes uncertainty, apply it consistently. For a maximum 180 and measured 179±3, the interval extends to 182; an “entire interval inside” training rule cannot accept it. Do not silently substitute this rule for a contract's actual one. Map failures for authorized evaluation, additional measurements or repair.

**Changed:** five required spots were planned but only four are recorded; no calculation turns four into full coverage.

## Q17 — Cure, recoat and service readiness

Time requirements depend on actual product and conditions. Distinguish touch-dry, handling, minimum/maximum overcoat intervals and cure for service. Track substrate temperature history where it controls the interval. A warm afternoon reading does not prove the coating cured overnight at that temperature. Missing records require evaluation, not invented values.

Check intercoat cleanliness and the applicable window before adding another layer. Exceeding a maximum interval may require a specified treatment or technical disposition. Do not assume extra solvent or a thicker next coat repairs the issue. Amine blush, moisture and surface contamination require appropriate identification and approved treatment rather than concealment.

S47 at the stated constant training temperature allows recoat from 8 through 24 hours and service only after 72 hours. At 10 hours recoat may be timely if the other conditions pass; service is still premature. At 26 hours the ordinary recoat window is exceeded. This is fictional product data, not a generic epoxy rule.

**Changed:** the batch was cold for part of the interval; obtain the actual product-specific evaluation rather than applying the constant-temperature table blindly.

## Q18 — Visible defects and bounded diagnosis

Record what can actually be observed: runs, sags, curtains, pinholes, blisters, wrinkles, dry spray, cracking, discoloration or non-drying film. Describe location, extent, layer and conditions before assigning a cause. Similar appearances can result from different combinations of preparation, material, application and exposure.

Use systematic evidence: batch and mixing history, film readings, environmental records, surface tests, elapsed time and adjacent unaffected areas. A photograph with scale and location can preserve appearance, but cannot prove adhesion or chemical cure. Do not scrape or cut production coating without authorization when the test is destructive.

Separate diagnosis from disposition. The basic inspector can identify and report a nonconformance under assigned criteria; the approved repair method must address the actual problem. Overcoating a soft underlying film can conceal rather than solve it.

**Worked:** a soft patch coincides with an undocumented component ratio. Preserve the mixing record gap and request technical evaluation; do not assert a proven ratio error without evidence. **Changed:** ratio was correct but the temperature history is missing; investigate that uncertainty instead of retaining the first diagnosis.

## Q19 — Continuity and holiday inspection

A holiday is a discontinuity in a protective coating. Continuity testing requires a method suited to coating thickness, substrate, cure, conductivity and service requirements. Low-voltage wet-sponge and high-voltage methods are not interchangeable defaults. Check the approved test method, contact/return arrangement, coverage and response verification before interpreting a result.

High voltage creates hazards and can damage unsuitable coating. Voltage selection must come from the applicable procedure and manufacturer basis, not a remembered rule of thumb; published recommendations can change.[S10] This basic course uses a de-energized setup exercise and an approved low-voltage training panel, never live high-voltage operation. Production authorization remains separate.

Mark and map valid indications, control repairs and retest the affected area using the approved plan. A passing DFT result does not demonstrate continuity. Conversely, no detected holidays does not prove correct thickness, adhesion or cure.

**Worked:** a detector fails to respond at the training panel's known defect. A silent sweep is invalid evidence. **Changed:** verification passes, but the return connection is lost midway; identify and repeat the affected coverage after correction.

## Q20 — Adhesion and supplementary tests

Adhesion testing may damage the film and substrate. Select the specified method and obtain permission, location and repair arrangements before testing. Pull-off results depend on dolly preparation, adhesive, curing, alignment, apparatus and failure location. A number alone is incomplete: failure in the test adhesive may limit what can be concluded about the coating bond.

Cut/tape methods, hardness, solvent resistance and cure checks answer different questions. They cannot be substituted for one another merely because one is easier. Use the actual method, product-specific interpretation and competent supervision. Basic inspectors should recognize the evidence limits and report the failure mode rather than overstate a result.

In the training case a pull reaches 5 MPa and fails entirely in glue; the supplied rule requires a valid coating/substrate failure result of at least 6 MPa. The result does not establish that requirement. It also does not prove the coating bond itself fails at 5 MPa.

**Changed:** a valid result of 7 MPa meets that numerical requirement at that location, but does not prove untested areas or other inspection requirements.

## Q21 — Other substrates and metallic protection

Concrete has different moisture, porosity, alkalinity and surface-strength considerations from steel. A steel DFT probe cannot simply be pressed onto concrete for a valid reading. Galvanized and nonferrous surfaces also require compatible preparation and coating systems; overly aggressive preparation can remove useful metallic protection.

Hot-dip galvanizing creates a metallic coating through a separate process. Bare regions, deposits and appearance need the applicable galvanizing acceptance basis; a paint specification does not automatically apply. A duplex system combines metallic protection with an organic coating and requires compatibility and preparation controls. Thermal-spray metallic coatings likewise have their own process and inspection requirements.

This course develops recognition and referral rather than pretending one basic steel-paint procedure covers every substrate. Identify the substrate before choosing an instrument, cleaning method or acceptance rule. Obtain specialist instructions where the work exceeds the demonstrated scope.

**Worked:** a concrete floor is assigned the steel magnetic DFT method. Stop and obtain a valid method and moisture/preparation basis. **Changed:** the substrate is galvanized steel; a valid thickness reading still does not authorize aggressive blast preparation.

## Q22 — Records and nonconformance

A usable record identifies component/zone, time, work stage, document revisions, product/batch, conditions, instrument and reference checks, raw readings, locations, evaluation and person responsible. Separate observed facts from reports by others and from interpretation. Corrections retain the original entry and explain the change; electronic edits require equivalent traceability.

A nonconformance states the requirement, actual evidence, affected extent, immediate control and responsible disposition route. “Bad paint” is too vague. “Zone B, spot B3, reading110 µm against S47 minimum120” is actionable. Closure needs approved correction and verified reinspection, not just an email saying it was fixed.

Maintain daily reports, measurement records, nonconformance/repair records and final turnover documentation as required. Where marine coating technical files or other specialized formats apply, obtain the actual required structure; a generic daily report is not automatically equivalent. Retain job safety documents separately but link relevant inspection interruptions.

**Worked:** a supervisor requests replacing a failed reading with the repaired value. Keep both, with repair identity and retest date. **Changed:** the first value was a transcription mistake; correct transparently using the retained raw evidence.

## Q23 — Communication, conflict and ethics

Explain an inspection issue using requirement, evidence and consequence for the next work stage. Keep the discussion about the condition rather than the person's motives. Notify the responsible contractor and inspection authority through the plan. A basic inspector must not promise technical approval beyond delegated authority to settle a dispute.

Pressure to sign unseen work, conceal failures, backdate checks or accept gifts tied to results creates a conflict with truthful reporting. Decline unsupported attestations and preserve a factual record. Escalate through the established quality or ethics route. Do not retaliate against a learner or worker who identifies an unsafe or unverified condition.

If the owner requests a change, obtain the authorized written revision and review affected work. An informal request is not evidence that the original condition complied. Clear communication can preserve schedule by identifying the exact hold and the evidence needed to release it, rather than issuing a vague stop with no route forward.

**Worked:** “Sign now; the missing salt test will pass later.” Record the missing test and retain the hold. **Changed:** a valid later test passes after cleaning; record the later evidence without rewriting the earlier state.

## Q24 — Integrated inspection and handover

Close a job by reconciling all zones, stages, hold releases, product batches, environmental periods, thickness results, defects, repairs and outstanding conditions. Match final records to the as-coated asset. Look for gaps between stages: a preparation release may be valid, but a later rain event can require reinspection before coating.

Use a disposition table with accepted, rejected, pending and not-inspected states. Do not collapse “pending information” into “accepted.” A final report should state scope and limits prominently enough that a receiving operator cannot mistake a partial inspection for full service release. Include the person authorized to decide readiness and the evidence that supports that decision.

In I47, the learner integrates raw records, revises the inspection plan after changed weather and produces a bounded handover. The instructor evaluates reasoning and actual instrument work separately. Successful completion means the learner demonstrated the stated preparation outcomes; production appointment and external credentials remain separate decisions.

**Worked:** all local DFT readings pass, but one zone lacks cure history and another is inaccessible. Handover lists both unresolved zones. **Changed:** new valid evidence resolves the cure history only; the inaccessible zone remains unresolved.

# VUC047 delivery, practical assessment and credential rules

## Delivery and entry

Planned learning48 hours:12 instruction/demonstration,8 supplied-record labs,16 supervised instrument practice and independent stations,4 integrated dossier,4 remediation/rehearsal,4 controlled final. Extend practice when competence is not established. These are VNDRLY planning hours, not AMPP minimums or a guaranteed learning duration. Learners need basic arithmetic, unit conversion, safe-work literacy and ability to use the required instruments; diagnose with W02/W04/W05, teach gaps before assessment. Provide language and accessibility support without supplying technical answers. Meet actual applicable vision/workplace prerequisites separately.

Teach Q01–05 first, then environment/preparation, materials/application, finished-coating evaluation and reporting. Demonstrate each instrument using its actual manual before coached practice. Independent assessment uses changed locations/specimens and no coaching. Instructor must have demonstrated coating-inspection and instrument competence for the tasks taught. Course authorship does not appoint an AMPP examiner or authorize hazardous work.

## Facility and actual specimen preparation

Provide a safe bench area, identified smooth/blast-prepared coated steel panels, safe edge/defect exemplars, actual environmental meter/surface probe, profile instrument or replica kit with correct consumables, DFT gauge with appropriate traceable references, wet-film comb and water-based benign surrogate, clean extraction kit and known safe salt solution for method practice, dust-test materials, measuring vessels/timer and low-voltage continuity equipment approved for the training panel. Have current equipment instructions, safe-handling information and suitable PPE. No production spraying, blasting, confined-space entry or live high-voltage testing is required or authorized.

Assessor establishes and signs a private specimen map before assessment: substrate/geometry, instrument suitability, reference readings and uncertainty, known clean/contaminated areas, profile regions, thickness locations, intentional holiday and sound comparison. Independent learner locations must differ from coached examples. Never introduce a dangerous fault; use a fault card for unsafe setup conditions. The supplied S47 numerical case is not a substitute for known actual specimens. If actual equipment or reliable specimen truth is unavailable, record the practical as incomplete; paper answers alone cannot earn the full preparation award.

## Practical scoring

Each station has four explicit criteria, each0–2:2 independently correct action and evidence;1 safe but incomplete or prompted;0 missing, incorrect or unsafe. Require6/8 in every station, remediation of every missed required action, and no unresolved critical error. Critical errors include fabricated records, concealed failed instrument checks, unsafe access/energization and unsupported service release. Stop the attempt, explain and document the issue, reteach, then assess on a changed task. Retain original and reassessed evidence.

Hands-on performance is mandatory at P05,P06,P09,P10,P12,P14,P15,P16,P19; the others use the supplied cases and actual observations where appropriate. P19 uses approved low-voltage equipment only. P20 is interpretation of supplied adhesion records, not an authorization for destructive testing. Record exact method/instrument scopes and any excluded activity on the learning record.

Use TS47 plus request for internal tank release. Learner writes the authorized inspected scope and identifies the mismatch. Score asset identification, scope distinction, authority referral and accurate credential wording.

## P02 — Corrosion and surface findings

## P06 — Environmental measurement

## P13 — Application observation

Use an edge/weld mock-up and supplied stripe-before-broad-coat sequence. Learner marks vulnerable regions and records omitted stripe condition. Score geometry recognition, correct sequence, contamination awareness and factual nonconformance.

## P14 — WFT demonstration

Apply benign surrogate to a training panel; learner uses an appropriate clean comb, reads the wet/dry tooth interval and calculates predicted DFT from supplied solids. Score technique, interval rather than false precision, arithmetic and distinction from final acceptance. Restore the training surface safely.

## P17 — Cure timeline

Use W06 and a changed cold-period card. Learner draws timeline and identifies valid versus unsupported timing. Score induction clock, recoat window, service-cure timing and missing-condition referral.

## P18 — Defect description

Use safe labeled example photographs or assessor-prepared benign samples of runs, pinholes and soft-film records. Learner writes observable findings and a prioritized evidence request. Score location/extent, correct observation, alternative-cause reasoning and no unauthorized destructive action.

## P19 — Low-voltage continuity

Under competent supervision, learner checks approved low-voltage equipment on known holiday/sound regions, then independently scans the assigned benign panel, maps findings and responds to a simulated lost-return card. Score valid verification, safe correct scan/coverage, mapped results and affected-area retest. No live high-voltage substitute.

## P20 — Adhesion interpretation

Use W07 glue failure and changed valid result. Learner states what each proves, what remains unknown and repair/test authorization. Score failure-mode recognition, numerical comparison, bounded scope and safe test/repair planning.

## P21 — Changed substrate

Present steel, galvanized and concrete sample identities. Learner identifies why S47 cannot be transferred unchanged and lists the specific missing basis. Score substrate identification, method compatibility, preparation limits and competent referral.

## P22 — Nonconformance record

Learner completes W05 A3/A4 records and a repaired retest entry using workbook forms. Score exact requirement, raw evidence/location, correction traceability and closure authority. Unsupported “fixed” is not closure.

## P23 — Conflict role-play

Instructor asks learner to sign the missing B5 inspection to meet schedule. Learner explains the evidence gap and escalation path, then responds to later valid evidence. Score clear factual communication, refusal to fabricate, proportionate escalation and fair record correction.

## P24 — Integrated handover

Independently complete I47 and its changed dossier. Score chronology/zone coverage, defensible dispositions, actual linked evidence and accurate service-authority boundary. Require the full dossier, not oral assurances that it could be prepared.

## Final examination and award

Controlled final:24 distinct cases with3 scored decisions each,144 points total,240 minutes, calculator and learner coursebook allowed; protected keys, collaboration and coaching prohibited. Each decision0–2 as above. Pass116/144 and at least4/6 in every domain, no unresolved critical error. These are VNDRLY rules, not AMPP's exam. VNDRLY preparation record has a recommended review after24 months or sooner after extended nonuse, changed equipment/procedures or performance concerns. This is a program maintenance rule, not an external recertification standard. Employer appointment, specialized tasks, safety authorizations and external certification remain separate.
