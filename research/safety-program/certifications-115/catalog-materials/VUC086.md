# VNDRLY/U Banksman and Slinger Initial Training

VUC086 · original materials · 18 November 2026

For new lifting-team members preparing for supervised workplace development. Planned 40 hours. The program teaches load information, rigging selection/inspection, attachment, signaling, exclusion zones, trial lift/landing and response. Practical participation requires individual readiness screening and a competent instructor’s safe facility assessment. Provide accessible instruction without waiving essential safety behaviors. No external OPITO certificate or independent operational competence is claimed; full controlled 9067 specification was unavailable.

## Q86-01 — Roles, supervision and stopping authority

The banksman directs the agreed movement through clear signals; the slinger selects and attaches approved lifting accessories within the lift plan. The operator controls the machine, and the lift supervisor coordinates the operation. A person may perform more than one role only when the plan and competence arrangements allow it without losing attention or control. Identify who has each role before the load is touched. Anyone identifying danger must be able to call a stop; that does not make everyone an authorized movement signaler.

Initial training prepares a learner for supervised development. It is not proof of independent competence in every lifting operation. Know the limits of your appointment and refer unfamiliar loads, complex geometry, personnel lifting, tandem lifts or offshore dynamic operations to the competent planner. A simple classroom calculation is not a lift plan.

Worked case: a supervisor asks a new trainee to direct a lift alone because the experienced banksman is late. Hold the operation and obtain the required supervision rather than treating the course attendance sheet as appointment. Changed case: the qualified banksman is present, but is also assigned to drive a vehicle during the lift. Resolve the conflicting duty before starting. Good teamwork means making the responsibility visible, not assuming someone else is watching.

## Q86-02 — Read the lift plan and inspect the work area

Read the task, load identity, mass, center of gravity, lifting points, rigging configuration, equipment, route, landing location, personnel and stop conditions. Confirm the actual situation matches the plan. Look for pedestrians, vehicles, overhead services, excavations, soft ground, restricted visibility, structures, weather and simultaneous work. A route that was clear yesterday may be occupied now. Do not alter a lift plan informally to save time.

The plan must address pickup and landing, not only travel. Decide where people stand, where they retreat and how access is controlled. Verify the landing surface and supports before lifting. A load suspended while the crew searches for dunnage creates avoidable exposure. Check communications and emergency arrangements during the briefing, using questions to confirm understanding.

Worked case: plan L86 specifies an eastward route, but a delivery truck blocks the landing zone. Stop and refer the change; do not swing the load over an occupied walkway. Changed case: the route is clear but an overhead cable has appeared in the work area. Treat it as an unresolved proximity hazard and involve the competent planner; do not estimate a safe electrical clearance from memory. The learner's job is to recognize mismatch and prevent an unauthorized workaround.

## Q86-03 — Establish load mass, center of gravity and stability

Use reliable load information: identification plate, drawing, verified inventory or competent calculation. Include contents, attachments and rigging in the appropriate load calculation. A label on an empty vessel does not include residual liquid. Do not use crane behavior to discover an unknown mass. Locate the center of gravity from reliable information and understand how it affects balance and leg loading.

For a simple static load supported at two points on one horizontal line, the support nearer the center of gravity carries more vertical share. If points are 2 metres apart and the center of gravity is 0.5 metre from the left point, a 1,000-unit weight gives left share 1,000 × 1.5/2 = 750 units and right share 250. This simplified equilibrium calculation excludes dynamic effects and does not approve lifting points. The hook arrangement and rigging must follow the competent plan.

Worked case: a skid is marked 900 kg but contains 120 kg of retained material and uses 80 kg of below-hook rigging. The total specified contribution is 1,100 kg; the planner must address the actual load and configuration. Changed case: one component is removed from one side. Both mass and center of gravity may change. Do not assume an earlier balanced lift proves the modified skid will remain level. A trial lift checks an already planned arrangement; it is not a substitute for knowing the load.

## Q86-04 — Select slings and understand angle effects

Select the actual sling for load, hitch, environment and configuration using its identification and manufacturer rating. Working load limit is not breaking strength. Do not calculate a new working limit by dividing an unknown strength by a guessed safety factor. Vertical, basket and choker arrangements can have different ratings and restrictions. A doubled-looking arrangement does not automatically double capacity. Sharp edges, bend radius, temperature, chemicals and contact pressure may require specific protection or a different accessory.

For a symmetric two-leg static teaching model, with angle theta measured from horizontal, tension in each leg is W divided by twice sine theta. At 60 degrees and W = 1,000 force units, each leg carries approximately 577.35; at 30 degrees it carries 1,000. Shallower angles increase tension. Real sling tags and approved geometry govern use; do not extrapolate outside permitted angles or assume all legs share equally in a multi-leg assembly.

Worked case: two identical legs each have a 600-unit permitted axial load in the fictional chart. A 1,000-unit symmetric load at 60 degrees is below that individual limit in this simplified check, but at 45 degrees each leg would be 707.11 and fails. Other components and the lift plan still need verification. Changed case: the center of gravity moves off-center. The equal-share formula no longer applies; obtain the correct leg shares and geometry rather than dividing total load by the number of legs.

## Q86-05 — Inspect accessories and isolate defects

Match each accessory's identity to its rating and examination status, then inspect its present condition using the actual manufacturer's removal criteria. Look for damaged webbing, broken wires, deformation, cracks, corrosion, heat or chemical damage, damaged threads and missing or illegible identification. Different sling types have different detailed rejection rules; this course does not invent a universal allowable number of broken wires or percentage of wear.

A current examination record does not make newly damaged equipment usable. Pre-use inspection and periodic competent examination serve different purposes. Quarantine suspected defective or unidentified equipment, tag it clearly and report through the organization's process. Do not return it to the rack where another crew might use it. Repair or proof testing requires authorized arrangements; a trainee must not hammer a hook back into shape or weld a chain link.

Worked case: sling S2 has a readable rating and current examination reference, but an edge cut is visible. Remove it from the task and seek the proper disposition. Changed case: S3 looks undamaged but its identity tag is missing. Do not borrow S1's certificate or invent a rating from its color. Obtain an identifiable suitable replacement. Inspection is a decision about the specific item in front of you, not about a similar photograph.

## Q86-06 — Attach and protect the load

Follow the planned connection points and accessory orientation. Check compatibility among hook, master link, shackle, sling and lifting point. Load must bear where the manufacturer intends; a hook latch retains the sling but is not the load-bearing seat. Avoid unintended side loading, point loading, twists, knots and improvised shortening. Do not substitute a convenient pipe or handrail for an engineered lifting point.

Keep hands out of pinch points while taking slack. Use the approved method to seat accessories without placing fingers between sling and load. Apply suitable edge protection so it remains in position as tension develops. A loose rag is not automatically adequate protection. Confirm the rigging cannot slip or release when the load changes orientation. Remove loose items or secure them through the plan before movement.

Worked case: the sling eye sits on the hook tip because the connecting link is too small. Stop and replace the incompatible arrangement; do not rely on the latch to force it into place. Changed case: the eye seats correctly but a sharp corner lies under the synthetic sling. Obtain the specified protection or revised rigging arrangement before tensioning. The correct sequence is planned connection, inspection, clearance and controlled take-up, not loading first and adjusting by hand under tension.

## Q86-07 — Communicate by agreed hand and voice signals

Agree the signal method, designated signaler, equipment identifier, operator perspective and stop response before movement. Keep signals visible and unambiguous. If communication is lost or a signal is unclear, stop safely and re-establish understanding. An emergency stop from any person must be taken seriously. Do not continue on a guessed instruction.

For the classroom's selected standard hand-signal set, stop uses one arm extended sideways with palm down moving side to side; emergency stop uses both arms similarly. Hoist uses the raised forearm and upward index finger making small circles; lower points the arm/finger down with small circles. Swing points horizontally in the intended boom direction. Slow movement adds the other hand in front of the action-signaling hand. Practice these without a suspended load and compare with the official chart. These six are a limited training set, not qualification for every crane function.

Voice commands identify the equipment where necessary and state function/direction, distance or speed, then the function stop command at completion. Example practice exchange: “Hoist up, slowly, 50 millimetres”; operator repeats the intended action; banksman gives “Hoist stop” at the planned point. Do not use “my left” from a different viewpoint. Changed application: two radios transmit conflicting movement commands. Stop, identify the designated signaler and resolve the channel/role problem before proceeding.

## Q86-08 — Control the exclusion area and personal position

Keep people clear of the load path, potential swing and trapping zones according to the plan. Barriers, signs and briefings must work in practice. A painted line without access control may not prevent someone walking through. Position yourself to see the relevant movement, remain visible or in reliable contact with the operator and retain an escape route. Do not stand between a moving load and a fixed structure.

Tag lines may be appropriate when specified, but can introduce entanglement or pull hazards. Do not wrap a line around a hand or body, stand in a bight or try to restrain a load beyond the planned method. If the load behaves unexpectedly, stop and lower safely under the plan rather than wrestling it into position. Nobody goes under the load to untwist a sling.

Worked case: a pedestrian enters the marked zone during take-up. Give stop and prevent further movement until the zone is controlled. Changed case: the pedestrian stays outside but the banksman's retreat route is blocked by stored material. Restore a safe position before continuing. The absence of someone directly underneath does not remove crush, swing and line-of-fire hazards. In the training fixture exercise, hands remain outside the marked footprint whenever the load is suspended.

## Q86-09 — Take slack, trial lift and respond to imbalance

Before taking slack, confirm attachments, people clear, communications, load free to move and the planned landing ready. Take tension gradually as instructed; observe seating, protection and unexpected snagging from a safe position. Do not use a sudden pull to free a stuck load. If it does not respond as expected, stop and investigate through the supervisor.

The controlled trial lift raises an already verified load only enough for the planned balance and security check. The plan defines the height and observations. Check whether the load hangs as expected, accessories remain seated and nothing shifts or snags. If it tilts or moves unexpectedly, stop and lower to a safe supported condition before adjustment. Never put hands into loaded rigging to correct it.

Worked case: during the L86 fixture trial, one end rises before the other and the protection moves. Stop, lower to the supports and refer the discrepancy. Do not continue because the total mass is small. Changed case: the load stays level but a loose tool slides on top. Lower and secure/remove the item using the approved method. A successful initial inch of travel does not prove the entire route or landing is safe; continue observing throughout the operation.

## Q86-10 — Travel, land and detach under control

Movement follows the agreed route and speed with ongoing attention to people, obstructions and changes. Avoid sudden starts, stops and side pulling. The operator controls the machine; the banksman communicates required movement and stop conditions, not improvised machine settings. If visibility changes, stop and use the planned handover or revised communication arrangement rather than walking into a hazardous position to keep seeing.

Before landing, confirm the destination, supports, stability and clearance for rigging removal. Lower under control until the load is fully supported and stable, then release tension and detach according to the plan. Do not pull a sling from beneath a load with the crane or reach under a partly supported item. Keep the hook and loose accessories controlled after detachment.

Worked case: the receiving area has only one of two required supports. Hold the lift in a safe condition under the supervisor's direction and correct the landing arrangement; do not improvise with an unstable stack. Changed case: both supports are present but the load rocks after contact. Do not detach until stability is established. In the fixture exercise, no horizontal travel occurs while suspended; travel planning is practiced with a floor-supported mock load and route cards. This limit must be reflected in the training record.

## Q86-11 — Manage abnormal conditions and stop/restart

Weather, visibility, communications, equipment condition, ground conditions or the load itself can change. Use the limits in the actual lift plan and equipment instructions; there is no universal wind speed in this course. A large sail area may make a load difficult to control even when a generic weather number looks acceptable. Stop and refer rather than arguing that a previous lift succeeded.

For an abnormal event, signal stop, keep clear, inform the supervisor and follow the planned safe securing or lowering method. Do not leave a suspended load unattended or attempt an improvised repair while it is loaded. If an electrical contact or other major emergency is suspected, apply the site emergency plan and keep others clear; do not approach to rescue by touching equipment. This initial course does not qualify emergency crane recovery.

Worked case: radio contact disappears halfway through a simulated move. The operator stops safely; the team restores communications and confirms a clear instruction before restart. Changed case: communication returns but a damaged shackle is reported. The restored radio does not resolve the hardware defect. Each failed control must be addressed. Restart requires the designated authority and a confirmed safe condition, not simply silence after an alarm.

## Q86-12 — Record supervised experience and build competence

A useful learning record identifies the task, equipment, load, configuration, signals used, learner role, supervision, observed performance and feedback. Record what the learner actually did, not what the crew accomplished. A signature on a logbook is not evidence of independent competence if the supervisor supplied every decision. Include changed situations and limitations so the later assessor can identify gaps.

After initial training, undertake workplace development under the required competent supervision and complete the applicable evidence process. Do not claim this original VNDRLY package is an OPITO logbook or automatically satisfies another scheme's entries. The employer or external scheme decides the competence and appointment requirements. Medical or physical readiness for actual tasks must be managed through the appropriate occupational process, not inferred from a written test.

Worked record: “Learner inspected identified sling, rejected damaged spare, attached approved rigging on supported load, gave hoist/stop signals during supervised fixture lift and detached after stable landing.” This is more informative than “lifting done.” Changed case: the learner observed a blind lift but never signaled it. Record observation only; do not tick blind-lift competence. Training completion means the defined learning and assessments were passed, while independent workplace authority remains a separate decision.

# VUC086 practical workbook and instructor delivery

## Facility and participation gate

A competent lifting instructor must approve the facility, actual equipment and task-specific risk assessment before any suspended-load exercise. Instructor must be competent with the selected lifting appliance, rigging and signal system; the operator must be authorized for that appliance. Classroom maximum eight; active practical group maximum four with one learner signaling at a time and instructor directly supervising. Screen individual readiness, explain physical demands and arrange appropriate occupational advice where needed. Do not pressure a learner into an unsafe physical task. A learner unable to complete an essential practical outcome can receive a knowledge record, not the full assessed training award.

Provide current actual manufacturer instructions, identifiable examined lifting appliance/accessories, approved stable anchor/structure, verified lightweight training load with designed lifting points, rated connectors, suitable protection, stable landing supports, barriers, official signal chart, radios or safe role-play substitutes, scale/load identification and forms. Damaged examples must be permanently segregated/marked NOT FOR USE; use inert replicas or photographs where handling a defect could injure. Never attach defective examples to the live training fixture.

The numerical A86/B86 problems are paper calculations only. Do not lift their 1,000-unit or 1,100-kg loads. Actual L86 uses a verified total suspended mass no greater than 20 kg including rigging, selected by the instructor; 20 kg is an exercise ceiling, not a universal safe threshold. The actual apparatus and all components must be suitable for the exact geometry and conditions. No participant passes under or reaches beneath a suspended load. No personnel lifting, crane operation by an unqualified learner, mobile-crane travel, tandem lifting, live electrical proximity, offshore dynamic lift or hazardous load is allowed in this initial exercise.

## L86 fixture lift plan template with mandatory completed fields

Before delivery the instructor completes these fields from the real facility and signs the approval. Blank or unverified fields mean no suspended-load exercise. This is a normal delivery requirement, not permission to invent equipment ratings. The learner must compare the actual setup with the completed plan. Manufacturer limits and the competent plan may be more restrictive than this teaching template and always govern.

Exercise sequence: supported load at designated pickup/landing position; inspect and attach; clear the marked zone; agree communications; take slack under operator control; raise only to the instructor-approved trial height, never above 50 mm for this exercise; stop and inspect from a safe position; lower straight down onto the prepared supports; confirm stable full support; release tension; detach and stow. No horizontal movement of a suspended load is part of L86. Floor-route practice uses a supported model, not a swinging suspended object. The instructor may reduce height/mass or use a different approved fixture; cannot waive demonstrated control and attachment outcomes for the full practical award.

If anything shifts, snags, tilts unexpectedly or becomes unclear, stop and lower safely under the approved response. No adjustment occurs while the rigging is loaded. Abnormal cues are announced verbally or presented on cards; do not deliberately damage equipment, destabilize a real load, create electrical contact or cause actual radio failure during a hazardous phase. Rehearse loss-of-communication response with the load supported before a controlled lift.

## A86 paper load and rating cards

A1: skid dry mass 900 kg, retained material 120 kg, below-hook rigging contribution 80 kg. Total contribution 1,100 kg. Actual crane chart treatment of hook blocks and other deductions must follow the crane manual and plan; this example is not a chart approval.

A2: static beam weight 1,000 force units, pick points 2 m apart, center of gravity 0.5 m from left. Left share 750, right 250. This is vertical support share only, not sling tension until geometry is considered. If the center moves to 0.8 m from left, left share 600 and right 400.

A3: symmetric static two-leg case W = 1,000 force units; each leg is 60 degrees from horizontal; ideal tension = 577.35 per leg. At 45 degrees tension = 707.11; at 30 degrees = 1,000. Fictional leg permitted axial load 600 units; the 45- and 30-degree cases fail that component check. The 60-degree case only passes this one ideal check; lifting points, master link, hook, appliance, environment and plan remain unverified.

A4: fictional assembly record states two matched legs, permitted 60-degree geometry, each axial limit 600 units, master link limit 1,100 vertical units, connection-point vertical limit 600 each, hook limit 1,100 vertical units. With symmetric W = 1,000, component arithmetic is compatible only under the stated ideal assumptions. Adding 150 units of load fails total master-link/hook limits and makes each leg 663.95 units at 60 degrees, also failing. Never assume a real component rating from this fictional card.

## B86 changed paper case

Weight W = 800 force units, symmetric two-leg angle 45 degrees from horizontal. Each leg tension 565.69. Fictional per-leg limit 550 means HOLD despite total load being only 800. At 60 degrees the ideal tension would be 461.88, but the learner cannot redesign the lift independently; refer to the competent planner and verify all components. If one leg is at a different angle or the center of gravity is offset, do not use the symmetric formula.

Changed mass case: base 650 kg, contents 75 kg, below-hook rigging 45 kg gives 770 kg. A label showing only 650 does not describe the total contribution. Changed offset case: points 3 m apart, center 1 m from left, weight 900 units gives left 600 and right 300. State the simplifying assumptions on every calculation.

## G86 inspection and attachment cards

G1: identified synthetic sling with current record and visible edge cut. Remove from task; use actual manufacturer's rejection criteria, do not minimize damage because paperwork is current. G2: sling in apparently good condition with missing identity. Quarantine pending proper identification/disposition; color is not a replacement for a rating. G3: connector with visibly deformed pin and damaged threads. Remove; no hammering or improvised repair. G4: intact identified accessory whose rating applies to a different hitch. It is not selected until the exact intended configuration is verified.

Supported-load attachment challenge: an undersized link would sit on the hook tip rather than seat. Learner rejects it and selects only the instructor-approved compatible replacement. Another card shows protection displaced from a sharp edge. Learner stops before tension, restores the approved arrangement and has it checked. Defect and incompatibility cards are evaluated with the load fully supported; no unsafe configuration is actually loaded.

## S86 signal practice card

Use the official chart at https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926SubpartCCAppA as the visual reference for this selected U.S. standard-method teaching set. Instructor demonstrates stop, emergency stop, hoist, lower, swing and move slowly. Learner physically performs each from several orientations without an active load; observer identifies the intended command. Correct any ambiguity before equipment practice. Other site standards require their own agreed training; do not mix gestures mid-lift.

For this course, all six selected signals must be recognized and performed correctly on two different randomized sequences. Instructor records each item, including any error and later reassessment. This limited set is not a full crane-function signal qualification. Swing is practiced only as a dry signal; L86 has no suspended horizontal motion. Voice rehearsal identifies equipment C1, then function/direction and distance/speed; at the planned endpoint give the function stop command. Use operator perspective. Example: “C1, hoist up, slowly, 50 millimetres” followed by “C1, hoist stop.” Actual approved L86 height may be lower; use that actual distance in its command.

Communication loss or conflicting movement commands → safe stop, restore one designated signaler and confirm understanding. Any person may give an emergency stop. A substituted banksman requires a clear handover and renewed agreement before movement.

## Z86 area map and changed conditions

Barriers exclude the planned load/swing/trap area and protect R. Instructor marks the actual safe fixture boundary separately; this paper map is not a universal separation distance.

Do not perform actual abnormal loading to test reactions; use verbal cues and safe supported reset.

### W86-6 — Record and changed-scope review

## I86 integrated supervised exercise

Learner reviews the completed actual L86, inspects and attaches, briefs the operator, establishes the zone, signals the controlled trial/landing and detaches after support. Then complete a changed paper load/route case and an accurate log. Ten dimensions: role/scope; plan/mass; rigging selection; inspection; attachment/protection; signals; zone/position; trial-lift response; landing/detachment; records/limits. Each 0–4, require 32/40 and every dimension at least 3, no critical error. All twelve station outcomes and both six-signal sequences must separately pass. Instructor intervention to prevent danger is recorded and requires a fresh independent attempt after training.

# VUC086 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q86-01-L1

Can a trainee replace an absent competent banksman alone?

**Answer:** No; obtain required competent supervision/appointment.

**Reason/remediation:** Initial training does not establish independent operational competence. Revisit Q86-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q86-01-L2

Who may give an emergency stop?

**Answer:** Anyone who identifies danger.

**Reason/remediation:** This does not authorize everyone to give movement commands. Revisit Q86-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q86-02-L1

The landing area is blocked. Can the crew improvise a route over a walkway?

**Answer:** No; hold and refer the plan change.

**Reason/remediation:** The approved route and exposure controls no longer match. Revisit Q86-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q86-02-L2

## Available primary sources

- OPITO 9067: https://opito.com/standards-and-qualifications/industry-standards-library/banksman-and-slinger-initial-training-previously-stage-1 . Public page describes initial combined-role training, three units, 24 hours and subsequent supervised workplace experience/competence assessment. Our original 40-hour package is not the unavailable controlled specification, an OPITO certificate, its logbook or a claim of the external theory/practical ratio.
- HSE LOLER overview: https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/loler.htm . Context for competent planning/supervision and suitable lifting equipment in the applicable jurisdiction. Not a universal law for every country.

## Delivery and claim boundaries

Paper masses/ratings are never used for a real lift. Actual facility plan fields must be completed from identified suitable equipment before supervised practical delivery. This is a delivery control, not a pre-issued learner result. Six physical stations and six observed review encounters are required for the training award. The actual trial lift is straight up/down only at approved low height; blind, mobile, tandem, personnel and dynamic offshore lifts are not certified. Materials completion, learner achievement, workplace competence and publication remain separate states.
