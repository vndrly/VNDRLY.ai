# VNDRLY/U Ultrasonic Examination — Phased-Array Crack Height Sizing

VUC061 · Original instruction · 2026-11-18

## Audience, scope and learning contract

This advanced course develops phased-array measurement of opposite-surface-connected planar feature height in cool carbon-steel weld training specimens. It is distinct from broad phased-array detection training and conventional single-probe sizing. Learners must already demonstrate conventional UT fundamentals, phased-array setup and safe equipment use. A coursebook is not an employer qualification or an API credential. The catalog's QUSEPA name is treated as legacy scope; current external program availability and examination requirements are not inferred from an old candidate guide. [S61-01]

By completion, the learner must independently establish a valid array setup, verify the relevant laws, distinguish actual tip evidence from misleading images, scan the whole assigned feature, calculate and report its maximum supported height, recover from invalid measurements and defend an honest limitation. Written knowledge and observed practical performance are separate requirements. This package specifies training equipment and specimen evidence; it does not claim that hardware has been supplied or a learner has performed the tasks.

## Q01 — Height is a defined physical quantity

Start with a cross-section showing the scanning surface, opposite surface, local wall thickness and crack connection. For a flat far-surface-connected feature, height equals local thickness minus normal depth to its established tip. Remaining near-surface ligament equals that tip depth. Length along the weld is a third dimension. A display cursor labeled depth must not be copied into a height field without determining its reference.

State the coordinate convention before scanning. If the feature is embedded or connected to the scanning surface, the far-surface equation is inapplicable. Establish both boundaries or use the appropriate approved method. No software label can establish a feature's surface connection.

**Worked:** local thickness 22 mm, verified tip depth 15 mm: height 7 mm, ligament 15 mm. **Changed:** connection is unknown. The arithmetic remains 7 but is not a supported height. Draw the alternatives, investigate the connection and report unresolved sizing until evidence supports one. P01 requires this distinction in a sketch and report.

## Q02 — Safe readiness and technical authority

Confirm the material, geometry, thickness range and feature class covered by the sizing technique. A generic PAUT detection procedure cannot automatically support crack-height measurement. The responsible UT authority approves the actual technique and references; the instructor assesses performance against it. Production acceptance and engineering remaining-life decisions remain outside this training award.

Use stable, cool, depressurized bench specimens. Inspect sharp edges, cable insulation, connectors, couplant compatibility and spill controls. Secure heavy samples before positioning the probe. Stop if the physical arrangement becomes unsafe. Do not use this course to justify hot work, process access or energized electrical repair.

**Worked:** technically suitable setup, unstable sample. Secure the sample before scanning; a valid procedure does not cure a handling hazard. **Changed:** stable sample but alloy outside the technique. Obtain an applicable validated technique rather than borrowing the steel velocity. P02 records both technical and physical readiness.

## Q03 — Array identity, active aperture and element condition

Record instrument/software, array serial number, element count/pitch, wedge, cable and active aperture. The programmed probe definition must match the connected hardware. Verify element performance using the approved manufacturer method; document failed or weak channels and apply the actual procedure's disposition. An attractive image does not prove every required element is functioning.

Do not silently reduce the aperture to hide a defective channel. That changes the beam and may invalidate focusing, calibration and demonstrated sizing performance. Save the original finding, obtain an authorized configuration and repeat applicable checks. A spare probe requires its own identity and validation.

**Worked:** a 16-element aperture includes one nonresponsive channel. Record the condition and hold use pending the specified disposition; do not declare a universal allowable percentage. **Changed:** replacement array passes its checks. Reestablish the relevant setup and reference evidence. P03 is an actual equipment task, not a screenshot quiz.

## Q04 — Focal laws, focus and demonstrated range

Focal laws control element timing and the intended beam directions/focus. Document aperture, angle range, increment and focus definition. A focus entered as sound path is not the same as normal depth. Establish the actual instrument convention and validate the resulting response on suitable references throughout the required region. A beam drawing is a planning aid, not proof that a small tip is resolvable.

Increasing the number of angles can affect acquisition performance. More laws do not repair inadequate access, incorrect material velocity or unsupported focus. Use a demonstrated set rather than adding laws until the image looks convincing. Any substantive change requires the procedure's applicable verification.

**Worked:** 40° through 70° inclusive at 1° increments gives 31 laws; at 0.5° it gives 61. **Changed:** the second setup has the same nominal sector but slower refresh. Recheck scanning performance and required coverage rather than assuming equivalence. P04 documents configuration, focus convention and actual reference demonstration.

## Q05 — Wedge delay and depth accuracy

The displayed depth depends on a valid time origin, mode velocity and geometry. Wedge delay is not material travel. Verify the required laws over the intended depth range; a correct middle-angle response does not prove edge-angle accuracy. Manufacturer guidance emphasizes calibration across the sequence. [S61-02]

Use known references with documented truth. Record measured versus reference positions and the configuration used. If a point fails the announced band, resolve the cause and repeat the affected checks. Do not offset the report by a guessed constant while leaving the setup wrong.

**Worked:** known 8 and 18 mm depths read 8.2 and 18.7: errors +0.2 and +0.7 mm. Against this course's fictional ±0.5 mm check band, the second fails. **Changed:** only a central law is corrected. Edge laws remain unsupported until verified. P05 requires actual readings and retained failed evidence.

## Q06 — Sensitivity normalization and TCG are not geometry calibration

Sensitivity normalization addresses differences in response among laws; TCG addresses amplitude response versus path under the selected method. These are different from establishing accurate coordinates. A normalized image may still have incorrect depth, and a geometrically correct setup may have inadequate tip visibility. Treat the checks separately and preserve both records. [S61-02]

Use only the specified reference reflectors and applicable gain method. Ensure signals are not clipped and the noise level remains usable. Increasing gain indiscriminately can display noise as apparent structure. Changing evaluation sensitivity requires the technique's controls; it does not authorize selecting whatever setting produces the smallest crack.

**Worked:** comparable reference response rises from 20% to 40%, an amplitude factor of 2 or 6.0206 dB. This does not show a doubled flaw size. **Changed:** the new response clips at full scale. Restore a valid measurement range before comparison. P06 records actual normalization and the separate depth check.

## Q07 — Ray path, depth and coordinate validity

For a flat first-leg path S at angle θ from normal, depth d=S cosθ and lateral offset x=S sinθ. These formulas require the assumed mode, leg and geometry. A reflected ray or curved part needs the applicable treatment. When measuring a far-surface-connected feature, use local thickness at the actual position, not an unrelated nominal dimension.

Explain every cursor coordinate in the report. A sloping distance in an image is not necessarily vertical height. If the display switches between sound path and true depth, confirm the saved data and report headings remain coherent.

**Worked:** S=24 mm at 60° gives d=12 mm and x=20.7846 mm. In local 22 mm thickness, supported height is 10 mm. **Changed:** at 50° the same path gives d=15.4269 mm and height 6.5731 mm. Verify the real angle; do not choose the value yielding a preferred result.

## Q08 — Read A-scan evidence behind the image

An S-scan or reconstructed view organizes acquired responses; its pixels are not a photograph of the physical crack. Select the relevant law and examine its waveform, gate, range and coordinate basis. Keep the raw response and setup linked to any annotated image. Manufacturer descriptions distinguish manual views from encoded position-linked imaging. [S61-03]

Color palettes, interpolation, persistence and display saturation can change apparent boundaries. Use the approved sizing evidence rather than a color transition chosen by eye. A cursor with two decimal places does not establish physical accuracy to two decimals.

**Worked:** an operator measures a 6 mm colored streak without identifying its waveform or law. That is an image measurement, not yet a supported crack height. **Changed:** the streak changes with palette while the waveform timing stays fixed. Preserve the actual response basis and investigate; do not average the two visual sizes. P08 requires an annotated image linked to its A-scan and configuration.

## Q09 — Tip, corner and branch identification

Opposite-surface features can produce a strong base/corner response and a weaker tip response. Weakness alone is not proof of a tip. Corroborate the candidate through approved probe movements, laws and geometric consistency. Complex branching can introduce additional echoes or hide deeper boundaries. The manufacturer application study illustrates these difficulties, not a universal sizing guarantee. [S61-04]

Do not automatically use the brightest response as the tip or pick the deepest pixel. Identify the physical meaning of each candidate and record why it is accepted or rejected. When a unique boundary cannot be supported, report the limitation and obtain an appropriate validated approach.

**Worked:** a weak candidate appears at one law but fails to track consistently with approved movement. Tip identity remains unresolved. **Changed:** repeated permitted observations establish its location. Calculate from that evidence and preserve the resolution history. P09 uses real known teaching features before blind specimens.

## Q10 — Beam width and amplitude boundaries

An amplitude-defined edge depends on the technique, beam response, orientation and reference. It is not automatically the physical crack boundary. Treat any amplitude-drop method as a procedure-dependent measurement requiring validation on suitable specimens. It cannot be substituted for a tip method merely because the tip is hard to see.

Record peak validity, threshold, evaluation settings and geometric assumptions. If the peak is clipped, the fraction of peak is not known. Coupling changes can move apparent endpoints without any change in the crack. Repeat under valid conditions and explain the limitation instead of writing a precise but unsupported result.

**Worked:** 72% and 36% valid comparable responses have ratio 0.5, or −6.0206 dB. **Changed:** the peak is actually above display range. The visible ratio no longer establishes the intended drop. P10 compares known-feature response behavior; no physical height is awarded from the ratio alone.

## Q11 — Access, crown and local thickness

Plan probe positions that can interrogate the required feature along its full extent. A retained weld crown may restrict position or contact. Use the validated technique and actual accessible surfaces; do not imply that nominal beam coverage overcame a physical access barrier. Record the local profile and thickness basis used for each calculation.

Do not modify a specimen or production surface without authorization. If an approved alternate access direction is available, demonstrate its validity rather than treating it as interchangeable by name. An unresolved region stays unresolved until actually examined under a valid method.

**Worked:** nominal thickness 24 mm, actual local 23 mm, valid tip depth 16 mm: height 7 mm rather than nominal 8 mm. **Changed:** crown blocks one required probe position. State the missing scope and obtain a demonstrated alternative or leave the result incomplete. P11 includes flush and retained-crown training conditions.

## Q12 — Scan acquisition and coverage

Maintain contact, required movement and complete assigned length. For encoded acquisition, verify position direction/scale and the procedure's acquisition settings. Missing data are not sound material. For manual acquisition, retain the required position map and response evidence; a stationary screen capture cannot prove the entire feature was scanned.

Estimate acquisition spacing using actual effective full-sequence rate, not a headline pulser specification. The simple spacing model is speed divided by completed frames per second; confirm performance using the actual instrument and method. Data dropout, lift-off or excess speed requires scope assessment and valid repeat scanning.

**Worked:** 60 mm/s at 120 complete frames/s gives 0.5 mm between frames. **Changed:** actual full-sequence rate is 60/s, giving 1 mm. Whether either meets the method depends on its predeclared requirement. P12 records measured acquisition performance and actual coverage, not merely theoretical arithmetic.

## Q13 — Track maximum supported height

Crack height may vary along the weld. Calculate local height from valid local thickness and established tip depth at each required position. The greatest supported height is not the average. Preserve position labels so the maximum can be relocated and reviewed.

If an interval is missing or a tip is ambiguous, distinguish maximum among measured points from maximum of the whole feature. A good measurement elsewhere does not resolve the gap. Complete required coverage and retain both original and repeat records.

**Worked:** supported heights 5.4, 6.1 and 5.8 mm yield maximum 6.1 mm. **Changed:** one unexamined interval remains. Report 6.1 only as the largest measured value, not the final whole-feature maximum. P13 requires a complete position/height table and an explanation of the controlling observation.

## Q14 — Multiple responses and limits of transfer

Separate genuine feature tips from mode-converted, geometric or secondary responses using the approved evidence. Two plausible tips do not justify selecting the smaller height. Conversely, the highest apparent response is not necessarily the actual crack boundary. Preserve the interpretation trail, including rejected candidates.

A method validated on simple opposite-surface weld features does not automatically qualify corrosion cracking networks, different alloys, curved parts, TFM, PCI or TOFD. These require their own applicability and performance basis. An instrument's ability to display a mode is not permission or evidence of competence in it.

**Worked:** in 24 mm local thickness, established tip depths 17 and 14 mm correspond to 7 and 10 mm heights; greatest supported height is 10 mm. **Changed:** the 14 mm response is proven geometric. Revise the interpretation traceably and verify the remaining feature and coverage before reporting 7 mm.

## Q15 — Repeatability, bias and uncertainty

Repeatability asks whether repeated readings agree; accuracy requires comparison with independent truth. Shared setup error can make every reading consistently wrong. Compute signed errors and inspect their pattern before summarizing. An average near zero can hide large over- and undersizing.

Apply the declared uncertainty/decision rule to the actual reference and measurement. Do not invent an allowance after seeing a failure. Where the evidence cannot support a pass, preserve the uncertainty and remediate the method or specimen basis. Truth uncertainty must be suitable for the precision being assessed.

**Worked:** errors +0.6, −0.6, +0.6, −0.6 mm have zero mean and RMS 0.6 mm. **Changed:** four +0.4 mm errors have mean +0.4 and RMS 0.4, indicating consistent positive bias. Neither summary alone proves every required individual result is acceptable. P15 retains all repeated readings, not a favorable subset.

## Q16 — Drift and configuration change recovery

Perform the procedure's checks at required intervals and after relevant changes. A failed verification identifies a potentially affected period. Determine which specimens and laws depended on the invalid condition, preserve the records, correct the cause and perform the required repeat work. A later passing check does not retroactively validate earlier sizing.

Changes to wedge, array, focal law, velocity, focus or processing can affect different parts of the evidence. Use an explicit dependency record. Never overwrite the old setup or measurements to make a failed sequence disappear.

**Worked:** four specimens were measured after the last valid check before failure. All four require affected-scope assessment. **Changed:** two are repeated validly. The other two remain unresolved absent a supported authorized disposition. P16 includes an instructor-controlled safe verification fault and actual recovery evidence.

## Q17 — Unit conversion and report precision

Use a consistent report quantity and units across instrument, worksheet and submission. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm. Keep intermediate precision and round only as the report rule permits. A value can look plausible while being wrong by a unit factor or by confusing height with ligament.

Have the learner independently check a conversion and the coordinate label. Do not use rounding to turn an exceeded tolerance into a pass. The report must identify what was actually measured, not merely include a number in a column.

**Worked:** 0.28 in equals 7.112 mm. **Changed:** 0.24 in equals 6.096 mm, a 1.016 mm difference. A 7.112 value under an inches heading requires a traceable correction, not a cosmetic dismissal. P17 requires paired-unit reporting and explanation of the physical quantity.

## Q18 — Independent demonstration and defensible handover

Blind assessment uses unfamiliar specimens with protected, independently supported truth. The learner must establish the setup, verify, scan, interpret and report without coaching. Familiar practice results do not demonstrate independent competence. Keep failed attempts and require a changed set after remediation.

Report component identity, actual technique/configuration, verification, coverage, maximum supported height, reference convention and limitations. Engineering disposition is separate. Preserve raw records and revisions under the training record policy; old external exam memory-erasure instructions are not a reason to destroy VNDRLY learner evidence.

**Worked:** nine valid measurements and one copied value do not constitute a 90% practical pass; fabrication is critical. **Changed:** the tenth is honestly unresolved. That is proper disclosure but still leaves required performance incomplete. I61 requires both integrity and actual sizing competence before the training award.
