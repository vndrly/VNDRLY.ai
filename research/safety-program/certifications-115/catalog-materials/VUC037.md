# VNDRLY/U Asset Integrity — Storage Tank Inspection

VUC037. Original VNDRLY teaching material. Available-source delivery basis, 2026-11-18: see available-source-completion.md for the user's revised source constraint, four supplementary decision laboratories, and limits on current-edition verification. This is not an API certification or a verified equivalent of API's examination program.

## Program purpose and boundaries

This program teaches the inspection decisions involved in maintaining aboveground steel storage tanks: establishing the tank's identity and operating envelope, finding deterioration, evaluating inspection evidence, selecting the correct engineering review, and assembling a defensible return-to-service record. The intended learners are inspectors, integrity technicians and maintenance engineers who already understand basic industrial safety, measurement units, elementary algebra and technical drawings. Before supervised practical work, the employer must verify the learner's access, instrument and task authorizations.

A VNDRLY learning credential is not API certification, an engineering license, an NDE personnel qualification, a confined-space authorization or permission to approve an operating tank. Assessment of a synthetic record demonstrates that decision skill, not actual field qualification. Work requiring an authorized inspector, engineer, specialist or owner approval stays with that role.

By the end, learners must be able to: (O1) establish the governing tank and service record; (O2) plan inspections for credible deterioration and inaccessible areas; (O3) evaluate shell, bottom and settlement data using controlled criteria; (O4) interpret materials, welding and examination evidence; (O5) organize repair and testing hold points; (O6) evaluate roofs, vents, protection systems and safe access; and (O7) produce a traceable disposition with limits and follow-up. Numerical examples are synthetic training cases. Supplied values are not permission to substitute these exercises for the applicable standard, engineering assessment or site procedure.

## Q01 — Identify the tank before assessing it

A cylindrical shape is not enough to choose an inspection code. Establish whether the asset is a field-erected welded tank, a riveted tank, a shop-built tank, a low-pressure tank or a pressure vessel. Determine the construction standard and edition, original design conditions, subsequent modifications and the inspection basis adopted by the owner. A drawing for the neighboring tank is not an acceptable substitute. Compare the nameplate, equipment register, drawings, dimensions and nozzle arrangement. Record disagreements rather than selecting the most convenient record.

Build an identity sheet containing the asset tag, location, diameter, shell height and course heights, roof and bottom construction, material specifications, weld arrangement, original joint examination, foundation type, anchors, service history, inspection dates and known repairs. Keep the source of every consequential value. A material grade inferred from appearance is an unknown, not a verified grade. Distinguish unavailable records from records that establish an unfavorable condition.

Ownership of the decision matters. The inspector documents and evaluates within their competence; engineering resolves design uncertainties; operations defines actual service and maintains restrictions; the owner controls disposition. A training certificate does not transfer those responsibilities. When a record is missing, state what decision depends on it and obtain the required investigation or conservative engineering basis. Do not fill a blank with a usual value merely to finish a calculation.

**Worked case.** Tank T-37 has a plate reading 60 ft diameter, while the latest general arrangement says 64 ft. A 2019 alteration file belongs to T-73. The correct first action is to quarantine the misfiled alteration as evidence for T-37, verify the diameter and retrieve the correct history. A shell calculation made with the smaller diameter could understate demand. The inspection may continue in areas independent of that uncertainty, but no fill-height approval can rely on the unresolved dimension.

**Changed case.** The diameter agrees, but material certificates are missing. This resolves geometry only. It does not establish allowable stress or toughness. Identify the permitted route for unknown materials in the governing standard and engineering procedure before assigning values. Record the actual route and approver.

**Practice.** Write three separate statements: the verified fact, the unresolved fact and the decision being held. An acceptable response is “tag and measured diameter verified; shell material unverified; allowable stress and brittle-fracture disposition held.” “Tank is probably acceptable” is not a disposition.

## Q02 — Service, liquid head and operating limits

The load on an atmospheric tank shell is strongly affected by liquid height and density. A change from a light product to a heavier one can increase liquid-head demand without changing the tank's dimensions. Pressure and vacuum also matter: atmospheric service does not mean a tank tolerates arbitrary internal pressure, blocked venting or pump-out vacuum. Temperature can change material behavior, product characteristics and deterioration mechanisms.

Write the operating envelope as limits with units and reference points: maximum liquid level measured from the stated datum, maximum product specific gravity, temperature range, permitted pressure/vacuum and relevant fill or withdrawal limitations. Separate normal operating level, alarm levels and structural maximum level. An alarm setting is a control setting; it is not proof of shell capacity. A high-high trip that has not been functionally verified is not an established protection layer.

For a simple comparison at the same height, liquid-head demand scales with specific gravity. If a tank changes from SG 0.75 to SG 0.90, the head-related demand increases by 0.90/0.75 = 1.20, or 20 percent. This ratio is useful for recognizing a change requiring review; it is not a complete rerating method. Shell, bottom, foundation, roof, seals, vents, containment and compatibility may all impose additional restrictions.

**Worked case.** Operations proposes storing a denser product at the old 30 ft level. The old SG was 0.80 and the new SG is 1.00. The head comparison is 1.25 times the old demand. Keeping equal head alone would suggest 30 × 0.80/1.00 = 24 ft, but the inspector must not authorize 24 ft from this shortcut. The owner needs the full service-change evaluation, updated controls, instructions and approved limits.

**Changed case.** Product density stays constant but pumping rate doubles. A thickness calculation cannot establish adequate venting. Request the vent-system capacity and operational review, including both fill and withdrawal cases and the credible failure modes of connected equipment. Record the changed condition rather than treating it as routine operation.

**Common mistake.** Copying the old maximum fill level onto a repair report without reconciling the service change makes the report look complete while leaving its central assumption wrong. Every assessment should identify which operating envelope it supports.

## Q03 — Inspection coverage and evidence quality

An inspection plan must connect a credible damage mechanism to a location, an examination method and a decision. Start with service history, water and sludge behavior, past thickness maps, leaks, settlement surveys, coatings, cathodic protection, repairs and changes. Then ask where deterioration could be worse than the locations previously measured. Repeating accessible readings can create a reassuring trend while missing a growing local problem.

Divide the tank into inspectable systems: shell courses and welds; nozzles and attachments; bottom and annular region; roof and its supports or flotation components; foundation and shell support; vents and other safeguards; and external protection. For each, specify the method, accessible coverage, limitations, required personnel, instrument suitability and how findings will be evaluated. Record surfaces hidden by insulation, lining, supports or equipment as limitations. “No defect found” must not become “no defect exists” where coverage was incomplete.

Use repeatable coordinates. Shell observations need course, elevation and circumferential location; bottom observations need a defined origin, plate identity and direction; settlement readings need stable benchmarks, point numbers and survey conditions. Photos should show location and scale, not merely an isolated close-up. Preserve raw data when interpretation could later change. A report that keeps only the lowest thickness loses the spatial information needed for local-area assessment.

**Worked case.** An earlier bottom survey covered 85 percent by a screening method and used UT to confirm selected indications. The next report says “100 percent acceptable” because no new leak was observed. That conclusion is unsupported. The remaining 15 percent, screening limitations and confirmation sampling still matter. A suitable plan identifies the inaccessible areas and a complementary method or justified engineering disposition; it does not promote lack of leakage to proof of bottom integrity.

**Changed case.** A previously accessible area is now covered by a new internal coil. The old coverage map cannot be copied forward unchanged. Update the access plan, identify which deterioration could occur under or near the coil and record the limitation until resolved.

**Practice.** For one suspected under-deposit area, write a coverage statement including the location, selected method, confirmation route and limitation. A useful statement is specific enough for another qualified inspector to repeat the work and understand what was not examined.

## Q04 — Safe access is part of inspection planning

Tanks create connected hazards: flammable or toxic atmosphere, oxygen deficiency, engulfment, unstable sludge or deposits, electrical and mechanical energy, fragile roof surfaces, falls and hazardous adjacent operations. Empty is not synonymous with clean or safe. A space can retain residues and vapors after product removal; a connected line or hidden compartment can reintroduce them. Safe access therefore depends on the actual isolation, preparation, testing and permit system, not the appearance of the tank.

Before entry, use the site's authorized confined-space process where applicable. Confirm the isolation boundary with the responsible personnel, verify the atmosphere-testing and monitoring plan, establish communication and rescue arrangements and stop when conditions depart from the permit. An inspector does not improvise rescue entry after a colleague collapses. Summon the planned rescue response and keep unprotected people out. Ventilation and a single favorable gas reading do not replace isolation or continued controls.

Before roof access, establish the walking surface's structural condition, openings, corrosion and permitted routes. A floating roof can change position and expose entrapment or vapor hazards. Before hot work, assess the tank, connected equipment and surrounding spaces; use the authorized hot-work system and continuing monitoring appropriate to the task. Do not assume water or a nominally nonflammable service excludes a flammable residue or connected vapor source.

**Worked case.** A crew proposes welding a small attachment while a nearby connected tank remains in service. The permit describes only the work location. The inspection hold concerns the incomplete hazard boundary: connected paths and adjacent vapor sources must be evaluated. Calling the weld “small” does not reduce ignition capability. Stop the task until the responsible permit authority resolves the boundary and establishes verified controls.

**Changed case.** Entry has been authorized, but the ventilation fails and an alarm sounds. Leave under the established procedure, account for personnel and require reassessment before re-entry. Do not stay to finish the final thickness reading. Inspection completeness cannot justify bypassing the entry conditions.

**Practice.** State who can authorize the entry, who monitors conditions and what triggers withdrawal. Use the actual site role names during delivery. A learner who says “the training certificate authorizes entry” has misunderstood the credential boundary and must repeat this lesson before practical access.

## Q05 — Thickness measurements that support decisions

Thickness is a measured quantity with a location and a method, not just a number. Before taking UT readings, identify the material, nominal range, surface condition, coating, curvature and likely damage. Select the probe and calibration arrangement under the approved procedure. Establish velocity and zero using suitable references, verify the response across the relevant range and repeat checks at the required times. A gauge that displays three decimal places does not necessarily measure to that accuracy on a rough, pitted surface.

Prepare the surface without damaging the component or creating an uncontrolled exposure. Establish adequate coupling and examine signal behavior. A changing number, loss of back-wall response or disagreement between repeat readings needs investigation. Do not retain the most reassuring result. Coatings, laminations, geometry and very small pits can mislead a simple thickness reading. Use the specified technique and complementary examination when necessary. Record inaccessible locations and instrument limitations.

The basic pulse-echo relationship is thickness = velocity × round-trip time / 2. In a synthetic calibration example, a 2.0 microsecond round trip at 5,900 m/s corresponds to 0.0059 m, or 5.9 mm. If the actual material velocity were 5,600 m/s but the instrument remained set to 5,900 m/s, that same time would be reported as 5.9 mm even though the thickness would be 5.6 mm. A wrong setup can turn a failing condition into an apparent pass. This calculation explains the hazard; it is not a substitute for practical calibration.

**Worked case.** Three repeats at a cleaned location are 5.82, 5.80 and 5.81 mm. An adjacent rough pit gives intermittent 6.4 mm and signal loss. The repeated clean readings are useful for their location; they do not validate the pit. Map and examine the pit with an appropriate method and preserve the limitation. Averaging the four displayed values would invent a thickness that describes neither location.

**Changed case.** The post-use calibration check fails. Identify the affected measurement period, quarantine its results pending investigation and repeat the required work under the procedure. Do not merely calibrate the instrument for tomorrow and leave today's unverified data in the acceptance calculation.

## Q06 — Corrosion trends, remaining life and inspection planning

Calculate a trend only from comparable measurements. Confirm the same location, same physical layer, compatible methods and actual elapsed time. If a repaired plate replaced the original, the old and new thicknesses do not represent a continuous corrosion history. If a previous value was nominal plate thickness rather than measured thickness, identify that uncertainty before treating the difference as metal loss.

For a consistent location, average rate = (earlier thickness − later thickness) / elapsed years. Remaining margin = current thickness − required thickness. A simple projected time to that limit is margin / selected corrosion rate. These relationships assume the selected rate is appropriate to future conditions. They are not guarantees of life and do not capture cracking, local penetration, changing chemistry or all structural constraints. A zero or negative apparent rate calls for review of measurement variability and the inspection basis; it does not establish infinite life.

**Worked case.** A shell location measured 8.0 mm six years ago, 7.4 mm two years ago and 7.0 mm now. Long-term rate is (8.0 − 7.0)/6 = 0.1667 mm/year. Recent rate is (7.4 − 7.0)/2 = 0.20 mm/year. With an established required thickness of 5.8 mm, margin is 1.2 mm. Selecting 0.20 gives a simple remaining-life estimate of six years. An inspection interval still must satisfy the applicable standard, the owner's program, risk and uncertainty; it is not automatically six years.

**Changed case.** A new measurement is 6.4 mm instead of 7.0. The recent rate becomes 0.50 mm/year and margin becomes 0.6 mm, giving 1.2 years. Investigate the accelerated loss and measurement comparability promptly. Keeping the old six-year estimate would hide the change. If an exercise separately supplies an approved interval rule of half remaining life and a two-year maximum, the mathematical result would be min(0.6, 2) = 0.6 year. This supplied exercise rule is not asserted as a universal tank interval.

Record both the calculation and its interpretation: rate selected, why it was selected, limitations, interval basis, operating assumptions and the person responsible for approval. A deferral is a managed decision requiring its applicable basis and authorization; a work-order date changed in scheduling software is not that decision.

## Q07 — Shell liquid-head calculations and the correct datum

A shell assessment starts with the applicable equation and its definitions. Do not combine a diameter in feet with a thickness in millimeters in an equation whose constant assumes inches. Identify whether the assessment concerns an entire shell course or a localized corroded area. The height reference differs, and a one-foot adjustment belonging to one case must not be imported into another.

For the supplied US-customary exercise relationship, a localized height is H = S × E × t / (2.6 × D × G), where S is psi, t inches, D feet and G specific gravity; H is feet above the defined assessment point. The corresponding supplied whole-course relationship adds one foot. These equations support the exercise's narrowly defined shell liquid-head check. They do not assess wind, seismic demand, pressure, vacuum, nozzles, settlement or the remaining tank components. Source and validity of S and E must be established, not guessed.

**Worked case.** Use S = 20,000 psi, E = 0.85, net thickness t = 0.25 in, D = 80 ft and G = 1.00. The numerator is 4,250 psi-in; the denominator's numerical value is 208. Localized H is 20.4327 ft. The whole-course result under its distinct supplied relationship is 21.4327 ft. A proposed 21 ft liquid head above the local assessment point is not supported by the localized calculation. Using the whole-course adjustment there would produce a false pass.

For the reverse problem, required net thickness is 2.6 × D × G × H / (S × E) for the localized exercise. At H = 24 ft, the result is 4,992/17,000 = 0.29365 in. If this exercise requires a future allowance of 0.030 in beyond that net value, the current target is 0.32365 in. Document whether the allowance has already been deducted from measured thickness so it is neither omitted nor subtracted twice.

**Changed case.** Keep geometry and thickness but change G from 1.00 to 0.80. Localized H becomes 25.5409 ft. This arithmetic illustrates density sensitivity; the approved level remains subject to the full tank review. A lower-density product does not authorize a fill level above the tank's roof or safeguards.

## Q08 — Local shell loss, pits and averaging

Averages can help characterize an area only when the governing method permits them and the sampling arrangement represents that area. A global average of a shell course can hide a thin local band. Distinguish broad local thinning, isolated pits, connected pits, grooves and crack-like indications. They do not all share the same acceptance route. Preserve the minimum, the distribution and the dimensions before deciding which method applies.

The controlled-reference task for this lesson requires the learner to locate the applicable local-area procedure, determine its critical assessment length, select the required measurement planes, calculate the prescribed average and compare every required condition. Keep pit treatment separate from the minimum used in an area calculation when the procedure requires that distinction. A passing average cannot cancel a failing minimum or a crack-like indication.

**Worked synthetic rule exercise.** For this exercise only, the approved assessment sheet requires an average of at least 0.200 in across five equally spaced readings and no individual non-pit reading below 0.120 in. The readings are 0.250, 0.220, 0.110, 0.230 and 0.240 in. Their mean is 1.050/5 = 0.210 in. The average passes but the minimum fails. The correct disposition is not accepted under the supplied rule. Document the failing location and request the appropriate repair or further assessment; do not add more thick readings to dilute it.

**Changed case.** Replace 0.110 with 0.150. The average becomes 0.218 in and both supplied numerical tests pass. That conclusion is limited to these two tests. It does not establish that the measurement spacing, area extent, weld condition or future corrosion allowance meets the real assessment basis.

Pitting evaluation also requires geometry, not depth alone. A line of pits can form a weakened path even when each pit looks small. Map depth, remaining ligament and aggregate dimensions along the prescribed assessment direction. If the indication could be a crack, stop using a pit acceptance shortcut and obtain suitable examination. During the practical exercise, the learner must show the selected measurement line on a sketch; quoting a total pit count without locations is insufficient.

## Q09 — Bottom examination and future thickness

A tank bottom can deteriorate from the product side, soil side or both. Sludge and water can concentrate internal attack; moisture, foundation conditions and protection-system performance affect the underside. A smooth-looking top surface does not establish underside condition. Plan examination for the expected loss shape, plate thickness, coating, welds, obstacles and the equipment's demonstrated capability.

Magnetic flux leakage is commonly used to screen bottom plates; UT can quantify selected areas. Neither method's name guarantees coverage or sizing accuracy. The procedure, personnel and equipment must suit the application. The July 2025 API 653 addendum points to newer RP 575 guidance for qualification and removes old Annex G content. Retain the map of scanned, confirmed and inaccessible areas.

**Current amendment check.** RP 575 Addendum 2 (June 2026) treats indication prove-up as essential and recommends quantitative UT for suspect scan areas. Establish coverage and supplemental examination for blind zones. Check the qualified procedure/equipment/examiner combination; a scanner pass is not a guaranteed thickness assessment. Record signal response separately from measured remaining thickness. Sensor lift-off and scanner speed are controlled qualification variables. Consult the amended Annex D for demonstration criteria rather than reusing a superseded qualification sheet.

**Worked coverage decision.** A contractor reports no alarms, but the coverage drawing omits five inaccessible plates. The owner has no evidence for those plates. Require an appropriate supplemental examination plan and identify the unexamined regions in the disposition; do not convert “no alarms” into “all plates acceptable.” If subsequent prove-up finds a deep, narrow pit missed by the scan, reassess the procedure's demonstrated capability and affected inspection scope before relying on the remaining results.

Projection is a separate step from measurement. In the supplied exercise, future bottom thickness equals the lesser post-repair remaining thickness from the two evaluated surfaces, minus the intended years multiplied by the sum of the applicable top-side and underside rates. Confirm that these inputs describe the same assessment basis and that overlapping loss has not been misrepresented. The applicable minimum can depend on location and configuration. A general bottom value is not automatically the critical-zone or annular requirement.

**Worked case.** Supplied post-repair values are 0.220 in and 0.200 in. The exercise rates are 0.004 in/year topside and 0.006 in/year underside. At eight years the projected result is 0.200 − 8(0.004 + 0.006) = 0.120 in. Against a supplied minimum of 0.100 in, the numerical margin is 0.020 in. This does not authorize an eight-year interval until coverage, rate basis, applicable interval limits and the other tank conditions are resolved.

**Changed case.** The underside rate is revised to 0.010 in/year. Eight-year thickness becomes 0.088 in, below the supplied minimum. The mathematical time to 0.100 in is (0.200 − 0.100)/0.014 = 7.1429 years, before any additional interval limit or margin. Do not round that into an eight-year pass. Document whether repair, a shorter approved interval or another justified assessment is required.

## Q10 — Annular region, shell junction and leak paths

The region near the shell carries a different structural role from a remote bottom plate. Identify whether the tank has an annular plate arrangement and establish the applicable material, thickness and geometry. Examine the shell-to-bottom junction, nearby repairs, corrosion, distortion and evidence of leakage. Local loads and settlement can affect this region; it is not enough to compare every bottom reading to one generic minimum.

At the external projection, inspect coating and sealant condition, trapped moisture, gaps and foundation contact where accessible. A fresh sealant bead can conceal evidence without solving its cause. Record staining, dampness and deposits before cleaning. Determine whether the source is product leakage, rainwater, a nearby line or another mechanism through a controlled investigation. Do not drill or probe a suspected live leak path to establish its source.

When a bottom is replaced or overlaid, review the complete configuration: retained plates, foundation changes, leak detection, penetrations, corrosion protection and the new load path. A second bottom changes access and may affect protection-system effectiveness. Maintain drawings of what remains between the new bottom and the foundation. An undocumented buried layer becomes a future inspection uncertainty.

**Worked case.** A record states “bottom minimum 0.100 in,” while the engineering assessment sheet specifies 0.160 in for a particular annular location. A reading there is 0.145 in. Applying the general minimum would be a false acceptance. The report must use the location-specific basis, identify the deficiency and preserve the related settlement and weld observations for engineering disposition.

**Changed case.** A repaired annular plate now measures 0.180 in, but its weld examination is incomplete. Thickness alone does not release the repair. Keep the examination hold open and trace the completed record to the specific weld, procedure and acceptance basis.

**Practice.** Sketch the shell, annular region, remote bottom, foundation and suspected leak location. State which criterion applies to each measurement. The assessor should be able to identify a mistakenly transferred criterion directly from the sketch and table.

## Q11 — Foundation and settlement surveys

Uniform settlement, overall tilt and localized differential settlement produce different observations. A survey should distinguish them rather than reducing the tank to its single lowest elevation. Establish a stable benchmark independent of the suspected movement. Record point identity, circumferential position, elevation, date, liquid level and relevant conditions. Repeat surveys at the same locations and datum. A changed benchmark can look like tank movement when none occurred.

Select the survey-point arrangement under the applicable procedure and standard. Adequate circumferential coverage is needed to identify the shape of settlement; bottom profiles are needed for localized depressions or bulges. The number of points is not chosen merely to fit a one-page form. Check for gaps where a local feature may lie between stations. Survey closure and instrument checks establish confidence in the data before structural interpretation.

**Worked data exercise.** Eight equally spaced points relative to a stable reference read 0, −4, −8, −12, −16, −12, −8 and −4 mm. Their smooth trend suggests a tilt-like component, but this observation alone neither fits a proper plane nor proves acceptability. If the fifth point changes to −34 mm while adjacent points stay the same, a new local depression is evident in addition to the broad trend. An average elevation would conceal that local departure.

Settlement evaluation uses the relevant geometry and acceptance method for the type of movement. Edge settlement, a near-shell bottom depression and a remote bottom depression are not interchangeable chart cases. Establish the measured length and depth, weld orientation and applicable conditions before reading a chart. Use the correct units and do not extrapolate beyond its scope. Where the method does not apply, obtain a qualified engineering assessment.

**Changed case.** A survey appears to show every point 10 mm lower, but the benchmark was reset after paving. The data do not yet prove uniform settlement. Reconcile the datum and resurvey as required. Reporting a precise displacement without a reliable reference is false precision.

**Practice.** Draw the eight-point plot, mark the altered fifth point and write a request for the additional survey information required. Include the benchmark history and liquid level; do not offer a numerical acceptance limit that was never supplied.

## Q12 — Roofs, seals, supports and drainage

Identify the roof type before planning access and inspection. A fixed roof transfers loads through its structure and supports; a floating roof depends on flotation and its associated compartments, seals, drains, guides and supports. Inspect the system as a set of interacting parts. Corrosion in a pontoon, blocked drainage or a damaged seal is not merely cosmetic because it can alter the roof's behavior and vapor control.

For fixed roofs, examine accessible plates, structural members, connections, columns and evidence of distortion or water accumulation. Establish safe access separately from the inspection result. For floating roofs, review deck and pontoon condition, leaks, roof level, drainage performance, seals and obstructions, support-leg settings, rolling ladder or guide arrangements and the relevant electrical connections. Procedures must address changes as the roof moves or lands. Do not walk onto a questionable surface to prove that it is sound.

Rainwater adds load. As a synthetic scale calculation, a 100 m² roof area retaining 0.10 m of water holds 10 m³, approximately 10,000 kg of fresh water. That arithmetic does not calculate the roof's capacity; it explains why a drainage failure requires attention. Draining must follow the approved arrangement and environmental controls, not an improvised opening that creates a new release path.

**Worked case.** A roof that previously floated level is now visibly low on one side after rain. The operator reports a blocked drain. Do not assume drainage is the only problem: assess the possible loss of buoyancy, compartment integrity and safe recovery plan. Restrict access and involve the responsible engineering and operating personnel. HSE's floating-roof safety bulletin emphasizes inspection and maintenance of buoyancy-related features; a visual appearance of flotation is not enough to establish reserve capability.

**Changed case.** Water is removed and the roof levels, but a pontoon leak was documented during the event. The level appearance does not close that defect. Preserve the finding and require its disposition before declaring the roof system restored.

**Practice.** Produce a roof inspection route showing accessible areas, inaccessible compartments, drainage path and stop conditions. The route must not require entry into an uncontrolled space or movement across an unverified roof surface.

## Q13 — Vents, overfill protection and auxiliary systems

A sound shell can fail when the tank's pressure or vacuum protection is defeated. Establish the installed vent types, service, settings and capacity basis. Inspect for obstruction, sticking, corrosion, damaged seals, incorrect weights, unauthorized modifications and discharge-path restrictions. A weight-loaded device must retain its specified configuration; adding weight to stop nuisance leakage changes its behavior. The inspection record must connect the device identity to the tank and its approved limits.

**Device-specific review.** A normal breathing vent and an emergency vent serve different demands; verify both against the system design. Under the authorized maintenance procedure, examine pallet movement, seats, seals, screens, hardware and any connected flame arrester. Suspect altered weights require verification against the specified setting. Consider icing even on a heated tank, where escaping vapor may condense and freeze. Record reassembly and operability checks. A visible clean discharge opening alone does not prove the pallet can move. These points follow RP 576 sections 4.3.2 and 6.7; they do not authorize removing protection from a tank in service.

Functional testing must follow a controlled procedure with suitable equipment and personnel. Record as-found condition before adjustment and as-left results afterward. A cleaned device is not automatically a tested device. Reinstallation requires the correct orientation, connections and removal of temporary isolation or test arrangements under the operating procedure. Where a vent is unavailable, establish the authorized operating response rather than continuing routine filling or withdrawal as though nothing changed.

Overfill protection is a separate protection function. Review the level-measurement path, independent protective features where required, alarm response, automatic actions, bypass status and proof-test records. The CSB's CAPECO investigation illustrates how inadequate level control and safeguards can culminate in a major tank overfill. Do not treat a gauge display as proof that the complete protective chain works.

**Worked case.** A level transmitter shows 60 percent, but its last three recorded values are identical despite a known receipt. The high-level alarm shares that transmitter. Two screen indications do not establish two independent measurements. Follow the operating response for unreliable level measurement and investigate before continued receipt. The inspector records the shared dependency and missing functional evidence, rather than simply marking both icons present.

**Changed case.** A separate high-high trip was tested successfully, but the shutdown valve is currently bypassed for maintenance. The earlier test does not prove protection in the current configuration. Report the bypass and require the responsible operating authority's disposition. Do not remove or alter a bypass without authorization.

## Q14 — Soil-side corrosion and cathodic protection

Cathodic protection addresses electrochemical corrosion under conditions where its protective current reaches the steel surface. Recognize the system type, intended protected surface, reference-electrode arrangement, monitoring points and operating records. Galvanic and impressed-current systems achieve protection differently. Neither a rectifier light nor one voltage value establishes adequate protection across an entire bottom.

Have competent corrosion personnel establish the applicable acceptance criteria and measurement procedure. Record the electrode type, position, polarity, operating state, measurement timing and environmental conditions with each potential. Electrical resistance effects can influence measured values; a reading without its method is not interpretable against every criterion. Avoid changing output settings to make a number look acceptable. Investigate outages, damaged cables, deteriorated connections, changed foundation layers and possible shielding.

**Worked synthetic record.** A monitoring sheet specifies a project criterion based on an instant-off measurement at designated points. A technician records an on-potential at a convenient perimeter point and marks the entire system acceptable. The defect is the mismatch between method and criterion. Obtain the required measurements and competent review; do not compare unlike values simply because they share units.

**Changed case.** The method is correct, but the reference electrode's condition check fails. Treat the affected results as unverified. Restore a valid measurement arrangement and repeat the necessary checks. The previous favorable values cannot support continued acceptance without resolution.

Corrosion protection does not restore lost metal. If a bottom was already below its applicable criterion, installing or repairing CP does not repair that thickness. It may change a justified future-rate assessment, but only through a supported evaluation. Similarly, an internal lining protects a different exposure surface; it does not establish soil-side protection.

## Q15 — Lining selection, application and inspection

A lining system must suit the product, contaminants, temperature, substrate and operating cycle. Establish the approved material system and manufacturer's instructions, including preparation, mixing, environmental conditions, application thickness, cure and inspection. Products that share a generic resin name are not automatically interchangeable. A change in service requires compatibility review even when the lining still looks intact.

Before application, document steel condition and required repairs. A coating cannot be credited as structural steel unless a specific engineered system and basis permit that role. Remove contaminants and prepare the surface to the specified condition. Record environmental readings at the required locations and times. Condensation, salts, inadequate profile, poor mixing, expired material and incomplete cure can produce failures that are not visible at first glance.

Inspect the installed system using the approved thickness, continuity and adhesion methods appropriate to that system. Select holiday-test equipment and settings under the applicable procedure; excessive voltage can damage a lining. Record defects by location, repair them by the approved method and repeat the specified checks. Ventilation and exposure controls remain essential during preparation and application. A cured-looking surface does not prove the tank atmosphere is safe.

**Worked case.** The synthetic application sheet requires steel at least 3 °C above dew point and a dry-film band of 400–600 micrometers. The steel is 18 °C and dew point 17 °C. Stop application under that sheet: the 1 °C margin fails. After conditions are corrected, readings of 450, 520 and 680 micrometers include an excessive thickness. More material is not automatically better; the affected area needs the specified disposition.

**Changed case.** All thickness readings pass, but the cure record is missing and the tank is scheduled for filling. Hold release pending the required cure verification. Thickness and cure are distinct acceptance conditions.

## Q16 — Damage mechanisms and selecting the right examination

Mechanism reasoning starts with material, environment, stress and operating history. Atmospheric and soil corrosion commonly involve moisture exposure; deposits and water pockets can create localized conditions unlike the bulk product. Insulation can retain water and conceal external loss. MIC is a possible contributor in suitable conditions, but pit shape alone does not prove microbial causation. Preserve samples and operating evidence through an appropriate investigation.

Different chemical services create different concerns. Caustic service can involve metal loss or stress-corrosion cracking depending on conditions. Ethanol-related cracking requires a service-specific review rather than reliance on a thickness trend alone. Sulfuric acid behavior is sensitive to conditions, including concentration and temperature; a dilution event can invalidate the previous corrosion expectation. Do not transfer a safe-service assumption from one concentration or temperature to another without a competent corrosion review.

Select examinations capable of finding the suspected form of damage. Thickness mapping addresses loss distribution; crack-sensitive examination is needed where cracking is credible. A report saying “UT acceptable” is insufficient unless it identifies the technique and what it can detect. Include welds, stress concentrations, waterlines, injection or mixing regions and stagnant areas as appropriate. Where mechanisms can coexist, do not force all findings into one diagnosis.

**Worked case.** A caustic tank has stable thickness readings but a fine linear indication beside a repaired weld. Treat the indication as a potential crack requiring characterization, not proof that the gauge is wrong. Review material, weld history, stress and service conditions. A satisfactory remaining-life calculation for wall loss does not accept a crack.

**Changed case.** A tank's acid concentration changes during an upset and subsequent loss accelerates. Reassess the mechanism and rate. Using the old rate because it covers more years gives unjustified confidence. Record the upset as a change in the prediction basis.

**Practice.** For each case, state the suspected mechanism, one confirming piece of evidence, one alternative explanation and an appropriate examination or specialist referral. “Corrosion” alone is too broad to direct a useful inspection.

## Q17 — Materials, toughness and brittle-fracture review

Strength and toughness answer different questions. A plate can satisfy a tensile-strength requirement yet resist crack propagation poorly under a particular temperature, thickness and loading condition. Identify material specification and grade, thickness, heat treatment, design metal temperature, service history and available impact-test evidence. Do not replace missing toughness information with a statement that the steel is strong or has been in service for many years.

Use the applicable tank-standard evaluation route and controlled material tables. Trace the selected material group and temperature/thickness condition. If an exemption is claimed, show every condition supporting it. If impact testing is required, verify specimen identity, orientation, size, test temperature and the applicable average and individual criteria. A passing average alone may not satisfy the requirement. Unknown material requires the specified investigation or engineering route rather than a convenient group assignment.

**Worked synthetic impact record.** The exercise specifies a three-specimen average of at least 27 J and each specimen at least 20 J at the required test temperature. Results are 35, 30 and 18 J. The average is 83/3 = 27.67 J, but the individual result fails. Reject acceptance under the supplied criteria and follow the permitted retest or material disposition process. Do not delete the low result as an outlier without a justified authorized basis.

**Changed case.** Results are 31, 29 and 25 J, but the test was performed 20 °C warmer than required. Numerical energy values alone do not establish acceptance at the required temperature. Obtain valid evidence or the applicable qualified assessment.

Hydrotesting and service changes deserve particular attention because they alter loading or temperature conditions. Evaluate brittle-fracture considerations before the test, not after filling begins. A prior hydrotest is evidence with a date and condition; subsequent deterioration, repairs or colder service can require renewed review. Record the actual assessment route and limits.

## Q18 — Welding records and production control

A WPS directs the production weld. A PQR records the procedure qualification test's actual variables and results. A welder qualification record establishes the individual's demonstrated range for the applicable process and variables. These records are related but not interchangeable. An experienced welder cannot compensate for an unsupported procedure, and a qualified procedure cannot substitute for the welder's qualification.

Review the intended joint before welding: base materials, thickness, joint design, process, filler, position, backing, preheat and interpass controls, heat treatment where required and examination. Compare the WPS with its supporting PQR and the applicable qualification rules. Then compare the production task with the welder's qualified range and continuity record. Verify record ownership, certification and identity. Do not alter a PQR's historical actual values to make a new WPS fit.

**Worked supplied-range exercise.** A controlled qualification review states that PQR P-37 supports the WPS for base thickness 6–24 mm and the named process. The proposed repair includes a 28 mm member. That task is outside the supplied range. Obtain a valid supporting qualification or revised approved arrangement before welding. A drawing note that says “qualified welder” does not resolve the procedure range.

**Changed case.** The thickness is 20 mm, but production changes from the qualified process to a different process for speed. Stop for the applicable procedure and personnel review. Do not assume all arc processes are equivalent. The same reasoning applies to any variable whose change requires requalification under the actual code.

During work, track welder identity and weld location, consumable control, joint preparation, fit-up, cleaning, parameter compliance and hold-point release. Preserve repair excavation and examination records. If an unauthorized deviation occurs, identify affected welds, hold them and obtain disposition; do not erase the deviation by writing the intended parameters into the completed record.

## Q19 — NDE selection, procedure and acceptance

Separate three questions: was the examination method suitable, was it performed correctly, and does the result meet the applicable acceptance criteria? ASME Section V supplies examination requirements when invoked; the referencing construction or inspection standard supplies relevant acceptance requirements. A report that only says “per Section V, acceptable” may omit the actual acceptance basis.

Visual examination establishes surface condition and geometry within its access limits. PT finds discontinuities open to a suitably prepared surface; contamination or an inappropriate process can mask them. MT applies to ferromagnetic material and requires an appropriate field arrangement, with directions selected to reveal relevant discontinuities. RT records a projected image and has orientation and technique limitations. UT techniques must suit the target; simple thickness measurement is not automatically a weld-flaw examination.

Check procedure identification and revision, personnel qualification, equipment checks, surface and environmental conditions, coverage, location references, indication characterization and the acceptance document. For radiography, verify image traceability, required coverage and technique-quality evidence such as IQIs, density or applicable digital requirements, markers and backscatter controls. A clear-looking image does not establish the prescribed sensitivity. Radiation work remains restricted to qualified authorized personnel and its controlled area.

**Worked case.** A weld report covers 900 mm of a 1,200 mm required length, but its conclusion says “weld accepted.” The examined portion's result does not close the missing 300 mm. Identify the uncovered segment and complete or formally resolve the required coverage before release. A signature cannot supply missing examination.

**Changed case.** Coverage is complete, but the report uses acceptance criteria from a different component code. Return it for evaluation against the correct basis. Do not discard valid raw data unnecessarily; determine whether it can support a correct reevaluation and whether technique differences require re-examination.

## Q20 — Repair design, geometry and execution

Classify the proposed work under the applicable standard: repair, alteration, reconstruction or another defined activity. The classification influences design, examination, testing and documentation. Write the scope precisely enough to identify affected plates, welds, nozzles, supports and protection systems. The phrase “repair as necessary” is not a complete engineering instruction for a critical junction.

The approved repair package must establish material, dimensions, joint details, spacing, welding, examination and testing. For replacement or lap-welded plates, inspect the actual standard's permitted locations, size and thickness limits and separation from existing features. A patch that fits the visible corrosion outline may be too close to a seam or too small for its approved detail. Record distances from the defined edges or weld toes; a center-to-center measurement cannot replace an edge-spacing requirement.

**Worked synthetic drawing check.** The approved detail requires a 300 mm minimum plate dimension, 75 mm minimum edge separation from a named feature and a 6 mm fillet. Proposed values are 320 mm, 60 mm and 6 mm. Two values pass and one fails; the repair is not ready to proceed. Refer the spacing conflict to the responsible designer. Do not move the patch in the field without checking what other constraints that movement changes.

**Changed case.** The spacing is corrected to 90 mm, but the welder lays a 4 mm fillet. The approved drawing has not been achieved. Record the actual condition and obtain the specified correction and re-examination. Do not change the as-built drawing to 4 mm merely to match the work.

Hot tapping is a specialist operation requiring its applicable engineering and operating controls. Determine whether the tank condition, contents, level, material, thickness, nozzle location and procedure support the operation. A successful spacing calculation is only one check. Never use this course's simulated exercise as authorization to perform a live hot tap.

## Q21 — Disassembly and reconstruction

Reconstruction requires traceability through dismantling, transport and re-erection. Mark reusable components before separation and preserve a map that links each piece to its original location, material and condition. Identify cuts, damaged edges, distortion and repairs. A pile of unmarked shell segments is not an equivalent record to an intact tank. Establish the permitted reuse and design basis before committing to a reconstruction plan.

Check the new site, foundation, service and geometry. An old tank moved to a different location does not automatically retain a valid design basis for the new environment. Review the applicable reconstruction requirements, material limits, new welds, dimensional tolerances, examination and test plan. Controlled tables distinguish requirements by component and condition; read the actual row and notes instead of applying a remembered value to all plates.

**Worked case.** A dismantled tank has twelve shell segments but only ten are linked to the material and original-course map. The two unidentified segments cannot silently inherit the neighboring segment's identification. Hold their reuse pending an authorized identification or material disposition route. The other segments may proceed through unaffected review steps, but the complete shell release remains unresolved.

**Changed case.** All segments are identified, but the proposed service has higher specific gravity and a lower minimum temperature. Recalculate and evaluate the new service and toughness requirements. “It passed at the old site” is evidence about the old conditions, not approval of the new ones.

Prepare an as-built dossier with final component location, repairs, new joints, qualifications, examination, dimensions, testing and approved operating limits. The dossier must make future inspection possible without relying on the reconstruction crew's memory.

## Q22 — Hydrostatic and leak testing

First determine which tests the applicable repair or reconstruction requires and whether any proposed exemption satisfies every condition and approval requirement. A leak test and a hydrostatic structural test serve different purposes. Passing a localized leak test does not automatically waive a required hydrotest. A test exemption must have a documented basis; schedule pressure is not that basis.

Plan test-water suitability and disposal, fill stages, level measurement, hold times, inspection positions, settlement monitoring, leak response, temperature, venting, exclusion areas and emergency arrangements. Verify the shell and foundation are suitable for the intended water load. Water can be denser than the service product. Establish stop conditions before starting and keep unauthorized people out of the exposure area.

**Worked scale exercise.** A cylindrical tank with diameter 20 m and test-water depth 10 m contains approximately π × 10² × 10 = 3,141.6 m³. At 1,000 kg/m³, the water mass is about 3.14 million kg. At the same depth, an SG 0.80 product would impose approximately 80 percent of that liquid weight. This comparison explains why a routine service history does not by itself approve the hydrotest load. It does not calculate foundation capacity or shell acceptance.

**Changed case.** During a staged fill, settlement exceeds the approved stop criterion. Stop according to the plan, establish the safe condition and obtain engineering review. Do not continue to full level hoping the foundation will settle evenly. Retain the stage, readings and time history.

Local pneumatic or vacuum-box methods require their own controlled parameters and safety arrangements. Never pressurize an atmospheric tank by improvising from a pressure-vessel test ratio. Stored energy, weak surfaces, blocked vents and test boundaries require explicit evaluation. After testing, verify restoration: remove temporary arrangements, reinstall devices, complete drying or cleaning as required and document the final operating configuration.

## Q23 — Integrating findings into a disposition

The weakest unresolved condition can control disposition even when most readings pass. Assemble findings by location and consequence, not just by report date. Reconcile thickness, cracks, settlement, roof, venting, protection and repair records against the same operating envelope. A shell calculation supporting one fill height and an operating instruction allowing a higher level are a conflict to resolve before release.

Distinguish observation, interpretation, recommendation and authorization. “Linear indication at weld W-4” is an observation; “potential crack requiring characterization” is an interpretation; “hold service pending examination and engineering review” is a recommendation; the owner-authorized decision is recorded separately. Do not write a final acceptance statement while a critical action remains open.

**Worked case.** A shell assessment supports 22 ft, the roof inspection is acceptable and a bottom projection supports the proposed interval. A vent test is overdue and an open repair finding concerns the shell junction. These favorable results do not collectively cancel the unresolved items. State the open conditions, required actions and authorization path. A report should tell operations what has and has not been established.

**Changed case.** All technical actions close, but the control-system maximum level remains 25 ft. The actual operating configuration still conflicts with the 22 ft limit. Verify the authorized setting change, labeling and operating instructions before the defined release. A drawing revision alone is insufficient if the active controls remain unchanged.

When uncertainty is significant, present it explicitly: missing coverage, uncertain rate, unknown material, questionable datum or unverified method. The correct response may be further inspection, repair, restricted service or specialist assessment under the governing process. Do not manufacture a precise remaining life to avoid documenting uncertainty.

## Q24 — Records, follow-up and the learning credential

A useful final report allows a qualified person to reconstruct the decision. Include tank identity and governing basis, inspection date and conditions, methods and personnel, location maps, raw and interpreted results, calculations with units and assumptions, source criteria, limitations, repairs and examinations, testing, approved limits and next actions. Link attachments by stable identifiers. Preserve as-found and as-left conditions rather than replacing unfavorable data with the corrected result.

Create an action register with owner, required evidence, due date and closure authority. Use status words consistently: proposed, approved, performed, verified and closed are different states. A contractor invoice proves billing, not technical closure. A future work order is not a completed repair. A conditional operating decision must state its conditions and duration under the responsible authority's process.

**Worked case.** Action A-7 requires re-examination of weld W-12. The register says closed because the welder reported completion, but no examination report exists. Reopen the evidentiary gap without erasing the repair record. Obtain the specified examination and acceptance record, then have the designated authority close the action. The timeline should preserve both the original omission and its correction.

**Changed case.** The examination report exists but refers to W-21. Resolve identity before closure. Similar numbering is not sufficient traceability. A corrected clerical link is acceptable only when supported by the underlying records; do not simply change the weld number without evidence.

The VNDRLY learning credential requires the complete course assessments and practical demonstrations specified in the instructor packet. It records demonstrated learning, not approval of any real tank. Missed critical decisions require remediation and reassessment even when an aggregate score is high. Actual employer authorization and external qualifications remain separate. Preserve the learner's original attempts, corrective work and final result so the credential has an honest basis.
