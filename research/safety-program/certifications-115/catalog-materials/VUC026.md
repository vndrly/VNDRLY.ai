# VUC026 — delivery, practical assessment and credential guide

## Audience, entry and outcomes

Learners need demonstrated routine rigging competence equivalent to this curriculum's Level I prerequisites: identify and inspect slings/hardware, select the applicable hitch rating, assemble routine connections, communicate, conduct a controlled lift and land safely. Review records and observe an entry task; possessing a card alone does not waive the demonstration. Learners must interpret the task language, units, drawings and elementary arithmetic. Provide calculator and trigonometry instruction where needed. Remediate gaps before advanced live tasks.

Outcomes A01–A16 correspond to the coursebook chapters: define authority; determine weight; locate center of gravity; determine reactions; resolve sling forces; select assemblies; install hardware; analyze hoists/blocks; select below-hook devices; execute level lifting; recognize rotation limits; perform planned rotation; transfer supports; communicate/land/respond; document a plan; perform an integrated task. Every outcome has instruction, formative practice and independent assessment.

The instructor/evaluator must have documented training and experience appropriate to the equipment and advanced tasks, recognize hazards and be authorized to stop the activity. The actual training-fixture and rotation plans require appropriately qualified planning and engineering where needed. Use authorized operators and qualified signal personnel as required. Do not assign one person distracting paperwork while that person is responsible for directing an active lift.

## Delivery schedule and laboratory preparation

Planned instruction: 32 hours excluding breaks, not an externally mandated duration. Day 1: A01–A05 instruction/worked problems 240 minutes; guided calculation practice 120; inspection/selection review 90; corrections 30 =480. Day 2: A06–A09 instruction/demonstrations 180; connection and device practice 180; A10 level-lift laboratory 120 =480. Day 3: A11–A13 instruction/models 180; approved rotation and transfer practice 240; A14 emergency/landing discussion 60 =480. Day 4: A15–A16 plan review 60; individual practical assessments 240; final 90; targeted correction/reassessment and records 90 =480. Increase the schedule for cohort size or learner needs; the plan does not authorize shortening individual demonstrations to fit a day.

Required teaching resources: this coursebook/checks; protected final/key; calculators in degree mode; drawings and verified load data; actual applicable equipment manuals; current site plan; synthetic load/CG problems; segregated defective specimens/photos. Equipment: inspected identified slings covering chain, wire, web, round, mesh and fiber; compatible shackles/hooks/links/adjustables/eyebolts/hoist rings; torque tool where specified; appropriate hoists, block/reeving demonstration, beam/trolley/clamp samples; jack/blocking/skate demonstration; barriers, radios, signal chart, measuring tools, angle indicator and dunnage. Specialized vacuum/magnet/grapple examples may be demonstrations or documented case studies; record that scope rather than implying operation qualification.

Live practicals require a low-energy stable training load, verified lifting points/CG, suitable rated lifting system/supports, and a **separately approved rotation fixture and staged plan**. Do not build a live fixture from the coursebook's hypothetical mathematical models. Before delivery, the qualified planner supplies: fixture/load identities and actual weights; lifting-point structural basis; allowed angle range and staged force demands; component/configuration ratings; installation/torque instructions; initial/final support and restraint; orientation tolerance; trial clearance; exclusion area; recovery path; stop criteria; operator/signal roles. The evaluator verifies the physical setup against that plan. Missing approval means the live station is not delivered and a full practical award cannot be issued.

Only one active lift team operates in a controlled area. All others remain in designated observation space. Check weather, lighting, floor/support capacity, overhead hazards, equipment inspections and emergency communication. Use training loads well within the approved system's limits; do not conduct learner proof tests or maximum-capacity demonstrations. Unsafe scenario injections are verbal/cards or disabled bench specimens, never secretly defective active gear.

## Evaluation rules

Complete all 32 formative responses and correct misconceptions with the relevant chapter and changed practice. Final: 48 constructed responses, 96 points, 90 minutes plus reasonable accommodations. Score each 2 for correct safe action/calculation with necessary reason, 1 for safe but incomplete, 0 for wrong/absent/unsafe. Pass at least 77/96 and 4/6 in each of the sixteen domains. A critical unsafe endorsement overrides a numeric pass: knowingly using overloaded/unidentified/damaged equipment; entering beneath an unsecured load; intentionally defeating retention; improvising a rotation beyond approved geometry; continuing after lost required communication/stop; relying on an unsecured jack-supported load; unauthorized in-air rigging adjustment. Document the exact response and correction.

Each of twelve practical stations has five criteria rated 0 unsafe/not shown, 1 safe with prompting or incomplete, 2 independently correct. Pass each station at least 8/10 and every starred criterion at 2. Stop any unsafe act. No averaging away a failed station. Every listed physical subtask must be observed; a discussion does not substitute for the level lift, controlled rotation, connection, inspection or support-transfer demonstration. Cases involving specialized devices not operated are recorded as selection/awareness only.

Preserve attempts. After targeted instruction and supervised practice, use changed equipment/data and a changed applicable station for reassessment. The separate examiner file contains sixteen changed three-part domain cases; score the same way and require at least 4/6 per reassessed domain without critical errors. Reassess the integrated station when a defect affects overall planning. After a second failure, document an individual development plan before another attempt. Do not coach answers during the final or simply repeat identical questions until memorized.

## P01 — Establish scope and validate the object

Task: compare an actual training load with its drawing and a work order. A second scenario adds undocumented contents. Tools: drawing, inspection access, synthetic roles sheet.

Criteria: 1 identifies task/roles; 2 compares dimensions/attachments and load identity; 3 distinguishes verified from assumed data; 4*holds the changed/unverified configuration; 5 identifies the required qualified resolution. Expected: original paperwork does not cover new contents or rotation. Record the specific hold, not just 'understands safety.'

## P02 — Calculate weight and center

Task: calculate a supplied composite body's weight and two-coordinate center using actual problem units, then explain a changed attachment. Use the original coursebook cases for instruction and changed numbers for assessment.

Criteria: 1 consistent units and defensible inputs; 2 correct volume/weight arithmetic; 3 correct weighted moments; 4 checks physical plausibility and attachment effects; 5*does not treat unresolved material/contents as verified. Expected: sums of weights/moments with coordinates and units, plus a hold for missing inputs. Preserve the worksheet and calculator checks.

## P03 — Reactions and bridle forces

Task: solve two-support eccentric reactions and an unequal-angle bridle, then inspect a three-leg proposal with unproven sharing.

Criteria: 1 correct free-body model; 2 moment and force balance; 3 correct angle reference and trigonometry; 4 checks component demands against actual configuration limits; 5*rejects negative tensile reactions or unsupported equal sharing. Expected: correct answer is insufficient without a valid model. Preserve diagrams and independent balance checks.

## P04 — Inspect and select sling families

Task: examine identified chain, wire, web, round, mesh and fiber examples plus isolated defects; select a hitch for the approved training task using actual data.

Criteria: 1 reads identity/rating; 2 checks full accessible body/terminations and material-specific damage; 3*isolates every unsafe/unidentified specimen; 4 selects compatible hitch/angle/bend/protection; 5 records item and finding. Observe each family individually. Expected: no use of generic running-rope criteria for sling removal or vertical rating for a choker.

## P05 — Install compatible connections

Task: install the plan's shackle and lifting-point connection, demonstrate the specified torque/retention and identify bench faults in a turnbuckle, hook and compression termination.

Criteria: 1 component/thread/pin compatibility; 2 correct seating/alignment; 3 correct specified torque/retention; 4*rejects side/tip loading or unapproved termination; 5 documents manual/configuration and final inspection. Expected: no hand-tightening substitution or construction sling-eye clips. No live loaded adjustment is used as a training shortcut.

## P06 — Hoists and block support

Task: trace a demonstration reeving arrangement, calculate an ideal return-block reaction, inspect the actual hoist/support and demonstrate unloaded controls followed by the approved low-load task.

Criteria: 1 correct rope path/supporting parts; 2 distinguishes ideal calculation from actual rated data; 3 verifies upstream support and hoist condition; 4*stops on side pull, unverified support or abnormal holding; 5 demonstrates normal stop/isolation and recovery communication. Record which manual, lever, electric or air model was actually operated; other types remain awareness-only.

## P07 — Below-hook device suitability

Task: choose among a beam, clamp and vacuum/magnetic device for supplied cases using actual instructions. Include incompatible thickness, eccentric beam loading and unsuitable contact surface.

Criteria: 1 identifies permitted load/material/configuration; 2 includes device weight and support effects; 3 evaluates contact/locking/power-loss limitations; 4*rejects an incompatible case despite sufficient nominal capacity; 5 states inspection and exclusion needs. Expected: three independent selection decisions with cited product evidence. This station does not confer all-device operating authority.

## P08 — Level-lift execution

Task: independently rig the approved eccentric training load and execute briefing, take-up, trial, movement and landing within the plan's measured tolerance. Use a safe scenario card to introduce an unexpected tilt before continuation.

Criteria: 1 verifies calculations/installed configuration; 2 establishes exclusion/signals; 3 checks trial balance and clearances; 4*stops and safely lowers/reviews the discrepant case; 5 lands stably and releases correctly. Record actual clearance and orientation against the planner's tolerances. Successful movement alone does not pass poor decisions.

## P09 — Rotation planning and model limits

Task: calculate the A11 warning model at changed assigned angles, identify its invalid range, and compare it with the approved real fixture plan.

Criteria: 1 correctly transforms coordinates; 2 balances moments/reactions; 3 identifies slack/negative/singular conditions; 4*rejects using the hypothetical model as a live 90-degree plan; 5 explains actual fixture stages/force directions and recovery. Expected: mathematical limits and actual approvals are kept distinct. Preserve the plotted values.

## P10 — Controlled rotation performance

Task: independently conduct the validated fixture's rotation from its approved start to finish through all hold points, then respond to a verbally injected early-slack discrepancy.

Criteria: 1 verifies fixture/points/rigging and approved plan; 2 briefs coordinated commands/exclusion; 3 checks each stage against expected orientation and support; 4*stops and follows safe recovery for the discrepancy without improvised opposing movement; 5 secures final support before releasing. Record actual angles and each hold-point observation. No tabletop-only pass; no arbitrary angle range imposed by this course on an unvalidated fixture.

## P11 — Jack, secure and transfer

Task: use an approved low-height stable load to demonstrate jack placement, controlled raise and prompt securing, then evaluate a skate route and narrow-support tipping case.

Criteria: 1 verifies jack/support/contact/rating; 2 follows planned increments and safe blocking method; 3*never depends on pressure alone or enters the crush area; 4 evaluates route/support polygon/changed load share; 5 lowers/removes equipment in the planned sequence. Expected: each new support state is checked before transfer. Actual rolling may occur only with its separately approved setup.

## P12 — Integrated plan, communication and closeout

Task: submit the complete advanced training-task plan, brief the team, execute its approved level-lift portion and demonstrate a communication-loss stop. Explain rotation as its separate approved operation, then inspect/store equipment and complete records.

Criteria: 1 reproducible inputs/calculations/component schedule; 2 clear roles/stages/contingency; 3*safe communication-loss and changed-load hold; 4 controlled landing/removal; 5 complete observations/defect reporting and honest qualification scope. Expected: no use of a generic certificate to approve an unassessed complex task.

# VNDRLY/U Rigger Level II — learner coursebook

This original course develops advanced rigging decisions and supervised performance. It is not a CCO examination, CCO certification, an engineering license, or permission to perform any particular lift. Employer task-specific qualification, actual equipment instructions and the approved lift plan remain necessary. Numerical cases are instructional models, not executable lift plans. Pounds in the calculations denote force; use consistent units throughout.

## A01 — Establish the task and decision authority

A routine known-load arrangement can become an advanced task when its center of gravity is unknown, the load must rotate, different lifting devices share the load, or the attachment geometry changes during movement. Advanced rigging starts by identifying those changes, not by choosing larger slings. The rigger must establish weight, dimensions, lifting points, structural integrity, travel path, landing arrangement and each stage of support. A machine can have enough rated capacity while its load is inadequately supported or its rigging is overloaded.

Before work, obtain the current load drawing, equipment data and plan. Compare the drawing with the object: attached machinery, residual liquid, insulation and temporary steel can invalidate the stated weight and center of gravity. Identify the lift director, operator, signal person, riggers and person authorized to approve changes. Explain the hold points and the stop signal to everybody. If a required fact is absent, record the uncertainty and obtain a qualified resolution. A guess written on a form remains a guess.

The employer's determination of a qualified rigger concerns the particular work. A certificate alone does not demonstrate capability for every complex rigging operation. Similarly, this course does not qualify a person to design lifting lugs, weld repairs, conduct personnel lifting or direct a multiple-crane lift. Those tasks require their own applicable expertise and controls. In construction, a multiple-crane lift requires a qualified plan, appropriate lift direction and a meeting with the involved workers; engineering expertise must be supplied when the qualified planner determines it is needed.

Worked decision: an approved plan covers lifting an empty pump skid level. The crew now wants to stand the skid on end with the pump still attached. Stop the change. The rotation alters force directions, load shares, stability, lifting-point loading and possible leakage. The original plan is evidence for neither the new geometry nor the pump's retention. Obtain an approved staged plan and verify the actual object before proceeding.

Application: mark every assumption on a sample work order as verified, unresolved or outside your authority. Name the person who can resolve each unresolved item. Do not begin tensioning merely because the crane is waiting.

## A02 — Determine weight with defensible inputs

Prefer traceable manufacturer or engineering information for the actual configuration, or a suitable verified measurement. Calculation from dimensions and density is useful only when the material, shape and contents are known. Break the object into simple solids, calculate their volumes, subtract verified voids and add attachments. Record dimensions, units, density source and uncertainty. Use external dimensions for a solid only if it really is solid; use wall dimensions for a hollow section. Never assume a vessel is drained because its valve is open.

For a rectangular solid, volume is length times width times thickness. Convert all dimensions before multiplying. For a cylindrical solid, volume is pi times radius squared times length. For a hollow cylinder, subtract the inner cylinder volume from the outer. Weight equals volume times weight density. These formulas do not establish lifting-point strength or the object's ability to remain intact when supported at proposed points.

Worked case: an exercise supplies a solid plate 36 by 24 by 1 inch and a hypothetical weight density of 490 pounds per cubic foot. Volume is 864 cubic inches divided by 1,728, or 0.5 cubic foot. Plate weight is 245 pounds. A documented 80-pound attachment produces a 325-pound assembly before rigging. The supplied density is a problem input, not a substitute for identifying a real material. If an 80-pound attachment is merely estimated, the total is not verified.

Include the weight carried by every part of the system. The crane's load accounting may include hook block and below-hook devices; the sling below a lifting beam does not carry the same components as the suspension above it. Draw boundaries and list the weight acting below each boundary. Do not deduct rigging twice or omit a spreader because it is reusable equipment.

Common mistakes are mixing inches with feet, confusing pounds with kilograms, forgetting trapped contents, and applying one density to a composite object. A successful trial lift does not validate an underestimated weight: the system may already be overloaded. Resolve weight before the trial.

Application: calculate a known component, then list the evidence needed for every addition and subtraction. If the object does not match the documented configuration, hold the lift rather than rounding the uncertainty away.

## A03 — Locate center of gravity in more than one direction

Center of gravity is the point through which the assembly's resultant weight acts in a uniform gravity field. For components with known weights and locations, choose a datum and calculate the weighted average: xCG equals the sum of each weight times its x coordinate divided by total weight. Repeat independently for y and z. State the coordinate system on the drawing. A component at a negative coordinate contributes a negative moment; changing the datum consistently changes coordinates, not the physical result.

Worked case: a 2,000-pound component acts at x=1 foot and a 1,000-pound component at x=7 feet. Total weight is 3,000 pounds. The moment sum is 9,000 pound-feet; xCG is 3 feet. Halfway between the outer ends would give the wrong answer. If those components have y coordinates 0 and 3 feet, yCG is 1 foot. The hook and connection geometry must account for the lateral offset as well as the longitudinal one.

For a freely suspended stable load, the combined center of gravity settles below its suspension. If the hook is horizontally displaced from the resultant center of gravity, the load may move or tilt as support is removed. Do not use people or a tag line to force an unstable load to appear level. A high center of gravity can create tipping hazards during transfer to skates or a landing cradle even if the suspended phase is stable.

Use documented component centers, not their outer edges, in the moment calculation. Account for movable contents and attachments. If liquid can shift, a single static center may not describe the full operation; a qualified plan must address that movement. A trial at minimum planned clearance can confirm expected balance under a verified plan, but unexpected tilt means lower safely and investigate. Never reach beneath the load to adjust a sling.

Application: draw the calculated center on two views of an assembly. Explain how removing a component changes both weight and center. Check the answer lies within the range of positive component coordinates; an impossible result often exposes a unit or sign error.

## A04 — Calculate support reactions and check the model

For a rigid body with two vertical supports, vertical forces must sum to the weight and moments must balance. If supports are at x=0 and x=L and weight W acts at x=a, the right reaction is W times a divided by L. The left reaction is W minus the right. These are vertical reactions, not automatically inclined-sling tensions. The model assumes the specified supports, a rigid body and no other applied forces or accelerations.

Worked case: a 12,000-pound load is supported at 0 and 8 feet, with its center at 2 feet. Right reaction is 12,000 times 2 divided by 8 =3,000 pounds; left reaction is 9,000 pounds. Selecting two 6,000-pound components because their sum is 12,000 would overload the left connection. Add the appropriate hardware and local geometry effects for each load path.

Verify the arithmetic two ways: reactions sum to 12,000, and right reaction times 8 feet equals weight times 2 feet. If the center lies outside the supports, one computed reaction becomes negative. A sling cannot push downward to supply a negative tensile reaction. That result identifies an invalid or unstable proposed support arrangement, not a permissible negative load.

Three or four legs do not necessarily share equally. Length differences, load stiffness, lifting-point elevations and elastic stretch affect sharing. Equilibrium alone may not determine every reaction. Use the applicable rated assembly data and a qualified load-sharing analysis rather than dividing by the number of legs. A slack fourth leg does not contribute simply because it is connected.

Application: draw separate free-body diagrams for the load, lifting beam and top connection. Label every force and verify that the assumed force directions match the actual geometry. Stop if the model omits a support, friction restraint or changing contact point that materially affects the answer.

## A05 — Resolve inclined sling forces

For a symmetric two-leg bridle supporting a centered load, each leg tension is W divided by twice the sine of the leg angle measured from horizontal. State the angle reference: a 30-degree angle from horizontal is not 30 degrees from vertical. As the legs become flatter, tension increases. The horizontal component also loads the object or lifting device and may require structural verification.

Worked case: W=4,000 pounds and each leg is 30 degrees from horizontal. Sine 30 degrees is 0.5, so each leg carries 4,000 pounds. The vertical component per leg is 2,000 pounds; its horizontal component is approximately 3,464 pounds. A 4,000-pound vertical sling rating alone does not prove the installed hitch, connector, bend and lifting point are acceptable.

For a coplanar two-leg bridle with unequal horizontal angles theta1 and theta2 and no other horizontal forces, equilibrium gives T1=W cos(theta2)/sin(theta1+theta2), and T2=W cos(theta1)/sin(theta1+theta2). These formulas apply only to the stated force model; the center of gravity and attachment positions must agree with it. Do not choose arbitrary angles independently of the actual load geometry.

Worked case: W=6,000 pounds, theta1=30 degrees, theta2=60 degrees. T1=3,000 pounds and T2 is approximately 5,196 pounds. Vertical components are 1,500 and 4,500 pounds. Horizontal components are equal and opposite, approximately 2,598 pounds. The steeper leg carries more in this particular unequal geometry because of the required equilibrium, illustrating why a memorized 'flatter always carries more' comparison cannot replace analysis of the whole system.

For wire-rope sling configurations, use the applicable manufacturer's rated data. OSHA guidance addresses using the next lower listed angle or qualified calculation for unlisted angles and special justification for very low horizontal angles. Calculation of tension does not create a rating for unidentified gear. Never use the design factor as extra usable capacity.

Application: calculate tensions, then independently resolve them into horizontal and vertical components. If they do not balance, check angle reference, calculator degree mode and geometry before selecting equipment.

## A06 — Select the whole rigging assembly

Selection is a comparison between calculated demand and the applicable working limit for every component in its actual configuration. Record sling material, length, hitch, angle, bend radius, edge contact, environmental exposure and compatible connectors. A sling tag may give different vertical, basket and choker ratings. A basket does not guarantee twice the vertical capacity under every angle or contact condition. A choker adds local bending and choke-angle effects; use its applicable data.

Alloy chain tolerates some conditions that damage synthetics, but damaged links, unsuitable heat exposure or incompatible components invalidate serviceability. Wire rope needs appropriate bending support and protection from kinks, crushing and sharp contact. Synthetic web and roundslings can be light and flexible but are vulnerable to cutting, heat and chemical attack; their protection must suit the edge, load movement and sling type. Metal mesh needs sound handles and flexible undamaged mesh. Natural or synthetic fiber rope requires identification, condition and application-specific ratings; an ordinary rope is not automatically a lifting sling.

Inspect identification and the full accessible sling before use, including terminations and contact areas. Isolate damaged or unidentified equipment and obtain the proper inspection decision; do not improvise a repair. Distinguish the material's removal criteria and scheduled inspection from a quick visual glance. Periodic inspection records do not replace the pre-use check. Running crane rope criteria are not interchangeable with wire-rope sling criteria.

Worked selection: a calculated leg demand is 4,500 pounds. A tag lists 5,000 vertical and 4,000 choker. The planned choker arrangement fails even though the vertical rating exceeds demand. Choosing a larger sling requires repeating fit, bend, connection and lifting-point checks, not merely changing the number on the form.

Application: prepare a component schedule with demand, actual configuration rating, evidence/manual and inspection identity. Mark any unknown value as a hold. Do not multiply nominal component ratings together; the load path is limited by its least adequate element and by structural/stability constraints.

## A07 — Connect hardware without creating hidden loads

A connection must fit, articulate and retain the load as intended. Inspect shackles, hooks, links, rings, swivels, chain components, turnbuckles and lifting points against their own identification and instructions. Verify pin/body compatibility, thread engagement, retainers, allowable loading direction and bearing contact. A physically fitting pin is not proof of an approved replacement. A hook's latch retains a connection; it is not a load-bearing support for tip loading.

Arrange a shackle so the applied forces act in its permitted direction and the sling eyes are not crowded or pinched. Multiple legs may require a suitable bow arrangement or other connector, but the manufacturer determines the limits and any side-loading reduction. Screw-pin rotation from a moving connection can loosen a pin; select the approved retained arrangement for the movement. Do not apply one manufacturer's angle table universally to every shackle.

Eyebolts and swivel hoist rings are different products. Verify thread size, engagement, seating surface, torque, permitted force direction and lifting-point structure. An eyebolt used at an angle may have a much lower limit or be unsuitable. A swivel ring must actually move freely into its intended loaded alignment and be installed to its instructions. Never substitute hand tightening where a torque requirement applies. A certification exercise's fixture instructions are not field installation instructions.

Turnbuckles and adjustable chain arrangements must have adequate engagement and approved locking/retention; the end fittings and side-load limits matter. Do not adjust a loaded assembly unless the actual device and approved procedure permit it. Compression terminations, wire-rope clips and wedge sockets require their specific rope, assembly, tail, orientation and inspection instructions. Do not invent clip spacing or torque. Construction wire-rope sling eyes must not be fabricated with clips or knots under the applicable sling rule; instructions for another rope termination do not waive that restriction.

Worked fault: a lifting ring is rated above the calculated force, but its shoulder cannot seat because a weld bead interferes. Stop. Torque alone cannot fix the missing support condition, and grinding the load or connector is not an authorized field solution. Obtain an approved lifting-point correction or a different approved connection.

Application: assemble a bench connection from its actual instructions and record torque/retention evidence. Have a second person identify the force direction and inspect the completed connection before using it in supervised lifting.

## A08 — Understand blocks, reeving and hoists

A pulley changes force direction and can provide mechanical advantage, but it also loads its support. For an ideal single fixed sheave with two parallel rope segments returning in the same direction, the support reaction is twice the rope tension. With 2,000 pounds in each segment, the ideal reaction is 4,000 pounds, not 2,000. For other angles, add force vectors. Include block weight and actual friction/dynamic effects as required by the equipment data and plan.

In an ideal multi-part system, the supporting rope parts share the load, but real reeving includes friction and unequal tensions. Use the manufacturer's permitted reeving, line pull, sheave and support data. Count only parts actually supporting the moving load. Trace the rope from its dead end through every sheave to the hauling end. Verify rope size, groove condition, fleet angle, guards and end termination. A theoretical mechanical advantage is not permission to exceed a winch or block rating.

Manual chain, lever, electric and air hoists have different controls and operating limitations. Verify the support, hooks, chain/rope condition, brake, controls, rated capacity and travel limits for the actual model. The hoist rating does not certify the beam or trolley. Keep the load aligned as permitted; side pulling, wrapping load chain around the object and extending a lever with a pipe are not acceptable shortcuts. A limit device is not a routine stopping target unless the manufacturer expressly designs it for that use.

For powered hoists, identify the disconnect or air isolation and what loss of power means for load holding and recovery. Never assume an emergency stop removes stored energy or lowers the load. Plan a safe suspended-load response without people entering beneath it. Test controls and brake response at the prescribed safe stage before the full movement; abnormal creep or noise requires stopping and isolation.

Worked case: a hand hoist is attached to a trolley whose beam support has no documented capacity. The load is below the hoist's rating. The operation is still on hold: the upstream support is unverified. No trial load is an acceptable way to discover the beam's capacity.

Application: trace the load path and identify the load on each block and support. Demonstrate the model's normal stop and emergency isolation on unloaded equipment before supervised operation.

## A09 — Choose below-hook devices for the actual load

Lifting beams and spreader arrangements affect forces differently. A lifting beam commonly resists bending; a spreader arrangement commonly carries compression with its top rigging. Neither may be substituted merely because it has the same marked capacity. Use the exact permitted span, attachment positions, top rigging and load distribution. Changing a pick point can change bending or compression demand. Account for device weight above and below each relevant connection.

Mechanical clamps, plate clamps, beam clamps and grapples depend on compatible material, thickness, orientation, surface condition and engagement. A clamp for one plate orientation may not permit turning or lifting stacked plates. Verify locking and the required minimum/maximum load conditions where specified. Do not assume teeth grip through paint, grease or a protective sheet. Keep hands clear of jaws and never suspend a person to test the grip.

Vacuum lifters require the specified surface, seal, load geometry and backup/monitoring provisions. Porous or dirty surfaces, curvature, leakage and power loss can alter holding ability. Magnetic lifters depend on material properties, thickness, contact, air gaps and the manufacturer's working conditions. A magnet can attract a piece without having sufficient rated holding capacity for it. Distinguish close-proximity and remote operation and maintain the planned exclusion area. Indicators are part of the system, not substitutes for suitability checks.

Worked decision: a vacuum device approved for smooth flat sheet is proposed for corrugated material of the same weight. Reject the substitution until the manufacturer/qualified planner approves the actual contact and holding conditions. Weight is only one selection variable. A second lifting device does not automatically cure the incompatibility because its load sharing and failure behavior also require planning.

Application: compare three device data sheets and select the one that expressly covers a supplied load. Identify what must be inspected, what loss-of-holding warning means and where people may stand. If no device fits, document the hold rather than selecting the closest-looking option.

## A10 — Plan and perform a controlled level lift

A level lift requires verified balance and connections that support the object without unacceptable deformation. Mark lifting points, center of gravity, sling lengths and angles on a drawing. Calculate individual demands and check all components. Provide edge protection that stays in place during take-up and movement. Define the load's intended orientation and the measurable acceptance tolerance in the approved plan; 'looks level' is not a repeatable criterion.

Brief the operator and signal person on take-up, trial, travel and landing stages. Establish an exclusion area and escape routes. Remove people from pinch points before tensioning. Take up slack slowly as directed. Check seating and alignment from a safe position; do not pull on a tensioned sling to reposition it. Lift only to the trial clearance specified in the plan, stop and verify balance, brake holding, load integrity and expected forces/clearances.

If the object tilts unexpectedly, lands on one edge, distorts or shifts in its sling, stop and lower to a stable support. Investigate the model and installation. Adjust only after the load is safely supported and the change is authorized. Do not use a worker's body weight to correct balance. Tag lines control appropriate movement from a safe location; they are not structural supports and must not entangle the handler.

Worked case: a calculated eccentric load should remain within the plan's level tolerance, but the first trial raises one end sharply. A sling may be the wrong length, the actual center may differ or an attachment may be binding. Lower and compare actual installation and load documentation; increasing speed to 'get it free' can introduce shock and uncontrolled release.

Application: in the approved low-energy laboratory, record pre-lift predictions, actual trial observations and the final decision. The evaluator must see independent recognition of a discrepancy, not just a successful movement under continuous prompting.

## A11 — Recognize why rotation changes the problem

Rotation changes the position of the center of gravity relative to supports and may change sling angles, contact points, clearances and force directions. A lug strong in one direction may be unsuitable in another. The object can pass through a position where a restraint becomes slack or the center moves beyond the supporting geometry. The rotation plan must address the full path, not only the initial and final positions.

Consider a deliberately simplified warning model. In body coordinates, support A is at (0,0), support B at (4,0), and the center at (2,1) feet. Weight is 1,000 pounds. Assume both support forces stay vertical while the rigid body rotates by phi. The horizontal coordinates become xB=4 cos(phi) and xCG=2 cos(phi)-sin(phi). Moment balance gives RB=1,000[0.5-0.25 tan(phi)] while the denominator is nonzero. RA=1,000-RB.

At zero degrees, RB=500 pounds. At 30 degrees it is about 356; at 45 degrees it is 250; at 60 degrees it is about 67. At approximately 63.435 degrees, RB reaches zero. Beyond that point the model demands a negative reaction at B, which a tensile support cannot provide. At 90 degrees the equation is singular. This model does NOT describe an approved method for turning the load upright. It demonstrates why a plausible initial arrangement cannot be extended through a rotation without analyzing its limits.

Real rigging may not maintain vertical forces or the assumed pivot, so actual calculations may differ substantially. A qualified plan must specify the actual connections, staged positions, allowable forces, controlled supports and load transfer. If an attachment or hoist must be changed, define a stable supported condition for that change. Never improvise while the load hangs.

Application: plot the model's reactions at the stated angles and mark the first invalid condition. Explain why increasing component capacity cannot solve a geometry that requires a sling to push. Then identify what additional engineering information a real rotation would need.

## A12 — Execute rotation and load transfer by hold points

Use a rotation fixture or real task only after its staged plan and equipment have been approved by the appropriately qualified planner. Identify the initial support, each lifting/controlling connection, the final cradle, the permitted direction and the means of preventing unplanned movement. Define measurable intermediate orientations and the hold decision at each. The plan must state where people stand and how the load is safely returned or secured if an abnormal condition develops.

Verify that every attachment is rated for the changing force direction. Establish who commands each hoist or crane and how commands are coordinated. With more than one lifting device, a movement by one can overload another even when total weight is unchanged. Do not assume equal load sharing from equal equipment ratings. Monitor the factors specified by the plan, such as orientation, displacement, clearances or properly selected load measurement; a sensor does not replace a sound force model.

At each stage, stop movement, verify the expected support/rigging condition from outside the danger area and obtain the planned release to continue. Unexpected slack, rapid force change, movement of a support or loss of communication is a stop condition. Use the predetermined safe recovery, not a rushed opposing movement. Keep hands and feet away from the closing space between the rotating object and its cradle.

Worked case: a controlling leg becomes slack earlier than the plan predicts. Do not pull faster on the other hoist. Stop and secure or return the load as the recovery plan permits, then investigate the actual center of gravity and geometry. Early slack can precede an uncontrolled roll or load transfer.

Application: complete an individual controlled rotation on a validated training fixture through the orientations expressly approved for it. Record the actual start, intermediate and final angles, equipment identities, commands and observations. A tabletop explanation is useful preparation but cannot be recorded as physical rotation proficiency.

## A13 — Jack, roll and transfer without losing stability

Jacking and rolling move the load through different support conditions. Verify floor capacity, route slope, openings, joints, obstructions, stopping arrangements and the object's center of gravity. A skate's rating does not prove the floor or load contact can support it. Dollies, rollers and skates must be compatible with the load and their instructions; steering and uneven surfaces can change sharing. Keep people out of the downhill path and pinch zones.

Use a jack with legible capacity, appropriate condition and positive overtravel protection. Provide a firm foundation and suitable cap contact to prevent slip. Raise in the planned controlled increments and promptly secure the raised load with the specified blocking or other support. Hydraulic pressure alone is not a substitute for securing it. Do not reach beneath the object to place blocking by hand where a loss of support would trap you; use the approved safe method.

An A-frame, trolley or beam clamp needs verified support, configuration, restraint and permitted direction of loading. Moving a trolley can introduce new forces and clearances. Do not pull an unverified frame sideways or use a beam flange clamp outside its specified orientation. Inspect the system as a connected load path, including anchorage and the structure beneath it.

Worked transfer: a tall cabinet is lifted from four floor contacts onto two rolling supports. The resulting support polygon is narrower, and a small lateral force can now tip it. Before transfer, verify the center projection remains safely within the planned support area throughout movement and turns, with appropriate engineered restraint. The cabinet's successful stationary lift does not establish rolling stability.

Application: carry out a low-height jack-and-secure exercise with a stable training load, then plan a separate route using skates. Identify each point at which support changes and demonstrate a stop before crossing an unverified floor joint.

## A14 — Communicate, land and respond to abnormal conditions

Use the agreed signal system and a designated signal person for the operation, with any required qualifications. Anybody can give an emergency stop; do not continue because that person is not the usual signaler. Test radios and agree on clear identification, function, direction, distance/speed and stop instructions as applicable. If the operator loses the needed communication or cannot interpret a command, stop safely until communication is restored.

Plan the landing before lifting. Verify support capacity, level, dunnage position, clearances and access for removing rigging. Lower in controlled stages while keeping people away from the underside and pinch points. Confirm stability and full intended support before releasing tension. If the load rocks or the sling is trapped, restore a safe condition and revise the removal method; do not yank the sling out with the crane.

Abnormal conditions include unexpected tilt, binding, support settlement, snagging, excessive wind, sudden force changes, device warnings and a person entering the exclusion area. Give stop, maintain communication and use the approved safe securing/recovery plan. Do not improvise a rescue beneath an unsecured load. Protect the area and summon appropriate emergency assistance where needed. Isolate suspect gear and preserve observations for investigation.

Worked event: the receiving area is obstructed after the lift begins. Do not land on an unplanned surface or hold indefinitely while workers move beneath the load. Use the preplanned safe holding/return option and re-establish the landing area. If no safe option exists, emergency direction must come from the responsible qualified team, not an improvised trainee decision.

Application: demonstrate a communication-loss stop and a controlled landing on the laboratory setup. After unloading, inspect gear, store it to prevent damage and document any defect, unexpected behavior and changed condition.

## A15 — Build a reviewable advanced rigging plan

A useful plan lets another qualified person reproduce the reasoning. Identify the actual object and revision, weight evidence, dimensions, center of gravity, lifting points and structural limitations. Show initial, intermediate and final configurations. Include free-body diagrams, component demands, applicable ratings, installation instructions, inspection identities and the load carried at each boundary. Record assumptions explicitly and resolve them before execution.

Include site/support checks, equipment configuration, weather limits, exclusion zones, path, landing and contingency arrangements. Define roles, communications and hold points. List changes that invalidate the plan: contents, attachment, lifting-point location, sling length, device type, orientation, support or environmental condition. A signature does not make an unsupported calculation correct; reviewers must examine the evidence and model.

Worked review: a plan calculates a sling demand of 5,196 pounds but lists a 5,000-pound applicable rating. The correct decision is no lift with that configuration. Do not round the demand down or use a breaking-strength value as a working limit. Revise the configuration with competent review, recalculate every affected force and update the installation drawing before briefing the crew.

The plan also defines the qualification boundary. Training on a small validated fixture does not establish competence for an industrial vessel, critical lift or multiple-crane operation. Record the actual demonstrated equipment and complexity. Where engineering design or a specialized operator is needed, state that requirement and obtain it rather than awarding authority by course title.

Application: submit a complete plan for the supplied training task. Exchange it for review using the same inputs. Resolve differing answers with diagrams, units and evidence, not majority vote. The approved plan is the basis for the practical, not paperwork completed afterward.

## A16 — Integrated worked review and independent performance

Start with the 3,000-pound assembly from A03: components of 2,000 pounds at x=1 foot and 1,000 pounds at x=7 feet. Supports at x=0 and x=8 feet produce a center at x=3 feet, a right vertical reaction of 1,125 pounds and a left reaction of 1,875 pounds. These vertical reactions can be used directly only for vertical support forces. If a common upper connection creates inclined legs, calculate their actual tensions and horizontal effects from compatible geometry.

Suppose the plan instead uses an approved beam providing vertical lower connections. Verify the beam's permitted support span and unequal loading, then include its own weight in the upper system. A beam advertised with sufficient total capacity may still be unsuitable for the eccentric distribution. Inspect the exact beam data and lifting points. Confirm that both lower connections remain vertical during the planned level lift, and account for any permitted movement.

The crew must now demonstrate the whole sequence: verify the object and plan; inspect and select components; install compatible hardware; establish exclusion and communications; take up slack; perform the low-clearance trial; confirm the expected condition; move through the approved route; land stably; release, inspect and record. An injected paperwork change—an additional component—must trigger a hold and recalculation before the next lift.

Rotation is a separate approved task, not an unannounced extension of this level lift. Use the validated rotation fixture and its own staged plan. Predict changes, execute hold points and stop on a simulated discrepancy before it becomes an actual hazardous condition. The evaluator injects discrepancies by scenario or safe instruction, never by secretly damaging equipment or destabilizing a load.

Independent performance means the learner makes the decisions and explains them without being coached through each step. An evaluator may stop danger at any time. A safe stop is evidence of judgment; continuing a known unsafe condition is a critical failure. Complete the written assessment separately and preserve the practical record. Only actual demonstrated scope may appear in the training achievement record, with employer authorization remaining separate.

# Formative checks and remediation

Attempt independently before reading the answer. Correct misconceptions using the named chapter and changed practice before final assessment.

## A01-L1

Why does turning a known skid upright require a new review?

**Answer:** Rotation changes geometry, force directions, support and stability; obtain an approved staged plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Review A01 and identify the changed assumptions rather than merely increasing sling size.

## A01-L2

Does a rigging certificate authorize every lift?

**Answer:** No; employer task-specific qualification and applicable planning are still required.

**Reason/remediation:** Explain the difference between a training achievement and authority for a particular task.

## A02-L1

Use the supplied density 490 lb/ft³ for a solid 36×24×1 inch plate. Find weight.

**Answer:** 864/1728=0.5 ft³; 245 lb.

**Reason/remediation:** A02: convert cubic units before multiplying; supplied density is an exercise input.

## A02-L2

Where do you count a lifting beam in load accounting?

**Answer:** At every boundary supporting it, including the upper system, not automatically in lower sling demand.

**Reason/remediation:** Draw each load-path boundary and list what hangs below it.

## A03-L1

Find xCG for 2000 lb at x=1 ft and 1000 lb at x=7 ft.

**Answer:** 3000 lb total; (2000+7000)/3000=3 ft.

**Reason/remediation:** Use component centers and weighted moments, not midpoint of length.

## A03-L2

What does unexpected tilt at trial require?

**Answer:** Stop, safely lower and verify actual load, center and installed geometry before authorized adjustment.

**Reason/remediation:** A03: do not correct a suspended imbalance with a person or tag line.

## A04-L1

Find reactions for 12000 lb at x=2 ft between vertical supports at 0 and 8 ft.

**Answer:** Right 3000 lb; left 9000 lb.

**Reason/remediation:** Check both force sum and moment balance.

## A04-L2

Why cannot four connected legs simply be assigned one-quarter weight each?

**Answer:** Stiffness, lengths and geometry affect sharing; use applicable rated data and qualified analysis.

**Reason/remediation:** A04: equilibrium alone may not determine all reactions.

## A05-L1

Find equal leg tensions for 4000 lb at 30° from horizontal.

**Answer:** 4000/(2 sin30°)=4000 lb each.

**Reason/remediation:** Resolve each vertical component to 2000 lb; do not confuse vertical angle.

## A05-L2

For 6000 lb and horizontal angles 30°/60°, give both tensions and a check.

**Answer:** T1=3000 lb, T2≈5196 lb; vertical components 1500+4500=6000, horizontals cancel.

**Reason/remediation:** A05: use a compatible two-force geometry and degree mode.

## A06-L1

A sling is rated 5000 vertical and 4000 choker; demand in choker is 4500. Decision?

**Answer:** Reject that arrangement; the applicable 4000 limit is exceeded.

**Reason/remediation:** Use installed hitch rating, not the largest tag number.

## A06-L2

Can a periodic inspection record replace today’s inspection?

**Answer:** No; inspect before use and isolate current defects or missing identification.

**Reason/remediation:** A06: records and present condition address different times.

## A07-L1

A hoist-ring shoulder is held off its seat by a weld bead. What now?

**Answer:** Hold; obtain an approved connection correction, not extra torque or unauthorized grinding.

**Reason/remediation:** A07: installation geometry is part of suitability.

## A07-L2

Can a similar loose pin replace a missing shackle pin?

**Answer:** Only the approved compatible component; appearance/fit is insufficient.

**Reason/remediation:** Verify identity, retention and manufacturer requirements.

## A08-L1

Ideal fixed return block has two parallel segments at 2000 lb tension. Support force?

**Answer:** 4000 lb before block weight and actual effects.

**Reason/remediation:** Add both rope forces on the block, not just one.

## A08-L2

The hoist is rated but the beam is undocumented. May a low trial prove it?

**Answer:** No; verify the support before loading.

**Reason/remediation:** A08: a trial is not a structural capacity test.

## A09-L1

Why does a flat-sheet vacuum rating not approve corrugated sheet?

**Answer:** Contact/sealing geometry and holding conditions differ; obtain actual suitability evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** A09: weight is only one input.

## A09-L2

Can an equal-capacity spreader replace a lifting beam?

**Answer:** Not without approval of load distribution, top rigging, compression/bending and configuration.

**Reason/remediation:** Identify how each device transmits forces.

## A10-L1

What must be checked during a planned low-clearance trial?

**Answer:** Expected balance/orientation, seating, integrity, holding and clearances from a safe position.

**Reason/remediation:** A10: trial checks a verified setup; it does not excuse unknown weight.

## A10-L2

A tensioned sling needs moving by hand. Action?

**Answer:** Stop, lower to secure support and adjust only after safe unloading and authorization.

**Reason/remediation:** Keep hands out of tension and pinch zones.

## A11-L1

In the warning model RB=1000(0.5−0.25 tanφ), find RB at 45°.

**Answer:** 250 lb; RA=750 lb.

**Reason/remediation:** A11: model assumptions must remain valid.

## A11-L2

Why does a negative computed sling reaction invalidate the model?

**Answer:** A tensile sling cannot provide the required pushing force.

**Reason/remediation:** More capacity does not repair impossible geometry.

## A12-L1

A controlling leg becomes slack early in rotation. Action?

**Answer:** Stop and follow approved safe securing/return, then investigate before continuing.

**Reason/remediation:** A12: do not speed up the other hoist to compensate.

## A12-L2

What must be defined at rotation hold points?

**Answer:** Expected orientation/support/force conditions, checks, release authority and safe recovery.

**Reason/remediation:** The whole path, not just end positions, needs planning.

## A13-L1

What follows a controlled jack raise?

**Answer:** Promptly secure using the planned blocking or other support; do not rely on pressure alone.

**Reason/remediation:** A13: prevent loss-of-support crush exposure.

## A13-L2

Why can a cabinet become unstable on skates after a stable lift?

**Answer:** Support polygon, center projection and movement forces change.

**Reason/remediation:** Verify each transfer and route condition, not only total capacity.

## A14-L1

Required radio communication is lost during movement. Action?

**Answer:** Stop safely and restore agreed communication before continuing.

**Reason/remediation:** A14: guessing a command is not a safe substitute.

## A14-L2

When may rigging be released at landing?

**Answer:** After verified stable intended support and safe unloading/removal conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not pull trapped rigging from an unstable load.

## A15-L1

What makes a calculation reviewable?

**Answer:** Inputs/sources, units, geometry/free-body model, assumptions, calculations and applicable limits.

**Reason/remediation:** A15: a signed answer without its basis cannot be reproduced.

## A15-L2

Demand is 5196 lb and applicable limit 5000. Can it be rounded down?

**Answer:** No; revise and recalculate the approved arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** Breaking strength/design factor is not spare working capacity.

## A16-L1

Find reactions for 3000 lb at x=3 between vertical supports 0 and 8 ft.

**Answer:** Right 1125 lb; left 1875 lb.

**Reason/remediation:** A16: check 1125×8=3000×3.

## A16-L2

Does passing the level task permit an unplanned rotation?

**Answer:** No; rotation has its own approved staged setup and observed assessment.

**Reason/remediation:** Keep demonstrated scope and authority explicit.
