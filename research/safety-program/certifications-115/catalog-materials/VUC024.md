# VUC024 delivery, assessment and credential guide

## Program definition

Audience: prospective/developing mobile-crane operators and employers arranging structured operator development. Prerequisites: ability to understand the instructional language, read the applicable manual/chart and perform basic arithmetic; employer determination of fitness and eligibility for supervised activity; required site induction/PPE. Training staff verify actual certification/licensing status before assigning any task requiring it. No learner may use this course to bypass operator-in-training restrictions. Accommodations may change presentation/time, not the requirement to interpret the actual operating documents and control the equipment safely.

Purpose: teach safe planning, inspection, configuration selection, load calculations, communication, controlled maneuvering and securing, with separate equipment-type demonstrations. Scope includes lattice, telescopic swing-cab, telescopic fixed-cab and service-truck distinctions. Specialized personnel, multi-crane, floating, free-fall and duty-cycle operations are recognition/planning-boundary instruction only; they require additional task-specific training and evaluation before assignment.

Objectives C01–C18 correspond to the coursebook chapters: distinguish authority/type; plan geometry and zones; verify support; prevent electrical approach; recognize assembly/access hazards; inspect/report; select and inspect rope/reeving; distinguish devices/aids; apply charts; evaluate weight/rigging; interpret signals/control loads; travel/secure/respond; apply lattice controls; apply telescopic controls; recognize specialized limits; solve integrated calculations; operate service-truck controls; interpret standard hand signals. Every objective has two formative checks, three final questions and an identified applied station or scenario.

## Instructor preparation and schedule

The lead instructor has documented current knowledge, training and experience with the crane types actually taught, applicable regulations, chart calculations and adult assessment. A competent operator trainer is onsite for each operating learner and performs no distracting simultaneous tasks. Qualified inspectors, signal personnel, riggers, site-support professionals and evaluators retain their own required roles; the instructor title alone does not establish those qualifications. No concurrent active learners share one trainer's attention. A proctor protects the final/key; the practical evaluator records observed individual performance.

Planned initial course: 40 instructional hours, excluding breaks, with additional supervised operating development as needed. This is a VNDRLY planning duration, not an OSHA-mandated hour count or guarantee of novice competence. One actual equipment type is the minimum practical delivery route. All types are taught conceptually; additional actual types require additional equipment stations and time. Record unobserved types as NOT EVALUATED, never presumed from the classroom discussion.

Equipment: current manufacturer manuals, serial/configuration-correct charts and notes, relevant inspection records; inspected crane(s) for each actual type claimed; verified-weight training loads; rated rigging selected by a qualified rigger; approved support and marked clear operating area; radios and posted signal chart; disabled rope/rigging defect specimens; measuring tools; suitable PPE; barriers; emergency contact/rescue arrangements; records and protected exams. Instructor verifies weather, ground and absence of live electrical exposure. Hazard scenarios use cards, photos or disabled specimens—not deliberately defective operational machinery.

## Assessment rules

Formative: attempt both checks after each chapter, then explain corrections using the answer/rationale. Practice again with a changed example when the learner misses a safety decision or calculation. All 36 checks must be attempted and misconceptions corrected before the final.

Final: 54 original constructed-response items, three per C01–C18, 108 available points. Each item earns 2 for correct action/calculation and reason, 1 for safe but incomplete reasoning, 0 for incorrect/absent/unsafe response. Pass at least 87/108 and at least 4/6 in every domain. Any explicit endorsement of a critical unsafe action fails the attempt regardless of total: knowingly overloaded/invalid-chart operation, deliberate electrical encroachment, bypassed required protection, people under a knowingly exposed load, unapproved trainee/special lift, or continued movement after a stop/lost required signal. Assessor identifies the specific response; an unanswered question alone is not labeled deliberate unsafe endorsement.

Allow 90 minutes plus appropriate accommodations. Supply a clean course fictional-chart sheet and any stated manufacturer reference required by a question; no answer key, coaching or formative answers. Preserve responses and marked rationale. The final is locally separated under `.local/private-training/mobile-crane-instructor/`; this file-location separation is not a claim of deployed application access controls.

Practical: each station below has five observable criteria, scored 0 not demonstrated/unsafe, 1 safe with prompting/incomplete, 2 independent and correct. Pass each required station at least 8/10, and all starred critical criteria at 2. Stop and fail an unsafe live action; return to supervised practice before reassessment. Never create a hazardous condition to see whether the learner notices it. Stations that require a real crane cannot be passed by discussion alone. Repeat J, K and L for every type/configuration claimed; also repeat applicable setup/control/chart tasks when equipment differences demand it.

Retake: diagnose the failed domains/stations, provide targeted instruction, then assess a changed problem or task using the protected reassessment guide. Preserve the first attempt. After a second failed attempt, require a documented instructor development plan before another assessment. No same-session memorized answer recital is a pass. This is the VNDRLY course policy, not an external certification provider's retake policy.

## A — Authority, task and lift-plan briefing

Equipment: fictional work order, actual machine documents, qualification/evaluation records with synthetic learner identity. Task: decide whether the assignment is within scope, then brief the lift team on the pick/path/place plan. Inject a request for a personnel lift that is outside the learner's scope.

Criteria: (1) identifies machine/type/work scope; (2) separates training, external certification/license and employer evaluation; (3)* rejects the out-of-scope trainee task; (4) identifies route/radius, landing and zone controls; (5) names communication and stop responsibilities. Record the actual restriction and briefing, not merely 'understands safety.' Correct outcome: the ordinary planned task may advance only after required conditions; the injected personnel task does not.

## B — Ground and setup decision

Equipment: site drawing with buried-tank area, support-reaction data, bearing limit, mat data and actual setup location. Task: identify an unsuitable area, solve a support-area screen and inspect the real approved setup without placing a load over suspect ground.

Criteria: (1)* identifies underground/support hazard and holds setup; (2) requests controlling-entity/site information; (3) calculates pressure/area and explains its limits; (4) verifies actual permitted support extension and level; (5) states settlement/weather reassessment triggers. Example instructor answer: 60,000 pounds at 2,500 pounds/square foot requires at least 24 square feet in a uniform-pressure screen, not proof any 24-square-foot mat is adequate.

## C — Electrical planning and emergency tabletop

Equipment: scaled fictional line/work-zone plan, voltage card, Table A and emergency scenario cards. No energized lines in the practice envelope. Task: distinguish planning distance from minimum approach and explain contact response.

Criteria: (1)* refuses unknown-voltage assumption or unplanned encroachment; (2) identifies full crane/load/rigging envelope; (3) selects the applicable planning and clearance process; (4) explains spotter/device limits; (5)* keeps people off potentially energized crane/load and calls utility/emergency assistance. A 230-kV operation uses the up-to-350-kV planning process; its Table A value is 20 feet. Do not award a pass for the number without the process.

## D — Assembly/access and complete inspection

Equipment: actual released crane, manual, inspection records and photographs of missing retainers/damaged members. Task: inspect systematically, identify a staged photo defect, decide disposition and document handoff. Do not install a real defect.

Criteria: (1) traces assembly/configuration and required release; (2) examines controls/structure/support/fluids/electrical/hooks/tires or tracks; (3)* recognizes the safety defect and stops affected use; (4) distinguishes shift/monthly/annual/additional inspection needs; (5) records defect, restriction and recipient. Learner demonstrates safe access and refuses unplanned elevated work. A missing assembly retainer cannot be waived by an annual inspection record.

## E — Rope, reeving and rigging compatibility

Equipment: isolated samples, actual approved reeving chart, hook/block data and inspection images. Task: identify rope construction/defects and compare the full suspended weight with the reeving and hardware limits.

Criteria: (1) locates specified rope requirements; (2)* rejects broken-strand/power-line-damaged sample; (3) uses the correct running/standing/rotation-resistant criterion; (4) identifies termination and block compatibility; (5) applies the governing line/reeving limit with all deductions. Do not ask learners to splice or re-terminate a live lifting rope as proof of operator knowledge.

## F — Controls, devices and configuration

Equipment: actual crane with functioning protection, manual and simulated fault cards. Task: identify power flow, controls, device/aid functions and physical/display configuration match; conduct only approved safe function checks.

Criteria: (1) identifies normal/emergency controls; (2) explains stored-energy and drift hazards; (3)* refuses operation with a failed required safety device; (4) distinguishes an aid's regulated alternative process from a bypass; (5)* detects an actual-versus-display configuration mismatch. Faults are described on cards, not created on operating equipment.

## G — Independent manufacturer-chart application

Equipment: fictional chart laboratory plus actual complete manufacturer chart, range/work-sector diagrams, notes, verified weights and actual practice configuration. Task: solve two fictional cases and independently calculate one planned real practice lift from pick through place. The assessor solves the actual case first and retains the reference pages and calculations.

Criteria: (1)* selects correct chart/configuration and refuses an unlisted case; (2) determines governing loaded radius and clearance; (3) applies every required deduction; (4) compares chart, reeving/hardware and operating restrictions; (5) gives a defensible go/hold decision and invalidating changes. Arithmetic tolerance is exact for stated integer examples; actual measurements use the documented equipment method, not favorable rounding. A numerical pass cannot waive support/weather/path conditions.

## H — Load identification and controlled rigging interface

Equipment: low, stable verified-weight training load, qualified rigger and selected inspected rigging. Task: verify weight/center of gravity and explain sling forces, observe rigging setup, take up slack under supervision and confirm balance at minimum safe clearance.

Criteria: (1) verifies weight including contents; (2) identifies center of gravity and unequal share; (3) checks rated/configured hardware and protection; (4)* keeps people/hands clear and rejects side pulling; (5) stops for unexpected shift or imbalance. This evaluates the operator's rigging interface, not a separate rigger qualification. Use a tabletop imbalance scenario rather than making a real load unstable.

## I — Signals and communication loss

Equipment: official posted hand-signal chart, radios, disabled-control rehearsal then approved no-load function practice. Task: interpret at least twelve signals, including hoist versus boom, main/auxiliary selection, slow, swing, telescoping, stop and emergency stop. Include type-specific travel and combined movement signals where applicable.

Criteria: (1) identifies designated signal source/handoff; (2) correctly distinguishes functions; (3)* obeys stop from any source; (4)* stops on ambiguous/lost required signal; (5) uses clear voice function/direction and agreed radio discipline. The assessor records each signal and response; missed signals are retaught and presented in a changed order before live work.

## J — Type-specific maneuvering

Equipment: actual type/configuration, clear marked course and approved verified training load. Task: after familiarization, lift, boom/swing and place the load through the planned path with controlled combined motions. The assessor marks a realistic landing target and clearances compatible with the equipment and task; no race or copied external exam course is used.

Criteria: (1) confirms type-specific setup/controls/chart; (2) takes slack smoothly and checks balance; (3)* maintains load/radius/clearance and exclusion controls; (4) controls pendulum/rotation with smooth movement; (5) lands stably and safely releases rigging. Record LAT truck/crawler/friction distinctions, TLL, TSS or service-truck model explicitly. A hydraulic TLL pass is not a friction LAT pass. If service-truck remote control is used, safe operator position/link-stop response is required.

## K — Travel and changed-condition decision

Equipment: real unloaded travel route plus scenario cards for weather, wrong travel chart and blocked route. Loaded travel is optional only where authorized for the actual machine and learner; no loaded-travel skill is claimed if not observed. Task: inspect and traverse the permitted route, then respond to a changed-condition card with the load safely landed.

Criteria: (1) checks route/support/clearance and travel configuration; (2)* rejects stationary-chart use for unauthorized loaded travel; (3) maintains safe visibility/signaling/speed; (4) identifies wind/sail-area or support-change hold; (5) safely secures and reports the changed condition. The evaluator marks unloaded travel and loaded travel separately.

## L — Shutdown, emergency decision and records

Equipment: actual crane and emergency cards. Task: land/secure the load, park/stow/lock using the manual, exit safely and complete the shift handoff. Discuss control loss, fire and electrical contact without inducing them.

Criteria: (1)* leaves no ordinary load suspended unattended; (2) follows prescribed boom/attachment/brake/control shutdown; (3) secures access and uses safe exit; (4) gives correct emergency isolation/keep-clear/notification actions; (5) records defects/restrictions/next-shift handoff. Returning an incident-affected crane to use without required inspection is an unsafe response.

## M — Specialized-work boundary conference

Equipment: five original work cards: paired-crane vessel lift; suspended personnel basket; barge-mounted lift; submerged pump; repetitive clamshell work. Task: identify added forces, roles, equipment and approval/evaluation needs for every card.

Criteria: (1)* refuses unqualified personnel/multiple-crane assignment; (2) explains changing load share; (3) identifies floating support/list and land-chart limitation; (4) identifies loss of buoyancy/trapped contents/suction; (5) identifies duty-cycle/dynamic/free-fall restrictions. Discussion can pass this recognition station only; it never records actual specialized operating competence.

# VNDRLY/U Mobile Crane Operator — learner coursebook

VUC024 · Original instructional edition 1 · 17 November 2026

This course teaches mobile crane decisions and controlled operating skills. It is for prospective and developing operators, not a grant of unrestricted operating authority. Construction employers must meet applicable training, certification/licensing and equipment/task evaluation obligations separately. A VNDRLY completion record is not CCO certification, an accredited crane license, a rigger qualification or permission to conduct specialized lifts. Actual operation requires a competent training organization, an authorized trainer, suitable equipment and a controlled site. Paper calculations never authorize a real lift.

## C01 — Identify the machine, task and limits of your authority

A mobile crane moves a suspended load through a changing working radius. Its advertised tonnage describes a favorable configuration, not every lift. Before accepting work, identify the machine by model and serial number, the installed boom and attachments, the actual configuration, the governing work rules and the employer's authorization. Construction crane requirements under OSHA Subpart CC and general-industry requirements under 1910.180 are not interchangeable simply because the same machine performs both jobs. Ask the employer to establish the applicable work scope before operation.

Lattice-boom cranes use a truss structure with configured sections and often rope-operated boom hoists. They may be crawler-mounted or truck-mounted. Telescopic cranes use nested boom sections; their capacity depends on section extension and the applicable configuration chart. A swing-cab station rotates with the upper works; a fixed station does not. That changes the operator's sight lines and relationship to the controls as the boom rotates. A remote station adds location, communication and emergency-stop issues; it does not eliminate operator responsibility.

Three separate questions govern construction operation: have you received the necessary training, do you hold the required certification or license for the type, and has your employer evaluated you for the equipment and assigned work? Answering only one is insufficient. An employer evaluation must address actual size, configuration, controls, safety systems and tasks. A course certificate cannot establish performance that was never observed. Record restrictions explicitly: for example, telescopic swing-cab practice on one model is not proof of lattice-friction-crane ability.

Operators in training require the supervision specified in 1926.1427(b). Do not accept personnel lifting, multiple-crane lifts, restricted power-line work or other prohibited trainee assignments because a trainer is nearby. This course's practice excludes those tasks entirely. The operator's trainer must be qualified for that equipment, remain onsite and provide the required monitoring without competing duties. Stop if supervision or communication is lost.

Worked decision: a worker holds a current external crane certificate but has never operated the assigned crane's luffing-jib configuration. The correct action is employer-directed familiarization, training and evaluation before that assignment. The certificate is relevant evidence; it is not a substitute for the missing equipment/task assessment. Common error: treating a card's expiration date as the only condition of permission.

## C02 — Plan the lift and establish the operating envelope

Start with the load, its pick point, its destination and every part of the route between them. Obtain a reliable weight, dimensions, center of gravity, lifting-point information and rigging plan. Identify the greatest loaded radius along the entire path, the needed hook height, overhead clearance, rotation sector and boom clearance. Include the block, slings and attached lifting devices in the load accounting. A lift that is possible at pickup may be impossible at placement.

Walk the site with the lift supervisor and signal person. Locate pedestrians, occupied structures, traffic, pits, excavations, utilities, pipe racks and adjacent equipment. Establish a swing/crush exclusion zone around rotating upper works and counterweights, plus load-path controls. Make barriers understandable to everyone who could enter. Coordinate adjacent cranes whose work areas overlap; neither operator should assume the other will move first. Arrange a landing area that supports the load and permits safe rigging removal without trapped hands or uncontrolled rolling.

Define one signal source for normal movement, how control transfers and what happens if visibility or radio contact is lost. Anyone may give a stop or emergency stop. Brief the operator, riggers and signal personnel on the actual sequence and stop conditions. A plan needs a practical way to stop, lower or secure the load if conditions change; 'we will work it out in the air' is not a contingency.

Height calculations support planning but do not replace the range diagram. A simplified right triangle with a 50-foot boom at 60 degrees has about 43.3 feet of vertical projection and 25 feet of horizontal projection from its pivot. The real crane has pivot height and offset, loaded deflection, block dimensions and clearance needs. Therefore these numbers do not independently establish either load radius or hook clearance. Use the manufacturer's diagram and dimensions.

Application: the pick radius is 25 feet, but the far landing corner requires 40 feet. Plan and chart the 40-foot condition, not the easier pick. If a barricade makes the planned route unavailable, stop and replan rather than swinging through a different, unchecked sector. Common error: calling an ordinary lift 'routine' to avoid checking its changed conditions.

## C03 — Ground support, leveling and setup

Crane support must resist the largest reactions produced by the actual operation. Total crane weight divided evenly among four outriggers is not a valid prediction of maximum pad load. Boom direction, counterweight, lifted load and configuration redistribute forces. Obtain the manufacturer's reaction information and a competent site assessment; use engineering where required. Hidden voids, buried tanks, trenches, unsupported slabs, recently placed fill and water-softened soil can fail beneath apparently hard surfaces.

The controlling entity has ground-preparation and known underground-hazard responsibilities under 1926.1402. The operator still must recognize unsuitable conditions and report them. Ask for relevant site information before setup. A missing soil assessment is not repaired by adding random timber. Mats require adequate area, strength, support and placement; bridging a void demands a designed solution. Keep supports away from excavation influence zones as determined for that site, not by a universal guess based on trench depth.

Deploy outriggers, stabilizers or crawlers according to the actual manual. Match permitted outrigger extension positions to the correct chart and control-system settings. Check both beam extension and jack support; a sensor display cannot prove the pad sits on adequate ground. Partial outriggers are acceptable only when the manufacturer permits that configuration and its chart applies. Follow the specified tire condition and crane leveling method. Recheck level and settlement as loads, weather or support conditions change.

Worked example, for reasoning only: an established maximum support reaction is 48,000 pounds and an engineer-specified allowable bearing pressure is 2,000 pounds per square foot. The simple minimum area is 24 square feet. A 4-by-4-foot mat offers 16 square feet, producing 3,000 pounds per square foot if uniformly loaded; it fails that comparison. A 5-by-5-foot area produces 1,920 pounds per square foot, but that calculation alone does not prove mat strength, uniform load spread or ground capacity. Obtain an approved support arrangement.

Stop for pad sinking, mat rotation, unexpected level change or cracking ground. Safely secure the load using the established procedure, clear personnel and have the support condition reassessed. Do not jack or shim a loaded crane as an improvised correction. Common error: accepting yesterday's setup after overnight flooding without reassessment.

## C04 — Electricity: prevent encroachment before motion

Treat overhead lines as energized unless the utility confirms they are deenergized and visibly grounded at the worksite. A covered-looking line is not evidence of safe insulation. Electricity can arc without physical contact. The relevant distance concerns every part of the crane, line, load and rigging, including sway and deflection. A person holding a tag line can become part of the current path.

Before operations, establish the work zone and determine whether equipment at its maximum working radius could approach within the regulatory planning distance. Under 1926.1408, that screening distance is 20 feet for lines up to 350 kV; 1926.1409 substitutes 50 feet for lines over 350 through 1,000 kV. These screening distances and Table A minimum clearances perform different jobs. Do not simply select a smaller distance from a table and start working. The employer must implement the selected compliant option, voltage determination, planning meeting, boundaries and required protective measures. Unknown voltage means resolve it, not assume a low value.

For reference, Table A clearances are: up to 50 kV, 10 feet; over 50–200, 15 feet; over 200–350, 20 feet; over 350–500, 25 feet; over 500–750, 35 feet; over 750–1,000, 45 feet. Above 1,000 kV, obtain the specified utility or qualified professional-engineer determination. Assembly/disassembly, traveling without load and work inside the Table A zone have separate provisions. This course does not authorize closer-than-Table-A work. Trainee restrictions also apply independently.

A dedicated spotter needs a reliable visual reference and continuous communication; that person cannot perform distracting duties. A proximity alarm, insulated link or grounding wire does not make deliberate approach safe. Work around radio-frequency transmitters may induce hazardous voltage in the crane or load line; obtain the appropriate engineering and protective measures instead of treating it as an ordinary overhead-wire case.

If contact occurs, stop movement, warn everyone away, remain in the cab when safe and have the utility isolate the supply. Do not let rescuers touch the crane or load. If fire or another immediate threat makes leaving unavoidable, use the trained emergency escape method without simultaneously contacting machine and ground; keep feet together and move away with short shuffling steps or hops to reduce step-potential exposure. Do not reenter until the utility and responsible personnel declare the condition safe. Scenario: a coworker proposes pulling the tag line to free an energized load. Refuse, keep clear and summon emergency/utility assistance.

## C05 — Assembly, disassembly and safe access

Assembly and disassembly are planned operations under an A/D director who meets the applicable competent/qualified-person requirements. Use manufacturer procedures or properly developed employer procedures where permitted. The operator follows that plan; possessing an operator certificate does not automatically qualify someone to direct assembly. Discuss crew duties, boom support, assist-crane loads, center of gravity, pin-removal order, stability and exclusion zones before work starts.

Lattice sections can collapse when the wrong pins are removed or when support is placed incorrectly. Never stand under a boom while removing pins unless the specific regulatory exception and protective measures apply; this course's practice does not use that exception. Use supports at approved locations and verify the section is unloaded before releasing its connection. Keep hands out of pin and joint pinch points. Never substitute mismatched pins or sections because their dimensions appear similar.

Telescopic attachments and swing-away jibs have their own deployment sequences. Confirm permitted boom angle, support, attachment retention, pin positions and stowage configuration before moving them. An assist crane must remain within its capacity through the changing geometry. Counterweight installation and removal can change backward stability; neither maximum counterweight nor minimum load is automatically the safest condition. Follow the specified erection and dismantling limits.

Use designated steps, handholds and walkways. Keep access clean, carry tools so hands remain available and use approved fall-protection arrangements for the task. The applicable crane fall-protection provisions distinguish work and equipment circumstances; do not assume ordinary platform guardrails protect someone walking a boom. The instructor's equipment supplement identifies actual access routes, anchorages and rescue arrangements before any elevated exercise. No learner climbs a boom during this program's introductory practice.

After assembly, obtain the required qualified-person inspection before use. Scenario: a boom section is pinned in place but the retaining devices are missing. Do not 'make one quick lift' while replacements are found. The assembly is incomplete. Common error: interpreting a successful no-load motion as a substitute for the post-assembly inspection.

## C06 — Inspect the complete crane and act on defects

Use the actual manual and inspection form, working systematically from carrier/supports through structure, boom, ropes, hook, controls and cab. Look and listen for damage, abnormal wear, loose components, leaks, missing retainers, deformation and unusual operating sounds. Check fluid levels by the safe method, tires or tracks, brakes, steering, hydraulic lines, electrical components, identification, charts and access. Never place a hand near a suspected pressurized-fluid leak; injection injuries can be severe even when the puncture seems small.

OSHA construction inspection requirements include shift inspections by a competent person, documented monthly inspections and annual/comprehensive inspection by a qualified person. Modification, repair, assembly, severe service and equipment idle for the specified period can trigger additional inspections. A current annual report does not replace today's inspection. Monthly records are retained at least three months; annual/comprehensive documentation at least twelve months. Manufacturer requirements may demand more frequent attention.

Classify the finding and follow the responsible person's disposition. A safety hazard requires removal from service until corrected as required. Report repairs or adjustments in writing to the employer's designated recipient and the next operator where shifts succeed one another. Record the condition, equipment identity, date, exact location, operating restriction and disposition. Do not erase the finding after repair; add the repair and release evidence.

Function checks occur in a clear, controlled area following the manual, beginning with no load where appropriate. Test controls and emergency functions without intentionally defeating a safety device or approaching a failure condition. Gauges provide evidence, not decoration: abnormal pressure, temperature, charge or engine indications require the prescribed response. A normal display cannot cancel visible structural damage.

Application: the monthly record is current, but today's walkaround reveals a leaking outrigger check-valve assembly. Stop and obtain competent assessment; if the safety device is not functioning, the crane cannot operate. Do not treat a spotter watching the pad as an equivalent safety device. Common error: writing 'checked' without specifying an uncorrected defect and its status.

## C07 — Rope, reeving, blocks and line pull

A wire rope is an engineered assembly of wires, strands and core. Diameter, construction, grade, lay, rotation characteristics and termination must match the crane manufacturer's requirements. A rope with greater published breaking strength is not automatically an acceptable replacement: bending behavior, drum/sheave compatibility, rotation and design factor matter. Installation, end connections, lubrication and replacement are performed under the required qualified procedures, not improvised by an operator.

Inspect accessible running and standing ropes, especially crossover zones, repetitive pickup points, reverse bends and terminations. Look for broken wires, diameter loss, kinks, crushing, corrosion, birdcaging, heat damage, displaced core and improper fittings. A rope lay is the distance in which one strand wraps once around the rope. Under 1926.1413, running-rope category-II criteria include six random broken wires in a lay or three in one strand in a lay; rotation-resistant rope has different shorter-length counts. Standing-rope criteria differ again. Use the correct rule and any stricter manufacturer requirement; never transfer a running-rope threshold to every rope.

Prior power-line contact, a broken strand or rotation-resistant-rope core failure is not a 'watch it next week' defect. Stop affected operation and follow the required removal/replacement disposition. Do not handle a moving rope or measure it while exposed to a pinch point. Isolate the equipment and use the authorized inspection method for close examination. Wedge sockets, clips and other terminations need the right size, orientation, installation and inspection; an apparently tight improvised connection is not acceptable.

Reeving uses several supporting parts of line to distribute the suspended load. The allowable load depends on the crane's reeving table, permissible line pull, rope, block and system efficiency. Simple multiplication is only a preliminary screen. Example: if an instructional reeving table expressly permits 24,000 pounds on four parts, a 25,000-pound total suspended load fails even if the structural chart permits 30,000. Adding parts must be an approved configuration change, with appropriate rope length, block and reeving—not an improvised fix.

Know the difference between an overhaul ball and a multiple-sheave block. Include their required weights in chart deductions, including an unused suspended device when the manufacturer's notes require it. Maintain required drum wraps; construction operations must not lower below two full wraps, and a manufacturer may require more. Common error: using maximum winch pull as the allowable lifting capacity of the whole crane.

## C08 — Safety devices, operating aids and power systems

Safety devices and operating aids have different failure rules. Required safety devices under 1926.1415 include applicable level indication, boom/jib stops, brake locks, outrigger/stabilizer holding devices and horn. Operations must not begin with a required safety device inoperative; if one fails, stop safely and take the equipment out of service until corrected. Alternative measures are not a general option for these devices.

Operating aids include specified boom/jib limiting devices, anti-two-block protection and indicators for boom angle, length, radius, load or rated capacity as applicable. Under 1926.1416, particular temporary alternatives and repair deadlines may apply to an aid during repair. The operator does not invent an alternative. Stop safely, identify the exact device and rule, notify the employer and follow only a documented compliant arrangement, including more protective manufacturer instructions. The course's learner practice uses functioning aids throughout.

Two-blocking occurs when the hook block or ball contacts the boom-tip or another upper sheave assembly, potentially damaging the rope or dropping the load. Hoisting, telescoping and booming can change the separation. Monitor the geometry continuously; a functioning alarm is not permission to hoist until it activates. If clearance is disappearing, stop the motions creating the hazard and use the model's safe recovery sequence. Do not assume lowering the boom always increases separation.

A load-moment or capacity system depends on correct inputs. Match actual counterweight, boom, jib, outrigger position, reeving and work sector. A false setup entry can produce a reassuring but wrong display. Verify against physical configuration and the manual chart. Never bypass a limiter to finish a lift.

Power may pass through mechanical clutches/brakes, hydraulic pumps/valves/cylinders, electric drives or combined systems. Neutral controls do not mean stored energy is gone. Hydraulic thermal expansion or contraction can affect cylinder position and pressure; load drift demands safe response. Lattice friction systems require practiced coordinated brake/clutch control; hydraulic-crane familiarity is not enough. Application: the display says full outriggers but one beam is at partial extension. Stop and resolve the mismatch before lifting; the electronic number is not the setup.

## C09 — Load charts: select, read and calculate

Read the chart's identity and notes before its numbers. Match model, boom and jib, counterweight, outrigger/crawler condition, tires where applicable, work sector and operating mode. A chart for another configuration is not made valid by reducing its number informally. Know whether a listed capacity includes or excludes the hook block and other devices, and identify every required deduction. The chart's notes are operating restrictions, not optional explanatory text.

Radius is the horizontal distance from the crane's center of rotation to the vertical load line under the relevant loaded condition, not simply boom length or distance from an outrigger. Loaded boom deflection can increase radius. Determine the most restrictive point along the path. When a value lies between chart entries, apply the manufacturer's stated selection rule. Do not interpolate or average capacities unless explicitly allowed. A blank chart cell means no capacity supplied for that condition, not a value to estimate.

Gross chart capacity minus required deductions yields the payload allowance under that chart's convention. Compare the complete suspended load to every applicable limit: chart, reeving/line pull, hook/block, rigging, attachment, support and planned operating conditions. The lowest governing limit controls. A structural-limit region cannot be exceeded merely because tipping is unlikely, and a stability limit is not a promise that the crane will safely lift until it tips.

Worked example: a valid chart supplies 18,000 pounds at the governing radius. Required deductions are a 900-pound block, 300 pounds of slings/shackles and a 600-pound lifting beam. Net payload allowance is 16,200 pounds. A verified 16,500-pound payload exceeds it by 300 pounds. Do not round away the excess. Reconfigure or relocate through an approved new plan and recalculate.

Use the original practice charts in the laboratory below, then repeat the method on the actual manufacturer's chart in supervised practical work. Finding a printed number is only the first step. Explain why that table and column apply, which notes constrain the lift and what change would invalidate the calculation. Common error: subtracting rigging once in a net calculation and then comparing that net number with a total that subtracts it again.

## C10 — Rigging, weight and center of gravity

Verify weight from a reliable source or recognized calculation, accounting for residual contents, attachments and shipping fixtures. A nominal equipment weight may omit fluid or added parts. Resolve uncertainty before a lift. The regulatory instrument-based method in 1926.1417(o)(3)(ii) has specific conditions and a 75-percent verification trigger; it is not permission to blindly pick an unknown load. This program uses documented weights before practice lifts.

The hook should be above the load's center of gravity so the load hangs as planned. If the center of gravity is offset, sling legs may carry unequal shares. For a simple two-support instructional beam weighing 12,000 pounds with its center of gravity 2 feet from the left support and supports 8 feet apart, moment balance gives 3,000 pounds on the right and 9,000 on the left. Equal-looking slings do not establish equal load sharing.

Select rated slings, shackles, hooks and lifting devices for the actual hitch, angle, edge contact and environment. Protect slings from damaging edges with appropriate protection, not loose improvised material. Lower sling angles increase tension. For an ideal symmetric two-leg bridle carrying 10,000 pounds at 30 degrees above horizontal, each leg carries 10,000 pounds: 10,000 divided by twice the sine of 30 degrees. Real rigging requires its qualified selection and applicable manufacturer ratings; this arithmetic is not a rigging qualification.

Hooks must engage the intended load-bearing portion, not be tip- or side-loaded without design authorization. Use the correct shackle pin and orientation; do not substitute bolts. Distinguish a lifting beam's bending arrangement from a spreader's compression arrangement and respect the device's rated configurations. Keep hands from pinch points as slack is taken up. Perform a controlled initial lift to verify balance and security without allowing anyone beneath the load.

Application: a vessel's drawing gives empty weight, but its drain is blocked and contents remain. Stop and determine the actual load and center of gravity. Do not use a 'small test lift' to discover whether the crane can handle it. Common error: assuming a load with four lifting eyes shares weight equally among four legs.

## C11 — Signals, movement and precise placement

Before moving, identify the designated signal person, agreed signal method and emergency-stop procedure. Use standard hand signals for the operation; agree any necessary nonstandard signals before beginning. Under 1926.1421, voice signals follow the agreed sequence: function/direction, distance and/or speed, then function/stop. For example, an agreed command sequence can be 'Hoist up; slowly, two feet; hoist stop.' The team must communicate effectively in the language used. Test radios and use a dedicated reliable method. Never act on an ambiguous command; stop and clarify.

The operator must obey a stop signal from anyone. Loss of required communication means stop safely until communication is restored and the proper signal is given. One normal signal source at a time prevents conflicting commands; an agreed handoff transfers that role. Blind lifts require the appropriate qualified signal arrangements. A video image can help but does not eliminate applicable signaling requirements or blind spots.

Start with smooth, low-speed motion. Coordinate hoist, boom and swing to keep the load controlled and within the planned radius. Abrupt starts and stops increase dynamic forces and can cause pendulum motion. Do not chase a swinging load with rapid reversing controls. Stop and stabilize through trained, manufacturer-consistent actions. Keep the load clear of obstructions; never drag it sideways or use the boom to pry it free.

Use tag/restraint lines where needed to prevent hazardous rotation, with a safe handler position and a plan preventing entanglement. Do not wrap a line around a hand or body. Keep personnel out of the fall zone except the limited necessary activities permitted by the rule and lift plan. No one rides an ordinary load or hook. Lower onto prepared supports, release tension only after the load is stable and permit rigging removal without putting hands beneath it.

Worked decision: a radio message says 'bring it toward me' while the operator cannot identify the speaker or direction. Stop, establish the designated signal person and use a clear agreed function/direction command. Common error: assuming silence means continue on a blind lift. The laboratory tests stopping, not speed competition.

## C12 — Travel, weather, shutdown and emergencies

Plan unloaded travel as well as lifting. Check route support, grade, overhead clearance, boom configuration, locks, stowed equipment and escort requirements. Traveling under power lines uses its own rule and planning; the absence of a load does not eliminate electrical risk. Maintain clear sight lines and follow the manufacturer's permitted travel position and speed.

Pick-and-carry is permitted only where the manufacturer allows it. A competent person must supervise and address capacity reductions, load/boom position, support, route, overhead hazards and speed. Correct tire pressures and crawler configuration matter. A stationary-on-outriggers chart is not a traveling chart. Smooth travel cannot rescue an unauthorized configuration. Practice travel with a suspended load only under an approved plan and after the learner demonstrates prerequisite control skills.

Wind acts on both crane and load. A large panel may be unsafe below the crane's headline wind limit because of its sail area and load-specific restrictions. Gusts, snow, ice, low temperatures and lightning require the appropriate manufacturer/site decisions. Monitor conditions where the load and boom operate, not only behind a sheltered cab. Stop before control or stability is lost. Do not invent a universal allowable wind speed for all cranes and loads.

For shutdown, land and secure loads, place the boom/attachments in the prescribed condition, apply brakes and locks, isolate controls and secure access according to the manual and site plan. Do not leave an ordinary suspended load unattended. Storm preparation may require a different approved configuration; raising a boom or swinging freely is not a universal storm solution.

For unexpected drift, loss of control, overload warning, support movement or mechanical failure, stop the hazardous movement, warn/clear people and use the model-specific safe securing procedure. Do not counteract a developing tip by guessing which lever to pull. After an incident, isolate equipment and obtain required inspection before return. Fire response prioritizes escape and emergency assistance; use an extinguisher only if trained, conditions permit and escape remains available. Common error: resetting an alarm repeatedly without identifying its cause.

## C13 — Lattice-boom applications

Identify the installed lattice sections, their approved order, boom length, pendant arrangement, jib and counterweight. Inspect section members and connections for damage, missing pins or inappropriate repair. A bent lattice member is not merely cosmetic. Verify crawler extension or truck-outrigger configuration and the corresponding chart. Erection capacity and boom-raising limits may control even when no payload is attached.

Rope-operated boom hoists, pendants and stops have limits distinct from hydraulic telescopic booms. Monitor boom angle, loaded radius and permitted backward-stability conditions. A near-vertical boom with excessive counterweight or an improperly released load can create backward instability. Multi-drum machines require correct identification of each line, brake and clutch. An inactive drum or hanging block can still create an interference or deduction issue.

On a friction machine, control of hoisting and lowering relies on coordinated clutch and brake operation. Do not transfer familiar hydraulic lever habits to it. Practice begins with the trainer demonstrating the actual power flow, brake holding method and emergency securing sequence without a suspended practice load. Learners operate only after they can identify the controls reliably. The program does not permit free-fall training or personnel hoisting.

For crawler travel, establish the permitted boom direction, load, route and support; crawler movement can alter level and load distribution. A chart valid over one end or side does not automatically apply throughout swing. For lattice truck cranes, check the applicable sector, outriggers and counterweight. The umbrella 'lattice boom' describes related equipment, not identical handling.

Application: a learner finds a chart for a 100-foot boom but the assembled boom includes an extra section. Stop and identify the actual configuration, then obtain its chart and erection/operating limits. Do not use the shorter-boom capacity. Common error: treating a lattice-boom crane as a telescopic machine whose boom can simply be shortened during operation.

## C14 — Telescopic swing-cab and fixed-cab applications

Telescopic extension changes boom length, section overlap and often capacity. Some machines require specific extension sequences or permitted section combinations. Read the actual chart and system configuration, not only total length. Verify whether telescoping under load is permitted, and under which limits. Extending a boom can increase radius and reduce hook-to-tip clearance; simultaneously check capacity and two-block risk.

For swing-cab cranes, the operator rotates with the upper works. Preserve awareness of the counterweight, carrier, outriggers and objects behind the cab. For fixed-cab or fixed-station machines, the boom rotates relative to the operator. Controls and line of sight require deliberate familiarization; do not rely on the visual orientation learned on a swing-cab machine. At remote stations, choose a clear location outside crush/fall zones, protect communication and know the stop response to a lost link.

Match full, intermediate or asymmetric outrigger settings only to configurations expressly supported by the manufacturer. 'One short leg' is not a standard derating. Sensors need correct physical inputs and setup. Tire-supported lifting and pick-and-carry use separate conditions. A roadable boom truck can require a lift configuration very different from its travel setup.

Jib stowage or installation may require deductions even when lifting from the main boom. Apply extension offset, boom angle and other notes precisely. Do not use main-boom capacity for a jib-tip lift. A service-truck crane is not automatically covered by a large mobile-crane practical demonstration; its configuration, controls and rating require their own assessment. This package teaches the distinction and requires explicit scope on records rather than implying every mobile crane subtype was evaluated.

Worked decision: the chart offers a permitted intermediate-extension setup, but the actual left and right beams differ from that diagram. Do not average the extensions. Restore an approved setup or obtain the exact permitted configuration and repeat planning. Common error: entering a favorable setup into the rated-capacity system to silence a warning.

## C15 — Recognize specialized lift boundaries

Several operations require additional planning, qualifications or equipment beyond ordinary single-crane training. A multiple-crane lift transfers load share as geometry changes; dividing total weight by the number of cranes is not a plan. A qualified person must develop the lift plan and the designated lift director must meet applicable requirements. The operator verifies assigned share, communication and stop conditions rather than improvising synchronization.

Personnel hoisting is a restricted operation under 1926.1431, including the threshold determination about conventional access, suitable equipment/platform, trial and proof-test requirements, inspection, communication and employee protection. It is not an alternative to finding an appropriate access method merely because the crane is present. This program's practical work does not lift people. A future assignment requires the full applicable procedure and employer evaluation.

Floating/barging operations introduce list, trim, buoyancy, wave action and vessel/crane support constraints; use the applicable floating-equipment rules and engineered/manufacturer arrangements. A land chart alone cannot authorize a crane on a barge. Lifting a submerged item changes apparent weight as buoyancy is lost and may add trapped water, suction or snagging. Determine those forces before the lift rather than relying on its initial underwater reading.

Duty-cycle work, such as repetitive material handling, differs from occasional lifting in fatigue, dynamic loading and equipment requirements. Clamshells, magnets, pile-driving attachments and multi-drum work need approved configurations and task training. Rapid load release can produce backward instability or boom recoil. Prohibited free-fall conditions and controlled-lowering requirements must be addressed under 1926.1426; a free-fall switch is not general permission to use it.

Scenario: a supervisor proposes using two cranes because one is undersized, with each operator taking 'half.' Refuse to proceed without the qualified plan, assigned roles, verified configurations and required competence. Common error: treating specialized lifts as ordinary lifts with more verbal caution. Recognizing the boundary is an assessed operator skill, not an excuse to omit the hazard from instruction.

## C16 — Original chart laboratory and integrated lift decisions

These fictional tables teach calculation only. They are not equipment ratings and must never be used on a real crane. All dimensions are feet and weights pounds. Practice Crane A is a fictional telescopic crane, full outriggers, specified counterweight, level approved support, stationary, approved rear/side sectors. No jib is installed. Its chart includes all suspended equipment. Only the following boom/radius combinations are authorized in this exercise.

| Loaded radius | 60-foot boom gross | 80-foot boom gross |
|---|---:|---:|
| 20 | 30,000 | 24,000 |
| 30 | 22,000 | 18,000 |
| 40 | 16,000 | 13,000 |
| 50 | 11,000 | 9,000 |

Exercise notes: select the next larger listed radius when between rows; no interpolation. Only exactly listed boom configurations may be used. Front-sector lifts, travel and partial outriggers are prohibited in this exercise. The 900-pound block, 300-pound rigging and 600-pound beam must be deducted. A separate approved reeving table limits this exercise setup to 20,000 pounds total suspended load. Blank/unlisted configurations are unavailable.

Worked lift A: 80-foot boom, loaded radius 36 feet, payload 11,000 pounds. Select the 40-foot row: 13,000 gross. Deductions total 1,800, giving 11,200 payload allowance. Total suspended weight is 12,800, below both the chart and 20,000 reeving limit. The numerical comparison passes with 200 pounds of remaining chart allowance; it does not waive support, weather, rigging or path checks. Do not call that allowance a universal safe margin.

Worked lift B: the same payload must reach 43 feet. Select 50 feet: 9,000 gross and 7,200 net. The lift fails. It cannot be saved by using the pickup radius. Worked lift C: 60-foot boom at 20 feet offers 30,000 chart capacity, but the reeving limit is 20,000; after 1,800 deductions the governing payload allowance is 18,200. A 19,000-pound payload fails despite passing the structural chart comparison.

Practice Crane B is a fictional lattice crawler, specified 90-foot boom, fully extended crawlers, level approved support, stationary over the permitted end sector. Gross capacities are 26,000 at radius 25, 20,000 at 35 and 14,000 at 45. Use the next larger radius; other sectors/travel are not rated by this exercise. Deduct a 1,200-pound block, 400-pound slings and 800-pound spreader. Reeving permits 23,000 total.

Worked lift D: radius 32 means the 35-foot row. Net payload allowance is 20,000 − 2,400 = 17,600. A 17,000-pound payload passes numerically. A request to travel six feet with that load fails because no travel rating or plan is supplied. A different ground position might produce a shorter radius, but setup and the entire plan must be reassessed before using it.

Independent applications: (1) A/80-foot boom/radius 29/payload 16,500; (2) A/60-foot boom/radius 45/payload 9,000; (3) B/radius 40/payload 12,000; (4) A/80-foot boom/radius 30 with a newly installed jib whose deduction is unknown. Answers: (1) 18,000−1,800=16,200, fail by 300; (2) 11,000−1,800=9,200, numerical pass; (3) 14,000−2,400=11,600, fail by 400; (4) stop, obtain the correct configuration chart/notes and deduction before calculating. Explain each selection and stop condition aloud, then complete an actual-manufacturer chart exercise with the assessor. No fictional result transfers to a real crane.

## C17 — Service-truck application and remote control

A service-truck crane places lifting equipment on a vehicle whose chassis, body, stabilizers, installed equipment and storage arrangement affect the approved system. A winch's pull rating is not the truck/crane's lift rating. Confirm the approved installation, actual load chart, support configuration and operating sectors. Do not assume a small boom or modest load removes crane requirements. Determine applicable regulatory scope from the actual equipment and work, not its nickname.

Walk around the truck before deploying stabilizers. Check traffic, pedestrians, buried hazards, ground, parking position and clearance for the boom and load. Follow the manufacturer procedure for parking brake, transmission, chocks, engine/PTO or electrical supply and stabilizer extension. The actual sequence matters; a generic instruction to lift the tires or leave them loaded can be wrong. Do not lift from an unsupported sector or change truck equipment/counterweight assumptions without approved information.

Identify remote functions before enabling them. Stand where you can see the load and travel path while remaining outside the load's fall zone, boom sweep and stabilizer pinch areas. Protect a pendant cable from snagging; protect a radio controller from accidental activation. Check the emergency stop and the specified response to loss of communication using a safe no-load procedure. Never defeat a deadman or hold-to-run feature. If the best viewing position requires walking backward into traffic, change the plan or signaling arrangement.

For a shop component, verify its lifting eyes and weight, place the hook over its center of gravity, remove the load's restraint only at the appropriate planned stage, take up slack gently and check balance. Keep the load clear of the truck body and people. Land onto stable supports, slack only after stability is established, stow the boom and hook as specified and verify all supports are secured for travel. Do not use the crane to pull a component sideways out of a tight installation.

Worked decision: a mechanic can operate the remote but cannot identify which chart sector covers the far side of the truck. Do not make the lift. Locate the correct chart and complete model-specific familiarization. A service-truck performance record must not be represented as proof of competence on every larger fixed-cab crane. Common error: letting convenience of the handheld remote replace a deliberate safe operator position.

## C18 — Read the hand signals and rehearse the response

Signals describe machine functions, not wishes. Learn them with the official OSHA Appendix A chart posted at the exercise. The following instruction explains the essential distinctions; the trainer demonstrates the full applicable chart while learners name the function, direction and required response. The crane remains disabled during the first rehearsal. Only after correct interpretation does the learner practice motion under supervision.

For stop, one arm extends horizontally with palm down and moves side to side; emergency stop uses both arms. Respond immediately to either, regardless of who signals. For hoist, the raised forearm/index finger makes small circles; for lower, the downward-pointing hand/index finger circles. These move the load line. Raising or lowering the boom uses a horizontally extended arm with thumb up or down; confusing boom movement with hoisting can change radius and capacity dangerously.

Swing uses an extended arm/index finger indicating the direction of boom rotation. Telescopic extension uses both hands at waist level with thumbs pointing outward; retraction points the thumbs toward each other. A second hand in front of an action signal requests slow movement. Hands together at waist level signal dog everything, meaning hold/secure the crane's functions as appropriate—not permission to abandon a suspended load.

For combined movement, an extended arm with thumb up and fingers opening/closing requests raising the boom while lowering the load; thumb down with opening/closing fingers requests lowering the boom while raising the load. Do not execute a combined function until the signal is clear and the planned geometry allows it. A tap on the head selects the main hoist before the motion signal; tapping the bent elbow selects the auxiliary hoist. Selecting the wrong hoist can move an unintended suspended device.

Travel signals must match the crane type. The standard general travel gesture uses an outward/back pushing motion in the intended direction. Crawler travel includes distinct both-track and one-track signals on the chart; the trainer demonstrates these for the actual crawler, including which track is locked. Never guess from a distant, partially obscured hand. Stop and obtain a clear signal.

Rehearsal: the instructor alternates hoist, raise boom, stop, lower, swing, slow swing, retract and emergency stop, then selects main/auxiliary hoist and combined movements. The learner speaks the meaning before moving a disabled control. Repeat any confusion with the chart and a changed sequence. During the live station, silence or lost visibility is a stop condition when signals are required. No timed target justifies continuing on an uncertain instruction.

**Reason/remediation:** Remote convenience cannot replace safe positioning.

## C17-L2

Does a winch pull rating establish a service truck crane rating?

**Answer:** No. The complete installed crane/truck configuration and chart govern.

**Reason/remediation:** Component capacity is not system capacity.

## C18-L1

How does the hoist signal differ from raise boom?

**Answer:** Hoist uses upward-pointing forearm/index circles; raise boom uses horizontal arm and thumb up.

**Reason/remediation:** Confusing them changes radius instead of only line length.

## C18-L2

What must happen before acting on a nonstandard signal?

**Answer:** The required participants agree its meaning beforehand; ambiguity means stop.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not guess a gesture during movement.
