# VNDRLY/U Ultrasonic Examination — Phased Array

VUC059 · Original practical-training materials · 2026-11-18

## Program scope

The catalog's legacy QUPA name maps to API's current QUTE (PA) program. This VNDRLY course teaches manually applied phased-array ultrasonic examination of benign carbon-steel training welds, with setup, detection, characterization, location, sizing and reporting. API uses performance demonstration; its credential is separate from this course. [U01] The package does not award API, ASNT or employer UT Level II certification. Actual learner practical performance is required for the VNDRLY completion record and cannot be replaced by a simulator score.

Prerequisites are supported conventional UT competence, safe instrument handling, geometry/trigonometry and the employer's required vision/authorization evidence for the eventual job. Learners lacking these begin with supervised foundation instruction and remain trainees. Course training hours do not automatically satisfy another body's requirements. SNT-TC-1A-based qualification belongs to the employer's written practice; a training provider cannot independently issue that employer credential. [U02]

All P59 acceptance values are explicitly fictional teaching criteria. Actual production procedures, equipment manuals, material conditions and applicable standards govern production work. No proprietary external exam specimens, keys or complete code-equivalence claim are included. The practicals use cool, stable, depressurized bench specimens, suitable couplant and a supervised PAUT system; no live plant, hot inspection, radiation or confined-space work.

## Q01 — The examination contract and honest scope

Before touching the instrument, establish the component, material, weld geometry, required examination volume, expected discontinuities, access and reporting basis. Detection asks whether a reportable response exists; characterization asks what it represents; sizing and location describe dimensions and position. Acceptance is a further decision against applicable criteria. A technician may produce data without authority to accept the component.

Write a technique sheet that another examiner could reproduce. Identify procedure revision, coordinate convention, equipment configuration, coverage and limitations. An attractive image cannot cure an undefined examination volume. If one side is inaccessible, assess the resulting coverage against the approved procedure rather than silently calling the examination complete.

**Worked:** a 300 mm weld requires both specified directions, but only one is scanned. Report the achieved scope and missing direction; no whole-weld acceptance. **Changed:** access becomes available but a different wedge is fitted. Establish the correct setup and required checks before completing the missing scan. Practical P01 requires a real technique sheet tied to the bench specimen.

## Q02 — Wave modes, velocity and time of flight

Ultrasound travels through material in wave modes with different velocities. The velocity entered must correspond to the actual material and mode used. For a simple pulse-echo path, material sound path equals velocity multiplied by corrected round-trip time divided by two. Electronic/wedge delays must be addressed before applying that relationship. A raw total time is not automatically all material travel.

Use consistent units: 3.2 mm/µs multiplied by 20 µs and divided by two gives 32 mm sound path. This is not automatically vertical depth. For a straight normal beam in the stated simple model, path and depth coincide; for an angle beam they do not. Incorrect velocity shifts reported position even if the display appears stable.

**Worked:** corrected material transit time 20 µs at 3.2 mm/µs gives 32 mm. **Changed:** corrected time is 24 µs, giving 38.4 mm. If 4 µs of wedge delay was incorrectly included, the apparent result is wrong by 6.4 mm. P02 calculates and explains the assumptions, then compares a real known reference under supervision.

## Q03 — Wavelength, frequency and resolution tradeoffs

Wavelength equals velocity divided by frequency. With velocity 3.2 mm/µs and frequency 5 cycles/µs, wavelength is 0.64 mm. Frequency affects wavelength but does not alone determine practical resolution or penetration. Pulse characteristics, beam size, noise, attenuation, coupling and the examination geometry also matter. A higher-frequency selection is not always a better examination.

Separate axial resolution along the sound path from lateral discrimination across the beam. Two nearby reflectors may overlap in the response even when the numerical cursor permits many decimal places. Display precision is not measurement accuracy. Demonstrate relevant resolution with suitable reference features and the actual technique rather than citing screen pixel count.

**Worked:** two reflectors produce one unresolved response; do not report two precise boundaries from imagined detail. **Changed:** an authorized setup resolves them but loses required far-path sensitivity. Evaluate the complete technique, not just the improved local image. P03 records the tradeoff using actual reference responses and keeps the unsuccessful setup as a teaching comparison.

## Q04 — Refraction and geometric paths

At an interface, the selected incident and refracted modes have different directions and velocities. The simplified Snell relationship is sin(angle 1)/velocity 1=sin(angle 2)/velocity 2, with angles measured from the interface normal. A calculated sine exceeding one is not a physical transmitted angle for that proposed mode; stop and revisit the assumptions. Mode conversion and critical-angle behavior require the approved technique, not a guess.

For a flat first-leg ray with sound path S and refracted angle θ from normal, depth=S cosθ and surface offset=S sinθ. These formulas do not by themselves handle curved surfaces or multiple reflections. Record which leg and reference are used. A wrong leg can produce a plausible but incorrect depth.

**Worked:** S=40 mm, θ=60° gives depth 20 mm and offset 34.641 mm. **Changed:** the part is only 15 mm thick, so a 20 mm first-leg depth cannot describe an interior point without considering reflection geometry. Do not silently clamp depth to thickness. P04 draws the ray and names the applicable model before calculating.

## Q05 — Probe, wedge and instrument configuration

Identify instrument/software, probe, cable, wedge and reference blocks by actual identifiers. Enter the correct probe/wedge properties and material model. PAUT uses those inputs to form its focal laws; wrong inputs can affect both the image and the intended beam. [U03] Automatic recognition is useful but does not eliminate checking the physical assembly against the selected configuration.

Inspect the probe face, wedge condition, connector and cable using the manufacturer's safe instructions. A worn wedge can change contact and geometry. Do not reuse an old setup merely because its file name resembles the new job. Record the saved configuration version and any subsequent changes so data remain traceable.

**Worked:** file says wedge W1 while the bench assembly is W2. Stop before inspection, reconcile the assembly and perform applicable setup checks. **Changed:** W1 is fitted correctly but the cable/probe identity differs from the record. Resolve the actual system before relying on prior checks. P05 requires a physical inventory, not transcription of a supplied list.

## Q06 — Elements, apertures and focal laws

A phased array combines timed element responses to form a desired beam. The active aperture may use only part of the probe. Electronic linear scanning moves that aperture; sectorial scanning changes beam angle. Focus and steering are configured variables, not guarantees that every point in the component is examined. Manufacturer focal-law setup requires coherent material, element, aperture and angular inputs. [U04]

Count the intended laws and verify they fit the actual hardware. For a simple 32-element array using a contiguous 16-element aperture stepped one element at a time, there are 32−16+1=17 positions. This counting model is not a beam-quality calculation. Missing or weak elements can affect performance even if the instrument still displays an image.

**Worked:** a 32/16/step 1 plan contains 17 apertures. **Changed:** aperture becomes 8, giving 25 positions, but that change does not automatically improve the required coverage or sensitivity. Obtain the approved performance basis. P06 compares the configuration with the actual instrument and documents element-health evidence under its instructions.

## Q07 — Unwanted lobes and misleading indications

Energy can propagate away from the intended main beam and produce responses that appear at misleading locations. Array pitch, wavelength and steering affect this behavior; a simple rule should not replace demonstrated beam performance. Manufacturer explanations show why unwanted paths can create spurious indications. [U05–06] Do not classify every bright pixel as a flaw.

Investigate a suspect response using approved changes of angle, position and scan direction, known geometry and reference behavior. Preserve the original response and investigation. Increasing gain can make both useful and unwanted responses brighter; it is not proof of relevance. Conversely, calling something a lobe without evidence can hide a real discontinuity.

**Worked:** a repeated off-axis image follows a known edge and changes with beam configuration. Record the geometric/beam hypothesis and verify through controlled observations. **Changed:** a separate response persists at a consistent material location from the opposite direction. Do not dismiss it under the earlier explanation; investigate and report according to the procedure. P07 compares identified reference features and documents reasoning without requiring a predetermined image label.

## Q08 — Surface, coupling and stable scanning

Assess surface condition, access, contour, temperature and couplant compatibility before setup and scanning. Couplant bridges the acoustic path; unstable contact can cause signal loss that resembles a clean region. Surface preparation must be authorized and must not damage or erase relevant evidence. Use the approved couplant and safety information, protect the instrument from liquids and control slippery work surfaces.

Demonstrate repeatable contact on the reference and actual bench specimen. Maintain the intended index and orientation. If contact is lost, identify the affected interval and rescan with valid conditions; do not leave a silent gap. Mark coverage on a separate record so interruptions remain visible.

**Worked:** required scan 0–200 mm loses coupling 80–95 mm. The missing 15 mm is not “no indication.” **Changed:** the interval is rescanned but the probe index differs from the approved track. Verify whether the required volume is actually covered before closing the gap. P08 requires actual contact control and an interruption/recovery record.

## Q09 — Reference blocks and range checks

Choose references suitable for the intended material, geometry and measurement task under the approved instruction. A known reflector supports a specific check; it does not imitate every service flaw. Verify block identity and documented dimensions before using it. An unidentified block or a convenient surface echo may not establish the required range/depth accuracy.

Set the applicable velocity and delays, verify known positions over the required range and preserve readings. A one-point adjustment can conceal slope or offset error elsewhere. For training, check at least two known path/depth positions appropriate to the setup and all required configurations. The actual tolerances come from the approved technique; P59 supplies only classroom criteria.

**Worked:** reference positions 20 and 60 mm read 20 and 62. The second error is+2 mm; correcting only the zero will not necessarily solve the scale issue. **Changed:** the second reads 60 but the first 18. Document both and resolve the configuration rather than choosing the favorable point. P09 requires actual setup/readback on identified references.

## Q10 — Wedge delay and sensitivity across laws

The inspection sequence contains multiple beam states. Relevant delay and response checks must cover the states actually used, not a single favored angle. Manufacturer calibration guidance emphasizes wedge-delay and sensitivity treatment across the sequence. [U07] A calibration screenshot at one angle cannot establish every law used in the scan.

Follow the instrument and approved technique workflow, then verify known references at the ends and intermediate portions of the sequence as required. Preserve the actual calibration data and exceptions. If a law does not meet requirements, determine whether the technique is invalid or an authorized restricted scope is possible; do not silently omit a difficult law while claiming full coverage.

**Worked:** nine required checks have eight valid outcomes and one failed state. Coverage of valid checks is 88.8889%, but the inspection is not simply “89% acceptable.” **Changed:** the failed state is corrected, then the wedge is replaced. Establish the required new checks for the changed system. P10 requires actual multi-law reference verification and a reproducible record.

## Q11 — Amplitude references and compensation

An amplitude value depends on the reference, gain, path, coupling and beam configuration. Distance compensation and reference curves address specified response behavior; they do not prove that equal amplitudes imply equal physical flaw sizes. Preserve the reference conditions and distinguish scanning gain from evaluation gain. Saturated responses cannot be reliably compared as if their true amplitudes were known.

For a simple linear amplitude ratio, decibel difference=20 log10(A2/A1). Doubling amplitude corresponds to about 6.0206 dB; halving to−6.0206 dB. These are mathematical relations, not a universal sizing procedure. A required amplitude-drop technique must be applied under its actual validated conditions and limitations.

**Worked:** reference 40% screen height and response 80% have a2: 1 amplitude ratio only if neither is distorted and conditions are comparable. **Changed:** response clips at the display maximum. Reduce/adjust only under the approved method and retain the proper reference basis; do not size from clipping. P11 establishes and documents a real reference response without claiming amplitude alone gives crack height.

## Q12 — Reading A, S and position-related views

The A-scan shows a response against time/path; a sectorial image combines angular information; other views relate data to scan or index position. Determine the axes and cursor basis before interpreting a location. A color scale represents a selected response measure, not an automatic flaw classification. Selected A-scan and image location should correspond to the same law and acquisition state.

Review gate positions, ranges and display processing. A feature outside a gate may not appear in a summary image even if present in the underlying signal. If data are not spatially encoded, do not claim the file automatically documents exact physical scan position. Manual position records remain important.

**Worked:** an image cursor is interpreted as depth while its axis is sound path. Recalculate only with the correct geometry and actual reference. **Changed:** the axis is correct, but the displayed A-scan belongs to another selected angle. Reconcile the views before reporting. P12 requires a learner explanation on actual saved bench data and an annotated original screenshot/export.

## Q13 — Weld geometry and coordinate systems

Define zero, positive scan direction, weld centerline, inspection surface and depth convention before measuring indications. Plate, pipe and curved geometry require appropriate models. A root, cap, counterbore or mismatch can produce a response; geometry must be considered without assuming every response near it is harmless.

Draw the cross-section and identify accessible paths to the required weld and adjacent volume. A first-leg location formula on flat plate cannot be transferred unchanged to a small curved pipe. Use the approved geometric treatment and demonstrate it with suitable references. Preserve the coordinate convention in every report and data filename.

**Worked:** indication starts at 40 and ends 70 mm from the designated zero, length 30 mm. **Changed:** another examiner uses the opposite end of a200 mm specimen as zero; corresponding interval is 130–160 mm, still 30 mm. Failure to state origin could make the same flaw appear different. P13 requires physical marking on permitted removable tape and a dimensioned sketch.

## Q14 — Scan plans, overlap and coverage

A scan plan links probe positions and laws to the required examination volume and expected discontinuity orientations. Beam tracing is a planning aid, not evidence that coupling and sensitivity were actually maintained. Include access restrictions, scan directions, index positions, overlap and interruption recovery. A single nominal beam line is not the full beam or a guarantee of detection.

For simple track arithmetic, number of intervals across a width is width divided by step, with endpoints included as specified. Do not confuse number of tracks with number of spaces. Review the actual required volume after changes to wedge, angle or component thickness. Manual scans require disciplined position and coverage records even without an encoder.

**Worked:** tracks from 0 through 100 mm every 10 mm require 11 tracks. **Changed:** step 5 mm requires 21 tracks; this count alone does not establish sufficient overlap or detectability. P14 prepares a plan and physically follows its declared paths, recording any inaccessible region rather than checking a generic complete box.

## Q15 — Detection and reporting thresholds

Detection requires a valid setup and sufficient examination of the intended volume. A reporting threshold is a rule for recording results; it is not a physical boundary below which all flaws cease to exist. Apply the actual procedure's threshold and record required contextual information. Avoid both missing relevant responses and reporting every geometric echo as a flaw.

Investigate repeatability, position and response behavior through the approved technique. Preserve uncertainty when evidence cannot support a definitive classification. A “no reportable indications” statement must identify the examined scope, technique and limitations. It does not mean defect-free material.

**Worked:** a teaching criterion requires recording responses at or above 30% of the specified reference; 29% and 31% receive different reporting treatment under that fictional rule. **Changed:** calibration drift invalidates the reference. The numerical threshold comparison is no longer a valid examination conclusion; assess affected data and repeat as required. P15 detects and records actual known training features without access to the protected truth map.

## Q16 — Characterization and corroboration

Distinguish observation from interpretation. Location, orientation, amplitude behavior, response shape, motion and evidence from other directions contribute to characterization. A single feature name selected from a menu is not an explanation. Weld discontinuities and geometric reflectors may overlap in appearance; document what supports the interpretation and what remains uncertain.

Use complementary views or techniques only within approved competence and scope. Do not convert a broad response into an exact crack claim without adequate evidence, or dismiss a planar response because its amplitude is modest. Record the reported type, surface connection where established, and uncertainty honestly.

**Worked:** an indication follows a known root geometry but also yields a distinct response away from that geometry. Evaluate the additional response separately. **Changed:** another direction supports a surface-connected interpretation; update the record with that evidence, preserving the original observation. P16 uses multiple known-feature specimens so recognition is demonstrated rather than memorized from one image.

## Q17 — Position and length sizing

Report start and stop using the declared coordinate and the approved endpoint method. Length equals stop minus start only when both use the same origin/direction and describe the same continuous indication. Separate nearby indications according to the governing procedure rather than merging them for convenience. Instrument cursor precision does not remove uncertainty in endpoint selection.

Repeat the measurement and investigate disagreement. Surface marking, probe reference offsets and angle/leg interpretation can introduce systematic errors. State the method and relevant limits so the result can be reproduced. Production acceptance limits must not be borrowed from the training performance rubric.

**Worked:** start 35, stop 67 mm gives 32 mm length. **Changed:** verified endpoints become 37 and 66, length 29 mm; the change is−3 mm, not an automatic pass or failure without the applicable criterion. P17 requires actual endpoint measurements against instructor-controlled reference truth after the attempt.

## Q18 — Depth, height and sizing limits

Depth to a response, distance from a surface and through-wall height are different quantities. Establish whether the method is locating a reflector, identifying a tip or estimating extent by amplitude behavior. A low-amplitude tip response can be confused with noise; a strong corner response may not identify the far tip. The selected method must be suitable and demonstrated for the task.

For two correctly identified depths on the same coordinate basis, height is their difference. This arithmetic does not validate the identification. For a surface-connected feature, reference the correct surface and local thickness. Avoid reporting unsupported height for a response whose boundaries cannot be resolved.

**Worked:** established upper/lower boundaries at 6 and 11 mm give 5 mm height. **Changed:** the 11 mm response is shown to be geometry; height is no longer established, not simply 5 mm with a disclaimer. P18 requires actual measurements on known features under the approved training technique and an explicit “not established” response where resolution is inadequate.

## Q19 — Verification drift and data integrity

Perform required checks at the intervals and after changes defined by the procedure. If a check fails, identify the affected examination interval since the last valid check, notify the responsible authority and determine re-examination under the procedure. A later passing check does not retroactively prove earlier data valid. Preserve failed results and corrective actions.

Save configuration, references, specimen identity, raw data where available and final reports with traceable filenames. Do not overwrite an original record to hide an error. Corrected reports must identify revision and reason. A PDF summary without enough setup/coverage evidence may be insufficient to reproduce the examination.

**Worked:** valid check 09: 00, failed check 11: 00; scans 09: 10–10: 55 are potentially affected. **Changed:** instrument is corrected 11: 15; re-examination must address the affected scope, not only the last specimen. P19 uses a controlled reference change or instructor-issued drift card; never damage equipment to create failure.

## Q20 — Independent performance and defensible reporting

The final practical combines preparation, actual setup, scanning, interpretation and reporting on an unseen training set. The assessor needs a validated protected truth map, including sound regions and known geometric responses, so false calls are evaluated alongside missed detections. Assessment conditions must support the taught technique; unknown specimen truth cannot be treated as objective grading evidence.

Submit equipment inventory, technique/setup records, coverage, indication table and limitations. The assessor compares actual work to the training rubric without coaching during the scored attempt. Failed domains require instruction and a changed specimen set; rehearsing the same memorized answers is not proof of transferable skill.

**Worked:** eight of ten reference features are found and two sound regions are falsely called. Report detection 80% and two false calls separately; do not combine them into a flattering single accuracy number. **Changed:** all ten are found but the coordinate origin is wrong. Position reporting still fails its criterion. I59 requires actual PAUT performance; missing equipment or supervision means the learner remains pending, regardless of written scores.

## P59 specimen and procedure design

Teaching set T: flat welded plate, curved welded pipe segment, known geometric reflector specimen, and reference sizing specimen. Scored set A and changed set B must each be independent of T and each other. Each scored set includes at least two plate weld regions, two curved weld regions and two sizing features, with ten independently established reportable features distributed across the set and twenty designated sound grading regions. Include at least one relevant planar feature, a volumetric feature, a base-metal feature and benign geometric responses. These are training-set design requirements, not claims that hardware has been procured or that API uses this set.

Before delivery, the technical authority documents actual specimen IDs, material/thickness/curvature, feature truth/uncertainty, feasible technique and safe handling. Use certified reference documentation or independent qualified characterization adequate to establish grading truth; never invent crack dimensions from a single unvalidated scan. If the truth cannot support the sizing tolerance, replace the assessment feature or establish a defensible approved rubric before any scored attempt. No post-hoc loosening to pass a learner.

Technique sheet must specify instrument/software; probe/wedge/cable; material mode/velocity; aperture/laws; scan directions/tracks; reference and calibration checks; sensitivity/evaluation basis; reporting method; location/length/height methods; required coverage; verification intervals; data retention; and acceptance of the training performance. The authority supplies actual equipment-specific button sequence from the instrument manual. Learner independently reproduces it and records achieved checks. A prefilled setup file is not a substitute for demonstrated setup competence.

# VUC059 sources, identity mapping and coverage

Reviewed2026-11-18. Cases, numerical examples, training thresholds and assessment sets are original VNDRLY materials. Source descriptions below support limited technical points; no external exam key/specimen truth is reproduced. Full proprietary standards and complete external equivalence were not verified.

**U01 API current program:** https://www.api.org/products-and-services/individual-certification-programs/certifications/api-qute-pa . Current nameQUTE(PA), whereas catalog retains legacyQUPA. Public page describes a hands-on examination of weld inspection skills and separate external eligibility. Linked candidate orientation https://apidam.widen.net/content/rf1xwwyo2s/pdf/=c&quality=80&x.app=portals has July2024 internal date, older metadata and apparent inconsistent height-reporting wording. It is not silently treated as a current unambiguous procedure. VNDRLY uses its own disclosed practical rubric and no claim of API scoring replication.

**U02 ASNT credential boundary:** https://www.asnt.org/me/26/0/advisory-on-snt-tc-1a-certification-practices-and-use-of-asnt-credentials . June2026 advisory distinguishes employer-based qualification from a training provider's completion document. No ASNT brand or employer credential is awarded here. https://education.asnt.org/local/catalog/view/product.php?productid=35 identifies PA training as only one part of qualification, requiring practical work; our80-hour plan is independently defined and does not claim automatic credit.

**U03 Evident display/setup context:** https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/phased-array/anatomy . Supports correct material mode/velocity and actual probe/wedge inputs and their importance to beam states. Original examples illustrate wrong-configuration consequences; no equipment-specific button sequence is invented.

**U04 Evident focal-law inputs:** https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/phased-array/focal-law-setup . Supports distinction among active elements, stepping, angle and focus. Aperture-count examples are original discrete arithmetic,not a claim that count establishes beam quality.

**U05 Evident unwanted lobes:** https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/transducers/lobes . Supports off-main-path responses and potential image ambiguity. No simplified element-size statement is promoted into a universal acceptance rule.

**U06 IMASONIC design context:** https://www.imasonic.com/industry/design-notes-for-phased-array/ . Supports dependence on pitch,wavelength,steering and the limits of simplified design estimates. Actual system performance must be demonstrated; this course does not design probes.

**U07 Evident calibration:** https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/calibration/calibrationnormalization . Supports checking wedge-delay/sensitivity across the used law sequence and reference-response compensation. No published amplitude acceptance table or universal flaw-size conversion is claimed.
