# VUC011 — instructor and credential guide

**Program:** VNDRLY/U Construction Worker Safety. Version 1.0, 17 November 2026. Audience: construction workers and new site entrants needing general hazard recognition. Prerequisites: no specialist credential; participation in an understood language with accessible materials. Purpose: identify construction exposures, choose safe worker-level actions, report accurately and recognize tasks requiring additional qualification.

This is VNDRLY-owned training and assessment addressing the source subject. It is not an authorized OSHA Outreach 10-hour course, does not issue an OSHA card and does not substitute for legally required task-specific training. Do not name the awarded credential “OSHA 10.” Material production records no actual attendance or learner competence.

## Outcomes and sequence

Teach C01 rights/reporting, C02 falls, C03 electricity, C04 struck-by, C05 caught-in/between, C06 PPE, C07 health, C08 access/scaffolds, C09 excavation and C10 tools/material/emergency decisions. Each written chapter provides explanation, realistic application, common error and worked reasoning. Twenty additional formative questions require attempted answers and correction. The final contains forty different prompts, four per domain. The six applied stations require observable decisions and safe inert demonstrations.

## Ten instructional hours

This planned delivery comprises six core hours, two elective hours and two expanded-topic hours. It reflects the source topic families while awarding only VNDRLY's credential. Actual facilitated duration must be recorded; document length is not proof of elapsed instruction. Breaks, administration and final/independent practical assessment are additional.

C02–C05 total four hours including ninety minutes falls. C08 and C09 provide two distinct electives; C10 expands practical construction hazards for two hours. If more time is needed, extend it; never compress a misunderstood critical decision to meet the plan.

For each exercise ask what mechanism causes injury, what prevents exposure, who can decide specialist details, and what changed fact would invalidate the plan. If a learner repeats “be careful,” request a concrete safe route or hold condition. Provide reading/language support and accessible oral responses without lowering technical criteria or supplying final answers.

## Preparation and training safety

Instructor needs demonstrated competence in construction hazard recognition and the ability to explain applicable scope limits. Read this package, primary references and protected instruments before teaching. Inert props and synthetic case data are sufficient; do not stage live work at height, energized contact, trench entry, rigging, vehicle movement, chemicals or emergency rescue. No actual complaints, alerts, operational reports or worker assignments are created by training.

Supply coursebook, case cards, paper, calculator and inert PPE/ladder/cord illustrations. Descriptions in the packet can substitute for photographs. Local emergency/contact orientation may be added using actual authorized information, but do not invent it or treat a fictional scenario as a site plan. For real equipment qualifications arrange the separately required authorized training and practical assessment.

## Evaluation, scoring and retakes

All twenty formative checks require an attempt. After an error, revisit the chapter and have the learner explain corrected reasoning in a new situation. Formative answers are intentionally explained; they are not the independent final.

Final: forty constructed responses, two points each. Nominal ninety minutes plus documented accommodations. Calculator and blank paper allowed, no completed exercises or key. Two points for required correct decision and reasoning, one for materially correct but incomplete without unsafe assertion, zero absent/incorrect/unsafe. Pass 64/80 overall and at least 6/8 in every domain. Endorsing unsafe exposure, bypass, unauthorized entry/operation or fabricated evidence identified in the key requires remediation and independent reassessment regardless of total.

Six practical stations A–F each have five observable criteria. Pass 8/10 per station and every critical criterion 2. First scenarios are coached learning; use fresh variants independently. Retain exact variant and observations. A verbal claim to have read the procedure or a completed quiz does not demonstrate the practical decision.

Following failure, teach the gap and provide fresh practice. Protected alternate cards support a new equivalent-demand instrument: four prompts per failed domain, same 6/8 floor. Retain valid same-version independent passing scores and recompute the 64/80 total; otherwise use a full retest. Keep original failures and every attempt. Two unsuccessful retakes require a documented learning plan before reassessing failed components. Do not reveal a key and immediately retest identical answers.

## Credential and records

Award VNDRLY/U Construction Worker Safety — Orientation after all instruction, corrected formative work, independent final and all six stations pass. Record learner identity, material version, actual dates/hours, domain scores, practical criteria/observations, examiner competence, attempts, accommodations and restrictions. A blank form means pending, never passed.

Recommend annual workplace review and additional learning after changed duties, site hazards/procedures or demonstrated gaps. This is a VNDRLY recommendation, not an external card-expiration rule. Site, employer or jurisdiction requirements may demand an actual authorized Outreach card and specific qualifications. This credential provides none of those by implication. Publication and live learner delivery remain separate from completion of these materials.

# VNDRLY/U Construction Worker Safety

Original worker-orientation instruction, VUC011. Read each case, make a decision, then compare your reasoning with the explanation. This course develops recognition, avoidance and communication. It does not make you a competent person, equipment operator, electrician, rigger, rescuer or authorized OSHA Outreach trainer.

## C01 — Your role, rights and a usable hazard report

A construction site changes as work advances. Yesterday's safe walkway may become today's delivery lane; a completed floor may have new openings. Start by learning the current work area, employer contact, alarm, assembly route and how to obtain assistance. Identify your own task and neighboring work before starting. Ask for instruction you understand when the task, equipment or conditions are unfamiliar. Training attendance is not permission to perform every trade.

Employers have responsibilities for a safe workplace and required hazard training. Workers can raise safety concerns and use OSHA's complaint process; reporting an injury or hazard should not lead to retaliation. Tell the responsible supervisor about immediate hazards and protect yourself from the affected exposure. For an imminent emergency, use the site's emergency arrangements rather than wait for routine paperwork. The right to refuse dangerous work has specific legal conditions; it is not a blanket rule that any disliked task may be refused without consequences. Obtain accurate current rights information from OSHA or the applicable state-plan agency.

A useful report says where, what was observed, who could be exposed and what immediate protection is needed. Separate observation from inference. “West stair opening uncovered at 08:10; two deliveries are due through that route” is stronger than “this site is unsafe.” Do not enter a dangerous area to get a better photograph. Preserve original messages and times; do not invent an inspection result. A near miss, injury report and retaliation concern are different records even if they arose from the same event.

To seek official help, use OSHA's worker complaint page or the applicable state-plan agency. Identify the employer and exact worksite, describe the hazardous task/location and timing, explain who is exposed and supply truthful contact information through the chosen route. Follow the agency's instructions for a signed complaint and any confidentiality request; do not promise a particular inspection outcome. For a retaliation concern, preserve the actual adverse action and dates and promptly use the relevant retaliation process. A hazard complaint and retaliation complaint are not automatically the same filing.

The protected refusal framework requires specific conditions, including a good-faith belief in imminent danger, a reasonable person's agreement that death or serious injury is genuinely threatened, insufficient time for ordinary enforcement, and seeking employer correction where possible. Explain the danger and request correction or other work; follow applicable guidance rather than walk away assuming every refusal is protected. In an actual emergency, get to safety and summon help. This lesson does not decide an individual's legal case.

**Worked example:** A laborer reports that a railing was removed for material delivery. The foreman says, “It will be back after lunch.” The laborer keeps out of the exposed route, identifies an approved alternative and asks the responsible person to arrange effective protection before anyone uses the opening. A delayed repair promise does not protect the interval. If another contractor controls the opening, coordination is required; the hazard does not disappear because it belongs to someone else.

The OSHA injury log is an employer record with recordability rules; it is not a suggestion box and not every event belongs on it. A worker supplies accurate facts through the employer's reporting route, without diagnosing recordability from this orientation. An illustrative record reads: “Fictional worker A; wrist injury during material placement; actual treatment/restrictions not yet supplied.” The missing information must remain missing. A separate mock near-miss report can state that a falling object struck no person. Do not fabricate medical details to complete a form.

**Practice:** A coworker wants a near miss deleted because no one was hurt. Explain your decision. **Answer:** preserve a factual report so the condition can be corrected; no injury is an important fact, not a reason to erase the event. If threatened for reporting, preserve the actual communication and promptly consult the appropriate official complaint/retaliation route. Different laws have different time limits; do not wait for an internal investigation to assume a deadline is extended.

## C02 — Recognize falls and choose a protected way to work

A fall begins with an exposure path: a person loses footing, steps through an opening, reaches beyond a protected area or uses an unsuitable access method. Identify the edge or opening, the lower surface and what the task makes you do. Carrying material can hide a hole; reaching to guide a load can move the worker beyond a rail. Good balance and experience do not eliminate these mechanisms.

Under the general construction unprotected-edge rule, protection is required at six feet or more above a lower level. Other situations have their own provisions; work over dangerous equipment and stepping through holes can require protection below that height. Scaffolds have separate rules addressed in C08. Never treat a single number as proof that a lower exposure is safe. The responsible competent/qualified people must determine the suitable arrangement and surface integrity.

Prefer a work method that avoids exposure. A suitable guarded work area or platform can remove a need to reach outside protection. Guardrails, secured suitable covers, nets and personal fall protection serve different functions and are not interchangeable decorations. Do not remove a rail or move a cover without the authorized alternative in place. An unmarked loose sheet is not a verified hole cover. Ask how the cover is secured and suitable for the actual loads; do not stand on it to test it.

A harness alone is not a complete fall-arrest system. The arrangement includes compatible components, suitable anchorage, clearance, swing-fall control, inspection, instruction and rescue planning. Clearance must allow arrest before striking a lower level. A pipe that looks sturdy is not automatically an anchor. This course uses drawings and inert equipment; actual fitting and use require the relevant equipment instruction and qualified assessment. Stop if the approved arrangement is unavailable or changed.

**Worked example:** A crew must move a duct near an open floor edge eight feet above a lower slab. One person proposes clipping to a temporary handrail. The correct response is to hold the exposed move and obtain the planned protected handling method, rather than invent anchorage from nearby steel. Repositioning the task within verified guarding may be possible; if not, competent planning must supply a complete alternative.

**Changed condition:** The original route is protected but a delivery requires temporary gate opening. The protection decision applies during the opening as well as before and after it. The worker stays outside the exposed zone until the authorized procedure protects that transition. “Only for a minute” describes duration, not protection.

**Practice:** A covered opening has been uncovered to run a hose, and a coworker says the drop is only three feet. **Answer:** keep people from the opening and arrange suitable protection and routing. Stepping into the hole and falling objects remain hazards; a blanket six-foot exemption is wrong. Report the changed condition instead of assuming yesterday's inspection still applies.

## C03 — Electricity: recognize the contact path

Electrical injury can follow direct contact, conductive tools or wet/damaged equipment. A cable may be hidden above a ceiling or below ground. Before work, identify known electrical sources and the employer's protective arrangements. Do not assume a line is dead because it is quiet, dark or covered. Treat uncertain electrical conditions as unresolved and ask the responsible qualified person to establish a safe method.

Look at portable equipment without opening energized parts. Worn insulation, damaged plugs, missing grounding pins, wet connections, repeated trips or makeshift repairs require removal from affected use and reporting. Do not wrap damage with tape as your own repair or replace a protective device with a larger one to stop tripping. Keep cords out of damaging vehicle paths and water using the approved arrangement. Equipment must suit the task and environment.

Construction ground-fault protection can involve the specified GFCI provisions or a compliant assured equipment grounding conductor program. The employer establishes the applicable program; a worker cannot declare “we have a program” to excuse damaged equipment. Follow the required checks and report a failed device. A GFCI can reduce certain ground-fault risks, but does not make every contact safe or repair a defective cord. Do not bypass it when it interrupts the job.

Overhead-line exposure includes the entire travel envelope of equipment, load and attachments. A worker's ability to pass beneath a line says nothing about a raised boom or long metal section. Required clearances and protective measures depend on the actual equipment, voltage and governing rules. Do not guess a universal distance or serve as a spotter without the required role/training. Stop outside the uncertain area and obtain the plan.

**Worked example:** A concrete saw trips its supply after rain. A coworker suggests a different unprotected outlet. The correct decision is to stop the affected use and have the fault and suitable supply assessed. Moving the plug bypasses the warning while retaining the exposure. Do not touch a person or machine in contact with electricity; summon emergency assistance and qualified isolation rather than become a second casualty.

**Practice:** A worker is asked to drill where a wall plan is outdated. **Answer:** hold drilling until the actual concealed-services risk is established and controlled by the responsible people. An old drawing or absence of a visible cable does not establish a safe path.

## C04 — Keep out of struck-by paths

Struck-by events involve moving vehicles, swinging or falling loads, ejected fragments and materials that shift unexpectedly. Look for the path the object can take, not just its present location. A suspended load can rotate; a vehicle can reverse; a tool can throw debris beyond the operator. PPE may limit some injury but cannot make a load path an acceptable walkway.

Use designated pedestrian routes and respect barriers. Do not assume the operator sees you because you can see the machine. Establish the site's controlled crossing/communication before approaching and keep out of swing and pinch zones. If a spotter loses the required communication or position, the operation must follow its stop arrangement. This orientation does not qualify a signal person or equipment operator.

Overhead work needs controls that prevent objects falling and protect the area below. A hard hat is not the complete plan. Keep materials secured, use the approved exclusion zone and report changes that defeat it. Wind, a new access route or another contractor can change who is exposed. Never step under a suspended load to save time or retrieve a tool.

**Worked example:** A telehandler delivers bundled pipe while a pedestrian route crosses the unloading area. The bundle is low, but movement and rolling still threaten a worker. The pedestrian waits in the designated safe area and the responsible team coordinates an alternative or controlled crossing. The worker does not squeeze between the load and a wall, stand on forks or guide unstable pipe by hand.

Cutting and grinding create another path: chips, sparks or fragments can reach coworkers. Required guarding and exclusion/positioning protect people beyond the operator. Report missing guards or an unsuitable setup before work. Do not create a spark stream toward flammable storage simply because the operator wears eye protection.

**Practice:** A barrier blocks the fastest route after an overhead installation begins. A coworker lifts it to pass. **Answer:** use the approved alternate route or ask for coordinated access after the hazard is controlled. A temporary inconvenience is not authority to enter the fall zone. Explain the object path in the report, not just “barrier moved.”

## C05 — Caught-in and caught-between hazards

These hazards trap or crush a person between moving parts, equipment and fixed objects, or collapsing material. Identify both sides of the trap and how energy can close the gap. A slowly moving machine can still crush. Loose clothing or hair can be drawn into rotating parts, and gloves can add entanglement risk at some equipment. Use the task-specific controls rather than assume more PPE is always safer.

Keep guards and barriers effective. Do not reach around a guard to clear a jam or hold a piece near a moving pinch point. Stopping a control is not proof that all motion, stored energy or unexpected restart is prevented. Servicing, adjustment and cleaning need the applicable authorized procedure and trained people. A worker outside that role reports the problem and keeps clear; they do not improvise an isolation sequence from this course.

Earth and structures can also close a space. A trench wall can collapse with little warning. A stack can lean after a support is disturbed. Do not enter between an unstable load and a fixed surface to “steady it.” Keep people out and obtain the competent stabilization/protective decision. C09 expands excavation recognition; this chapter explains why entering to inspect a defect can itself be the exposure.

**Worked example:** A mixer stops with material lodged near its drive. A coworker says the switch is off and asks you to pull it free. You decline the reach and report the jam for the authorized safe servicing process. The missing evidence concerns all relevant energy and unexpected movement, not whether the motor currently sounds quiet.

**Practice:** A worker wants to walk between an excavator counterweight and a wall while the operator adjusts position. **Answer:** remain outside the crushing zone and obtain controlled separation. Eye contact does not enlarge the gap or eliminate unexpected swing. Do not use yourself as the object that tests clearance.

## C06 — PPE must fit the person and the task

PPE is selected for identified hazards and must fit properly. Oversized gloves can interfere with grip; loose eyewear can leave a gap; incompatible face and hearing protection can defeat both. Inspect condition and use the employer's supported selection, not whichever item is nearest. Report missing, damaged or poorly fitting equipment before the affected exposure.

Know the protection and its limits. Eye protection for impact is not automatically splash protection. A face shield may supplement rather than replace the needed eye protection. Ordinary gloves do not establish chemical compatibility or electrical protection. A hard hat does not authorize entry under a suspended load. The employer must ensure suitability even when employees supply their own equipment.

Respirators involve a separate applicable program, including medical evaluation, fit testing for tight-fitting use and training where required. A dust mask found in a truck is not a substitute for task-specific exposure controls. Do not select a cartridge by smell or assume a seal from appearance. Tell the responsible person when a required respirator cannot be used correctly and hold the exposure.

**Worked example:** A worker's issued glasses slide during bending and a replacement face shield interferes with hearing protection. The worker asks for a compatible properly fitting arrangement and demonstrates fit in a safe area. The solution is not to remove the hearing protection to make the shield comfortable while the noise continues.

**Practice:** An intact glove is supplied for an unidentified cleaning liquid. **Answer:** its condition is not enough; product identity and task compatibility must be established. Keep the task on hold rather than testing the glove by touching the liquid. In the practical, inspect inert samples and explain these limits without handling hazards.

## C07 — Health hazards can be invisible or delayed

Dust, noise, chemicals, heat and repetitive loading can harm workers without an immediate dramatic event. Identify the material and task before disturbance. Drilling concrete, removing old coatings and working in an enclosed space create different exposure questions. Lack of odor or visible dust does not prove a healthy atmosphere. Stop an unassessed change and obtain the relevant plan.

For construction silica work, the specified-control route requires the actual task and conditions to match Table 1 and all specified controls to operate properly; otherwise the applicable exposure-assessment route is needed. Owning a wet saw or vacuum is not enough if water delivery fails or the collection arrangement does not match the task. Do not continue dry because the tool still cuts. Follow the assessed housekeeping method rather than redisperse dust. This orientation does not authorize exposure-limit selection or respiratory-program decisions.

Use labels and safety data sheets to understand chemical identity, hazards, controls and response. Match the product, not just the brand family. A mock extract for fictional Seal-R lists skin/eye irritation, flammable vapor and incompatible ignition sources; its control note requires specified ventilation and compatible gloves. That extract teaches information use, not an actual product procedure. Find the full current SDS and site method before real work. An unlabeled bottle is an unresolved identity, not a safe substitute.

Use the SDS structure to locate information: section 1 identifies the product, section 2 its hazards, section 4 first aid, section 6 accidental-release measures, section 7 handling/storage, section 8 exposure controls/PPE and section 10 stability/reactivity. Confirm the document matches the actual formulation. In the Seal-R exercise, a second sheet marked Seal-R Plus cannot establish the original product's glove selection. Preserve the mismatch and obtain the correct information. A warning symbol or signal word alerts you to a hazard; it does not by itself supply the complete safe procedure.

Noise protection and heat controls need actual work assessment. Report difficulty hearing alarms and signs of heat illness. Confusion, collapse or severe symptoms warrant immediate emergency response, cooling assistance as trained and prompt medical help; do not leave the person alone or wait for a routine form. Water, rest, shade/cooling, acclimatization and workload planning are preventive measures, not a promise that anyone can work indefinitely in heat.

**Worked example:** Outdoor wet cutting moves indoors while water supply is unavailable. The crew proposes using the same mask. The material is unchanged, but environment and control have changed. Hold the unsupported task, notify the supervisor and obtain an effective supported method. Do not use lack of visible dust as acceptance evidence.

**Practice:** A newly hired worker reports shoulder pain while lifting repeated bags. **Answer:** report symptoms through the appropriate route and evaluate height, weight, grip, repetition and assistance. Merely changing the worker without changing the demanding task may transfer rather than control the exposure.

## C08 — Ladders, stairs and scaffolds are planned access

Choose access suitable for the task, load and environment. A ladder is not always the right work platform for a task requiring force, two-handed material handling or sustained reach. Inspect accessible components before use and report defects. Keep access areas clear, secure the setup as required, face the ladder and maintain a safe climbing method. Do not carry a load that prevents a secure hold or climb on the top of a stepladder as an improvised platform.

For a non-self-supporting ladder, the common setup rule places its foot about one-quarter of the working length from the upper support. Use the actual approved ladder instructions and stable support; do not confuse total ladder length with the supported working length. For access to an upper landing, rails normally extend at least three feet above it, with the specified secured/grasping alternative where extension is not possible. This is not permission to use a damaged or electrically unsuitable ladder.

A scaffold's safe use depends on its actual design, erection, load, access, protection and condition. A competent person must inspect for visible defects before each shift and after events affecting integrity. A tag can communicate status but does not repair a removed brace or overloaded platform. Users report changes and keep off unresolved equipment; they do not modify it themselves. Do not climb cross-braces as access or stand on a bucket to gain height.

Scaffold fall protection is governed separately; the general scaffold threshold is more than ten feet above a lower level, with requirements varying by type and activity. Do not borrow the six-foot construction-edge rule as the full scaffold plan or assume lower scaffolds are free of hazards. Actual access, falling-object protection and suitability still matter. Erection, dismantling and alteration require additional trained roles beyond this orientation.

**Worked example:** A scaffold was cleared at the start of shift, but a delivery struck its base. A worker points to the morning tag. The correct response is to keep off and obtain competent reassessment after the event. The earlier inspection described an earlier condition.

**Practice:** A straight ladder has a twelve-foot supported working length. The one-quarter model gives a three-foot base offset. Explain why that arithmetic alone is insufficient. **Answer:** ground/support, securing, condition, load, electrical exposure, landing access and actual manufacturer's instructions remain to be checked. Do not climb to test a doubtful setup.

## C09 — Excavations: keep uncertainty outside the trench

An excavation can expose workers to collapse, falling material, utilities, water, equipment, hazardous atmosphere and inadequate escape. Recognize the whole arrangement before entry. A worker does not determine soil stability by eye or choose a protective system after taking this orientation. The competent person and applicable design information govern inspection and protection.

Cave-in protection is required except for the standard's stable-rock exception or an excavation less than five feet deep where competent examination finds no indication of a potential cave-in. “Under five feet” by itself is not permission. A trench box protects within its intended arrangement; it does not make every location outside it safe or automatically solve water, atmosphere and access issues.

Trenches four feet or deeper need safe egress arranged so employees travel no more than twenty-five feet laterally to reach it. Keep escape usable; a ladder blocked by pipe is not usable because it exists on a drawing. Rain, vibration, changing loads and new cracks can change conditions. Follow required competent inspection and removal from hazardous conditions. Do not wait in the excavation for someone to verify a newly observed failure.

Utilities must be located and protected through the applicable process before excavation can expose them. Spoil, equipment and falling loads need control at the edge. Keep out from under lifting/digging loads and do not enter an unverified atmosphere. A gas reading or an open top alone does not decide every hazard. If someone collapses, call the designated emergency response; do not make an untrained entry rescue.

**Worked example:** A five-foot-deep trench has an access ladder, but the route from the work point is thirty feet and rain has entered overnight. The ladder does not satisfy the twenty-five-foot travel arrangement, and yesterday's condition cannot settle today's protection. Hold entry and obtain current competent inspection, protection and usable access.

**Practice:** A four-foot trench has fresh cracks and a coworker says shoring is unnecessary below five feet. **Answer:** keep out and report the cave-in indication for competent action; the shallow-depth exception is conditional. A worker must not convert a threshold into a personal soil classification.

## C10 — Tools, materials and coordinated emergency decisions

Before using a tool, identify the intended task, correct accessory, condition, guards, supply and people nearby. Do not use a cracked handle, removed guard or incompatible attachment because the job is short. Follow the manufacturer's and employer's trained method. Keep hands and other people outside cutting, pinch, fragment and spark paths. Disconnect and otherwise make safe through the applicable procedure before permitted changes; do not adjust moving parts.

Store and move material so it remains stable and does not block access, exits or protection. Plan the route and destination before lifting. A mechanical aid may reduce lifting but introduce traffic and crushing hazards; use qualified operators and clear interfaces. Do not climb unstable stacks or try to catch falling material. Report a leaning stack and keep others from the threatened area while competent stabilization is arranged.

Construction emergencies require precise location. A street address alone may not identify a deck, excavation or access gate. Learn the alarm, caller role, assembly and responder access before work. Give observed facts and hazards, keep access clear and follow the plan. Do not invent firefighting, suspended-worker rescue or confined-space rescue duties. A tank or vault can be a confined/permit space even while other construction is happening nearby. Entry requires the applicable evaluation/program and trained roles; this course supplies recognition and no-entry decisions only.

**Worked example:** Material delivery blocks the only designated evacuation route while another crew cuts nearby. The worker reports both the route and spark/material interaction and obtains coordinated correction before the affected work. Clearing a narrow personal path does not necessarily restore the site's emergency access.

**Integrated exercise:** A crew finds a damaged grinder guard, bags stacked against an exit, and an open vault where a coworker dropped a tool. Decide in order without entering danger. **Explained answer:** hold grinder use, protect access from unstable/obstructing material through responsible coordination, and prevent unauthorized vault entry while reporting retrieval through the designated process. Do not repair the guard, shift an unstable stack or enter the vault on your own authority. Record each condition and who will verify correction.

**Teach-back:** Explain what would make you restart the grinder task. A complete response requires serviceable suitable equipment and guards, the trained method, appropriate protection and controlled surroundings. “Supervisor said hurry” or “I passed the course” provides none of that evidence.

# VUC011 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Explain corrected reasoning after each error.

## C01-L1

What belongs in a useful opening-hazard report?

**Explained answer:** Observed location/condition/time, people exposed and immediate protection needed, with uncertainty preserved.

**Correction/remediation:** Report facts without entering danger to collect evidence.

## C01-L2

Does a near miss belong in a fabricated injury record?

**Explained answer:** No; accurately report the near miss separately without inventing injury or treatment.

**Correction/remediation:** Different reporting purposes require truthful facts.

## C02-L1

Does a harness alone establish a safe fall-arrest arrangement?

**Explained answer:** No; suitable compatible equipment, anchorage, clearance, inspection, training and rescue arrangements remain necessary.

**Correction/remediation:** A component is not a system.

## C02-L2

Why can a three-foot floor opening still need protection?

**Explained answer:** Stepping/tripping through and other hazards remain; six feet is not a universal exemption.

**Correction/remediation:** Apply the actual opening and task provisions.

## C03-L1

What should happen after a tool repeatedly trips in wet conditions?

**Explained answer:** Stop affected use and obtain proper fault/supply assessment; do not bypass protection.

**Correction/remediation:** The interruption can indicate unresolved exposure.

## C03-L2

Does an old services drawing prove drilling is safe?

**Explained answer:** No; establish actual concealed-service risk and controls before drilling.

**Correction/remediation:** An outdated drawing is not current verification.

## C04-L1

Does seeing the operator establish a safe pedestrian crossing?

**Explained answer:** No; the controlled communication/separation arrangement must be established.

**Correction/remediation:** Do not assume the operator sees or can stop for you.

## C04-L2

Why is a hard hat insufficient beneath a suspended load?

**Explained answer:** It does not control the load path or prevent crushing; remain outside the exclusion area.

**Correction/remediation:** PPE does not authorize hazardous positioning.

## C05-L1

Why is a stopped mixer not automatically safe to unjam?

**Explained answer:** Stored energy and unexpected movement/restart may remain; authorized safe servicing is needed.

**Correction/remediation:** No reach based on switch position alone.

## C05-L2

Name both sides of the trap beside an excavator and a wall.

**Explained answer:** Moving counterweight/equipment and fixed wall, with worker in the closing gap.

**Correction/remediation:** Identify the mechanism, not just the machine name.

## C06-L1

Why does PPE fit matter?

**Explained answer:** Poor fit can expose the worker, impair grip or interfere with other required protection.

**Correction/remediation:** Select suitable compatible protection, not just a nominal size.

## C06-L2

Can a worker select a respirator cartridge by odor alone?

**Explained answer:** No; use the applicable assessed respiratory program and required competence/fit/training.

**Correction/remediation:** Smell does not establish contaminant protection.

## C07-L1

What changes when wet outdoor cutting becomes dry indoor cutting?

**Explained answer:** Exposure/control conditions and environment; hold unsupported work and obtain the appropriate control route.

**Correction/remediation:** Unchanged material does not mean unchanged exposure.

## C07-L2

A heated worker becomes confused and collapses. What is the priority?

**Explained answer:** Activate emergency help promptly, assist cooling as trained and do not leave them alone.

**Correction/remediation:** Do not wait for routine paperwork or send them away alone.

## C08-L1

What is the one-quarter base offset for a twelve-foot supported ladder length?

**Explained answer:** Three feet, with actual instructions and all other safe-setup conditions still required.

**Correction/remediation:** Arithmetic alone does not approve a ladder.

## C08-L2

What happens after equipment strikes an already inspected scaffold?

**Explained answer:** Keep off and obtain competent reassessment for changed integrity.

**Correction/remediation:** The morning tag does not settle the new condition.

## C09-L1

Does a depth less than five feet always remove cave-in protection concerns?

**Explained answer:** No; the shallow exception requires competent examination finding no potential cave-in indication.

**Correction/remediation:** Fresh cracks invalidate a blanket depth argument.

## C09-L2

What is wrong with thirty-foot lateral travel to a ladder in a five-foot trench?

**Explained answer:** It exceeds the twenty-five-foot maximum travel arrangement; safe egress must be corrected before affected entry.

**Correction/remediation:** A ladder merely existing is insufficient.

## C10-L1

Can a worker enter an unassessed vault to retrieve a dropped tool?

**Explained answer:** No; preserve no-entry boundary and use the responsible evaluated process.

**Correction/remediation:** Property retrieval is not an entry authorization.

## C10-L2

What information improves an emergency location report?

**Explained answer:** Exact site area/elevation or excavation, access point, observed emergency and relevant hazards.

**Correction/remediation:** A street address may not locate the person within the site.

# VUC011 — applied learning and observed worker assessments

Use only synthetic drawings, case cards and inert props. Equipment: paper/drawing tool, calculator, inert damaged/serviceable PPE and ladder/cord photographs or the descriptions below. No climbing, energized tools, actual trench entry, chemical handling, live traffic, suspended loads or rescue entry. An instructor competent in construction hazard recognition assesses worker decisions; specialist operation is outside this credential.

First work each case with discussion. Explain the supplied answer. For independent assessment use the fresh variant, with no answer/rubric visible. If already coached on that variant, prepare and retain a new equivalent case. Five criteria each score 0 absent/unsafe, 1 incomplete/prompted, 2 independently correct. Pass 8/10 and every **critical** criterion 2. Stop a hazardous demonstration, record failure and remediate. No score compensates for an unsafe critical decision.

## A — report a changing opening and preserve facts

**Inputs:** fictional site West Deck; 08:10 rail removed for delivery; 08:20 pedestrian route remains open; no injury reported; 08:35 inspection booked but not performed. Another contractor controls the opening. Worker has a safe alternate ground route. Supervisor asks to record “inspected 08:10.”

**Tasks:** make a 60-second report; select immediate worker action; distinguish observed facts, scheduled inspection and unknowns; draft a truthful record; explain escalation if correction is refused. Do not actually send a complaint or alert.

**Worked answer:** keep away/use approved alternate, report exact opening/time/exposure and seek coordinated protection. Do not backdate inspection or invent injury. Keep reports factual and use the appropriate safety/emergency/rights routes as circumstances require.

**Criteria:** precise factual report; protects exposed access **critical**; identifies contractor coordination; preserves true times **critical**; appropriate escalation rather than unsafe evidence gathering.

**Fresh variant:** rail is restored, but a cover is now displaced for a hose and a new crew was not told. Correct response addresses the current hole/communication; it does not keep reporting the already repaired rail.

## B — fall and access decision

**Inputs:** sketch a deck seven feet above a lower slab; duct destination near open edge; no approved anchor; pipe nearby; ladder has sixteen-foot supported working length, damaged rung and unstable base; scaffold has a morning tag but was struck at noon. No climbing is required.

**Tasks:** mark three exposures and safe hold areas; calculate the ladder model offset; explain what that does not approve; choose a protected method through the responsible people; list what would require reassessment.

**Worked answer:** hold exposed duct move; do not assume pipe anchorage. Four-foot model offset does not cure damaged ladder/base. Keep off struck scaffold pending competent reassessment. Obtain suitable protected access/work and relevant system/rescue evidence rather than improvise.

**Criteria:** fall mechanism; no invented anchor/exposed work **critical**; ladder arithmetic and limits; respects scaffold changed condition **critical**; supported alternate/restart evidence.

**Fresh variant:** ladder working length twenty feet gives five-foot offset, ladder is serviceable but overhead-line relationship unknown; scaffold base is intact but a brace was removed. Hold unresolved conditions instead of repeating “damaged rung.”

## C — electricity, struck-by and trapped-space map

**Inputs:** draw a ten-metre-wide yard with a wall at east, excavator swing next to wall, pedestrian crossing through unloading zone, hanging pipe bundle, wet cord with damaged insulation and an unassessed overhead line above the proposed raised-load route. Safe pedestrian route is west of a fixed barrier.

**Tasks:** mark object/contact/crush paths; select worker route; report electrical defect; explain why eye contact and a low load do not suffice; state limits of your role.

**Worked answer:** use west controlled route; keep out of swing/load zone, no squeezing beside wall or passing under bundle. Hold damaged equipment and raised-load uncertainty for competent correction/planning. No touching/repairing live parts or becoming an untrained spotter.

**Criteria:** identifies paths and both crush surfaces; safe route **critical**; no suspended-load entry **critical**; electrical hold/no bypass **critical**; reports and preserves qualified-role boundaries.

**Fresh variant:** overhead issue resolved and cord replaced, but a new cutting operation directs fragments toward west route. Coordinate a verified safe alternative or hold; do not rely on a route that has newly become exposed.

## D — PPE and task-health change

**Inputs:** inert eyewear with loose fit; face shield interfering with hearing protector; unidentified liquid in bottle; mock Seal-R extract from C07; concrete-cutting job moved indoors with failed water feed; worker reports confusion in heat. No real substances or dust.

**Tasks:** inspect/adjust inert PPE or explain why replacement is needed; match information to product; decide cutting disposition; prioritize the heat case; explain respiratory boundary.

**Worked answer:** select compatible fitting protection, no guessed liquid identity or glove compatibility. Hold unsupported dry indoor task and obtain complete assessed method. Severe heat symptoms need immediate emergency assistance and care/cooling as trained. A cabinet mask does not establish respiratory authorization.

**Criteria:** safe fitting/compatibility demonstration; truthful product-information limit; stops unsupported dust exposure **critical**; urgent heat response **critical**; no independent hazardous testing/respirator prescription.

**Fresh variant:** water feed works, but the task/equipment differs from the previously assessed method; PPE fits but is cracked. Reassess actual method and replace damaged protection rather than treating water alone as compliance.

## E — excavation entry boundary

**Inputs:** fictional five-foot trench, worker point thirty feet laterally from ladder, water after overnight rain, spoil at edge, box present but work point outside protected area, utility location unresolved. A supervisor calls it “the same as yesterday.”

**Tasks:** mark entry hold, protection/access/environment uncertainties; calculate excess lateral travel over the twenty-five-foot arrangement; name responsible competent reassessment; state response if someone collapses.

**Worked answer:** no entry into unverified conditions; travel exceeds arrangement by five feet. Box presence is not complete work-point protection. Obtain current competent inspection, utilities/protection/access/water decisions. Call trained emergency response, no untrained entry rescue.

**Criteria:** identifies multiple hazards; no unsupported entry **critical**; correct travel reasoning; competent current reassessment; no improvised rescue **critical**.

**Fresh variant:** depth four-and-a-half feet, travel twenty feet, fresh cracks and damaged protective arrangement. Egress distance is not the failure; shallow depth does not override cave-in indication. Preserve current facts.

## F — tools, material and emergency coordination

**Inputs:** grinder guard missing and accessory unsuitable; stack leaning into exit; unassessed vault with dropped tool; fictional emergency caller knows street but not gate/level. Worker is not a repairer, rigger or rescuer.

**Tasks:** prioritize affected holds, write a coordinated report, rehearse precise emergency call without sending it, explain no-entry/retrieval boundary, describe actual restart evidence.

**Worked answer:** do not run grinder; keep people clear of stack and arrange supported stabilization/alternate route; prevent unauthorized vault entry. Provide exact area, safe gate/access and observed hazard in call. Restart only on actual corrected conditions and appropriate authority; do not invent specialist work to finish the exercise.

**Criteria:** tool hold **critical**; no personal stabilization/unsafe stack approach **critical**; vault no-entry **critical**; usable emergency-location communication; evidence-based restart/coordination.

**Fresh variant:** grinder repaired but delivery blocks exit again, and a new worker offers to enter vault wearing a harness. Keep current access and entry limits; equipment possession does not create rescue/entry capability.
