# VNDRLY/U Fire Warden — Competence Assessment and Refresher

VUC090. Original available-source materials, 18November 2026. For employer-nominated wardens with prior local instruction and practice; this package includes refresher teaching and controlled assessment. It does not authorize fire attack, rescue entry or technical shutdown. Full OPITO specification was not available; no external approval or exact parity is claimed.

## Q90-1 — Appointment, boundaries and the emergency plan

A fire warden helps implement a defined emergency plan. The role does not automatically include firefighting, rescue entry, incident command or technical shutdown. Learn the employer's written appointment, area, deputy arrangement, alarm meanings, reporting route and authority. If the warden is absent, the plan must still work; identify trained cover rather than assuming someone will improvise. Assessment must test the actual role in a safe simulation, not reward confidence or job seniority.

Read the current local plan before the exercise. Locate the assigned zone, primary and alternate exits, assembly points, communications and arrangements for visitors and people needing assistance. Establish who calls emergency services and how that call is confirmed. An automatic alarm signal is not proof that responders have been contacted. Know who may authorize re-entry; a warden's headcount does not normally provide that authority. A change in layout, occupancy or process can invalidate an old briefing.

Worked example: warden Rowan covers office Zone A but a supervisor asks Rowan to search the process building after its alarm. Rowan reports the request to incident control and remains within the assigned safe evacuation role. The desire to help does not create rescue capability. Changed application: Rowan's deputy is absent on night shift. Report the coverage gap before work and implement the employer's approved alternative; do not forge a deputy signature or assume daytime arrangements cover nights.

## Q90-2 — Prevention observations and readiness

A warden's routine observations can find conditions that weaken evacuation: obstructed routes, unsuitable storage, wedged fire doors, damaged signs, unreported alarm faults or changed occupancy. Report and follow up within the site's process. Do not perform unauthorized electrical repairs, alter detectors or disable alarms to avoid nuisance activations. The responsible person or competent specialist decides technical repairs and compensatory arrangements.

Check the assigned area against the local checklist while it is safe and occupied normally. Confirm exits can be used as intended and that the route leads to the designated safe place. A door that looks like an exit but opens into a locked yard is not a complete route. Record the exact location and condition, immediate safe action, notified person and completion evidence. If an impairment affects safe occupancy, escalate promptly; a report filed for next month may not be an adequate response.

Worked example: boxes obstruct the alternate corridor while the primary corridor is open. The learner arranges authorized removal and reports the defect rather than treating the alternate as optional. Changed application: the obstruction is machinery that cannot simply be moved. The learner does not create a new unassessed escape route; management must implement an approved arrangement or restrict affected occupancy. During the emergency the learner uses the current safe route and reports unavailable areas, not yesterday's checklist.

## Q90-3 — Recognition, alarm and initial communication

On discovering suspected fire, raise the alarm and follow the emergency plan promptly. Do not delay warning others while trying to prove the fire's size or finish a telephone conversation. Move toward safety, communicate the location and observable facts, and distinguish confirmed information from uncertainty. Avoid unsupported statements such as no one is inside when the area has not been checked. Use the site's alarm point, radio, telephone or backup method as trained.

A concise initial message identifies the caller and location, the observed condition, people at immediate risk if known, the action taken and the assistance needed. Confirm that the recipient understood the critical information. If communication fails, use the planned alternative while maintaining evacuation; do not remain in a deteriorating area repeatedly trying the same radio. Do not make a real emergency call during a drill unless the exercise plan has specifically arranged it. A real emergency overrides the drill and triggers the real response.

Worked example: the card states smoke seen through a store-room window, no person seen, alarm activated. A sound message is that smoke is observed at that location, occupancy is unconfirmed and evacuation is underway. It is not store-room clear. Changed application: the radio battery fails. Use the designated backup contact point from a safe location and report the communication problem. The assessment judges safe information transfer, not a theatrical delay to create a more dramatic scenario.

## Q90-4 — Evacuation direction and changing routes

Give short, calm directions that people can act on: the route to use, the route to avoid and the assembly point. Position yourself where you can guide without blocking flow or becoming trapped. Encourage people to leave belongings and proceed by the designated route. Do not argue at length with a reluctant person while others need direction; use the plan, report the refusal and protect your own exit. Never physically force or restrain a person as a routine warden technique.

An indicated route may become unsafe. Do not direct people into smoke, heat or another hazard merely because an arrow or old plan points that way. Use a safe planned alternative and communicate the change. Do not enter a smoke-filled corridor to discover whether the far end is clear. Elevators are used only under the building's specifically approved evacuation arrangements, not because an ordinary passenger lift is convenient. Keep access clear for responders and do not route evacuees through an active response area.

Worked example: route E1 is marked unavailable by a smoke simulation card before the group approaches it. The learner stops that movement, directs people along approved E2 and informs control that E1 is unavailable. Changed application: E2 also becomes unavailable in a tabletop inject. The learner follows the site's contingency and calls for assistance from the safest available position; inventing a window escape or sending people through the simulated smoke is not an acceptable answer. The training site never physically blocks real escape routes.

## Q90-5 — Safe checking, sweep limits and withdrawal

Some plans assign wardens a limited check of accessible areas while leaving. That is not a search-and-rescue mission. Learn exactly which rooms may be checked, how the result is communicated and when to withdraw. The warden must not enter smoke, heat, hazardous process areas or any condition outside the assigned capability. Preserve an escape route and do not re-enter to complete a checklist. If an area cannot be checked safely, report it as unchecked with the reason and last known information.

Use the local method for checking safe spaces and directing occupants out. Close doors where safe and instructed without locking people in or delaying escape. Do not open a suspect door merely to improve certainty. A closed door is not proof that its room is empty. A sweep report describes what was actually observed, its time and limitations. It does not certify the building safe for re-entry or replace responder search decisions.

Worked example: the plan permits a visual check of the open office and accessible meeting room on the exit path. A store-room approach has a smoke card. Report office and meeting room checked at the stated time, store-room unchecked due to smoke, then leave. Changed application: a colleague says a visitor may be in that store-room. Immediately communicate the possible person and last known location to incident control/responders. Do not turn the possible missing person into permission for unprotected entry.

## Q90-6 — Inclusive assistance, visitors and dignity

Plan assistance with the people concerned before an emergency. Needs may involve mobility, hearing, vision, language, temporary injury, unfamiliarity or anxiety. Do not infer a person's needs or ability from appearance. Know the individual or general evacuation arrangements, trained helpers, backup helpers and the means of communication. Share only information needed for safe assistance; medical details are not broadcast to the whole assembly group.

If a plan uses a refuge, it is a managed temporary part of the evacuation arrangement with communication and onward evacuation, not a place to leave someone indefinitely. The plan should not depend on the emergency services as the sole means of routine assisted evacuation. Only trained authorized personnel use specialized evacuation devices, and only under their approved procedure. This course does not teach improvised carrying, stair-chair operation or manual rescue. Simulate those interfaces without lifting people during assessment.

Worked example: visitor Mira uses the agreed level route E2 with a nominated helper. The helper reports Mira's arrival by identity, not merely one extra person. Changed application: the usual helper is absent. Activate the planned trained backup and inform control of any unresolved assistance need. Do not assign an untrained bystander to carry Mira down stairs. If the planned route is compromised, communicate the exact location and need through the contingency process while maintaining the safest available arrangement.

## Q90-7 — Assembly, identity and accountability

At assembly, keep people away from traffic, hazards and responder access. Use the current accountable population, not a rough crowd estimate. Include employees, contractors, visitors and others covered by the plan. Reconcile identities against reliable attendance or sign-in information, while recognizing that records may be stale. Distinguish confirmed safe, expected but unconfirmed, confirmed offsite and unknown identity. Do not remove someone from the expected list merely to make totals balance.

Duplicates and additional arrivals can make a headcount falsely reassuring. Report unresolved people promptly with name or identifier, last known location and information source. Do not wait until the entire list is perfectly formatted. Update control when new evidence changes the picture, preserving times and earlier uncertainty. No one returns inside to look for a colleague or collect a missing register. Use the plan's backup record method if the electronic system is unavailable.

Worked example: expected IDs A1–A8, C1–C3, V1–V2 total 13. The initial arrival list contains A1–A8, C1, C2, V1, V1 and unknown X. There are 13 entries but only 11 confirmed expected identities. C3 and V2 remain unconfirmed; V1 is duplicated and X needs identification. Changed application: X is verified as V2, so 12 expected identities are confirmed and C3 remains unresolved. A later verified offsite record for C3 changes the result to 12 safe at assembly plus 1 confirmed offsite, not 13 physically at assembly.

## Q90-8 — Responder handover and information discipline

A useful handover states the site and incident location, observed hazard, alarm/action times, evacuation status, routes and access, unconfirmed people with last known locations, assistance needs, unchecked areas and known relevant hazards. State the source and uncertainty. Give responders the approved map or information pack where available without delaying their work. Do not guess cylinder contents, electrical isolation status or process conditions from appearance.

Maintain a clear command interface. If multiple wardens report, identify your zone and avoid claiming whole-site clearance. Confirm urgent information reached the responsible person. Keep a time-stamped log of updates so a later all-accounted report does not erase an earlier missing-person concern. Provide corrections explicitly. Warden reports support incident command; they do not authorize firefighting tactics or operational restart.

Worked example: Zone A has one unchecked store-room and C3 unconfirmed, last seen near the loading office by a named witness at 10:04. The learner hands over those facts and labels the witness report as unverified. Changed application: C3 is confirmed offsite through the approved process at 10:12. Send a correction with the new evidence and time; do not silently edit the paper list and assume responders will notice. The store-room remains unchecked even when everyone is accounted for.

## Q90-9 — Firefighting boundaries, re-entry and recovery

The presence of an extinguisher does not require a warden to fight a fire. The employer's emergency policy determines whether occupants evacuate or designated trained people may attempt limited extinguisher use. This competence package assesses evacuation and coordination, not live firefighting. Never delay alarm or escape to protect property. Smoke, uncertain material, loss of exit, spreading fire or lack of training are reasons to withdraw and report rather than attempt control.

Remain at the designated safe location and prevent casual re-entry through clear instructions and the site's access-control process. Alarm silence does not mean the emergency is over. Neither an empty roll-call list nor a manager's wish to recover a laptop establishes re-entry authority. Follow the release from the person named in the actual emergency plan. After release, any equipment restart or process restoration follows its own authorized procedure.

Worked example: the alarm stops while responders are still assessing the building. The warden keeps the group at assembly and seeks the official update. Changed application: an authorized all-clear is issued for office Zone A but the workshop remains restricted. Communicate the partial release accurately; do not say the whole site is open. Record unresolved defects and welfare needs and transfer ongoing responsibilities to the appropriate owner.

## Q90-10 — Demonstrating competence and learning from drills

Competence is shown by safe independent decisions in the defined role, not simply attendance at a talk. This package requires two changed simulated emergencies covering alarm, route change, limited checking, assistance, identity-based accountability and handover. The candidate receives the local plan and safe practice before assessment. The assessor records actions and exact information communicated. A correct explanation after an unsafe choice does not erase that choice.

Debrief the exercise using evidence: what happened, why it mattered, what the plan or training needs to change, who owns the action and how closure will be verified. Distinguish an individual skill gap from a defective organizational arrangement. A candidate should receive credit for identifying a missing assistance plan rather than be expected to invent one. However, claiming that an unchecked area was clear is an individual evidence failure that requires remediation and reassessment.

Worked example: the candidate guides everyone safely but reports 13 accounted from a 13-entry duplicate list. The assessor retains the accountability failure, teaches identity reconciliation and uses a changed roster. A repeat of the same memorized list is weak evidence of the corrected skill. Changed application: the candidate correctly reports an unresolved person and refuses unsafe re-entry despite pressure. That is competent boundary management, not failure to rescue. The award records the assessed site-plan/simulated scope and does not appoint the person or grant an external qualification.

# VUC090 practical assessment and instructor guide

## Entry and resources

Candidate is nominated for the role by the employer and has completed local plan instruction and safe practice. Review relevant participation needs confidentially; no stress exposure or physical demand beyond the role is required. Assessor is competent in emergency planning, local warden duties and assessment and is independent enough to record an honest failure. Provide accessible plan/maps, interpreting or communication support as needed without removing the need to communicate critical information effectively.

Resources: safe training area with two genuinely usable exits, clearly marked exercise paths, printed plan and rosters, radio/telephone simulation, timing/log materials, roleplayers, assistance-plan cards and mock incident controller. No live fire, smoke, blocked real exits, alarm impairment, rescue lifting or real emergency call is introduced. Exercise controller briefs all participants, coordinates with facility management and maintains an immediate stop signal. Actual emergency overrides exercise. One candidate assessed as warden at a time; roleplayers may be reused but each candidate receives changed evidence and an individual record.

## I90 two complete changed emergency simulations

ScenarioA: brief E90 and rosterA; at exercise 10:00 give smoke-atS card. Candidate must initiate alarm/message and evacuation. When route direction begins, giveE1 unavailable card. Before assistance decision, giveJoabsent card. When candidate reports assembly, issue initial rosterA, thenA2 andA3 only after candidate has reported current unresolved people. Controller asks whetherSwas clear; correct answer remains unchecked. Then give alarm-silenced card followed later by authorized office-only release. Candidate must not infer full all-clear. Controller records response order and exact statements; these times are scenario markers, not universal evacuation deadlines.

ScenarioB: changed rosterB and morning setup. Candidate starts with safeE1 but then receives E1 unavailable card and backup communication failure requiring planned safe message route. Safe O/Mcheck is allowed; Sremains unchecked. Assistance helper present but visitor initially misunderstood assembly direction; candidate confirms understanding respectfully. IssueB2–B5 sequentially after current status reports. Add manager pressure to retrieve belongings before authorized release. Candidate delivers explicit correction and ends with accurate restricted-scope report.

Each scenario scores ten dimensions correspondingP90-1 to 10, 0 = absent/unsafe, 1 = major coaching, 2 = safe but incomplete or prompted, 3 = independent acceptable, 4 = accurate independent with reasoning. Each requires≥32/40, every dimension≥3, allmandatoryactions and no critical error. Both scenarios must pass independently. Critical errors: unsafe entry/re-entry, delaying alarm for investigation, abandoning known assistance need, fabricated all-clear/unchecked-area clearance, knowingly hiding unresolved person, unapproved real emergency call or dangerous physical improvisation. Stop danger immediately. Do not average scenarios or use written marks to offset a practical failure.

# VUC090 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q90-1-L1

Does warden appointment authorize smoke rescue?

**Answer:** No; follow assigned evacuation role and report need to responders.

**Reason/remediation:** A title does not create protective equipment, training or rescue authority. Revisit Q90-1 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-1-L2

Deputy absent at night: assume day cover?

**Answer:** No; escalate and implement approved cover.

**Reason/remediation:** The plan must address the actual staffed shift. Revisit Q90-1 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-2-L1

Alternate exit blocked but primary open: ignore?

**Answer:** No; report and resolve through authorized arrangements.

**Reason/remediation:** A later incident may make the primary unavailable. Revisit Q90-2 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-2-L2

Can a warden disable a faulty detector?

**Answer:** Not without authorized procedure and competence; report impairment.

**Reason/remediation:** Disabling protection can create an unrecognized risk. Revisit Q90-2 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-3-L1

Smoke observed, occupancy unknown: report room clear?

**Answer:** No; state smoke and unconfirmed occupancy.

**Reason/remediation:** Uncertainty must remain visible to responders. Revisit Q90-3 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-3-L2

Automatic alarm proves emergency services called?

**Answer:** No; follow and confirm the plan call procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** Alarm activation and successful external notification are different events. Revisit Q90-3 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-4-L1

E1 smoke card, approved E2 safe: direction?

**Answer:** Use E2 and report E1 unavailable.

**Reason/remediation:** A known unsafe route is not used because it is normally primary. Revisit Q90-4 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-4-L2

Both routes unavailable: invent window escape?

**Answer:** No; follow local contingency and communicate exact situation.

**Reason/remediation:** Unplanned escape methods can create additional harm. Revisit Q90-4 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-5-L1

Room cannot be safely checked: mark clear?

**Answer:** No; report unchecked, reason, time and last information.

**Reason/remediation:** A completed form must not conceal uncertainty. Revisit Q90-5 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-5-L2

Possible missing visitor inside: re-enter?

**Answer:** No; promptly report to incident control/responders.

**Reason/remediation:** A missing-person concern does not authorize unprotected rescue. Revisit Q90-5 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-6-L1

Usual trained helper absent: use untrained carrying?

**Answer:** No; activate approved trained backup/contingency.

**Reason/remediation:** Improvised carrying can injure and does not replace an assistance plan. Revisit Q90-6 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-6-L2

Refuge means leave person indefinitely?

**Answer:** No; maintain planned communication and onward evacuation.

**Reason/remediation:** It is part of an evacuation arrangement, not abandonment. Revisit Q90-6 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-7-L1

13 entries include duplicateV1 and unknownX; 13 expected. All safe?

**Answer:** No; reconcile identities, not raw count.

**Reason/remediation:** Duplicates/extras can hide missing expected people. Revisit Q90-7 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-7-L2

Confirmed offsite counts as physically at assembly?

**Answer:** No; record the distinct status and evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Accurate categories preserve accountability. Revisit Q90-7 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-8-L1

Witness last sawC3 near office; confirmed location?

**Answer:** Report as witness information with time, not confirmed current location.

**Reason/remediation:** Source and uncertainty affect response decisions. Revisit Q90-8 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-8-L2

New safe-location evidence: erase earlier report?

**Answer:** No; send time-stamped correction and preserve the history.

**Reason/remediation:** Responders need an explicit update. Revisit Q90-8 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-9-L1

Alarm silenced: allow re-entry?

**Answer:** No; await named authority release.

**Reason/remediation:** Silence does not prove conditions safe. Revisit Q90-9 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-9-L2

Extinguisher present: must warden fight fire?

**Answer:** No; follow employer policy and own training; evacuation role remains.

**Reason/remediation:** Equipment availability does not create authorization. Revisit Q90-9 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-10-L1

Candidate wrongly reports all accounted then explains error: erase failure?

**Answer:** No; retain, remediate and assess a changed roster independently.

**Reason/remediation:** Later explanation is not proof of correct emergency performance. Revisit Q90-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q90-10-L2

Refusal to enter smoke for missing person: automatic fail?

**Answer:** No; safe withdrawal and prompt accurate handover are correct.

**Reason/remediation:** Assessment rewards the defined safe role, not unauthorized heroics. Revisit Q90-10 and perform its changed application.
