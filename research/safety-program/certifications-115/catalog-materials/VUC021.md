# Program definition and delivery

VUC021 — VNDRLY/U Respirator Fit Test. Audience: required respirator wearers and employees designated to administer fit tests. Purpose: obtain reliable, traceable wearer-specific results through appropriately trained and observed operators. The full administrator pathway covers T01–T12, all learning checks, the controlled final and stations A–H. Wearers receive the applicable instruction and actual test; they are not required to become test administrators. Neither pathway replaces the employer's respiratory protection program.

Administrator prerequisites: ability to follow the selected protocol, understand instructions and numerical/time records, and access to the program administrator, accepted protocol and actual equipment manuals. Evaluators must be demonstrably proficient in the methods assessed and able to identify equipment/protocol errors. For anyone wearing respiratory protection during a station, verify the medical prerequisite and other test conditions first. Use actor/document simulations for contraindications; do not induce symptoms or expose people deliberately to hazardous substances.

Objectives: distinguish fit/medical/selection/seal checks; protect medical confidentiality; select a permissible method; inspect/select/don a compatible facepiece; conduct seal checks/exercises; administer agent sensitivity screening and complete saccharin/Bitrex tests; prepare/run standard CNC; interpret valid results; preserve records and act on retest triggers; organize a hygienic and truthful testing service.

Suggested administrator delivery is twelve instructional hours, excluding breaks, meals and individual medical appointments. Day one: T01–T05 discussion/demonstration 120 minutes; T06–T08 and demonstrated qualitative procedures 120; coached practice A–D 120. Day two: T09–T12 instruction/demonstration 90; CNC practice and record workshop 90; independent practical assessments/changed scenarios 120; final and feedback 60. Total 720 minutes. This is a VNDRLY delivery plan, not an OSHA minimum course duration. Add supervised time until each person has completed the actual required demonstrations; a large class cannot share one person's assessment. Use stations with sufficient evaluators and instruments, ordinarily no more than four learners per supervised practice station. Do not shorten an accepted test to fit the class schedule.

Teaching sequence for each chapter: explain the procedure and its reason; demonstrate once correctly; let learners explain the worked decision; attempt its two checks independently; discuss mistakes using the rationale; practice without risk; then evaluate independently. Provide accessible reading, language assistance and accommodations that preserve the actual protocol. A translated stop/taste signal must be understood by both parties before testing.

Final: 36 constructed responses, three per domain, 72 possible points. Two points for correct action plus reason, one for safe but incomplete reasoning, zero for wrong/absent/unsafe. Pass at least 58/72 and at least 4/6 in every domain, with no critical unsafe endorsement such as testing without the required medical determination, deliberately exposing a contraindicated person, fabricating a pass, ignoring a failed validity check, or transferring a result to an untested facepiece. Time 60 minutes with documented accessibility adjustments. Answer keys are held separately by the examiner.

Practical scoring: each of five criteria per station scores 2 independently correct, 1 safe but prompted/incomplete, 0 incorrect/omitted. Pass each station at least 8/10; criteria marked **critical** must score 2. Stop unsafe activity immediately. A critical failure cannot be offset by a high written score. After explanation and additional practice, repeat the failed station with a changed case and independently repeat the affected complete protocol. Keep original attempts. The final reassessment uses protected changed-domain tasks and the prescribed scoring; no same-session answer recitation counts as independent learning.

Credential issue: administrator training/proficiency only after all learning, final and applicable complete practical demonstrations. The full scope requires both qualitative agents and standard CNC; an assessed subset must be named explicitly and is not completion of the full administrator pathway. No statement of OSHA/manufacturer certification. Employer designation and actual device authorization remain separate. Annual respiratory-use training and annual wearer fit testing are separate from a tester course certificate. VNDRLY recommends annual observed tester review and immediate reassessment after protocol/equipment changes, invalid administration or evidence of lost skill; that is a program quality rule, not an invented regulatory expiration date.

A wearer result is issued only after an actual valid test and is restricted to the tested facepiece and method. No final-exam score earns a wearer pass. This authored package contains no actual wearer/tester qualification.

# Practical evaluation packet

Common equipment: current Appendix A/B-1, program procedure and facepiece instructions; documented medically eligible volunteers; multiple suitable sizes/models; mirror; appropriate safety equipment; timer; accepted qualitative hood and separate labeled screening/test nebulizers for each agent; correct solutions/SDS; cleaning supplies; compatible CNC instrument, software, filters, tubes, probes/adapters and current manual; blank records. Use synthetic personnel and document cases for privacy. Actual testing requires suitable participants and operationally sound equipment; never simulate a real pass by typing a result.

## A — Intake and method decision

Give three synthetic files: cleared half-mask wearer; full-face wearer requiring the full-face quantitative criterion; and wearer with no medical recommendation. Learner checks identity and permitted recommendation, identifies respirator and suitable method, and communicates a safe hold for the third. Assessor asks where the questionnaire is stored. Score: identity/configuration; **critical medical prerequisite**; **critical permissible method**; privacy; explanation/disposition. Expected: appropriate QLFT or QNFT for the first, QNFT meeting 500 for the second, no actual test for the third. Reassessment changes the third to new respiratory symptoms despite an older recommendation.

## B — Selection, independent donning and seal check

Provide approved candidate facepieces, instructions, mirror and helmet/eyewear used with them. Learner inspects, allows wearer selection and independent donning, checks five-minute comfort/fit and applicable interference, and observes the specified seal check. Assessor supplies an obviously damaged spare for rejection without use. Score: inspection; **critical no hair/interference compromise**; normal strap tension/comfort; **critical correct independent seal check**; equipment identity and explanation. Reassessment uses a different approved size/model with its actual instructions, not assumed interchangeability.

## C — Saccharin complete administration

Learner prepares and identifies correct screening/test solutions and nebulizers, explains signals, verifies intake restrictions, conducts the actual threshold screen, prepares the wearer and conducts all seven exercises with correct initial/replenishment doses and timing. Assessor independently logs squeezes and exercise times; do not contaminate the agent or alter the equipment mid-test. If screening is unsuccessful, correct safe disposition is recorded and another eligible participant is used to observe a complete administration. Score: **critical valid screening**; **critical solution/dose/timing**; **critical complete valid exercise sequence/no adjustment**; monitoring/maintenance; truthful record and comfort. Reassessment uses a different participant or facepiece after appropriate preparation; the result may legitimately fail even when the operator performs correctly.

## D — Bitrex complete administration

Use separately identified Bitrex supplies and an eligible participant. Learner explains bitterness, screens correctly and executes the full accepted method with its own threshold and dose log. The evaluator presents a document-only scenario of a clogged nebulizer after the actual test and requires invalidation of that hypothetical run. Score: **critical agent/threshold**; **critical dosing and replenishment**; **critical full protocol**; hygiene/equipment checks; failed/invalid disposition. Reassessment changes the sensitivity count and asks learner to derive doses before a new actual complete demonstration. Do not force a taste outcome.

## E — CNC readiness and invalid-equipment recognition

Using the actual instrument instructions, learner confirms maintenance status, sets the correct facepiece/filter mode and threshold, identifies both sampling paths, installs the approved adapter and completes instrument operational checks. An unconnected spare kinked tube is an inspection defect; never falsify a machine's operational check. Score: identity/manual; **critical correct mode/threshold**; **critical intact correct sampling path**; **critical passed required checks before proceeding**; documented readiness. Reassessment changes to a different compatible facepiece/filter class and requires mode revalidation.

## F — Standard CNC complete administration

After eligibility and preparation, learner runs a complete standard CNC test, observes every exercise, monitors warnings, asks about comfort and preserves the full recording. Score: **critical preparation and exact facepiece**; **critical complete accepted protocol/no adjustments**; **critical valid result interpretation**; restoration/disposal of test-only modifications; complete traceable record. If the wearer fails, correct administration can still earn operator proficiency while the wearer receives no pass. To evaluate recognition, supply separate synthetic records: half mask 99, full face 499, full face 500. Expected: fail, fail, numerical pass subject to all other validity conditions. Reassessment uses a new eligible wearer and different safe configuration within assessed scope.

## G — Records and retest decisions

Supply synthetic results: an incomplete run, a valid qualitative pass, a quantitative full-face result 520 with recording, and an old pass followed by dental change. Learner builds separate records and dispositions. Score: **critical no invented pass**; all required identity/method/configuration/date/result fields; quantitative recording linkage; **critical retest trigger**; privacy/retention. Reassessment changes the physical-change trigger to a different make of facepiece and an employee's unacceptable-fit complaint. Both need action; calendar validity alone is insufficient.

## H — Service capstone and wearer instruction

Learner organizes the workstation and runs the T12 three-worker document scenario, then teaches an eligible wearer safe donning, seal check, reporting and the boundaries of the result. Assessor checks separation of learning credentials from actual test records and asks what happens if the company changes respirator stock tomorrow. Score: clean/used separation; correct intake dispositions; **critical honest bounded credentials**; usable wearer instruction; **critical no transfer to untested stock**. Reassessment adds a supervisor requesting backdating and a solution bottle with an unreadable label. Expected: refuse falsification and quarantine the unidentified solution.

# VNDRLY/U Respirator Fit Test — learner and tester coursebook

Version 1.0, 17 November 2026. Original instruction for United States workplaces using OSHA 1910.134. A completed course is not a completed fit test. The wearer must actually pass the appropriate test on the exact facepiece used. This program also supplies a separately assessed tester pathway for saccharin, Bitrex and standard ambient-aerosol CNC methods. It does not confer medical, industrial-hygiene, NIOSH or manufacturer approval.

## T01 — What a fit test establishes

A tight-fitting respirator protects only when both its filtering or supplied-air system and its face seal are suitable. A fit test challenges the seal while a particular person moves, talks and breathes in a controlled procedure. A user seal check is the wearer's brief check every time the respirator is put on. Medical evaluation asks whether that person can safely use respiratory protection. Hazard selection asks whether the respirator is appropriate for the contaminant, concentration, oxygen level and work. None replaces another.

The employer's respiratory program administrator identifies the required respirator and permissible test method. The tester confirms that instruction against the facepiece actually presented. Record make, model, style and size, not merely “N95” or “medium.” Two products with the same filter class can seal differently. A passed test on one cannot be transferred to the other by visual resemblance. A fit factor is a test result, not an assigned protection factor or a measurement of the wearer's actual exposure on tomorrow's shift.

**Worked decision.** A worker has a passing test for an elastomeric half mask but is issued another manufacturer's disposable N95. Do not copy the pass. Select and train on the issued facepiece, obtain the required medical determination, and conduct an appropriate new test before required use. A classroom completion record may remain valid while the new facepiece result is pending.

**Common mistake:** issuing a universal “respirator certified” card. The useful record names the person, tested configuration, date, method and result, with limitations. An untested employee needs a safe alternative assignment or appropriately selected protection under the employer's program, not permission to enter an exposure while waiting.

## T02 — Medical readiness, consent and confidential information

Before fit testing or required respirator use, obtain confirmation of the PLHCP's written recommendation and applicable limitations. The tester does not interpret a medical questionnaire, diagnose a condition, or demand the worker disclose diagnoses to a supervisor. The employer supplies the clinician with respirator type/weight, duration/frequency, exertion, protective clothing, environmental extremes, the program and the standard. The clinician gives the employer the permitted recommendation, restrictions and follow-up needs; detailed medical information stays confidential.

Explain the agent, test sequence, reporting of taste or discomfort, and stop signal in language the wearer understands. Ask about test-agent concerns through the approved screening process; use the agent's safety information and clinical advice when necessary. Do not expose someone to a known problematic agent simply to complete a schedule. Offer an appropriate alternative accepted method through the program administrator. No workplace toxic gas is ever a fit-test challenge agent.

If the person develops breathing difficulty, dizziness, chest discomfort, distress or an inability to continue, stop the procedure, remove the challenge exposure safely and obtain appropriate assistance. Refer respiratory-use concerns to the PLHCP. An incomplete test is not a fail caused by poor attitude and is never a pass. Medical restrictions can change; a prior clearance is not permission to disregard new symptoms.

**Worked decision.** A trainee arrives without a medical recommendation and offers to sign a waiver. Teach the theory and demonstrate equipment on an appropriate training setup, but do not fit-test that person. Record the pending prerequisite and arrange the proper evaluation. Do not retain their private questionnaire in the course folder.

## T03 — Select the method before selecting the pass button

Qualitative fit testing (QLFT) depends on detecting a challenge agent and produces a pass/fail result. For negative-pressure air-purifying respirators it is limited to respirators required to achieve a fit factor of 100 or less. Do not use a qualitative pass to support full-facepiece protection requiring a quantitative fit factor of 500. Quantitative fit testing (QNFT) measures leakage using an accepted method. For the standard CNC procedure, minimum passing overall fit factors are 100 for a half facepiece, including a filtering facepiece, and 500 for a full-face elastomeric facepiece.

Tight-fitting supplied-air and powered air-purifying facepieces still need fit testing in negative-pressure mode using an appropriate accepted configuration. For qualitative testing this can be the actual facepiece temporarily fitted with suitable filters or an identical surrogate with the same sealing surfaces. For quantitative testing use the specified sampling arrangement in the breathing zone. Restore temporary modifications before workplace use. A loose-fitting hood has no tight facial seal to test, but still needs correct selection, medical/program requirements as applicable and training; it is not a universal substitute selected by the tester alone.

Saccharin and Bitrex require successful sensitivity screening. A person unable to taste the screening agent cannot pass by reporting no taste during the fit test. Standard CNC requires equipment, adapters and filters that prevent significant challenge-particle penetration. An N95 cannot simply be attached to any particle counter in any mode. Confirm the instrument's capability and the approved configuration before use.

**Worked decision.** A full-face mask gives an overall quantitative result of 230. This is not a full-face pass because it exceeds 100. Apply the 500 criterion; investigate fit and retest appropriately. Do not lower the programmed threshold to turn the display green.

## T04 — Facepiece choice, inspection and independent donning

Provide a range of suitable models and sizes. Demonstrate how the actual product is positioned, how straps are adjusted and how its seal check is performed. Use its instructions, not a memorized method for a different mask. Inspect sealing surfaces, straps, valves, connections, filters and cleanliness. Reject damaged or incomplete equipment. A disposable facepiece needs intact straps and nose components and must not be crushed or modified for work.

The wearer selects a comfortable option and dons it independently. Check chin placement, fit over the nose bridge, nose-to-chin coverage, tendency to slip, room for needed eye protection and the ability to speak. A mirror helps the wearer recognize incorrect positioning. Straps should provide normal usable tension, not an artificially tight test-only seal. Wear the respirator at least five minutes before evaluating comfort and testing; for CNC this also permits trapped ambient particles to clear.

Facial hair between the sealing surface and skin, or hair interfering with valve function, prevents acceptable tight-fitting use. Do not tape over a beard or force the seal through it. Glasses, hard hats and other equipment that could affect the fit must be worn as applicable during the test. Eyeglass temple pieces must not cross a full-face seal. The employer must arrange a compatible solution rather than removing needed protection only for the test.

**Worked decision.** A helmet pushes a strap downward during head movement. Stop preparation and resolve compatibility before testing. A pass without the helmet would not represent the working configuration. If the wearer later finds the chosen respirator unacceptable, provide a reasonable opportunity to select another and retest.

## T05 — User seal checks and exercise coaching

Perform a user seal check after each donning. For a suitable elastomeric design, a positive-pressure check closes the exhalation valve and uses gentle exhalation to develop slight pressure without outward leakage. A negative-pressure check closes the inlets using the permitted technique, uses gentle inhalation to draw the facepiece inward slightly, and holds the breath for ten seconds; continued slight collapse without inward leakage indicates that check is satisfactory. Replace any removed cover correctly. Many filtering facepieces require a different manufacturer method; use an equally effective specified method rather than crushing the facepiece or blocking it in a way that disturbs the seal.

Resolve leakage before starting. The seal check is not an annual fit test and cannot establish the numerical fit factor. Explain the exercises and the stop signal in advance. During a standard test the wearer must not adjust straps or the facepiece; adjustment voids the test and requires repeating it. Coach the prescribed movement without telling the wearer to minimize movement to obtain a pass.

The standard sequence is normal breathing, deep breathing, head side to side, head up and down, talking, then for QNFT only a grimace, bending over, and normal breathing again. Each exercise lasts one minute except the QNFT grimace, which lasts fifteen seconds and is not scored. Thus QLFT has seven one-minute exercises; standard QNFT includes the additional fifteen seconds. Modified approved protocols have different timings and cannot be assembled from selected pieces of this sequence.

For head turns, pause and inhale at the extremes; when looking up, inhale in the upward position. Deep breathing must not cause hyperventilation. Talk aloud clearly, for example counting backward from 100. Bend at the waist as though reaching toward the toes; jogging in place substitutes where the test enclosure prevents bending. Ask about comfort at the end and change the respirator if unacceptable.

**Worked decision.** The mask slips during talking and the wearer tightens a strap. Stop and mark that run invalid. After correction and preparation, repeat the full accepted protocol; do not save the earlier successful exercises and append only talking.

## T06 — Saccharin sensitivity screening

Use the accepted hood/enclosure, approximately twelve inches in diameter and fourteen inches tall, with a clear front, free head movement and the specified three-quarter-inch nozzle opening. Use a DeVilbiss Model 40 or equivalent nebulizer. Keep separate, clearly identified nebulizers for sensitivity and fit-test solutions. Use labeled commercial solutions meeting the protocol or appropriately controlled preparation; do not substitute table sugar. The screening formulation is 0.83 gram sodium saccharin USP in 100 mL warm water. The fit formulation is much stronger and must not be mistaken for screening solution.

With no respirator on, the wearer puts on the hood and breathes through a slightly open mouth with tongue extended. Explain that sweet taste is the signal. Direct the aerosol away from the person's nose and mouth. Fully collapse the bulb and let it fully expand for each squeeze. Deliver ten rapid squeezes, asking whether sweetness is detected. A response within that first group is recorded as threshold ten, even if perceived at squeeze four. If absent, give another ten and record twenty if detected. If still absent, give a third ten and record thirty if detected. No detection after thirty makes this method unsuitable for that wearer; do not keep increasing the count until they agree.

Use approximately one mL in the nebulizer body as specified and check that aerosol is produced. Rinse, shake dry and refill at least every four hours and at the morning/afternoon intervals specified by the protocol. Keep the opening clear. Manage preparation/labeling and solution use under the kit instructions and safety information; a bottle of unknown concentration is not acceptable.

**Worked decision.** The wearer detects sweetness at the eighteenth squeeze. Record threshold twenty, not eighteen. The later fit-test initial dose will be twenty and its replenishment dose ten. If the person detects nothing at thirty, choose an appropriate alternative method; “no taste” is not an excellent seal because no respirator has yet been tested.

## T07 — Complete saccharin fit test

For the fifteen minutes before the test, the wearer must not eat, drink except plain water, smoke or chew gum. Verify this and record the sensitivity result. Use a particulate-filter configuration appropriate for the test, complete independent donning, comfort period, seal check and applicable equipment check. The fit solution is 83 grams sodium saccharin per 100 mL warm water, kept distinct from the screening solution. Do not correct a concentration error by changing squeeze counts.

Place the hood without disturbing the respirator. The wearer continues mouth-open breathing with tongue extended and reports any sweetness immediately. Introduce the initial dose corresponding to threshold ten, twenty or thirty. Start the seven-exercise qualitative sequence from T05. Every thirty seconds replenish with half the initial dose: five, ten or fifteen full squeezes. Use a timer and dose record; an observer who becomes distracted can lose the challenge concentration even while the wearer performs perfect movements.

If sweetness occurs at any time, stop and record a failed fit, try a different respirator and repeat the entire procedure including threshold screening. If the nebulizer clogs, the challenge may have disappeared. Check periodically and at the end; clogging found at the end invalidates the test rather than proving protection. A pass requires valid screening, valid maintained challenge, all required exercises, no taste and acceptable comfort. Do not alter the record because the supervisor wants a shift staffed.

**Worked decision.** A threshold-thirty wearer has no taste, but the operator replenished with only five squeezes. The prescribed replenishment was fifteen. The run is not valid; repeat after correcting the process. This is a test administration problem, not evidence that the mask passed or failed.

## T08 — Bitrex procedure and agent-specific controls

Bitrex is denatonium benzoate and is detected as a bitter taste. Explain the complete procedure and use the agent's safety information. Its screening solution is 13.5 mg denatonium benzoate in 100 mL of five-percent sodium-chloride solution in distilled water. Its fit-test solution is 337.5 mg in 200 mL of five-percent sodium-chloride solution in warm water. Prefer correctly labeled supplied solutions; these concentrations are identity checks, not authorization for untrained chemical preparation. Never swap sweet and bitter solutions into an unlabeled common bottle.

Use the same accepted hood dimensions, separate identified nebulizers, full bulb collapse/expansion and ten/twenty/thirty screening progression as T06, but ask specifically about bitterness. Conduct screening without a respirator. If no bitterness is detected after thirty squeezes, this method cannot be used for that person. Record the threshold and then prepare a suitable particulate respirator, five-minute comfort period, seal check and applicable safety equipment.

Apply the fifteen-minute no-food/drink-except-water/smoking/gum rule. With the hood in place, start with the threshold number of fit-solution squeezes; replenish every thirty seconds with half that number. Perform the qualitative sequence, without a grimace. Any bitter taste fails the test and requires another respirator and the entire procedure repeated. Maintain and inspect nebulizers, including the periodic cleaning/refill requirements; a compromised challenge cannot yield a valid pass.

**Worked decision.** A wearer unable to detect saccharin can detect Bitrex at twenty. A Bitrex test may be appropriate if the wearer and program are otherwise suitable. Do not use the saccharin sensitivity result or a sweet-solution replenishment bottle during the bitter test. Method, solution and threshold belong to one traceable procedure.

## T09 — Prepare standard CNC quantitative equipment

CNC testing compares challenge particles outside and inside the respirator. The system must sample the breathing zone through a suitable probe or temporary adapter without creating a leak, blocking the sampling path or changing the working seal. The instrument and mode must suit the filter class. Inspect tubes for damage, kinks, liquid and incorrect connections. Use the designated ambient and respirator ports and the manufacturer's tubing arrangement; a blocked sample line can produce misleadingly high results, while leakage can produce low results.

Before testing, obtain the exact instrument operating instructions, record its identity and maintenance status, and perform its required operational checks. For a PortaCount 8040/8048 example, daily checks include particles, zero and maximum fit factor, plus the classifier check when the 8048 N95 function is enabled. Follow the software prompts for when the filter is removed or attached. A failed check requires correction before testing. Selecting the appropriate respirator record must also select the proper filter mode and pass level. Consult the actual manual for startup, consumables, environmental conditions, calibration and shutdown; those device-specific operations cannot safely be generalized to every CNC instrument.

Fit-test-only probed respirators are not workplace respirators. A disposable facepiece punctured for sampling must not be issued for protection afterward. Reusable temporary adapters must be removed and the facepiece restored according to its instructions. A surrogate must match the actual sealing surfaces and make/model/style/size. Have enough equipment and time to avoid cross-contamination between wearers.

**Worked decision.** A machine reports an implausibly high fit factor while a hose is pinched under the table. Stop and investigate the sampling path. An impressive number is not evidence of validity. Correct the setup, repeat checks as required and run a new complete test. Do not manually enter a pass from the suspect run.

## T10 — Conduct and interpret the standard CNC test

Explain the test before connecting the wearer. Confirm medical prerequisite, facepiece identity, independent donning, five-minute wear period, fit conditions and seal check. Confirm the instrument is ready, the correct standard protocol is selected and the wearer can perform its exercises. Run the complete standard sequence in T05, observing the wearer and equipment. Follow the instrument instructions for sample and purge intervals and warnings. Do not replace those with an improvised stopwatch sequence in software. Record interruption, adjustment, equipment failure or incomplete movement honestly; an incomplete run is not a pass.

The standard overall result must meet at least 100 for a half facepiece or 500 for a full-face elastomeric facepiece. Individual exercise results help diagnose issues, but do not cherry-pick the highest result. Fit factors are ratios: a larger ratio indicates less measured inward particle concentration under the test conditions. Overall combination uses penetrations, not a simple arithmetic average. For illustration only, two equal-duration values of 100 and 400 combine as 2/(1/100 + 1/400) = 160, not 250. Two exercises are not a complete accepted test. Retain the instrument's valid full test recording and protocol-specific calculation.

If the test fails, inspect donning, seal geometry, valves, adapter, filter configuration and sampling system; correct the actual cause, try an appropriate alternative and repeat. Do not tighten straps artificially, suppress failed records or alter the threshold. Reassess comfort after completion. Any modified CNC, controlled-negative-pressure or REDON method requires its own complete accepted protocol and equipment instructions; this standard-method competency does not authorize invented abbreviated tests.

**Worked decision.** A half-mask overall result is 99, with six high exercises and one leak. It fails the 100 criterion. Do not round it to 100 or average only the high exercises. Correct the problem and repeat. A valid full-face overall result of exactly 500 meets that numerical requirement, but still needs all other prerequisites and valid administration.

## T11 — Records, retesting and usable restrictions

Record the employee's name or identification, test type, exact respirator make/model/style/size, date, and qualitative pass/fail or quantitative fit factor with its test recording. Add operator, protocol/version, equipment identity, preparation checks, sensitivity threshold where applicable, exercise completion, interference equipment and deviations so the result can be audited. Keep a failed or invalid attempt linked to its correction; do not overwrite it with the retest. Protect medical information separately. Fit-test records under 1910.134(m)(2) are retained until the next fit test; longer employer, jurisdictional or other applicable requirements may also apply.

Fit testing is needed before initial tight-fitting use, at least annually, when a different facepiece make/model/style/size is used, and when physical changes may affect fit. Facial scarring, dental changes, surgery and an obvious weight change are examples. A complaint of unacceptable fit warrants selection and retesting opportunity even after a pass. Each donning still requires the seal check. Training and program evaluation have their own requirements; a calendar reminder does not replace a field response to a leaking respirator today.

**Worked decision.** A record says “passed March, blue medium mask.” It is insufficient to establish which approved facepiece the wearer can use. Retrieve the original traceable record or conduct a proper test; do not guess from stock color. A new fit-test date must describe an actual event, not the date someone reprinted the card.

## T12 — Run a reliable fit-testing service

Arrange a clean, suitable room with privacy, space for movement, controlled test-agent handling and a stop/emergency process. Separate clean and used equipment. Clean and disinfect reusable facepieces and kit components according to their instructions; use appropriate drying and storage, and do not share contaminated mouth/face-contact equipment. Keep sufficient sizes, compatible adapters, fresh identified solutions, timers and recording supplies ready before inviting workers. Plan enough time for failed sensitivity screens and alternative facepieces rather than creating a quota of passes.

The tester must demonstrate preparation, correct administration, recognition of invalid tests and equipment maintenance. The evaluator must observe complete tests, not just collect course attendance. A wearer must demonstrate independent donning, seal check, reporting of problems and proper test movements. Operator coaching for learning is appropriate before assessment; during a scored test, do not move the wearer's mask or conceal an error. Record the actual model and methods for which the tester demonstrated proficiency.

**Capstone.** Three workers arrive. One lacks medical clearance; one has a beard across the seal; one is cleared and clean-shaven but cannot taste saccharin. The first is held from fit testing pending evaluation. The second needs the employer's acceptable facial-hair/protection solution before tight-fitting testing. The third can be screened for a suitable alternative agent or tested quantitatively with appropriate equipment. None is a valid saccharin pass. Record each disposition without embarrassing the workers or exposing diagnoses. Fit testing succeeds when it produces truthful, usable results, including a safe refusal or invalidation when needed.

The credential distinguishes “VNDRLY/U fit-test administrator training and observed proficiency — named methods” from “wearer fit-test result — named facepiece.” Neither permits work in an unknown atmosphere, removes the need for the respiratory program, or confers external accreditation.

# Formative learning checks

Attempt independently. Read the answer and rationale afterward, correct the response and explain a changed example. Practical performance remains separate.

## T01-L1

Does an annual fit test replace the seal check tomorrow?

**Answer:** No; check the seal at every donning.

**Reason/remediation:** A controlled periodic test does not establish each later donning. Revisit the four separate decisions.

## T01-L2

A worker changes N95 brands but keeps size medium. What changes?

**Answer:** The exact facepiece identity changes and requires a new appropriate fit test.

**Reason/remediation:** Filter class and size names do not establish identical sealing surfaces.

## T02-L1

May the tester accept a liability waiver instead of the medical recommendation?

**Answer:** No; required medical evaluation precedes fit testing.

**Reason/remediation:** A signature cannot establish medical ability. Arrange evaluation and keep testing pending.

## T02-L2

Who receives the detailed medical questionnaire?

**Answer:** The designated confidential medical evaluation process, not the course roster or supervisor.

**Reason/remediation:** The employer receives the permitted recommendation and restrictions. Review privacy boundaries.

## T03-L1

Can a qualitative pass establish a required full-face fit factor of 500?

**Answer:** No; use an appropriate quantitative test.

**Reason/remediation:** QLFT has limited permitted applications and does not measure 500.

## T03-L2

Does a tight-fitting PAPR avoid fit testing because it supplies positive pressure?

**Answer:** No; use the appropriate negative-pressure fit-test configuration.

**Reason/remediation:** Positive-pressure operation does not remove the tight-facepiece test requirement.

## T04-L1

Why include the actual helmet and eyewear in fit preparation?

**Answer:** They may interfere with straps or the seal.

**Reason/remediation:** A test-only configuration can conceal working interference; resolve compatibility.

## T04-L2

Should the tester tighten the mask unusually hard to achieve a pass?

**Answer:** No; the wearer independently dons at normal comfortable tension.

**Reason/remediation:** Artificial test-only fit is not a usable result. Revisit selection and comfort.

## T05-L1

A wearer moves a strap during talking. Keep the preceding results?

**Answer:** No; the adjustment voids the test and the full test must be repeated.

**Reason/remediation:** The seal configuration changed; a partial splice is invalid.

## T05-L2

Which standard exercise is omitted from QLFT?

**Answer:** The fifteen-second grimace; QLFT uses seven one-minute exercises.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not mix quantitative and qualitative exercise rules.

## T06-L1

Saccharin is tasted at squeeze eighteen. Record what threshold?

**Answer:** Twenty.

**Reason/remediation:** Screening is recorded in groups of ten, not the exact detection squeeze.

## T06-L2

No taste at thirty screening squeezes means what?

**Answer:** Saccharin screening is unsuccessful; select another suitable method.

**Reason/remediation:** Absent sensitivity cannot demonstrate absent leakage.

## T07-L1

For threshold thirty, give the initial and replenishment doses.

**Answer:** Thirty initially, fifteen every thirty seconds.

**Reason/remediation:** Replenishment is half the initial dose. Use a timer and record.

## T07-L2

The nebulizer is found clogged at the end. Pass despite no taste?

**Answer:** No; invalidate the test and repeat after correction.

**Reason/remediation:** The challenge may not have been maintained.

## T08-L1

May a saccharin threshold be copied into a Bitrex test?

**Answer:** No; screen with Bitrex and record its own threshold.

**Reason/remediation:** Sensitivity is agent-specific.

## T08-L2

What happens if bitterness appears during a Bitrex fit test?

**Answer:** Fail the fit, try a different respirator and repeat the entire procedure including screening.

**Reason/remediation:** An agent detected inside indicates an unsatisfactory test result; do not ignore it.

## T09-L1

Can the operator skip a failed daily check because the display later gives high numbers?

**Answer:** No; correct equipment readiness before testing.

**Reason/remediation:** A result from an unverified system is not reliable.

## T09-L2

Can the punctured disposable test facepiece be issued to the worker afterward?

**Answer:** No; it is fit-test-only equipment.

**Reason/remediation:** The sampling modification is not a workplace protection configuration.

## T10-L1

A standard half-mask overall result is 99. How is it reported?

**Answer:** Fail, not rounded up.

**Reason/remediation:** The minimum is 100 and all validity conditions still apply.

## T10-L2

Why not average 100 and 400 as 250 for a fit-factor illustration?

**Answer:** Equal-duration penetration averaging gives 160, using 2/(1/100+1/400).

**Reason/remediation:** Ratios combine through penetration; the illustration is not a complete fit test.

## T11-L1

A wearer reports dental changes before the annual due date. What next?

**Answer:** Evaluate the fit concern and conduct the required additional fit test.

**Reason/remediation:** Annual is not the only trigger.

## T11-L2

Is a record reading blue medium mask sufficient?

**Answer:** No; recover exact make, model, style and size or perform a proper test.

**Reason/remediation:** Color is not traceable facepiece identity.

## T12-L1

Can operator proficiency be observed during a correctly administered wearer failure?

**Answer:** Yes; correct administration and truthful failure can demonstrate tester skill.

**Reason/remediation:** Tester competence and wearer pass are separate outcomes.

## T12-L2

A supervisor requests backdating a test to yesterday. What should the operator do?

**Answer:** Refuse, record the actual test date and communicate the truthful disposition.

**Reason/remediation:** A useful service preserves traceable facts instead of meeting a pass quota.
