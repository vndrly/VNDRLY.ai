# VUC022 — definition, instructor guide and evaluation packet

Audience: eligible employees assigned to powered industrial trucks, their trainers and evaluators. Purpose: safe, demonstrated operation within a named truck/workplace scope. Prerequisites: employer eligibility check including applicable age restrictions; ability to understand instructions and signals with appropriate language/accessibility support; direct supervision during training; actual truck manual, capacity information and workplace risk assessment. Trainers/evaluators must have the knowledge, training and experience to teach and evaluate the truck and tasks. A car license alone does not establish that competence.

Objectives P01–P12: explain qualification boundaries; identify and operate actual controls; inspect and remove unsafe equipment; read configuration-specific capacity and recognize instability; prepare loads/attachments; pick/place safely; protect pedestrians and maintain visibility; handle permitted grades/surfaces; verify dock/trailer/elevator controls; fuel/charge within scope; recognize atmosphere/ventilation hazards; park/respond and trigger refresher evaluation.

The course provides all common formal instruction and actual evaluation tasks. Truck-specific instruction is delivered using the equipment in the model familiarization sheet below. Every truck-related and workplace-related topic in 1910.178(l)(3) is covered or explicitly marked inapplicable with the employer's reason. A missing required topic is pending, not inapplicable. Different types/attachments require appropriate additional training and evaluation. This package is not a universal license for all industrial vehicles, cranes, earthmoving equipment, personnel lifting or public-road driving.

Suggested novice delivery: sixteen instructional hours before breaks, with additional supervised practice and workplace evaluation whenever needed. Day one: P01–P03 90 minutes; P04–P06 120; P07–P09 120; P10–P12 90; written scenarios/checks 60. Day two: trainer demonstrations/model familiarization 90; coached individual practice 180; independent stations and workplace observation 150; final/feedback 60. Both days total 480 minutes. This is VNDRLY's planning allocation, not a federally mandated number of course hours. Never shorten observed individual performance to make a class fit. Only one trainee drives a truck at a time; use a segregated practice area and an evaluator able to stop the activity safely. Expand the schedule for additional truck types and applicable tasks.

Instructor method: establish hazards and show the correct action; explain why it matters; demonstrate with an empty truck before adding a stable low-risk training load; have the learner describe controls; provide coached practice; then observe without prompts. Discuss worked examples and the two formative checks per chapter. Do not use production traffic, an unguarded dock edge, an unstable load or a real defect as a surprise assessment. Simulate emergencies on documents or a secured stationary truck. A training area must not endanger trainees or anyone else.

Final examination:36 original constructed responses, three per domain,72 points. Score 2 correct action and reason,1 safe incomplete,0 wrong/absent/unsafe. Pass 58/72 and 4/6 each domain with no critical unsafe endorsement. Critical examples include operating an unsafe truck, lifting without established capacity, permitting people beneath raised forks, driving into an unrestrained/unsupported trailer, bypassing safety devices or claiming quiz-only operating authority. Time 60 minutes plus documented accommodations; no coaching/key access. Preserve attempts. After targeted teaching and practice, use the separate protected reassessment prompts for failed domains plusP12, then complete any failed practical again with changed conditions. Never erase an unsafe attempt or average it away.

Practical scoring: five criteria per station,0–2 each; pass 8/10 with every **critical** criterion 2. Score 2 independent correct,1 safe prompted/incomplete,0 omitted/incorrect. Instructor intervention to prevent hazard is a critical failure. All applicable stations, including actual workplace evaluation J, must be completed before an operator credential is issued. A document-only substitute is used only for dangerous emergency cases or for topics genuinely not present in the authorized scope, not to replace actual driving/lifting. Mark unavailable required equipment/tasks pending. Assess each additional truck type/attachment and relevant workplace condition before adding it to scope.

Credential: VNDRLY/U training/evaluation record naming actual demonstrated types, attachments and workplace. Employer certifies the required operator/training/evaluation/evaluator fields and grants its own authorization. No OSHA-issued license or external accreditation is claimed. Evaluation at least every three years and relevant refresher/evaluation upon the triggers inP12; do not automatically repeat all unchanged instruction or ignore immediate triggers until year three. Materials authorship does not establish that any person has operated or passed.

# Practical stations

Equipment: actual safe authorized truck(s), current manuals and readable plates, approved attachment(s), stable known-weight pallets, suitable inspected rack/ground deposit, segregated route with cones and marked crossing, workplace traffic plan, safe grade/dock setup where applicable, inspection and evaluation records. Use only loads safely within verified actual ratings. Synthetic paper ratings below are for calculation only and must never label a real truck.

## A — Inspection and defect decision

Learner conducts static and operational inspection with the actual checklist, explains defect handling and shows safe access. Assessor presents a photograph of a cracked fork and a document case of a failed brake; do not introduce real dangerous defects. Score: systematic static check; power-source items; **critical safe operational check**; **critical remove unsafe equipment**; accurate report. Reassessment changes defect to missing capacity plate or damaged restraint. Required result is no operation until safe condition is restored.

## B — Controls, access and unloaded maneuvering

Learner identifies controls with power off, mounts correctly, adjusts/restraints as applicable, starts, moves, stops, reverses and parks in the segregated area. Assessor confirms each control against actual instructions. Score: safe access/stance; **critical restraint/interlock compliance**; smooth starts/stops; steering/tail clearance; **critical controlled stopping and no exposure**. Reassessment changes the route and stop positions. A new truck type needs its own demonstration, not this score copied forward.

## C — Capacity and attachment decision lab

Use the real plate for an actual safe load decision, then give paper cases: synthetic 4000 lb/24 in rating versus 3600 lb/30 in; unknown-weight crate; attachment absent from plate. Learner identifies missing information and refuses unapproved loads. Score: plate/configuration identification; actual weight/center identification; **critical no overload/unknown authorization**; explains moment limitation; **critical attachment approval evidence**. Expected paper answer:96000 versus 108000 lb-in, reject attempted justification; do not derive a substitute rating. Reassessment uses synthetic 5000 lb/24 in versus 4200 lb/32 in:120000 versus 134400 lb-in, reject.

## D — Load pickup and low travel

Using a verified stable known-weight pallet, learner inspects it, sets fork spacing/retention, approaches squarely, inserts safely, lifts minimally, confirms stability and lowers to actual prescribed travel position. Assessor observes from outside the route and gives no movement prompts. Score: load preparation; fork engagement; **critical capacity/clear zone**; controlled lift/tilt; **critical low stable travel/no body exposure**. Reassessment changes pallet orientation and safe destination. Do not use deliberately unstable cargo as a live exercise.

## E — Route, intersection and pedestrian controls

Learner follows a cone route with a marked blind intersection and stationary pedestrian marker outside the operating area. A spotter stands only in a preplanned safe location. Learner slows/warns, keeps view, controls tail swing and stops when the evaluator raises the agreed stop signal. Score: route scan; **critical pedestrian separation**; intersection behavior; **critical stop on lost view/signal**; smooth turns and direction change. Reassessment changes the route/obstructed-view case using a safe approved load. No person steps unexpectedly in front of the truck.

## F — Stacking and unstacking

Use a stable inspected rack or stack within verified truck/rack/load limits, adequate headroom and an exclusion zone. Learner deposits and retrieves a load, checking support, tilt, clearance and fork withdrawal. Score: destination/overhead assessment; square stationary positioning; **critical stable supported deposit**; controlled withdrawal/lowering; **critical no elevated-load travel or people beneath**. Reassessment changes bay or safe load dimensions. A paper case of a damaged beam requires isolation and alternative storage, not a live attempt.

## G — Grades, dock and special route readiness

Where these tasks exist, learner first performs a walk-through and independently verifies actual grade/truck limits, orientation, traction, dockboard/floor capacity, trailer restraint/support and clearances. Only after evaluator confirms the setup safe does the learner demonstrate the applicable route at low speed. For workplaces without these tasks, record genuine inapplicability and retain scope restrictions; add training/evaluation before future assignment. Score: **critical suitability/capacity**; correct route/orientation; **critical restraint/support before entry**; safe slow movement; clear stopping/communication. Paper case:8200+200+500+2100=11000 lb exceeds 10000 lb board. Reassessment changes board to 12000 but floor/overhead evidence missing: still hold pending all evidence. Elevator/railcar tasks require actual additional workplace demonstration if assigned, usingP09 and the adaptation sheet.

## H — Fuel or charging procedure

Observe the actual assigned procedure using the manufacturer's instructions, designated safe station and trained supervision. If operator duties end at parking for service, assess that boundary and do not authorize fuel handling. Use document cases for leaks, smoke or damaged cables; no live spill. Score: **critical secure truck/ignition control**; correct equipment/PPE; prescribed connection/handling sequence; **critical stop on defect**; restored safe condition and report. Reassessment changes defect to incompatible charger or unapproved battery. Any chemical preparation or cylinder exchange beyond assessed duties remains excluded from authorization.

## I — Parking and emergency decisions

Learner parks the actual truck safely, then describes/demonstrates posture only on the secured stationary truck for its specific emergency instructions. Assessor gives a near-miss report and a scenario of leaving the truck out of sight five feet away. Score: **critical lowered/neutral/braked**; **critical unattended shutdown**; route/fire access preserved; correct model-specific emergency response; near-miss/refresher reporting. Expected: out of sight is unattended regardless of the short distance; no actual tipover demonstration. Reassessment uses an incline parking case and a different truck-type emergency instruction requiring familiarization.

## J — Independent actual workplace performance

After coached practice, evaluator observes a complete authorized work cycle in the actual workplace under controlled safe conditions: inspect, plan, pick up, travel, deposit, return and park. Include actual typical loads, visibility and traffic controls without manufacturing hazards. Evaluate each assigned type and relevant condition. Score: **critical appropriate equipment/load/route decision**; **critical continuous people protection and control**; correct manipulation; actual workplace rules and communication; **critical independent safe completion**. Record observations and locations, not just pass checkboxes. Reassessment changes a safe load/route after remediation. Until the learner demonstrates this stage, label training complete if appropriate but operating qualification pending.

# VNDRLY/U Powered Industrial Truck Operator

Original coursebook, version 1.0, 17 November 2026. Federal US 1910.178 baseline. This teaches powered-truck operation and supplies actual workplace evaluation materials. It does not issue an OSHA license or authorize every truck type. The employer must establish competence on the truck and conditions actually assigned. Truck instructions, capacity plate and workplace controls are part of the learning and evaluation, not optional appendices.

## P01 — Operator responsibility and authorization

A forklift is a lifting machine that also travels, not a car with forks. Rear steering on many counterbalanced trucks makes the tail swing outside a turn. The load and lifting mechanism move the combined center of gravity; high loads, sharp turns and abrupt stops can overturn a truck even when the load is below the weight printed on the plate. A pedestrian may be trapped between the truck and a rack before the operator sees the person. The operator's first job is to control that risk, not to maximize pallets per hour.

Qualification combines formal instruction, trainer demonstrations and learner practice, and evaluation of performance in the workplace. A trainee may operate only under direct supervision of a knowledgeable, trained, experienced person and only where doing so does not endanger anyone. A successful online quiz alone is not authorization. The employer certifies the operator's name, training date, evaluation date and the trainer/evaluator identity. Prior training can avoid duplication only when it is appropriate to the actual truck and conditions and competence is evaluated.

Know the limits written into your authorization: truck type, attachments, locations, load tasks, slopes and fueling/charging duties. Report a change before operating outside them. A person trained on a sit-down counterbalanced truck has not automatically demonstrated a narrow-aisle reach truck, walkie pallet truck or rough-terrain telescopic machine. Non-agricultural forklift work generally excludes employees under eighteen; employer eligibility review must occur before assigning operation.

**Worked decision.** An experienced driver is asked to use a different truck on a night shift. Experience supports efficient targeted training, but does not remove unfamiliar control, visibility and workplace evaluation requirements. Hold the unfamiliar task until the employer verifies competence and scope.

## P02 — Truck types, controls and model familiarization

Identify the power source, operator position, steering arrangement, drive wheels, brakes, mast/reach mechanism, attachment, stability system, alarms and emergency controls. With power safely off, point to each control and explain its effect from the manual. Then observe the trainer operate it in a clear area. Do not discover an unfamiliar lever's function with a load in the air. An inching control, service brake, parking brake and directional control are not interchangeable. Some electric trucks decelerate when the accelerator is released; that does not justify bypassing the prescribed stopping method.

For a sit-down truck, use the designed access points and handholds, face the truck and maintain secure contact while mounting; never pull on controls or jump down. Adjust the seat and mirrors and use the provided restraint. Keep limbs within the operator compartment. For a stand-up truck, learn the exact operator-presence devices, stance, guarding and emergency exit instructions. For a walkie, know the tiller operating/braking range and reversing safety device, and keep feet clear of drive/load wheels. Do not ride a walkie unless it is specifically designed and authorized for riding.

Start only with the area clear and controls positioned as instructed. Read warning lamps, gauges and messages before travel. Demonstrate smooth starting, stopping and neutral selection without a load. Know how to shut down normally and respond to a malfunction. Do not bypass an interlock with tape, a weight or a coworker's hand. Defective protection means remove from service.

**Worked decision.** A learner recognizes the steering wheel but cannot identify the attachment lever. Leave the attachment unloaded and stationary, review the exact manual and obtain a demonstration. “Similar to the last forklift” is not a control identification method.

## P03 — Pre-use inspection and defect control

Inspect before placing the truck in service, at least daily; round-the-clock use requires examination after each shift. Use the actual truck's checklist. Begin with power off and the truck secured. Inspect tires/wheels, forks and retaining devices, mast/chain condition from a safe position, hydraulic hoses, leaks, guards, backrest, restraint, steps, labels, plate and manual. Keep hands out of mast pinch points; do not probe hydraulic leaks with fingers. High-pressure fluid can penetrate skin. Do not crawl under raised forks to inspect them.

Check power-source-specific items within your assigned duty: battery security, cables and connectors; engine fluids and cooling components when safe; LPG cylinder mount, connections, condition and orientation as specified. A hot pressure cap must not be opened. Unidentified leaking fluid, damaged forks, missing retention hardware, inoperative restraint or illegible capacity information require a stop and proper review rather than an improvised repair.

After a satisfactory static inspection, conduct the operational check in a barricaded clear area: steering, service/parking brakes, directional control, accelerator, lift/lower/tilt, attachment functions, horn, lights, alarms and safety devices. Use low speed and the manufacturer's test method. Listen for unusual sounds and observe drift or irregular motion. Record and report defects immediately. Unsafe equipment is taken out of service until authorized personnel restore it; an operator's note does not make continued use acceptable.

**Worked decision.** The brakes feel weak but the route is short. Park safely, identify the defect and prevent use under the site's process. “Drive slowly” cannot replace functioning brakes. Do not return to service until the authorized repair/release process is complete.

## P04 — Stability, capacity and the load center

The counterweight and truck balance the load about the supporting wheels. The combined center of gravity must stay inside the truck's stability region; it moves when the load moves or the truck accelerates. Lifting raises the center of gravity, making sideways overturn easier. Forward tilt, longer loads and attachments move load weight farther outward and increase the overturning moment. Smooth driving matters because dynamic forces can destroy the margin that existed while stationary.

Read the plate for the actual truck and attachment: rated capacity, load center, lift-height limits and any configuration restrictions. The load center is measured from the fork face toward the load's center of gravity, not necessarily half its overall length when contents are uneven. A longer pallet does not carry the same rated weight simply because it fits on the forks. Know the load weight from reliable identification, documentation or weighing; guessing is unacceptable near a limit. Do not add people or extra counterweights to increase capacity.

**Teaching example only.** A synthetic plate permits 4,000 lb at a 24-inch load center. The reference moment is 96,000 lb-in. A 3,600 lb load centered 30 inches out gives 108,000 lb-in and cannot be justified by “less than 4,000.” The moment comparison identifies a problem; it is not a replacement capacity chart and cannot authorize 3,200 lb at 30 inches by division. The actual manufacturer rating for the configuration governs, including height, attachment and lateral stability.

**Common mistake:** checking weight but ignoring offset, high elevation or a heavier attachment. Stop when any required plate/chart information is missing. Have the employer obtain the correct approved information; do not pencil in a new rating.

## P05 — Forks, attachments and safe load preparation

Inspect the load before approaching: weight, center of gravity, broken pallet boards, loose packaging, liquids that may shift, sharp projections and items that could fall backward. Secure unstable loads through an approved handling method before lifting. Place heavy portions closest to the carriage when the approved configuration allows, center the load laterally and spread forks appropriately to support it. Verify fork locks and attachment engagement. The forks should extend under the load as far as possible without striking another load or person behind it.

Attachments change reach, mass, visibility and capacity. Use only the approved truck/attachment combination and corresponding plate. Capacity/safe-operation modifications require manufacturer prior written approval under the baseline rule and updated instructions/markings; an operator cannot approve homemade extensions, hooks or welded repairs. Even an empty truck with an attachment must be treated as partially loaded. A clamp's pressure and contact surfaces must suit the product; a side shift can move the load off center; a rotator changes the load's behavior. Learn and demonstrate the actual attachment separately.

Approach squarely at low speed, stop and position the forks at the correct height with adequate clearance. Check behind the load before inserting forks. If the pallet catches or the load is bound to a rack, do not use hydraulic force to tear it free. Withdraw safely, secure the area and resolve the obstruction. Never let a helper hold the load upright while the truck moves.

**Worked decision.** A 2,000 lb crate on a split pallet seems well below capacity. The defect is load integrity, not capacity. Repack or use an approved stable lifting method before movement; a low weight does not prevent falling material.

## P06 — Pickup, transport height and depositing

Plan the entire lift, including where the load will be deposited and how the truck will withdraw. Confirm floor/rack support and overhead clearance before raising anything. Approach squarely, align forks and engage fully. With the truck controlled and people clear, raise just enough to establish that the load is stable and clear. Use only the tilt needed to stabilize it. Keep anyone from standing or passing beneath raised forks, loaded or empty. The overhead guard protects against some falling objects; it is not designed to make a falling rated-capacity load safe.

Lower to the prescribed travel position before moving out. Travel with the load low enough for stability and high enough to clear the surface, according to the model and route; do not treat a single generic height as correct on every truck. Look in the travel direction. Do not travel with an elevated load to save time at the next rack. Keep the load back as applicable and avoid forward tilt during travel.

At the destination, stop squarely and establish the clear zone. Raise only when stationary and correctly positioned. Approach the deposit position slowly using the prescribed controls. Tilt forward with an elevated load only when in the proper deposit position over the rack/stack, not while approaching it. Set the load fully on its support, relieve fork load, check clearance, withdraw without dragging the pallet, and lower to travel position before turning. Verify the stack is stable and does not exceed rack capacity or block fire protection.

**Worked decision.** A rack beam is bent and the pallet will not seat evenly. Do not force or leave a partly supported load. Return it to a safe location, isolate/report the rack issue and obtain a safe destination. The completed move must include a stable deposit, not just lifting the pallet.

## P07 — Travel, steering, visibility and pedestrians

Survey the route for people, intersections, doors, overhead services, floor defects, spills and tight turns. Follow the site's traffic plan and speed limits, but reduce speed further whenever visibility, traction or stopping distance requires. Maintain approximately three truck lengths from the truck ahead and increase separation when needed. Do not pass at blind spots, intersections or other dangerous locations. Slow and sound the horn where vision is obstructed; a horn is a warning, not permission to enter an occupied path.

Rear-end swing can strike a person outside the front wheel path. Look in the direction of travel, check both the load path and tail clearance, slow before turning, turn smoothly and stop before reversing direction. Do not turn with a raised load. If a load blocks forward view, travel with the load trailing where the route and slope rules permit, looking directly in the travel direction. When safe visibility cannot be maintained, stop and arrange an appropriate spotter or different route under the site procedure. Stop if the spotter is lost from view; agreed signals do not eliminate operator responsibility.

Separate pedestrians physically where possible. Do not approach a person standing between the truck and a fixed object. Never permit riders unless authorized and provided a designed safe place. Keep arms and legs inside the protected running lines. No one rides a pallet or forks. Use a suitable purpose-designed personnel access method; this course does not authorize improvised lifting of people.

**Worked decision.** A pedestrian waves the truck through while standing in a pinch zone against a rack. Wait for the person to move to a safe position. Consent cannot remove the crushing hazard. Productivity does not justify accepting a route with no reliable view.

## P08 — Ramps, grades, surfaces and narrow aisles

Check whether the truck is approved for the surface and slope. Know grade as rise divided by horizontal run, multiplied by 100; it is not the same number as degrees. A two-foot rise over twenty feet is ten percent. The manual may impose lower limits or restrictions on loaded/unloaded travel. Inspect traction, transition edges and load clearance. Never use a warehouse truck on rough ground merely because its tires can reach it.

For conventional counterbalanced trucks, keep the loaded end upgrade when traveling ramps; ascend loaded forward and descend loaded in reverse. The regulation specifically requires load-upgrade travel on grades over ten percent; the safer operating guidance is not permission to point a load downhill on smaller grades. Unloaded conventional trucks travel with forks downgrade as instructed. Attachments change the unloaded condition. Follow the actual type's instructions for walkies, reach trucks and other arrangements rather than copying a sit-down pattern blindly.

Go slowly straight up or down; do not turn, traverse diagonally or park on a grade as a convenience. Keep the load/engaging means tilted back if applicable and only high enough to clear the surface. If a load blocks view while the slope requires a particular orientation, arrange an approved route/control solution rather than reversing the load downhill. A spotter cannot make an unsuitable grade suitable.

In narrow aisles confirm truck/load dimensions, turning envelope, tail swing, rack projections and pedestrian exclusion. Wet floors, ice, loose debris and damaged surfaces reduce control. Stop and correct or reroute when safe traction/clearance is unavailable. **Worked decision:** an aisle is wide enough for the chassis but not for the side-shifted pallet. Recenter within the approved operating setup or select another safe route; do not squeeze past occupied racks.

## P09 — Docks, trailers, railcars and elevators

A trailer can roll, creep away or tip even though its doors are open and a driver says it is ready. Confirm the site's positive restraint/brake/chock system and communication procedure before entry. The OSHA baseline contains brake/chock requirements; enforcement boundaries for regulated commercial vehicles and approved restraint arrangements need employer determination, not improvisation by an operator. Verify the actual restraint indication and trailer support. An uncoupled semitrailer may need fixed jacks to prevent upending.

Inspect the dockboard/bridge plate for condition, secure placement and rated capacity. Consider the whole crossing load: truck, operator, attachment and cargo, not cargo alone. Check trailer flooring for damage and support, overhead clearance, interior visibility and weight distribution. Keep a safe margin from dock edges and protect open doors. Cross slowly without exceeding board or floor capacity. If the trailer moves or a restraint signal changes, stop safely and secure the situation; never accelerate to “make the gap.”

Railcars require positive protection against movement while loading or while bridge plates are in position. Trucks must not be used to open freight doors. Cross tracks diagonally where possible and do not park within eight feet of the track center. For an approved freight elevator, verify capacity and leveling, approach slowly and squarely, then neutralize controls, shut off power and set brakes once aboard. Motorized hand trucks enter elevators/confined spaces load-end first. Confirm these tasks are authorized and demonstrated before attempting them.

**Worked calculation.** Truck 8,200 lb, operator 200, attachment 500 and cargo 2,100 total 11,000 lb. A 10,000 lb board is unsuitable even though the cargo is only 2,100. Obtain a suitable crossing and verify all other limits; do not deduct a “short crossing” allowance.

## P10 — Fuel, batteries and fire prevention

Perform only the fuel/charging duties for which trained and authorized. Park in the designated area, lower load-engaging means, secure controls and brake, and follow the model's shutdown procedure. Do not fill a fuel tank with the engine running or operate with a fuel leak. Eliminate ignition sources, avoid spills and restore caps correctly. A spill requires the site's safe cleanup and ignition-control process before restart; never use an open flame to check liquid levels. LPG cylinder work requires its own correct valve, connection, orientation, leak-check and protective-equipment procedure. Do not loosen a pressurized connection to experiment.

For battery charging, use the designated ventilated area, suitable charger and manufacturer sequence. Protect chargers from vehicle impact. Inspect connectors/cables and isolate defective equipment. Keep smoking, flames, sparks and metal tools away from exposed battery tops. Lead-acid charging can release hydrogen; electrolyte presents splash/burn hazards. Required flushing/neutralizing and fire provisions must be available. Use appropriate handling equipment for a battery's weight and secure it correctly afterward. Only trained assigned personnel handle electrolyte; when preparing acid mixture, acid goes into water, not water into acid. This is not an instruction for ordinary operators to mix electrolyte.

Lithium systems do not use the lead-acid watering procedure. Use the battery/charger-specific instructions and emergency plan; abnormal heat, swelling, smoke or damage warrants isolation and emergency response from a safe location. Never bypass a battery management fault to complete a shift. A differently weighted replacement battery can affect truck balance and must match approved specifications.

**Worked decision.** A damaged charging cable still works. Stop using it, report and isolate it; functionality is not electrical safety. A worker must not hold the wires apart while another operates the charger.

## P11 — Hazardous atmospheres, ventilation and changing conditions

Choose the truck for the classified location before entry. D, E, LP and enhanced-protection designations are not interchangeable. The employer must determine location classification and suitable truck approval under the standard's location tables. An electric truck is not automatically explosion-protected. Some hazardous atmospheres prohibit powered trucks, and an EX marking is not a universal permission for every gas or dust. The operator verifies the approved truck/location assignment and stops if conditions change.

Internal-combustion exhaust can create carbon monoxide and other harmful exposures in enclosed spaces, trailers and poorly ventilated buildings. An open door does not prove adequate ventilation. Do not rely on smell to detect carbon monoxide. Know the ventilation/monitoring plan, warning symptoms and evacuation process. Headache, dizziness or an alarm means stop exposure safely, leave and seek appropriate assistance; do not drive an affected coworker around to “get some air” while continuing work.

Maintain suitable lighting and visibility through doors and between bright and dark areas. Protect the route from overhead obstructions, low pipes, sprinklers and electrical hazards. Outdoor weather can alter traction, stability and load behavior. A load with broad surface area can catch wind. Site hazards such as a chemical leak, damaged rack, unknown floor capacity or newly blocked walkway require reassessment rather than treating yesterday's route approval as permanent.

**Worked decision.** A propane truck is requested in a building with a suspected flammable vapor release. Do not enter to investigate with the truck. Secure from a safe location and use the site's emergency process. The approval marking and normal ventilation plan do not authorize operating through an abnormal release.

## P12 — Parking, shutdown and emergency response

Park in the designated safe place without blocking exits, fire equipment, stairs or traffic. Fully lower the forks/load-engaging means, neutralize controls, set the brake and shut down as required. A truck is unattended when the operator is at least twenty-five feet away while it remains in view, or whenever out of view after dismounting. Unattended shutdown includes power off; block wheels on an incline. Even when dismounted within twenty-five feet and in view, lower the engaging means, neutralize and set the brake. Do not leave an elevated load because the operator intends to return quickly.

Learn the emergency procedure for the actual truck before driving. For a sit-down counterbalanced truck tipover, the protective response is to remain in the operator position with the restraint already fastened, hold on, brace feet and lean away from impact rather than jumping into the crush path. Other types differ: some rear-entry stand-up trucks specify stepping backward out. Read and practice the actual model's instructions on a stationary secured truck. Never stage an actual tipover or lift wheels to test a learner.

For a malfunction or collision, stop safely, secure the area and summon assistance. Do not attempt to catch a falling load or reach through the mast. Protect people from unstable loads and damaged racks until authorized recovery personnel plan the work. Report near misses as well as injuries; the absence of injury does not mean the controls worked. Refresher training is required after unsafe operation, an accident/near miss, an evaluation showing unsafe performance, a different truck type or relevant workplace changes. Performance evaluation is required at least every three years, not just when a card expires.

**Capstone.** A late order needs an unknown-weight long crate moved across a wet dockboard into an unrestrained trailer. The correct sequence is to stop, establish weight/center and approved capacity, stabilize packaging, restore safe traction, verify board/floor/clearance and restraint/support, then move only with qualified personnel and a clear route. Skipping any one because the others look good leaves an uncontrolled hazard.

# Formative learning checks

Attempt independently before reviewing answers. Correct errors, revisit the linked chapter and explain a changed example. These checks do not replace physical workplace evaluation.

## P01-L1

Does passing the written course authorize independent forklift work?

**Answer:** No; practical training and actual workplace evaluation plus employer authorization are required.

**Reason/remediation:** Revisit the three components of competence.

## P01-L2

Can prior training reduce duplicate instruction?

**Answer:** Yes, if appropriate to the actual truck/conditions and competence is evaluated.

**Reason/remediation:** Experience is evidence to assess, not a universal exemption.

## P02-L1

What should happen before trying an unfamiliar attachment lever?

**Answer:** Identify it from the actual manual and obtain safe demonstration/practice without a raised load.

**Reason/remediation:** Guessing can move a load or create a pinch hazard.

## P02-L2

Can a walkie be ridden because the trip is long?

**Answer:** Only if specifically designed and authorized for riding; otherwise no.

**Reason/remediation:** Operating position is a design limitation.

## P03-L1

A brake defect is noted before a short trip. Continue slowly?

**Answer:** No; remove unsafe equipment from service and report it.

**Reason/remediation:** Speed reduction cannot repair a safety defect.

## P03-L2

How often are round-the-clock trucks examined?

**Answer:** Before service and after each shift; the baseline otherwise requires at least daily examination.

**Reason/remediation:** Shift use changes the examination interval.

## P04-L1

Why can a light long load still exceed capacity?

**Answer:** Its farther center and configuration can exceed permitted moment/stability limits.

**Reason/remediation:** Weight alone is not the rating.

## P04-L2

May a calculated moment ratio replace the capacity plate?

**Answer:** No; it is a teaching/risk check, not approved capacity information.

**Reason/remediation:** Real ratings include configuration and stability factors beyond the simple ratio.

## P05-L1

What should happen to a low-weight load on a broken pallet?

**Answer:** Stabilize/repack through an approved method before lifting.

**Reason/remediation:** Low weight does not make a weak support safe.

## P05-L2

Does an empty attachment have no stability effect?

**Answer:** No; operate the attached truck as partially loaded and use the approved combination rating.

**Reason/remediation:** Attachment weight/reach changes the truck.

## P06-L1

May anyone walk beneath empty elevated forks?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** The raised mechanism itself can fall or crush.

## P06-L2

The rack will not support an even deposit. What next?

**Answer:** Return the load to a safe place and isolate/report the problem.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not force or leave a partly supported pallet.

## P07-L1

A horn sounds at a blind intersection. Does that give right of way?

**Answer:** No; slow, establish a clear safe path and keep control.

**Reason/remediation:** A warning is not confirmation that people are clear.

## P07-L2

The spotter disappears from view. Continue by memory?

**Answer:** No; stop and restore safe communication/visibility.

**Reason/remediation:** Past position does not establish current clearance.

## P08-L1

What is the grade of a two-foot rise over twenty horizontal feet?

**Answer:** Ten percent.

**Reason/remediation:** Rise/run times 100; degrees are different.

## P08-L2

Should a loaded conventional truck turn across a ramp?

**Answer:** No; use the permitted straight route with load upgrade and appropriate slow control.

**Reason/remediation:** Turning on a grade can overturn the truck.

## P09-L1

Does the cargo weight alone determine dockboard suitability?

**Answer:** No; include truck, operator, attachment and cargo crossing load.

**Reason/remediation:** The board supports the combination.

## P09-L2

An uncoupled trailer has good brakes. What other support issue exists?

**Answer:** It may need fixed jacks to prevent upending, plus verified floor, restraint and board conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Preventing rolling does not prevent tipping or floor failure.

## P10-L1

A fuel leak is small. May the truck finish its load?

**Answer:** No; stop safely and remove it from service until corrected.

**Reason/remediation:** Ignition and exposure risks do not depend on convenience.

## P10-L2

Can lead-acid watering instructions be applied to a lithium battery?

**Answer:** No; follow the actual battery/charger procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** Different chemistries require different controls.

## P11-L1

Does electric automatically mean safe for a flammable area?

**Answer:** No; verify actual truck approval against the classified location.

**Reason/remediation:** Power source is not explosion protection approval.

## P11-L2

Can an open warehouse door prove carbon monoxide is controlled?

**Answer:** No; use the ventilation/exposure-control plan and respond to alarms/symptoms.

**Reason/remediation:** CO cannot be reliably detected by smell or door position.

## P12-L1

The operator leaves the truck out of sight but only ten feet away. Is it unattended?

**Answer:** Yes; apply full unattended shutdown.

**Reason/remediation:** Out of view triggers unattended status regardless of being within 25 feet.

## P12-L2

Must refresher evaluation wait for the three-year date after a near miss?

**Answer:** No; relevant refresher training and evaluation are triggered now.

**Reason/remediation:** Periodic evaluation does not replace event-triggered correction.
