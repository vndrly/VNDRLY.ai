# VNDRLY/U Banksman and Slinger Competence Development and Assessment

VUC087 · original available-source materials · 18 November 2026

For personnel with relevant initial training and authenticated supervised experience. Fourteen chapters include the full reviewed common rigging instruction as refresher plus scope, unequal-loading and competence-decision instruction. Planned 40 hours of refresher, development and assessment; workplace experience is additional and has no invented guaranteed duration. This is not OPITO 9068 approval or its unavailable controlled four-unit specification. Actual combined-role competence requires the independent practical and workplace evidence below; a classroom-only result cannot earn it.

## Q87-01 — Define the competence scope before accepting the assessment

Competence is a supported conclusion about performance in a defined role and range of conditions. It is not a reward for attendance or years of service. For this VNDRLY assessment, identify the actual equipment family, load types, signaling methods and work environment that the evidence will cover. The candidate must demonstrate both banksman and slinger duties; doing one while watching the other is not a combined-role result.

Review prior training and authenticated supervised experience before scheduling practical assessment. A résumé is an entry clue, not proof of every skill. Check the candidate's actual role in each job, supervisor identity, plan, equipment and observations. Where the records are incomplete, agree additional supervised development rather than inventing missing experience. The assessor must be technically competent and authorized for the defined scope and independent enough to make an honest decision despite production pressure.

Worked case: a candidate presents twenty lift logs, all signed by the same supervisor, but every entry says only “assisted.” Ask what the candidate actually selected, inspected, attached and signaled. Collect specific corroboration and directly observe the gaps. Changed case: the records clearly establish slinging, but the candidate has never directed a restricted-visibility movement. That outcome remains unestablished. Do not expand a certificate to include it simply because the person is experienced with the load.

This program provides refresher instruction, development exercises and a controlled competence-assessment process. The refresher is completed before the final attempt. During final assessment, the assessor may stop danger but must not coach the candidate to a pass. A safety intervention is evidence requiring remediation and a fresh independent attempt, not a hidden successful action.

## Q87-02 — Read the lift plan and inspect the work area

Read the task, load identity, mass, center of gravity, lifting points, rigging configuration, equipment, route, landing location, personnel and stop conditions. Confirm the actual situation matches the plan. Look for pedestrians, vehicles, overhead services, excavations, soft ground, restricted visibility, structures, weather and simultaneous work. A route that was clear yesterday may be occupied now. Do not alter a lift plan informally to save time.

The plan must address pickup and landing, not only travel. Decide where people stand, where they retreat and how access is controlled. Verify the landing surface and supports before lifting. A load suspended while the crew searches for dunnage creates avoidable exposure. Check communications and emergency arrangements during the briefing, using questions to confirm understanding.

Worked case: plan L87 specifies an eastward route, but a delivery truck blocks the landing zone. Stop and refer the change; do not swing the load over an occupied walkway. Changed case: the route is clear but an overhead cable has appeared in the work area. Treat it as an unresolved proximity hazard and involve the competent planner; do not estimate a safe electrical clearance from memory. The learner's job is to recognize mismatch and prevent an unauthorized workaround.

## Q87-03 — Establish load mass, center of gravity and stability

Use reliable load information: identification plate, drawing, verified inventory or competent calculation. Include contents, attachments and rigging in the appropriate load calculation. A label on an empty vessel does not include residual liquid. Do not use crane behavior to discover an unknown mass. Locate the center of gravity from reliable information and understand how it affects balance and leg loading.

For a simple static load supported at two points on one horizontal line, the support nearer the center of gravity carries more vertical share. If points are 2 metres apart and the center of gravity is 0.5 metre from the left point, a 1,000-unit weight gives left share 1,000 × 1.5/2 = 750 units and right share 250. This simplified equilibrium calculation excludes dynamic effects and does not approve lifting points. The hook arrangement and rigging must follow the competent plan.

Worked case: a skid is marked 900 kg but contains 120 kg of retained material and uses 80 kg of below-hook rigging. The total specified contribution is 1,100 kg; the planner must address the actual load and configuration. Changed case: one component is removed from one side. Both mass and center of gravity may change. Do not assume an earlier balanced lift proves the modified skid will remain level. A trial lift checks an already planned arrangement; it is not a substitute for knowing the load.

## Q87-04 — Select slings and understand angle effects

Select the actual sling for load, hitch, environment and configuration using its identification and manufacturer rating. Working load limit is not breaking strength. Do not calculate a new working limit by dividing an unknown strength by a guessed safety factor. Vertical, basket and choker arrangements can have different ratings and restrictions. A doubled-looking arrangement does not automatically double capacity. Sharp edges, bend radius, temperature, chemicals and contact pressure may require specific protection or a different accessory.

For a symmetric two-leg static teaching model, with angle theta measured from horizontal, tension in each leg is W divided by twice sine theta. At 60 degrees and W = 1,000 force units, each leg carries approximately 577.35; at 30 degrees it carries 1,000. Shallower angles increase tension. Real sling tags and approved geometry govern use; do not extrapolate outside permitted angles or assume all legs share equally in a multi-leg assembly.

Worked case: two identical legs each have a 600-unit permitted axial load in the fictional chart. A 1,000-unit symmetric load at 60 degrees is below that individual limit in this simplified check, but at 45 degrees each leg would be 707.11 and fails. Other components and the lift plan still need verification. Changed case: the center of gravity moves off-center. The equal-share formula no longer applies; obtain the correct leg shares and geometry rather than dividing total load by the number of legs.

## Q87-05 — Inspect accessories and isolate defects

Match each accessory's identity to its rating and examination status, then inspect its present condition using the actual manufacturer's removal criteria. Look for damaged webbing, broken wires, deformation, cracks, corrosion, heat or chemical damage, damaged threads and missing or illegible identification. Different sling types have different detailed rejection rules; this course does not invent a universal allowable number of broken wires or percentage of wear.

A current examination record does not make newly damaged equipment usable. Pre-use inspection and periodic competent examination serve different purposes. Quarantine suspected defective or unidentified equipment, tag it clearly and report through the organization's process. Do not return it to the rack where another crew might use it. Repair or proof testing requires authorized arrangements; a trainee must not hammer a hook back into shape or weld a chain link.

Worked case: sling S2 has a readable rating and current examination reference, but an edge cut is visible. Remove it from the task and seek the proper disposition. Changed case: S3 looks undamaged but its identity tag is missing. Do not borrow S1's certificate or invent a rating from its color. Obtain an identifiable suitable replacement. Inspection is a decision about the specific item in front of you, not about a similar photograph.

## Q87-06 — Attach and protect the load

Follow the planned connection points and accessory orientation. Check compatibility among hook, master link, shackle, sling and lifting point. Load must bear where the manufacturer intends; a hook latch retains the sling but is not the load-bearing seat. Avoid unintended side loading, point loading, twists, knots and improvised shortening. Do not substitute a convenient pipe or handrail for an engineered lifting point.

Keep hands out of pinch points while taking slack. Use the approved method to seat accessories without placing fingers between sling and load. Apply suitable edge protection so it remains in position as tension develops. A loose rag is not automatically adequate protection. Confirm the rigging cannot slip or release when the load changes orientation. Remove loose items or secure them through the plan before movement.

Worked case: the sling eye sits on the hook tip because the connecting link is too small. Stop and replace the incompatible arrangement; do not rely on the latch to force it into place. Changed case: the eye seats correctly but a sharp corner lies under the synthetic sling. Obtain the specified protection or revised rigging arrangement before tensioning. The correct sequence is planned connection, inspection, clearance and controlled take-up, not loading first and adjusting by hand under tension.

## Q87-07 — Communicate by agreed hand and voice signals

Agree the signal method, designated signaler, equipment identifier, operator perspective and stop response before movement. Keep signals visible and unambiguous. If communication is lost or a signal is unclear, stop safely and re-establish understanding. An emergency stop from any person must be taken seriously. Do not continue on a guessed instruction.

For the classroom's selected standard hand-signal set, stop uses one arm extended sideways with palm down moving side to side; emergency stop uses both arms similarly. Hoist uses the raised forearm and upward index finger making small circles; lower points the arm/finger down with small circles. Swing points horizontally in the intended boom direction. Slow movement adds the other hand in front of the action-signaling hand. Practice these without a suspended load and compare with the official chart. These six are a limited training set, not qualification for every crane function.

Voice commands identify the equipment where necessary and state function/direction, distance or speed, then the function stop command at completion. Example practice exchange: “Hoist up, slowly, 50 millimetres”; operator repeats the intended action; banksman gives “Hoist stop” at the planned point. Do not use “my left” from a different viewpoint. Changed application: two radios transmit conflicting movement commands. Stop, identify the designated signaler and resolve the channel/role problem before proceeding.

## Q87-08 — Control the exclusion area and personal position

Keep people clear of the load path, potential swing and trapping zones according to the plan. Barriers, signs and briefings must work in practice. A painted line without access control may not prevent someone walking through. Position yourself to see the relevant movement, remain visible or in reliable contact with the operator and retain an escape route. Do not stand between a moving load and a fixed structure.

Tag lines may be appropriate when specified, but can introduce entanglement or pull hazards. Do not wrap a line around a hand or body, stand in a bight or try to restrain a load beyond the planned method. If the load behaves unexpectedly, stop and lower safely under the plan rather than wrestling it into position. Nobody goes under the load to untwist a sling.

Worked case: a pedestrian enters the marked zone during take-up. Give stop and prevent further movement until the zone is controlled. Changed case: the pedestrian stays outside but the banksman's retreat route is blocked by stored material. Restore a safe position before continuing. The absence of someone directly underneath does not remove crush, swing and line-of-fire hazards. In the training fixture exercise, hands remain outside the marked footprint whenever the load is suspended.

## Q87-09 — Take slack, trial lift and respond to imbalance

Before taking slack, confirm attachments, people clear, communications, load free to move and the planned landing ready. Take tension gradually as instructed; observe seating, protection and unexpected snagging from a safe position. Do not use a sudden pull to free a stuck load. If it does not respond as expected, stop and investigate through the supervisor.

The controlled trial lift raises an already verified load only enough for the planned balance and security check. The plan defines the height and observations. Check whether the load hangs as expected, accessories remain seated and nothing shifts or snags. If it tilts or moves unexpectedly, stop and lower to a safe supported condition before adjustment. Never put hands into loaded rigging to correct it.

Worked case: during the L87 fixture trial, one end rises before the other and the protection moves. Stop, lower to the supports and refer the discrepancy. Do not continue because the total mass is small. Changed case: the load stays level but a loose tool slides on top. Lower and secure/remove the item using the approved method. A successful initial inch of travel does not prove the entire route or landing is safe; continue observing throughout the operation.

## Q87-10 — Travel, land and detach under control

Movement follows the agreed route and speed with ongoing attention to people, obstructions and changes. Avoid sudden starts, stops and side pulling. The operator controls the machine; the banksman communicates required movement and stop conditions, not improvised machine settings. If visibility changes, stop and use the planned handover or revised communication arrangement rather than walking into a hazardous position to keep seeing.

Before landing, confirm the destination, supports, stability and clearance for rigging removal. Lower under control until the load is fully supported and stable, then release tension and detach according to the plan. Do not pull a sling from beneath a load with the crane or reach under a partly supported item. Keep the hook and loose accessories controlled after detachment.

Worked case: the receiving area has only one of two required supports. Hold the lift in a safe condition under the supervisor's direction and correct the landing arrangement; do not improvise with an unstable stack. Changed case: both supports are present but the load rocks after contact. Do not detach until stability is established. For this competence assessment, planned travel and landing must be directly observed on suitable actual equipment under the site-specific L87 plan. A floor-supported rehearsal is preparation only and cannot substitute for the required movement evidence. No generic height, travel distance or load capacity is authorized by the course text.

## Q87-11 — Manage abnormal conditions and stop/restart

Weather, visibility, communications, equipment condition, ground conditions or the load itself can change. Use the limits in the actual lift plan and equipment instructions; there is no universal wind speed in this course. A large sail area may make a load difficult to control even when a generic weather number looks acceptable. Stop and refer rather than arguing that a previous lift succeeded.

For an abnormal event, signal stop, keep clear, inform the supervisor and follow the planned safe securing or lowering method. Do not leave a suspended load unattended or attempt an improvised repair while it is loaded. If an electrical contact or other major emergency is suspected, apply the site emergency plan and keep others clear; do not approach to rescue by touching equipment. This initial course does not qualify emergency crane recovery.

Worked case: radio contact disappears halfway through a simulated move. The operator stops safely; the team restores communications and confirms a clear instruction before restart. Changed case: communication returns but a damaged shackle is reported. The restored radio does not resolve the hardware defect. Each failed control must be addressed. Restart requires the designated authority and a confirmed safe condition, not simply silence after an alarm.

## Q87-12 — Authenticate experience and make a bounded award

Trace each claimed outcome to candidate-specific evidence. Record job date, plan version, equipment, load, geometry, candidate role, supervisor and the action actually observed. Check provenance through the approved employer channel and compare documents for consistency. A copied plan may be genuine while proving nothing about who performed the work. A photograph of a landed load does not establish safe signaling during travel.

This original program requires two distinct authenticated workplace jobs covering both roles and the assessment range, plus current direct observation of the candidate performing both roles through complete planned operations. At least one required observation occurs in the actual workplace. A suitably representative assessed facility task can supply the other current observation if its conditions genuinely cover the declared scope. These are VNDRLY criteria, not a substitute for OPITO's controlled logbook or entry requirements.

Worked case: job E1 proves the candidate inspected and slung a skid; job E2 proves they signaled a different planned load through restricted visibility with an agreed relay. Current observations confirm both roles independently. Map each criterion and check the remaining range before a bounded decision. Changed case: E2 is an unverified story and no direct banksman observation exists. Withhold the combined-role award, specify the missing evidence and arrange development/assessment. Do not convert a knowledge pass into a competence pass.

The final record states assessed scope, evidence and limitations. Personnel lifting, tandem lifts, specialized offshore dynamic operations and unassessed equipment remain excluded unless a separate competent assessment explicitly covers them. Employer appointment and jurisdiction-specific qualifications remain distinct. A candidate receives a partial evidence record when requirements are incomplete, not a misleading full certificate.

## Q87-13 — Manage unequal loading and configuration changes

A competent slinger notices when a convenient formula no longer describes the arrangement. Equal load sharing requires appropriate geometry and assumptions. Offset centers of gravity, unequal leg lengths, different pick elevations and rigid multi-leg assemblies can produce unequal tensions. Do not divide by four simply because four legs are attached. Use the approved lift design and manufacturer ratings; refer complex configurations rather than improvising a new design during attachment.

For an original static paper example, a 1,200-unit load has two supports 3 metres apart, with its center 1 metre from the left. Vertical shares are 800 left and 400 right. If each leg is at 60 degrees from horizontal, ideal tensions are 800/sin 60 = 923.76 left and 400/sin 60 = 461.88 right. A 900-unit left-leg allowance fails although the total load seems modest. These calculations are only a screening exercise; lateral forces, lifting-point suitability and full arrangement remain for the competent plan.

Changed example: moving the center to 1.5 metres makes the shares 600 each and ideal 60-degree tensions 692.82. That numerical improvement does not authorize moving contents or changing pick points without approval. The actual load may shift during travel, and an apparently balanced trial can conceal an insecure internal component. Verify the load is prepared as specified.

When a sling or connector is substituted, check the entire configuration, not only a larger number on the tag. A stronger component can still be geometrically incompatible or impose a harmful load direction. Stop, document the proposed substitution and obtain the required competent approval before using it.

## Q87-14 — Assess independent performance, remediate and review

Use observable criteria and the supplied evidence matrix to judge the candidate. Independent performance means the candidate makes the required choices and communicates the sequence without solution prompts; normal supervision and operator cooperation remain present. Neutral clarification of task wording is permitted. Telling the candidate which sling to reject or when to stop is coaching and must be recorded. A coached completion cannot support the failed criterion.

A competence result requires every mandatory criterion, specified range and authentic evidence. Written scores support underpinning knowledge but cannot compensate for a practical critical error. If the candidate loads defective gear, enters a prohibited zone, ignores an unclear command or fabricates evidence, stop and record the exact behavior. Give focused feedback, practice the gap and use a changed task for reassessment. Retain the first attempt rather than overwriting it.

Worked case: the candidate scores 90 percent on knowledge but needs a prompt to stop when a pedestrian enters. The practical boundary-control criterion is not independently met. Withhold the competence award, provide targeted training and observe a changed incursion scenario safely. Changed case: the assessor misrecords a stop that was actually given and the candidate supplies a corroborating observation. Review the evidence and correct the record transparently; provide an independent appeal route without retaliation.

Before issue, another competent reviewer checks scope, critical criteria, evidence authenticity and unresolved gaps. This is not a requirement for another software deployment. It is a review of the learner's actual assessment record at delivery. Keep blank forms blank until the learner performs. Materials completion means the course and assessment resources exist; it does not assert that any person has passed them.

# VUC087 competence workbook and assessor guide

## Entry, scope and preparation

Candidate presents relevant initial training or an assessed equivalent learning record and authenticated supervised experience. Assessor verifies actual task participation and physical readiness for the planned activities through the delivery organization's appropriate process. Missing evidence leads to a development plan, not a fictional pass. This original VNDRLY route does not assert satisfaction of OPITO's controlled entry/logbook rules. If an external OPITO award is sought, its approved centre must apply the actual current scheme requirements separately.

Before assessment, record equipment family, load types, operating environment, signal methods and excluded work. Competent assessor and authorized operator must understand the actual lift plans. The assessor must be qualified for the technical scope and able to judge independently. A production supervisor's desire to fill a shift cannot override evidence. Candidate may complete refresher chapters and coached practice first; controlled attempts begin only after the boundary between practice and assessment is stated.

Resources: approved lifting appliance and site, actual manuals and rated accessories, identified loads with verified lifting points/CG, competent plans L87-A/B, examination/inspection records, barriers, appropriate communications, supports, PPE, actual emergency arrangements, forms, official signal chart and segregated defect examples. Do not use damaged examples in an actual lift. No equipment rating or site clearance is inferred from this workbook.

## Required evidence range — original VNDRLY criteria

Two distinct authenticated workplace jobs, E1 and E2, must establish relevant supervised development and cover both slinger and banksman roles. Each has plan/reference, date, equipment, load, candidate actions, supervisor identity and verification. They may be prior jobs; neither may be invented for this course. Current direct assessment requires two complete planned operations A and B with a meaningful difference in load/configuration or route/visibility. The candidate performs both roles across those operations, with role changes only after the load is safely supported and the handover agreed. At least one current direct observation is in the actual workplace. The other may be a representative approved training facility operation that genuinely covers the declared scope.

Required range: load/CG verification; plan-based selection; inspection/rejection; supported attachment/protection; agreed hand and voice signaling; one planned restricted-view communication relay or handover; exclusion/retreat control; take-up/trial check; controlled movement; stable landing/detachment; change/stop response; accurate records. Restricted-view practice must use a competent plan and reliable agreed communication, not an improvised blind lift. If the site scope does not permit a required activity, arrange an approved representative assessment rather than pretend it occurred. No award until all required range is evidenced.

Excluded: personnel lifts, tandem/multiple-crane lifts, unplanned load rotation, emergency recovery, live electrical proximity, dynamic offshore lifts and equipment/functions not assessed. Additional scope requires its own competent plan, development and assessment. The certificate lists actual assessed scope; it is not unlimited lifting authority.

A3: symmetric two-leg W 1,000 at 60 degrees from horizontal → 577.35 axial units per leg; 45 degrees → 707.11; 30 degrees → 1,000. Fictional leg limit 600 fails the latter two. B: W 800 at 45 degrees → 565.69, failing fictional limit 550; at 60 degrees → 461.88, but changing geometry still requires competent planning. No actual lift uses these fictional ratings.

## G87 condition and substitution cards

Readable tag/current report with edge cut → remove from task. Missing tag despite good appearance → do not infer rating. Deformed connector/thread damage → quarantine, no improvised repair. Correct strength but wrong hitch or incompatible geometry → not suitable until the competent approved configuration is established. Loose protection or hook-tip seating → correct only while supported/unloaded. A substituted component can change load direction and fit; approval must cover the whole arrangement, not just a nominal number.

## S87 communication and Z87 change cues

Review the official selected hand signals at https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926SubpartCCAppA . Demonstrate stop, emergency stop, hoist, lower, swing and move slowly twice in different random orders, without load movement. These are the common refresher set. Add and assess every additional signal function actually needed by L87-A/B before those operations. A missing required signal prevents that task; the six selected signals do not certify all crane functions.

Agree voice commands and operator perspective before each operation. Identify the appliance when needed; use function/direction, distance/speed and function stop. Plan relay responsibility so only the agreed signal source directs movement at a time. Handover occurs in a safe stopped state and is acknowledged by operator, outgoing and incoming signaler. Loss/conflict/ambiguity means safe stop and re-established understanding.

Z1: planned landing blocked. Z2: pedestrian approaches boundary. Z3: retreat route obstructed. Z4: new overhead service on revised drawing. Z5: weather/visibility beyond actual plan. Z6: missing support. Z7: radio loss announced during a safe controlled phase. Z8: reported damaged hardware after communications restored. Each requires the appropriate stop/hold and competent resolution; fixing one does not fix the others. Do not physically introduce any danger to create the cue.

## C87 mandatory practical criteria and decision rule

C1 verifies task/scope/role and actual plan. C2 verifies load/CG and configuration basis. C3 selects identified suitable rigging. C4 inspects and rejects defects. C5 attaches/protects correctly while supported. C6 establishes zone/position and maintains it. C7 communicates clear agreed signals and handovers. C8 performs controlled take-up/trial observation. C9 controls planned movement and responds to change. C10 lands stably and detaches only when safe. C11 records authentic evidence and limits. C12 demonstrates both roles and all required range independently.

Every C criterion must be MET with direct evidence where applicable and all required range complete. C3–C10 and truthful C11 are critical: known unsafe action or fabrication prevents award. A criterion may be NOT MET or NOT OBSERVED; neither is silently converted to MET. Score each station for feedback, but no percentage can replace all-criterion competence. Assessor prompts that supply a critical decision invalidate independent evidence for that outcome.

## I87 integrated competence assessment

Conduct L87-A/B as defined, with candidate performing both roles and all required range, one direct workplace observation, authenticated E1/E2 and individual oral explanation of U87. Twelve dimensions correspond to C1–C12, each 0–4. Require at least 39/48, every dimension at least 3, every mandatory criterion MET, all range and no critical error. Quantitative threshold is an additional quality check, not a substitute for complete competence. Assessor stops real danger immediately. A failed attempt is retained and followed by targeted development and a changed task, not coached continuation labeled independent.

## Delivery and credential rules

Planned 40 hours: refresher instruction 8; development labs 10; individual stations 10; integrated operations/evidence review 8; controlled final 2; feedback/review 2. Workplace experience accumulation is additional and depends on genuine opportunities, not a fictional fixed number of hours. Breaks and necessary remedial training are additional.

Controlled final fourteen cases, three decisions each, 84 points, 120 minutes. Pass 68/84, at least 4/6 each domain and no critical error. Coursebook/calculator allowed; keys, collaboration and generative assistance prohibited. Changed Form B after remediation. Practical failure requires fresh practical evidence; written retake alone is insufficient.

Issue “VNDRLY/U Banksman and Slinger — assessed competence in recorded scope” only after entry/authenticity, corrected formative checks, fourteen stations, I87, all C87/range and final pass with independent competent review. Record scope, equipment, roles, dates, evidence, assessor and program/hash. If workplace evidence is absent, issue only an accurate incomplete/development record, not a competence certificate. No OPITO award or automatic employer appointment is conferred. Review when work/equipment/ability changes and follow applicable employer or external renewal rules; do not invent universal validity from this original package.

## Delivery and claim boundaries

Paper masses/ratings are never used for a real lift. Actual facility plan fields must be completed from identified suitable equipment before supervised practical delivery. Tandem, personnel and dynamic offshore lifts remain outside the defined scope. Materials completion, learner achievement, workplace competence and publication remain separate states.
