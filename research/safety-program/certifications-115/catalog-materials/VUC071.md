# VUC071 — Foundation and Anchor/Micropile Drill Rig Operation

## Purpose and boundaries

This original VNDRLY/U course teaches safe preparation, controlled operation and accurate handover for foundation drill rigs and anchor/micropile rigs. These are separate operating scopes. A combined award requires observed performance on both; passing one produces only that named scope. This is not CCO certification, geotechnical design approval, shaft-entry authorization, crane certification or oil/gas well-control qualification.

Use actual manufacturer manuals, capacity/stability information, approved tooling and the engineered site/work plan. F71 numbers are fictional paper data for learning, never actual rig ratings, working-platform design or foundation acceptance criteria. The instructor validates actual equipment and safe training conditions before operating exercises. The learner must identify missing evidence and stop rather than invent it. Complete each chapter, its explained checks and its changed application before independent assessment.

## Q01 — Rig types, scope and responsibilities

A foundation rig commonly uses a mast, rotary drive, Kelly bar and interchangeable drilling tools to construct larger shafts. Anchor/micropile rigs commonly manipulate sectional drill steel/casing and work vertically or at specified inclinations. Equipment varies widely: the same control label or attachment appearance does not establish the same function, limit or safe sequence. An operator trained on one arrangement must receive appropriate familiarization and assessment before another.

Separate the operator's task from the designer's and competent site personnel's responsibilities. The operator follows the approved location, geometry, support, installation and reporting plan; they do not authorize a foundation change because a hole is difficult to drill. Establish who controls site access, validates the working platform, locates utilities, selects rigging, directs signals, receives quality records and approves changes. Stop authority applies when conditions are unsafe or required information is missing.

Worked: an experienced foundation-rig operator is assigned an unfamiliar inclined micropile rig with a different clamp/breakout system. General drilling experience is useful but is not evidence of safe operation of those controls. Obtain actual instruction and observed competence. Changed: the same operator returns to their familiar rig, but the task now includes a personnel platform. Familiarity with drilling does not authorize that additional activity.

## Q02 — Site assessment and the area of influence

Walk and assess the site before mounting the rig. Identify nearby structures and foundation depths, overhead obstructions, buried installations, slopes, access restrictions, other trades, traffic and public routes. The area of influence includes mast movement, counterweight/tail swing, tool and pipe handling, spoil discharge, open shafts, delivery equipment and potential falling objects. A fence around the carrier alone does not protect this entire area.

Translate findings into a job hazard analysis and briefing with actual controls and named responsibilities. Mark controlled access and safe operator/crew positions; keep emergency access available. Reassess when work advances, weather changes or another contractor enters. A supervisor's schedule does not remove an unresolved risk. Document the specific condition and required resolution so a relief operator does not mistake a stop for ordinary delay.

Worked: the rig itself clears an existing wall, but its mast-raising envelope conflicts with a scaffold. Hold assembly until the complete movement envelope is assessed and protected. Changed: the scaffold is removed but spoil trucks now cross the pedestrian route. The site plan must address that new interaction before production resumes.

## Q03 — Working platforms and stability

Obtain a suitable working-platform assessment and preparation for the actual rig's reactions, geometry and operating states. Tramming, mast erection, drilling, extraction and lifting can impose different demands. Average machine weight divided by total track area cannot establish the worst edge pressure or stability. The platform and underlying ground, not just a visibly compacted surface, must support the actual work.

Confirm boundaries, slopes, drainage, buried voids, excavations and permitted travel paths. Use actual track/outrigger configuration and level limits. Platform maintenance matters: water, rutting, displaced material or an adjacent excavation can invalidate yesterday's condition. Stop if settlement or cracking develops; do not improvise counterweight movement or track repositioning under an unstable mast. Obtain a qualified recovery and support review.

F71 worked paper comparison: supplied reaction 600 kN and allowable pressure 200 kPa give an ideal minimum effective area of 3.0 m². A supplied effective area 2.5 m² gives 240 kPa and fails. This simple calculation is not a platform design. Changed: effective area 4.0 m² gives 150 kPa, but an unknown buried vault still prevents acceptance without appropriate assessment.

## Q04 — Utilities, existing foundations and shaft protection

Establish current utility-location information and the approved drilling point through the applicable owner/locate process. Reconcile markings, records and site evidence; establish exact location by required safe means as work approaches an estimated installation. No markings do not prove no service. Angled drilling needs consideration of the full subsurface trajectory, not only the entry point. Existing foundations can be undermined or struck even when no utility is present.

Protect the open shaft or hole using the applicable engineered/site arrangement. A loose sheet or warning tape is not automatically a suitable cover or guard. Keep spoil and equipment from threatening the edge, and maintain protection during shift changes and tool exchange. This course prohibits entry into drilled shafts; entry, atmospheric hazards and rescue require separately authorized procedures and qualifications.

Worked: a clear surface entry point is approved, but an inclined anchor trajectory passes under an undocumented utility corridor. Hold until the trajectory and required clearance are verified. Changed: utility information is complete but a nearby building's footing depth remains unknown. Resolve that separate structural interaction before drilling.

## Q05 — Rig identity, manuals and inspection

Identify the carrier, mast, rotary head, Kelly/steel system, winches, ropes/sheaves, clamps, hydraulics, electrical system, guards, alarms, emergency controls and installed tooling. Match the actual manual and chart to configuration. Inspect required structure, pins, connections, fluids, hoses, rope, controls, aids and attachments at the specified intervals. Shift inspection cannot replace required periodic or post-repair examinations.

Use safe access and three points of contact; do not jump from tracks or climb a mast without the appropriate system and authorization. Inspect accessible items without entering pinch or suspended-component zones. Report defects precisely: location, symptom, time, operating state and restriction. Do not label a rig ready when a structural crack, missing retainer, unintended movement or required control fault remains unresolved. An alarm disappearing after restart is not a repair record.

Worked: inspection finds damaged mast retention hardware and normal fluid levels. The normal fluid check does not offset the mechanical defect. Hold affected operation and obtain qualified correction/release. Changed: hardware is replaced, but required post-repair verification is absent. Record the unresolved release rather than assume replacement proves readiness.

## Q06 — Controls, safety functions and communication

Before motion identify each actual control and direction: travel, swing, mast tilt/raise, rotary/head movement, crowd, pullback, winches, clamps and auxiliary supply as applicable. Know the quickest safe emergency stop and the effect of stopping on supported loads and stored energy. Familiarization occurs in a validated clear area with no unauthorized person in the working envelope. Do not discover a control by operating it near a worker.

Agree standard hand/voice signals, equipment identity and directing responsibility. The operator stops for unclear/conflicting instructions or loss of required communication and obeys emergency stop from anyone. Interpret actual warning pictograms and keep them legible. Distinguish a warning aid from a required safety function; only an applicable authorized alternative can address a failure where permitted. Never bypass a guard/interlock to keep production moving.

Worked: the signaler means retract the rotary head, but the operator interprets the gesture as reverse travel. Stop and clarify before movement; coordinate terminology using the actual machine. Changed: signals are understood but the emergency-stop check fails. Good communication does not substitute for the missing safety function.

## Q07 — Transport, unloading and tramming

Loading/unloading needs a suitable transporter, ramps, restraint plan, route and support. Check the rig's actual mass, center of gravity, dimensions and permitted travel configuration. Establish a clear controlled area and reliable directing arrangement. Do not assume the machine can travel with the mast raised because it drilled safely in that position. Use actual slope, cross-slope, speed, mast and attachment restrictions.

During site travel consider track edge support, trenches, soft patches, overhead clearance and turning sweep. Secure loose tooling and keep people clear of tracks and blind spots. Stop if the travel surface deteriorates or the intended path leaves the validated platform. Carrier malfunction requires a controlled safe response and qualified recovery, not a helper pushing or guiding by hand beside moving tracks.

Worked F71: a fictional travel instruction permits 5 degrees maximum longitudinal slope in the specified configuration; the proposed route is 7 degrees. It exceeds the supplied limit by 2 degrees and cannot be approved from this plan. Changed: a 4-degree route is proposed but cross-slope is unknown. The passing longitudinal value does not settle the missing lateral condition.

## Q08 — Assembly, mast erection and setup

Follow the actual assembly/disassembly sequence with designated competent personnel, correct supports and inspected lifting equipment. Identify pinch zones, temporary stability states and stored energy before removing pins or restraints. Verify overhead and side clearance through the entire mast movement. Never work beneath an unsupported mast or rely on hydraulic pressure alone where mechanical support/isolation is required.

Place and level the rig within the approved platform and manufacturer configuration. Establish the shaft center and planned mast alignment with suitable measurements. Fit attachments through the approved process; verify retention and compatibility before function checks. Recheck the controlled access zone after deployment because the working envelope changes. Record setup checks and obtain any required independent confirmation before drilling.

Worked: mast erection is ready but an overhead cable lies within the sweep. Stop and establish the required electrical clearance/protection plan before raising. Changed: the cable issue is resolved but one assembly pin is unverified. All critical setup conditions must be satisfied; one resolved issue does not clear the rest.

## Q09 — Capacity, stability charts and load arithmetic

Read actual charts and notes for the equipment state, radius, mast angle, track/outrigger configuration, attachment and lifting point. Geometry diagrams alone do not establish capacity. Include tools, retained soil, pipe, rigging and required deductions; lifting capacity is not automatically permissible extraction force. The lowest applicable limit governs, including line, tool connection and attachment limits.

F71 fictional auxiliary-lift chart at 2/4/6 m radius is 4, 000/2, 800/1, 800 kg gross. Use the next larger radius for intermediate values and no extrapolation. All supplied suspended components count. At 5 m, pipe 1, 200 kg plus lifting head 200 and rigging 100 total 1, 500, leaving 300 below 1, 800. If retained material adds 400, total becomes 1, 900 and fails by 100. These classroom numbers do not authorize any actual lift.

Check the complete travel/lift path and actual machine permission for suspended-load handling. A favorable pickup value is insufficient if radius or configuration worsens later. Do not assume the rig is a general-purpose crane or that its pullback number can be used as its hoist rating. Changed application: move the same 1, 500 kg total beyond 6 m. No chart value is supplied, so stop rather than extrapolate.

## Q10 — Rigging, ropes, sheaves and mechanical handling

Verify actual load weight and center of gravity, approved attachment/lifting points and rated compatible rigging. Inspect running rope, slings, hooks, swivels and sheaves against their own instructions and removal criteria. An auxiliary-line rating cannot make an unidentified hook or attachment safe. Rope damage, poor spooling, incompatible sheaves or incorrect terminations require appropriate correction before operation.

Prefer suitable approved mechanized handling of heavy rods, casing and tools to keep hands away from rotation and pinch points. The handler must match the item, configuration and environment; a magnet or clamp is not universally suitable. Support pipe against rolling and plan laydown. Do not use fingers to align a suspended joint or place a person beneath it. Any manual handling must be specifically assessed rather than prescribed from a generic two-person rule.

Worked F71: ideal symmetric two-leg rigging carries 2, 400 kg at 30 degrees above horizontal. Each leg sees 2, 400 kg-force equivalent, not 1, 200. Changed: one sling is shorter and the load center is offset. The symmetric formula no longer establishes actual shares; obtain an appropriate rigging assessment.

## Q11 — Tooling, rotary, Kelly and connection integrity

Select approved tools for the machine, material and work method: augers, buckets, core barrels, drilling steel, casing, drifters and adaptors have different handling and connection requirements. Inspect wear, cracks, retention and compatible connection dimensions. Confirm installation with the prescribed pins/locks and verification, not visual resemblance. A larger tool changes weight, torque demand and spoil behavior.

Kelly systems require correct engagement and control of sections under the actual instructions. Unintended release, jammed sections or abnormal movement is a stop-and-review condition. Do not shake a stuck Kelly or tool free through uncontrolled swing/impact. Keep personnel outside the falling-tool and rotary envelope. Tool cleaning and inspection require safe support and isolation; never reach into a bucket or auger that can rotate or fall.

Worked: a replacement Kelly pin fits the hole but has no approved specification. Fit alone does not establish capacity or retention suitability. Hold until the correct part is verified. Changed: the correct pin is installed but the locking device is missing. A correct primary part does not eliminate the need for its required retention.

## Q12 — Foundation drilling sequence and spoil control

Confirm approved shaft identity, location, geometry, tooling and ground-support method. Position accurately and verify alignment before penetration. Use the prescribed rotation, crowd and hoist sequence, monitoring response and limits. Avoid excessive tool loading, impact against casing/mast or fouling the line. Keep personnel clear of rotating equipment, suspended spoil and the discharge area.

Withdraw and empty the tool under the approved controlled method; do not spin it where material can strike people or equipment. Track depth and strata using appropriate instruments and records, not number of cycles alone. Stop for unexpected obstruction, groundwater, instability or loss of returns as required by the plan. Clean/secure the tool and protect the shaft during breaks. Actual hole quality and foundation acceptance belong to the approved inspection/design process.

Worked F71 paper exercise: declared training depth 1.20 m with ±0.05 m tolerance gives 1.15–1.25 m. Safe measurement 1.12 m is 0.03 below the lower bound. Correction must remain within the validated training plan, not increased force against a bind. Changed: depth is 1.18 m but the hole wall is collapsing. Passing depth does not establish a safe or acceptable shaft.

## Q13 — Casing, groundwater and changing formation

Ground behavior can change rapidly with water, loose layers, obstructions or adjacent activity. The approved method may require casing, fluid support or another engineered approach. Operators observe and report behavior and follow authorized instructions; they do not design a substitute support method from memory. Maintain platform and shaft protection while seeking disposition.

Casing installation/extraction involves suspended weight, friction and potentially large breakout forces. Use the actual equipment's permitted systems, limits and qualified plan. A free casing weight does not include unknown resistance. Do not discover extraction force by pulling until something moves. Monitor alignment, connection integrity and surrounding ground; protect people from sudden release and trapped points. Fluid/spoil management must prevent uncontrolled discharge and slippery access.

Worked: a hole begins taking water and sloughing while the planned dry method assumed stable ground. Stop and obtain the responsible technical review rather than drill faster to finish. Changed: temporary casing is approved, but the operator is asked to extract it with an auxiliary winch not rated for that task. The revised method still requires suitable authorized equipment.

## Q14 — Anchor/micropile positioning and inclined geometry

An anchor/micropile rig requires verified travel configuration, supported setup and alignment for the actual entry point and trajectory. Establish whether the specified angle is measured from horizontal or vertical. Confusing those datums changes the subsurface route. Verify head travel, clamp positions, rod length, obstructions and required alignment before introducing steel.

Use appropriate measurement equipment and the actual plan's tolerances. A display must be matched to its datum, units and calibration/reference. Keep the rig stable as mast orientation changes. Protect the full drill-steel envelope and ensure the path is cleared underground. Neither correct angle nor depth proves anchor holding capacity or micropile design acceptance.

F71 worked geometry: a straight 6 m path at 30 degrees below horizontal has vertical component 3.0 m and horizontal component approximately 5.196 m. This is geometry only, not a permitted utility-clearance calculation. Changed: 30 degrees is actually specified from vertical; components interchange. Resolve the datum before setup rather than choosing the more convenient trajectory.

## Q15 — Drill steel joints, clamps and breakout

Control sections with the approved handler, supports, clamps and breakout system. Inspect threads, couplers and steel for damage and compatibility. Align without forcing cross-threaded engagement. Keep hands and loose clothing away from rotation and clamp jaws; do not manually thread steel while the head rotates. Do not leave an improvised wrench on rotating steel or use body weight to overcome a joint.

Understand which component holds the string at each step and verify holding before releasing another support. Follow the machine-specific make/break sequence and energy-control requirements. A stopped head is not necessarily isolated. On a jam, unusual movement or uncertain holding, stop and obtain the approved recovery method. Maintain control during vertical and inclined placement; gravity acts differently when supports are repositioned.

Worked: a clamp is opened before the string's alternate support is confirmed. The string can drop or slide. Stop and restore the approved holding arrangement. Changed: alternate support is confirmed, but a helper puts a hand between the rod and mast to align it. Resolve the pinch exposure using the approved handling/alignment method before motion.

## Q16 — Air, water, grout and pressure systems

Identify actual pressure ratings, compatible couplings, restraints, relief devices, isolation and depressurization points. The lowest component rating limits the system; a high pump rating does not authorize a lower-rated hose. Secure and inspect lines and protect their routes from damage. Never loosen a connection to check whether pressure remains or look for a pinhole leak with a hand. Follow qualified procedures for blockage and stored-energy release.

Manage returns and grout under the approved containment plan. Cementitious materials can harm skin and eyes; use suitable PPE, washing facilities and the actual SDS/emergency response. Promptly remove exposure and seek appropriate medical help for injury or eye contact. Do not improvise chemical neutralizers from an informal toolbox suggestion. Respiratory protection must follow the applicable exposure assessment/program, not a blanket dust-mask assumption.

Worked F71: pump limit 200 bar, hose 150 and fitting 120 give a supplied system ceiling no higher than 120 bar, before other restrictions. A proposed 140 bar exceeds it by 20. Changed: the fitting is upgraded to 180 but the hose remains 150. The new comparison ceiling is 150, not 180 or 200.

## Q17 — Dust, noise, weather and the work environment

Assess dust from drilling, spoil and cement handling. Where construction silica requirements apply, implement the appropriate complete control method or assessed alternative; a partly working shroud or absent water flow cannot be treated as fully implemented control. Maintain the specified cab, ventilation or suppression system and housekeeping. Required respirators need the appropriate program, training and fit/medical provisions; this course does not authorize selection by guesswork.

Use actual controls for noise, visibility, lighting, wind, temperature and fatigue. Stop when conditions prevent safe control even if one numerical limit passes. Keep access clear of slurry and hoses and use appropriate hearing/eye protection. Hidden fabric, discarded rope or loose material near an auger can entangle and pull a person toward it; clear the work zone through a safe isolated process.

Worked: drilling begins with a broken dust-control hose and a worker says the wind will carry dust away. Stop and restore the required exposure controls; wind direction is not a substitute. Changed: dust control is restored but poor lighting hides a clamp zone. Resolve the visibility hazard before continuing.

## Q18 — Emergencies, isolation and maintenance

Brief responses for power contact, instability, fire, flooding, control/travel failure, a fall into a shaft, pressure release and injury. Stop and protect people using the actual emergency plan. Do not rush into a potentially energized zone or shaft to attempt an unplanned rescue. Emergency stop does not guarantee release of hydraulic, pneumatic, gravity or rotational stored energy.

Service work requires authorized isolation, verification and support of components. Operators perform only the maintenance allowed by their competence and actual instructions; qualified personnel handle critical repair/adjustment. Record defects, actions and separate return-to-service verification. Preserve restrictions through handover. Do not erase an incident because the machine eventually functioned normally.

Worked: a hydraulic hose fails and a suspended component remains held by residual pressure. Keep people clear and obtain the approved supported recovery/isolation procedure; do not disconnect more fittings. Changed: the component is safely supported but a worker reports a tiny puncture from the jet. Activate immediate professional medical response for suspected injection injury.

## Q19 — Shutdown, securing and records

For a break, end of shift or transport, follow the appropriate actual sequence. Land/support tools, secure strings and open shafts, set controls and brakes, isolate/secure access as required and stow the mast/attachments only in the permitted configuration. End-of-day security is different from a brief operational pause. Do not leave an unsupported rod or unsecured hole for the next crew.

Record shaft/anchor identity, actual location/alignment/depth, equipment/tool configuration, relevant strata/groundwater observations, interruptions, defects and unresolved technical decisions. Distinguish measured facts from estimates. Foundation acceptance is made by the authorized inspection/design process; the operator's log supports it but does not replace it. Handover requires the receiving party to understand remaining restrictions.

Worked: depth was estimated because the instrument failed, but the outgoing report labels it measured. Correct the record and require valid verification. Changed: depth is verified, but the shaft protection is removed for a delivery. Protect the opening immediately through the approved arrangement; a complete log does not guard a physical hazard.

## Q20 — Independent integrated decisions

Combine scope, site, platform, utilities, equipment, tooling, load limits, signals, drilling and securing as one coherent operation. A pass in arithmetic cannot cancel missing locate information, and a good inspection cannot authorize a changed tool or trajectory. Rebrief changes and record the actual basis for proceeding. Safe voluntary stops show judgment; hiding uncertainty does not.

F71 integrated paper case: pipe 1, 400 kg plus handling equipment 250 at 5 m totals 1, 650 against the fictional 1, 800 chart, margin 150. An unverified platform extension still holds the move. Changed: extension is validated but retained material adds 200; total becomes 1, 850, failing by 50. Replan before motion rather than relying on the previous calculation.

Actual integrated assessment has two separately scored routes: foundation rig inspection/setup, pipe handling and controlled digging; anchor/micropile rig inspection/travel, steel joint and vertical/inclined positioning. A full combined operating award requires both routes. A knowledge-only or single-route record states its limits explicitly. Successful authored materials do not constitute actual operator qualification or a completed foundation.

# VUC071 — Delivery, applications and observed assessment

## Delivery preparation

Plan 88 hours for the combined program: 18 instruction/checks, 10 application labs, 32 supervised stations, 20 integrated practice/assessment, 4 controlled examination/review and 4 remediation/records. Extend practice when needed; record actual hours. A single-route course must explicitly identify the omitted operating route and cannot issue the combined award. Instructors and assessors require appropriate actual rig/task competence, with qualified site, lifting, geotechnical and utility personnel fulfilling their responsibilities.

Provide an inspected foundation rig and anchor/micropile rig with actual manuals/charts, approved tools/steel/casing/handlers, compatible inspected rigging, validated platform and travel paths, nonhazardous verified loads, safe cleared drilling medium, supported pipe/rod racks and alignment fixtures, measuring instruments, barriers, radios, emergency supplies and forms. Actual training drilling has predeclared safe location, geometry and maximum depth; nobody enters a shaft. Do not create live utility, collapsing ground, equipment failure or pressurized blockage to demonstrate a fault. Use photographs/tabletop cases for those conditions.

Validate machine configuration, ground reactions/platform, tool/rod retention, actual capacity and line limits, control function, clearances, utility/structure information, drilling plan and protection before delivery. Mechanical handling must be approved for the actual item and rig. Learners receive necessary data before assessment; essential operating limits cannot be hidden in the answer key.

Diagnostic: read a configuration chart, distinguish radius from tool length, interpret an angle datum, calculate weight/pressure, identify emergency stop and explain rotation/clamp exposure. Teach by explanation and a complete safe demonstration, then supervised practice with a changed case, then independent performance. Do not award from attendance or a trainer operating the controls for the learner.

## F71 paper data

Auxiliary lift gross ratings at 2/4/6 m are 4, 000/2, 800/1, 800 kg. Select next larger radius; no extrapolation; all listed suspended components count. Other limits are explicitly supplied in each case. These numbers are fictional, not actual manufacturer data. Trigonometric angles are from the stated datum, with calculations in degrees. Real rig work uses its actual validated limits.

A foundation-rig pass cannot fill an anchor-rig observation blank.

Unsafe rotation/clamp exposure, unsupported loads, ignored utility/platform uncertainty, capacity violation, bypass, unauthorized shaft entry or fabrication is critical. Stop actual unsafe work, record the first result, remediate and reassess with a changed case. ## Independent practical I71

Prepare teaching set T and assessment sets A/B separately. Qualified instructor/site personnel validate actual rig/manual/chart, tool and load IDs/weights, support/reactions, safe trajectories, approved handling fixtures and all capacity/line limits. Provide a known safe drilling medium with complete facility clearance, no shaft entry and protected holes. State actual drill location, diameter, depth, alignment tolerances and maximum safe limit in the learner packet before work. These are assessed exercise targets, not foundation design prescriptions.

Use generous capacity margins, lightweight boundary markers, supported laydown racks and adequate clearance for the actual rig/tool/load. No person is an obstacle or suspended-load guide by bodily force. Familiarization is separate from assessment. Schedule about four hours per route plus practice as required; safety and accurate performance, not speed, determine pass.

**Foundation route F71:** independently inspect and identify controls/emergency stop; review/brief platform, utilities and influence area; perform approved setup/alignment; verify actual capacity and handling arrangement; pick up a supported test pipe, place it at two approved target supports and lay it down under controlled support; position the approved drilling tool; complete two controlled drilling/withdrawal/spoil cycles within the validated target and safe maximum; safely measure and record results without entry; protect the hole and perform the appropriate shutdown/handover.

**Anchor/micropile route AMP71:** independently inspect/identify controls; verify platform and route; tram forward in approved configuration to the validated station; set up and align; handle steel using the approved system; make and break one vertical joint while maintaining confirmed support and protected hands; place steel into the validated vertical fixture and the separate inclined fixture at the declared datum/tolerance; safely secure steel and mast; reverse-tram along the approved path; shut down and hand over. Actual pressurized grouting, anchor proof testing and production foundation acceptance are outside this operator exercise.

Pass criteria for both: correct configuration and actual limits; effective signals and stop response; no person exposed to rotation, clamp, fall or track hazards; no collision, uncontrolled swing, side pull or unplanned dropped/dragged item; all required targets/tolerances met and safely supported before release; drilling remains within approved location/geometry and maximum; opening protected; all restrictions and actual results recorded. Every listed element must pass. Safe voluntary pauses are permitted and recorded; assessor intervention to prevent danger fails independent performance.

Set B changes load/target path and safe drill point for F71, or steel length/fixture angle and approved travel route for AMP71, within equivalent validated conditions. Recalculate/rebrief. Weather/equipment interruption is recorded separately from learner error; restore conditions and repeat the complete affected task. A different machine requires fresh familiarization/configuration and a complete route attempt. Retain failed evidence and retest observations.

## Controlled assessment, remediation and credential rules

Form A and changed Form B each have twenty original cases, three scored decisions per case, 120 points. Allow 180 minutes with calculator/coursebook, no keys or assistance. Pass at least 96/120 and at least 4/6 in every domain with no unresolved critical error. These are original VNDRLY assessments, not external CCO core/specialty examinations. Protect keys outside the learner packet; provide original situation context with changed reassessment questions.

Require all corrected formative checks, ten labs, twenty stations, written final and the actual route(s) for the award. Combined scope requires both F71 and AMP71. If only one route is demonstrated, issue a precisely named single-route record; incomplete actual work supports knowledge-only status. Never claim CCO certification, engineering authority, shaft-entry competence, well-control competence or unassessed equipment categories.

Remediation names the error, reteaches the principle with a worked example, includes changed supervised practice and then independent changed examination/performance. Keep critical-failure and correction history. Accommodations may change access or communication but cannot remove essential safe operation. VNDRLY uses a one-year review reminder, not a claim about external certification duration; reassess sooner for material task/equipment change or unsafe performance.

## Reproducible record forms

**Enrollment:** learner ID, diagnostic findings, prior experience, requested F71/AMP71 scope, accommodations, instructor/assessor competence, actual hours and version.

**Delivery validation:** machine/manual/chart IDs, configuration/tool/handler/rigging IDs, actual loads and limits, platform basis/validator, locate/structure information, drilling/trajectory plan and tolerances, barriers, support fixtures, weather/exposure/emergency arrangements and date.

**Observed performance:** station/route/set, actual actions and instrument readings, five rubric scores, critical error/intervention, safe stop, result, assessor/date, remediation and changed retest. Separate columns for foundation and anchor/micropile rigs where both are required.

**Drilling/handover:** work ID, location/angle datum/depth and measurement method, tooling, strata/water observations, spoil/return containment, shaft protection, deviations and authorized disposition, defects, secure state, receiving acknowledgment.

**Credential decision:** checks/labs/stations/final/route evidence, unresolved failures, exact earned scope and exclusions, version/hash, reviewer/date and review triggers. Do not prefill actual results or real site approvals.

Can rain require a platform reassessment?
