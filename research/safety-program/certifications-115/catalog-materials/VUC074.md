# VUC074 — Crane Inspector

## Program definition

Original VNDRLY/U training in inspection planning, evidence collection, condition assessment, functional-test oversight and defensible reporting for articulating, mobile, overhead and tower cranes. This is an inspector curriculum, not another operator course. Its award records the crane families actually assessed. It does not confer CCO certification, professional-engineering authority, nondestructive-testing qualification or permission to operate every crane.

The learner will identify the inspection trigger and applicable basis, inspect accessible components systematically, measure against identified criteria, identify unverified areas, oversee safe permitted tests, classify findings and communicate restrictions and follow-up. Actual equipment inspection is required for each family claimed. A written scenario cannot establish access, measurement and examination skill by itself. An inspector must work within demonstrated competence and refer work beyond it.

Use the actual manufacturer documentation, governing requirements, service history and approved procedures. Do not substitute a familiar crane's limits, a generic checklist or this course's fictional values. Inspectors need enough operating knowledge to understand configurations and tests; only properly authorized operators perform crane movements. No deliberately defective crane is operated during training.

## Fictional packet C74

For paper exercises only: component H has original throat 52 mm and a supplied fictional rejection boundary above 54 mm; component P has nominal pin 40 mm and minimum permitted measured diameter 39.0 mm; plate S original 10 mm, minimum supplied acceptable thickness 8.5 mm. These are exercise-specific criteria, not ASME or manufacturer limits. Any crack indication, unknown criterion or unverified coverage is separately assessed; a dimensional pass is not a complete acceptance.

Fictional mobile chart M74: radius 4/6/8 m gives gross capacity 10000/7000/4500 kg in the stated full-support configuration. Select next listed radius at or above actual requirement; no interpolation/extrapolation. Deduct block 450 and rigging 150 kg. At radius 5.5 m, net payload 6400 kg. A6300 kg payload plus 600 deductions totals 6900, leaving 100 kg gross margin; the same payload at 7 m exceeds 4500 gross rating by 2400 kg. These figures are not practical-test instructions.

## Q01 — Inspection purpose and professional boundaries

Inspection produces a bounded statement about observed equipment against stated requirements at a particular time. It is not a guarantee that no hidden defect exists or that every future lift is safe. Start by identifying crane type, ownership, application, location, configuration and requested inspection. Clarify who will provide access, manuals, operator, maintenance information and authority to restrict use.

Separate observation from interpretation and disposition. “Linear indication at weld toe” is an observation; “requires qualified examination before release” is a disposition. Calling it a harmless paint line without support is neither. Independence means recording unfavorable facts even when a client wants a ready sticker. Declare conflicts and limitations instead of adjusting the result to suit scheduling pressure.

Worked case: a customer asks for an annual certificate from photographs taken by someone else. Photos may support a records review, but do not establish a complete actual inspection. Record the restricted review scope and missing access. Changed: the crane is available but the inspector has experience only with overhead equipment and it is a tower crane. Obtain appropriately qualified participation; a general certificate is not expertise in every family.

## Q02 — Applicable requirements and inspection triggers

Determine whether the equipment and work fall under construction crane rules, general-industry overhead/mobile rules or another applicable regime. Manufacturer requirements and recognized standards may add specific methods or intervals. Identify the actual edition and contractual basis; a bibliography is not proof that every provision was checked. Use a requirements matrix linking each required item to inspection method, evidence and result.

Scheduled inspections are not the only trigger. Assembly/erection, modification, repair affecting safe operation, severe service and return after inactivity may require additional examination or testing. The inspection needed after a boom repair is not necessarily satisfied by an existing monthly checklist. Assign competent/qualified personnel as required by the applicable trigger. Keep interval, scope and required documentation distinct.

Worked case: a current annual report predates a load-bearing repair. Review repair approval and the applicable post-repair inspection/testing before use; the annual date does not clear the repair. Changed: no repair occurred but an overload is reported. Treat the event as a new condition requiring the appropriate qualified assessment, rather than waiting for the next calendar inspection.

## Q03 — Identity, configuration and records

Reconcile serial numbers and configuration between the machine, chart, previous reports, maintenance records and engineering documents. Include boom/jib sections, counterweight, supports, reeving, attachments and any remount. For overhead equipment record bridge/hoist/trolley IDs and combined-capacity restrictions. For tower equipment record erected height, jib, base, ties and climbing configuration. A document for a similar unit is not transferable evidence.

Trace previous defects to actual corrective records and follow-up inspection. Review who performed work, the approved repair procedure, replacement material/component identity and required test results. Do not infer closure from an invoice reading “service completed.” Missing records can be a substantive limitation, especially when a modification changes load path or capacity.

Worked case: the chart permits a jib arrangement that is not installed, while the report lists another serial number. Hold configuration acceptance until the correct documents and actual assembly agree. Changed: identity is corrected but a repaired boom section has no approval trace. Record the remaining hold separately; correcting an administrative error does not validate a structural repair.

## Q04 — Safe access and energy control

Plan how every component will be reached without exposing inspectors to movement, stored energy, falls, electrical conductors or dropped objects. Identify adjacent cranes sharing a runway, suspended components, pressure accumulators, batteries, rotating machinery and access equipment. A stopped engine or released pendant does not prove isolation. Use the applicable energy-control procedure, verified safe state and approved supports before intrusive work.

Separate isolated examination from controlled functional tests. Reconfigure deliberately between them: account for people/tools, restore required guards, brief the operator and establish a protected test zone. Nobody remains inside a pinch point to watch a component operate. Qualified electrical work and elevated access require their separate competence and protection. If access cannot be made safe, record the unexamined area and arrange a supported method; do not mark it satisfactory.

Worked case: an overhead crane is isolated but another crane can enter the same runway section. Provide the required separation/control before access. Changed: runway separation is established but a suspended block has not been safely supported. Resolve that energy source too. Isolation is a system of controls, not a single padlock photograph.

## Q05 — Instruments, measurements and decision rules

Use instruments suited to the feature, range, resolution and environment. Check identification, calibration/status, damage and zero/reference response. Clean the relevant surfaces without altering the component, choose the specified measurement locations and record actual readings and units. Do not average a localized minimum thickness out of existence. Repeat unexpected measurements and investigate disagreement rather than selecting the most favorable reading.

State the acceptance rule before deciding. If uncertainty or access limitations prevent a reliable comparison, report indeterminate/hold and obtain suitable measurement, not a rounded pass. Course arithmetic uses fictional exact values; actual measurement uncertainty and applicable decision rules must be considered. Keep original observations when a later remeasurement changes the result.

Worked C74 pin: readings 39.2, 39.1 and 38.9 mm; the minimum is 38.9, below 39.0 by 0.1 mm. Average 39.07 does not erase the local failure. Changed: readings 39.02±0.05 mm under a stated rule requiring the entire interval at or above 39.0. Lower bound 38.97 fails that acceptance demonstration; obtain a more capable measurement or prescribed disposition.

## Q06 — Structural members, welds and corrosion

Follow the load path: supporting structure, connections, boom/jib/mast/bridge members and attachment points. Look for deformation, cracks, corrosion, damaged weld areas, missing material, impact evidence and unauthorized attachments. Use appropriate lighting, cleaning and viewing angles. Record location and extent with a recognizable reference and scale; a close-up without location is difficult to act on.

Visual examination has limits. Suspected cracking, inaccessible areas or required subsurface assessment must be referred to qualified NDT/engineering under the applicable procedure. Do not grind out an indication, add a weld or approve a weld based only on its smooth appearance. Thickness loss may require structural evaluation even when a single point exceeds a nominal exercise limit.

Worked C74 plate: 10 mm original, 8.7 mm minimum measured gives 13% loss and exceeds the supplied 8.5 minimum by 0.2. A crack-like indication still requires hold/referral. Changed minimum 8.3 gives 17% loss and fails by 0.2. A favorable nearby reading is not a basis to accept the thinned region.

## Q07 — Pins, bolts, bearings and mechanical connections

Inspect connection identity and retention, not just visible tightness. Pins, keepers, bolts, locking devices, bearings and joints transmit loads in specified ways. Look for missing retainers, deformation, fretting, movement, corrosion, wear and evidence of incorrect replacement. Verify grade, size, orientation and installation/torque procedures where required. Paint witness marks can reveal movement but are not proof of correct preload.

Do not indiscriminately retorque structural bolts during inspection. Some systems need particular sequences, tools, replacement practices or engineering direction. Measure clearances with the prescribed safe loading/isolation condition and compare with the exact criterion. Keep bearing play, bolt torque and structural alignment findings distinct; correcting one does not necessarily correct another.

Worked case: a mast bolt has an intact paint stripe but its identification does not match the approved assembly record. Resolve identity and installation basis rather than marking “tight.” Changed: identity is correct but a keeper is absent from an adjacent pin. The complete connection is not acceptable merely because the major pin remains in place.

## Q08 — Wire rope identity and examination

Determine rope construction, diameter, service, reeving and terminations before applying criteria. Running, standing and rotation-resistant ropes have different concerns. Inspect the required length and critical areas, including bends, drum crossovers and terminations, using safe access and movement procedures. Never run a hand along a moving rope. Document inaccessible length and required follow-up instead of treating visible portions as the entire rope.

Use the governing rope-specific criteria and manufacturer instructions for distortion, corrosion, heat/electrical damage, broken wires, diameter loss and end connections. Counting broken wires requires the correct length basis and rope classification. Do not transfer a single number between rope types or decide from broken-wire count alone. A rope with no counted breaks may still be unsafe because of crushing or core failure.

Worked paper measurement: nominal 20 mm, measured 18.8 gives 6% reduction. Compare with the applicable criterion and other condition evidence; do not approve from a generic tolerance. Changed measured 19.4 gives 3%, but a broken strand is shown. The smaller diameter loss does not override the strand defect. Record rope identity, location and specific observed condition.

## Q09 — Reeving, terminations, drums and sheaves

Trace the actual rope path against the approved reeving diagram. Check equalizers, dead ends, sockets/clips, drum anchorage, wraps, sheave alignment/grooves, guards and block orientation. Incorrect reeving can cause wear, unequal loading or unexpected capacity limits even when the rope itself is new. Inspect terminations for correct parts, installation and damage; do not invent clip count, torque or spacing.

A drum's layer affects line pull on many systems. Parts-of-line arithmetic is only a screening calculation; actual reeving efficiency, permissible line pull, block and other limits govern. Examine the travel endpoints using approved controlled methods so required rope retention and clearances remain. Do not deliberately run the hook into a limit or strip rope off the drum to demonstrate a defect.

Worked paper case: four parts with stated allowable line pull 22 kN give an ideal 88 kN before all other limits. A requested 94 kN fails even that ideal by 6. Changed request 82 passes the ideal comparison but the actual reeving diagram is wrong. Correct the reeving and verify actual approved capacity; multiplication does not certify the arrangement.

## Q10 — Hooks, blocks and load attachments

Identify each hook/block and its rating and inspect the complete assembly: body, throat, twist, wear, latch where applicable, swivel, nut/retention, bearings, sheaves and guards. Compare dimensions with the actual baseline and rejection criteria. A working latch does not repair a damaged hook; a block rating does not determine whole-crane capacity. Include installed below-the-hook devices in scope and deductions where required.

Record measurements at specified locations and any heat, chemical, impact or unauthorized repair evidence. Do not straighten or weld a hook under an inspection-only task. Refer repair/replacement decisions to the applicable authority and manufacturer procedure. Keep removed components identified and segregated so a failed item is not returned accidentally.

Worked C74 hook throat 53.5 mm versus boundary 54 is within that one fictional criterion; expansion from 52 is 2.88%. Changed throat 54.4 fails by 0.4 mm and expansion is 4.62%. These are exercise values, not universal hook limits. Either specimen also fails acceptance if a separate unacceptable crack or repair condition exists.

## Q11 — Fluid power and load-holding systems

Inspect hydraulic/pneumatic lines, fittings, cylinders, valves, pumps and motors for condition, routing, leaks, damage and abnormal performance using the actual procedure. Stored pressure and suspended loads remain hazards after stopping. Never loosen a fitting to see whether it is pressurized or use a hand to find a pinhole leak. Authorized specialists perform pressure tests and repairs with proper isolation and instruments.

Load holding needs controlled functional evidence where required, not only dry visual appearance. Identify the required test condition, measurement and permissible behavior from the manufacturer. Unexpected drift, noise, temperature or pressure response is a finding requiring evaluation. Do not adjust relief settings or block a load-holding valve as a diagnostic shortcut.

Worked fictional drift test permits no more than 4 mm over its specified observation period. Observed 6 mm exceeds by 2. Changed 3 mm meets that one test value, but hose reinforcement is exposed. Report the independent hose defect. A passing movement test does not establish the remaining life of damaged pressure components.

## Q12 — Electrical controls, aids and safety devices

Inspect controls and wiring for correct identity, condition, labeling, protection and function. Cab, pendant and radio controls may have different transfer/enable arrangements. Establish which station is active before testing and prevent unintended commands. Electrical enclosure inspection and energized testing require appropriate competence and procedures; a crane-inspector title alone does not confer electrical qualification.

Distinguish safety devices from operational aids and apply the specific defect rules. Do not assume every failed indicator may be replaced by a spotter, or that every alarm has the same legal consequence. Document any permitted temporary measure with its exact basis, conditions, responsible person and deadline; no generic waiver is issued. Test required functions using approved methods without defeating safeguards or deliberately overloading equipment.

Worked paper instrument comparison: true reference load 4000 kg, indication 4200 gives+5% error relative to reference. A fictional±3% criterion fails by 2 percentage points. Changed reading 4080 gives+2%, but an intermittent control remains unresolved. Instrument accuracy and control reliability are separate findings.

## Q13 — Mobile crane inspection route

Mobile inspection joins upper works, carrier and support configuration. Identify telescopic/lattice type, boom/jib assembly, counterweight, reeving, tires/tracks, outriggers, pads, slew system and travel/steering/brakes. Check structural and mechanical items along the entire load path, including inaccessible telescopic sections through the prescribed access/examination method. Do not assume a carrier road inspection covers crane systems.

Verify the chart and configuration data used by the operator and aids. Compare actual support extension/position, level and counterweight with the documented configuration. Inspect tire identity/load basis and condition where relevant. Remounting or structural changes need their proper engineering trail. Any required functional/load testing is planned under the appropriate approved procedure, not improvised during the walkaround.

Worked M74 radius 5.5 m: 7000 gross minus 450 block and 150 rigging leaves 6400 payload. A6000 payload gives 400 net margin. Changed radius 7.2 m selects 4500 gross, 3900 net; the same payload exceeds by 2100. An inspection cannot certify a load chart that describes a different support or boom state.

## Q14 — Articulating crane inspection route

Follow the articulated load path through base, mounting/subframe, slew system, column, main and outer booms, extensions, pins, cylinders and attachment. Folding creates pinch zones and can hide components. Plan safe positions and supports for examination. Inspect hose routing through joints and extension sections, stabilizers, locking systems and transport restraints. A compact folded appearance is not evidence of sound structure.

Review mounting and remount engineering, chassis compatibility and attachment/winch configurations. Check remote identity and emergency functions through approved procedures. The inspection report should specify which attachments and extensions were examined. A loader with no winch does not require pretending that rope-system inspection occurred; mark truly nonapplicable items with a reason, not a blank checkmark.

Worked case: the crane was moved onto another truck and its old inspection sticker retained. Inspect actual installation and obtain the remount documentation; previous base/chassis acceptance does not transfer. Changed: mounting records are complete but a hose is pinched during the prescribed fold check. Stop and report the routing defect; document both the verified record and failed function.

## Q15 — Overhead and gantry inspection route

Overhead inspection includes bridge, trolley/hoist, runway, end trucks, wheels, stops, buffers, rails, electrification, access and controls. Identify multiple hoists/trolleys and combined loading restrictions. A pair of individually rated hoists does not automatically double bridge capacity. Travel skew or wheel wear can indicate alignment or structural issues needing specialist assessment, not simply lubrication.

Coordinate isolation and test movements with all equipment sharing the runway. Examine hooks, ropes/chains, brakes, limit devices and controls under appropriate safe conditions. Verify capacity markings and permanently attached device effects. Required tests follow the actual applicable procedure; a regulatory maximum test percentage is not an instruction to load every crane to that percentage. Record the tested configuration and any unexamined areas.

Worked fictional bridge maximum 10 t with two 6 t hoists: proposed 6+6=12 t exceeds bridge maximum by 2. Changed 4+5=9 t passes only the total comparison; trolley spacing and local/combined restrictions still govern. This is not a load distribution design or permission for a tandem hoist operation.

## Q16 — Tower crane inspection route

Tower inspection connects the machine to its erected structure: foundation/base, mast sections, fasteners, ties/braces and host structure, climbing arrangement, slewing assembly, jib/counterjib, ballast/counterweights, hoists/trolley/luffing systems and access. Verify engineering documentation matches the actual erected height and tie arrangement. The inspector checks evidence and condition; the course does not qualify the inspector to redesign foundations or ties.

Plan component inspections before erection where later access becomes difficult. After erection, verify the required inspection and approved test evidence. Review actual wind, configuration and out-of-service arrangements against instructions. Do not loosen tower bolts or conduct torque checks without the required method and competent control. A climb or changed tie arrangement must not be hidden under an earlier report.

Worked fictional plan specifies ties at 18, 36 and 54 m; inspection finds 18 and 36 only with the crane erected to the planned final height. Record the missing required tie and hold for responsible evaluation. Changed: a revised signed design permits the observed arrangement but does not match the actual mast type. Resolve that mismatch; a signature is not enough without applicability.

## Q17 — Functional inspection and test control

A functional test asks whether specified functions behave as required under defined conditions. Prepare a sequence with authorized operator, safe envelope, communication, test loads if prescribed, weather/support requirements, expected results and stop criteria. Complete required static examination first. Do not operate a known unsafe component merely to make a failure visible. Account for personnel and tools before restoring power.

Observe and record actual response for each function, including braking/holding, travel, hoisting, slew/telescope where applicable, controls and protective features. “Operated normally” is insufficient if the required test was never performed. Unexpected response means stop and evaluate; do not repeatedly cycle a failing system to obtain one favorable result. Restore the crane to a safe state afterward.

Worked case: nine required functions are listed, eight observed and one inaccessible. Coverage 8/9=88.89%, but that percentage is not a pass criterion. Record the missing function and arrange completion. Changed: all nine are observed but one gives an intermittent fault. Full coverage does not mean full acceptance.

## Q18 — Load, proof and other tests

Clarify the test purpose: operational, rated-load, proof, stability, structural, line-pull, device or remount-related. They answer different questions. Obtain the applicable manufacturer/engineering procedure, exact configuration, verified load and instrumentation, support/clearance controls, personnel, sequence and acceptance limits. A percentage remembered from another crane is not a test plan. Never improvise an overload to prove that a machine “has reserve.”

Verify mass evidence includes all relevant suspended items and that the scale/calibration evidence is applicable. Apply the chart and procedure exactly, including test-specific provisions where authorized. Check for damage after testing. If the required controlled test cannot be performed safely, report it incomplete; paperwork or a normal production lift is not substituted without an explicit valid basis.

Worked fictional procedure expressly calls for 1.10 times a specified 8 t reference in an approved test configuration: 8.8 t total test mass. Verified weights 8.4 plus rigging 0.25 total 8.65, short 0.15 t. Changed weights 8.55 plus 0.25 equal 8.8. This arithmetic demonstrates mass accounting only; it is not a universal test percentage or permission to exceed a working chart.

## Q19 — Defect classification, repairs and release

For every finding, state the component/location, observation, criterion, significance, restriction and required action. Distinguish an immediate safety hazard, a condition requiring authorized monitoring, an unresolved information gap and a nonapplicable item. Do not downgrade a hazard to “monitor” for convenience. Monitoring needs an applicable basis, responsible party, method and interval, not merely a colored sticker.

Repair closure requires evidence that the correct work was completed by suitable personnel and any required reinspection/testing passed. A repair invoice, an NDT report and an engineering approval serve different purposes. Trace each to the actual component and revision. An inspector's report may support return-to-service decisions but must not claim authority beyond the applicable role. Preserve original defects and amendments.

Worked case: a cracked component has a work order marked complete but no approved repair or follow-up evidence. It remains unresolved. Changed: the repaired component is re-examined satisfactorily but a different defective safety device remains open. Close only the supported finding; do not release the entire machine by association.

## Q20 — Reporting, handover and continuing competence

A defensible report lets another competent person reconstruct what happened. Include equipment/configuration, scope/trigger, standards/manual revisions, date/location, personnel, instruments, access limitations, items/results, measurements/photos, tests, defects, restrictions and follow-up. Explain what was not inspected and why. Avoid phrases such as “all safe” when only a selected component was examined.

Communicate urgent restrictions immediately to responsible people; a report emailed later is not sufficient protection while the crane keeps working. Confirm receipt and the safe equipment state. Retain records for applicable periods and preserve corrections with an audit trail. Review competence when equipment, rules, methods or scope change. Honest limited scope is more useful than an unsupported broad certificate.

Worked case: a report says “tower inspected” but omits inaccessible ties. Correct the scope, record the missing work and restriction, and arrange safe access. Changed: another inspector completes tie examination. Add the traceable supplementary result rather than silently rewriting the original report. The combined evidence must still refer to the same actual crane configuration.

# VUC074 — Applied work, delivery and assessment

## Instructor preparation and sequence

Plan 96 hours: 24 instruction, 12 application labs, 36 supervised inspection practice, 20 independent integrated inspection and 4 final/review. Extend practice as needed. Instructor/evaluator competence must cover each actual crane family and the inspection methods observed. Operating, electrical, elevated-access, NDT and engineering functions require their separate qualified personnel. This curriculum does not fabricate the external certification experience prerequisite or waive employer qualification decisions.

Provide safe actual articulating, mobile, overhead and tower equipment or controlled actual crane assemblies with suitable access, current manuals/charts, applicable inspection/test procedures, inspection/repair records, calibrated instruments, safe sample components, photographs, known loads where appropriate, qualified operators and emergency arrangements. A component bench alone cannot demonstrate a whole-machine inspection. If access to a family is unavailable, award only the actually completed family scope and retain the other route as uncompleted learner work.

Validate every access/isolation/test plan. Protect from adjacent cranes, stored energy, drops, falls and electrical exposure. Do not introduce faults into operating equipment. Defective samples are isolated, marked and never installed for a test. Live operations use sound equipment and approved normal test conditions; hazardous symptoms and absent records are injected on paper during safe pauses. Required proof/stability/structural tests are not improvised for training. Their planning and evidence assessment can be simulated; execution authority is excluded unless separately qualified and observed under an approved actual procedure.

Diagnostic: crane-family identification, scope/trigger selection, measurement units, percentage change, gross/net load, uncertainty, observation versus conclusion and safe stop/isolation. Demonstrate a complete inspection-to-report sequence, practice changed examples and then assess independently. Do not let a learner memorize a checklist without explaining the criterion and disposition.

## Labs

### W01 — Scope and record reconciliation

Packet: old annual report, current crane serial/configuration sheet, safe-operation repair invoice and missing approved repair procedure. Worked response: annual date predates the repair; identify required post-repair evidence and restrict unsupported acceptance. Changed: serial matches but counterweight differs. Submit a requirements/evidence matrix and exact unresolved items. Actual portion traces identifiers on a safe machine to its documents.

### W02 — Access and instruments

Packet: shared overhead runway, isolated target crane, neighboring crane still able to enter, unsupported block and gauge status sheet. Worked response: establish whole access/energy plan, including neighboring travel and suspended energy; reject expired or unsuitable measurement equipment. Changed: all isolation is established but one component remains inaccessible. Submit limited-coverage record and safe access request. No person enters the unresolved area.

Learner locates, measures and photographs features, identifies missing keeper/incorrect bolt evidence and writes a referral without prescribing unauthorized weld repair. Actual work inspects sound equipment; no failed paper load is lifted.

### W08 — Overhead/tower systems

Tower packet specifies ties at 20/40/60 m, final tie absent and revised design for a different mast. Submit separate structural-document holds. Actual work traces safe accessible systems with validated access and competent support.

### W09 — Test plan and post-test condition

Fictional approved paper procedure specifies 1.05×12 t=12.6 t total test load. Weights 12.1 plus rigging 0.35=12.45, short 0.15. Changed weights 12.25 plus 0.35=12.6. Learner reviews calibrated mass evidence, procedure/configuration, safe zone, sequence, acceptance and post-test examination. The calculation does not authorize a real overload. Actual ordinary functional test observation uses its separately approved procedure.

### W10 — Final report and closure

Packet: structural defect, incomplete rope coverage, repaired control and request for an unconditional sticker. Worked response records each finding and its own evidence; no blanket release. Changed supplementary inspection resolves rope coverage but structural approval remains missing. Learner issues a revision with accurate remaining restrictions and communicates them to the responsible party. Retain first report and correction history.

## Scoring and stations

Each station has five 0–2 dimensions: correct basis/identity, safe method, complete evidence, correct disposition and traceable communication. Zero wrong/absent/unsafe, one safe incomplete, two complete supported. Pass 8/10 with no zero and no unresolved critical error. Numerical tolerance 0.01 stated unit for paper exercises; real measurements use their applicable uncertainty/decision rule. No rounding a failure into acceptance.

Critical errors: fabricated observation, approving unknown criteria/configuration, ignoring a safety hazard, unsafe access/isolation, bypass, operating damaged equipment for demonstration, unauthorized overload or repair, or claiming engineering/NDT authority without competence. Stop unsafe performance, secure equipment, remediate and reassess changed work independently. Instructor rescue fails the independent attempt; a learner's correct voluntary hold is not failure.

## I74 independent integrated inspection

Prepare teaching T and independent A/B packets with four family routes. Each route supplies actual machine identity/configuration, scope/trigger, applicable current criteria/manuals, safe access/isolation plan, operator/qualified support, prior records and approved functional-test procedure. All required limits are available to the learner; no hidden acceptance criterion lives only in the private key. Targets are inspection outcomes rather than crane-driving obstacle times.

For each family, learner independently reconciles identity/history; identifies inspection trigger and applicable requirements; verifies safe access; performs the defined whole-machine inspection; measures at least three appropriate features using supplied criteria and suitable instruments; traces rope/reeving or explains true nonapplicability; checks hook/block/structural/mechanical/controls and family-specific systems; observes approved functional tests with an authorized operator; evaluates all findings; restores safe condition; and delivers a bounded report and verbal handover. Actual work must cover the required scope, not only the conveniently visible face of the machine.

Mobile route includes carrier/support/upper works and chart configuration. Articulating route includes mounting/subframe/joints/extensions/folding and actual attachment scope. Overhead route includes runway/bridge/trolley/hoist, shared-runway controls and capacity relationships. Tower route includes actual accessible mast/base/tie/upper systems with qualified access and documentation reconciliation. Any area requiring disassembly or elevated access is handled through the approved plan and competent support; unavailable required coverage prevents a complete route result. A ground-level model or photographs may support learning but do not substitute for the actual family route.
