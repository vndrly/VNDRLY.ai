# VNDRLY/U Ultrasonic Examination — Detection and Characterization

VUC062 · Original materials · 2026-11-18

## Course scope and entry requirements

This course addresses manual conventional ultrasonic detection, characterization and location of discontinuities in carbon-steel plate and pipe weld training specimens. It retains the catalog's legacy QUTE detection emphasis. Current QUTE (M) has separate current documentation and is the next catalog program; this package does not claim an old orientation is today's examination specification. [S62-01, S62-02]

Learners need established UT fundamentals, safe instrument handling, basic weld geometry and calculation ability. The instructor verifies those prerequisites before independent scored work. Training completion is not API certification, employer NDT qualification or production acceptance authority. Learners must actually perform scanning on validated specimens; reading and written answers cannot establish detection competence.

## Q01 — Detection is evidence, not an alarm

An indication is a recorded response that requires evaluation. A reportable discontinuity is an interpretation supported by the applicable method and reporting criterion. A defect is an acceptance classification under a specified criterion and authorized process; do not use the words interchangeably. An alarm can be caused by a geometric response or invalid setup, while a significant feature may not produce the largest amplitude.

Start with examination scope, reporting threshold and required evidence. Record enough position, response and configuration information for another competent examiner to understand what was found. Neither “beeped” nor “screen looked clear” establishes a complete examination.

**Worked:** a gated echo appears, but the gate covers a known corner response. Investigate and document geometry rather than automatically reporting a crack. **Changed:** a repeatable response meets the reporting criterion but type is unresolved. Report the supported indication and limitation; do not omit it because a precise type is uncertain. P01 evaluates this decision chain.

## Q02 — Procedure and safe bench readiness

Confirm material, weld geometry, thickness, access and equipment are within the approved examination technique. Identify reporting versus acceptance rules and who can authorize disposition. The responsible UT authority must establish the actual technique and reference basis; a course title is not an inspection procedure.

Use cool, depressurized, stable training samples. Inspect sharp edges, cables, connectors, couplant compatibility and slip risks. Secure pipe sections against rolling. Stop when handling or electrical condition is unsafe. This course does not authorize process entry, high-temperature examination or live electrical repair.

**Worked:** suitable UT setup but unsecured pipe section. Secure it before positioning the probe. **Changed:** stable part but alloy outside the technique. Resolve applicability instead of assuming carbon-steel settings. P02 requires actual readiness observations and a clear stop/resolve record.

## Q03 — Weld geometry and coordinate convention

Identify the weld centerline, start datum, positive direction, examination side and inside/outside surfaces. The crown can be offset from the actual joint centerline; do not use appearance alone as a coordinate system. Draw the weld preparation, root, thickness and known geometric features from supported records and accessible measurements.

Keep the same convention through scan notes and final report. If the part is turned, reestablish orientation before continuing. A correctly detected response reported on the wrong side can lead follow-up examination to the wrong location.

**Worked:** start 35 mm, stop 57 mm from the same datum gives length 22 mm. **Changed:** the stop was measured from the opposite end of a 100 mm segment. Convert it to 43 mm from the first datum before calculating the 8 mm interval. State both original readings and the corrected basis.

## Q04 — Probe, wedge and material selection

Select the approved probe/wedge and mode for the weld volume and likely reflector orientations. A straight beam and an angle beam serve different geometries; one cannot simply replace the other because both produce echoes. Record frequency, size, angle definition, cable and actual equipment identity. Manufacturer teaching explains why weld orientation can require angle-beam examination. [S62-03]

Check wear, contact and wedge condition. Use the actual material velocity and verified refracted angle. A wedge label is a nominal selection aid, not proof of the angle in every material. Replacing hardware requires the applicable new checks.

**Worked:** a planned angle-beam pass is replaced with straight beam solely for convenience. Required directional coverage is not established. **Changed:** an approved alternative is demonstrated on suitable references. Record its scope and validation rather than pretending the original method was used. P04 requires actual inventory and selection reasoning.

## Q05 — Time base and distance calibration

Calibrate using suitable known references spanning the relevant path range. Separate zero/delay from velocity as required by the instrument and method. Manufacturer guidance describes angle-beam setup using known sound paths; use the actual manual for button operations. [S62-04] A single favorable point cannot establish the entire range.

For simple material-only pulse-echo transit, path S=vt/2. Raw transit containing wedge/electronic delay is not material-only time. Preserve reference identity and actual readings. If verification fails, fix the setup and assess affected work; do not change only the final report.

**Worked:** corrected 20 µs at 3.2 mm/µs gives path 32 mm. **Changed:** raw time 22 µs includes 2 µs delay; uncorrected path 35.2 mm is 3.2 mm high. P05 requires actual calibration and independent range verification, with arithmetic used to explain the result.

## Q06 — Index point, angle and first-leg location

Establish the beam exit/index point and actual refracted angle using the prescribed reference method. Position is measured from the correct index, not automatically the wedge front edge. For flat first-leg path S and angle θ from normal, depth is S cosθ and surface offset is S sinθ. Label the assumptions on the sketch.

An error in probe position propagates into reported reflector position. Preserve the actual index value and any front-edge-to-index offset used. Do not compensate by moving the drawing to a more plausible weld location.

**Worked:** S=30 mm at 60° gives depth 15 mm and offset 25.9808 mm. **Changed:** the recorded probe reference is displaced 4 mm from the intended index. Correct the coordinate traceably using the actual direction; do not alter depth to hide the position error. P06 requires actual index/angle checks and a plotted response.

## Q07 — Reflected legs and full-volume planning

A sound beam reflected from the back surface can interrogate another portion of the weld. Determine the applicable leg before calculating depth. For a flat plate of thickness t and a second-leg path, unfolded normal travel is S cosθ; within the second leg, actual depth is 2 t−S cosθ. This requires correct mode, geometry and leg identification.

Plan scan limits and required directions against the actual weld cross-section. A theoretical ray line is not evidence that the probe could reach each position or that the relevant feature orientations were interrogated.

**Worked:** t=20 mm, θ=60°, S=60 mm gives unfolded normal travel 30 mm and second-leg depth 10 mm. **Changed:** using first-leg depth would place the reflector at 30 mm, outside the plate. Resolve the leg instead of accepting an impossible location. P07 uses a labeled sketch and actual reference verification.

## Q08 — Reference sensitivity and reporting levels

Set and verify reference sensitivity using the required reflector, path and method. Distinguish reference level, scanning sensitivity, evaluation sensitivity and reporting threshold. Record applied corrections explicitly. A large echo at excessive gain is not automatically a large flaw; a weak echo at inadequate sensitivity cannot support a clear examination.

Avoid clipping and preserve usable signal-to-noise conditions. A dB change is a response ratio, not a direct physical size change. Do not lower sensitivity to eliminate difficult indications or raise it until every region appears flawed.

**Worked:** increasing a comparable amplitude from 25% to 50% is a factor of 2, or 6.0206 dB. **Changed:** the peak clips after adjustment. Reestablish a valid range before evaluation. P08 requires actual sensitivity records, including the level used for final evaluation.

## Q09 — Scan patterns and directional coverage

Use the approved pattern to interrogate the required volume and reflector orientations. Coverage requires deliberate probe movement, controlled indexing and contact. A pass across the surface is not proof that the whole volume has been examined. Manufacturer examples discuss patterns for longitudinal and transverse features; actual procedure requirements govern the learner's work. [S62-05]

Record direction, side, limits and interruptions. Resume from a known overlap after loss of contact rather than guessing where coverage stopped. Do not treat a required second direction as optional because the first direction found nothing.

**Worked:** a training plan specifies 12 mm effective swath and 6 mm index spacing, giving 50% overlap. **Changed:** actual spacing becomes 15 mm, leaving a 3 mm geometric gap in this simplified model. Rescan deficient scope; a clean screen does not establish coverage. P09 observes actual movement and records its limits.

## Q10 — Coupling, surfaces and curved parts

Check contact and stable response throughout the movement. Couplant quantity, roughness and wedge fit affect transmission. Loss of signal can be poor coupling rather than absence of discontinuities. Curvature changes contact and ray geometry; flat-plate calculations cannot automatically represent pipe examination.

Use an appropriate validated wedge/technique for the curvature and access. Record any inaccessible area. Do not claim a favorable isolated reading proves the whole curved surface was adequately coupled. Stop and correct an unsafe or uncontrolled setup.

**Worked:** a flat wedge rocks on a small pipe, with intermittent reference response. The examination basis is unstable. **Changed:** suitable contoured arrangement is supplied. Verify actual performance and coordinate model before examination. P10 requires observed contact checks and a traceable limitation where coverage cannot be established.

## Q11 — Geometry versus discontinuity

Compare a response with known root, crown, mismatch and boundary geometry. Use approved probe movements and plotting to test whether the response fits a geometric explanation. A location near the root does not automatically prove either harmless geometry or a crack. Preserve observations that support or contradict each interpretation.

Do not label every uncertain echo as a flaw to avoid misses; false calls can misdirect repairs. Conversely, do not dismiss a repeatable reportable response as geometry without evidence. Escalate unresolved classification while reporting the supported finding.

**Worked:** echo position agrees with a known boundary and tracks consistently through permitted movements. Record the geometric interpretation and evidence. **Changed:** an additional response persists at a distinct location inconsistent with that boundary. Evaluate it separately rather than hiding it under the original explanation. P11 uses known sound and flawed teaching regions.

## Q12 — Planar feature characterization

Planar features can respond strongly or weakly depending on beam orientation. Evaluate position, directional response, surface connection and relationship to the weld preparation. Distinguish supported characterization from an unsupported label. Lack of sidewall fusion, root-related conditions and cracking may demand different evidence and follow-up.

Do not equate amplitude with severity or assume a low-amplitude feature is harmless. A plausible type assignment needs the technique's evidence. When feature identity remains uncertain, report what is established and seek appropriate corroboration rather than inventing a precise mechanism.

**Worked:** repeated evidence places a planar response on the fusion face. State the supported location and characterization basis. **Changed:** another view suggests a surface-connected feature instead. Resolve the inconsistency and retain both observations; do not average incompatible categories. P12 requires an explanation grounded in actual teaching-specimen responses.

## Q13 — Volumetric indications and laminations

Distributed or irregular responses may support a volumetric interpretation, but waveform appearance alone is not a universal classifier. Compare response behavior, position and applicable method evidence. Straight-beam examination may reveal laminar features that affect sound access to the weld; the region behind a reflector is not automatically examined.

Separate detecting a lamination from proving the weld behind it is clear. Use the approved response to access limitations and report unresolved scope. Do not relabel an inconvenient planar response as porosity merely because that category seems less serious.

**Worked:** a laminar response interrupts the expected backwall and blocks required interrogation. Record the feature and affected coverage. **Changed:** alternate validated access examines the missing volume. Add that actual evidence and revise coverage; do not erase the original limitation. P13 includes a known laminar teaching condition and interpretation discussion.

## Q14 — Position, start/stop and length

Use the method's endpoint definition and record start and stop from a consistent datum. The measured response length is technique-dependent; do not imply unvalidated physical boundaries. Record the response criterion, gain and probe orientation used. Oversizing can wrongly implicate adjacent sound regions.

Check ordering, units and coordinate direction before calculating length. A reversed datum or mixed inches/millimeters can produce plausible but wrong values. Preserve raw endpoint observations so a reviewer can reconstruct the result.

**Worked:** start 42 mm, stop 71 mm gives 29 mm response length. **Changed:** revised valid stop 68 mm gives 26 mm, a 3 mm reduction. Document why the endpoint changed. P14 requires actual endpoint work under the supplied technique and a relocated indication check.

## Q15 — Misses, false calls and denominator discipline

Independent detection performance needs both known flawed regions and known sound regions. Detection rate uses actual known flaws as denominator; false-call rate uses the defined sound regions. Reporting everything as flawed can inflate apparent detection while failing discrimination. Counting only the flaws the learner found hides misses.

Separate characterization and positioning outcomes from initial detection. State the denominator for each measure. Training scores are not a statistically established probability-of-detection curve or a guarantee on production components.

**Worked:** 9 of 10 known flaws detected gives 90%; two false calls in 20 sound regions gives 10% false-call rate. **Changed:** only eight of the nine detections are characterized correctly: conditional characterization is 8/9=88.8889%, not 80% unless using all ten known flaws and labeling that different measure. P15 requires explicit denominators and error analysis.

## Q16 — Verification failure and affected-scope recovery

Perform checks at required intervals and after relevant changes. A failure makes the dependent examination period suspect; identify affected specimens, configurations and recorded results. Correct the cause and repeat required work under valid conditions. Do not erase the failed check or call all prior results valid merely because the new check passes.

Use evidence to bound the affected interval. If the last valid check is unknown, do not invent a convenient start time. Record authorized disposition, actual repeat work and remaining gaps.

**Worked:** six specimens since the last valid check before failure require affected-scope assessment. **Changed:** two are validly repeated; four remain unresolved absent supported disposition. P16 includes a safe instructor-issued check fault and actual recovery records, not only a written response.

## Q17 — Report, review and acceptance boundaries

Provide identity, procedure/version, equipment, calibration, scan coverage, indications with coordinates, characterization basis, endpoints and limitations. Differentiate “no reportable indications within examined scope” from “no defects anywhere.” A valid UT report does not automatically establish fitness for service or authorize repair.

Review coherence before handover: every indication belongs to the correct specimen, coordinates use the same datum, limitations are visible and original records remain linked. Correct errors traceably rather than replacing the old report invisibly.

**Worked:** a report says “clear” despite a documented inaccessible quadrant. Rewrite it to state examined scope and unresolved quadrant. **Changed:** authorized engineering disposition addresses that exact limitation. Attach its conditions without pretending the missing UT examination occurred. P17 assesses a complete evidence-based report.

## Q18 — Blind demonstration and remediation

Use unfamiliar, independently validated specimens with protected truth. The learner establishes the setup, examines all assigned scope and submits records without coaching or knowing flaw counts/locations. Familiar practice samples and written tests cannot substitute. Practical failures require diagnosis and changed demonstration, not repeated guessing of the same specimen answers.

Analyze whether the cause was setup, access, direction, interpretation, position or reporting. Teach that cause and preserve unsuccessful attempts. A course completion record describes the actual demonstrated scope and does not transfer to other techniques or external credentials.

**Worked:** learner reports every region as flawed, finding all ten true flaws but producing twenty false calls. Detection alone is not a pass. **Changed:** a changed set is examined with sound discrimination and valid evidence. Score all announced categories independently; do not retroactively lower criteria to compensate for the original failure.

# VUC062 sources and coverage

Reviewed 2026-11-18. Original available-source instruction, examples and assessment. No external certification equivalence or complete proprietary-standard conformity claimed.

**S62-01 Legacy detection context:** [API QUTE candidate orientation, July 2015](https://www.api.org/~/media/files/certification/icp/icp-certification-programs/ut%20programs/qute/api%20qute_candidate%20orientation_rev-072015.pdf). Used for historical scope of detection, characterization, positioning and false calls on plate/pipe welds. Old approximate grading percentages, sample dimensions and administration are not asserted as current. S62 thresholds are independently defined VNDRLY training criteria.

**S62-02 Current boundary:** [API QUTE (M)](https://www.api.org/products-and-services/individual-certification-programs/certifications/api-qute-m). Current page identifies hands-on conventional single/dual-element work and four error categories, but one introductory sentence inconsistently calls it phased array. The inconsistency is disclosed rather than used to redefine manual conventional technique. #63 addresses the separately cataloged current manual scope; #62 retains legacy detection emphasis. No claim of API approval is made.

**S62-03 Beam/application concept:** [Evident angle-beam inspection](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/flaw-detection/weld-inspection). Supports choosing beam geometry appropriate to weld/reflector orientation. No manufacturer example is copied into a universal operating procedure or acceptance criterion.

**S62-04 Calibration concept:** [Evident angle-beam setup](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/flaw-detection/straight-beam-delay). Supports suitable known-path references and material-aware calibration. Actual instrument operations and tolerances must come from the approved technique/manual.

**S62-05 Coverage and contact:** [Evident common test practices](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/flaw-detection/common-test-practices). Supports deliberate directional coverage and attention to curved-surface contact/access. No example index distance or wedge contour is treated as a universal rule. Original swath arithmetic is explicitly a simplified teaching model, not proof of volumetric coverage.
