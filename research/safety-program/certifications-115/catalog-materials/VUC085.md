# VNDRLY/U Gas Tester Knowledge — Digital Delivery

VUC085 · original materials · 18 November 2026

A standalone digital knowledge course for personnel preparing for gas-testing duties. Fourteen teaching chapters include the reviewed gas-testing core and two delivery-specific chapters. The common safety instruction is deliberately retained in full so this course does not require purchase or prior completion of VUC084. This program uses synthetic screen scenarios, not actual gas measurements or a physical skills award. Planned 24 hours includes supported study, applications, individually observed knowledge decisions and controlled final. It is not OPITO 9242 approval; the external public product lists four hours, while this original extended curriculum makes no duration or controlled-unit equivalence claim. Workplace training, competence assessment and appointment remain required.

## Q85-01 — Role, authorization and the work boundary

Gas testing supplies evidence about an atmosphere at particular places and times using specified equipment. It does not by itself authorize entry, hot work or rescue. Before accepting a task, identify the responsible permit authority, the hazards to measure, the instruments and methods, the acceptance conditions and the action on change. The person signing a gas-test record must know what the signature means. Initial training is preparation for workplace development and assessment, not automatic appointment as an authorized tester.

Our exercise concerns an isolated mock vessel drawn on paper. The tester may sample from outside using a screen scenario and report simulated results. No one crosses a real confined-space boundary. The permit issuer decides whether all controls are satisfied, including mechanical isolation, access and rescue arrangements. A reading within a numerical band cannot compensate for an absent isolation certificate.

Worked case: the supervisor says “The meter is green, so sign the entry permit.” You verify your remit, report the actual channels, locations, time and limitations, and leave the wider authorization to the designated person. Changed case: the same vessel will now be welded instead of visually inspected. Stop the old assessment path; the changed activity can create different hazards and requires the relevant permit and testing plan. Do not reuse a cold-work result as an unrestricted hot-work clearance.

## Q85-02 — Identify atmospheric hazards before selecting a meter

Review process contents, cleaning materials, residues, connected systems, work-generated contaminants and nearby operations. Consider oxygen displacement or enrichment, flammable gases or vapors and specific toxic substances. A familiar four-channel instrument does not measure every possible hazard. Normal oxygen does not exclude a toxic atmosphere. A zero hydrogen-sulfide channel says nothing about an unmeasured solvent.

Use the hazard inventory to build a target list and have unresolved substances or mixtures evaluated by a technically qualified person. Include non-atmospheric hazards in the referral: engulfment, moving equipment, electrical energy and restricted escape remain relevant even when atmospheric results are acceptable. Odor is not a measurement method; hydrogen sulfide can defeat smell as a warning. Do not approach a suspected release to identify it by sniffing.

Worked case: the historical contents were water, but yesterday a contractor used a solvent cleaner. The old target list names only oxygen and methane. Obtain the cleaner identity and competent evaluation before testing for release. Changed case: the cleaner is removed, but a nearby engine exhausts toward the opening. The plan must consider that changing source as well. Record what is known, what is uncertain and who resolves the gap; do not turn unknown into “none.”

## Q85-03 — Read units and limits correctly

Percent by volume, parts per million and percent of lower explosive limit describe different things. One percent by volume equals 10,000 ppm. Percent LEL is relative to a specified flammable substance and calibration basis; it is not percent oxygen or a direct toxic-exposure value. For arithmetic practice only, fictional gas G has an LEL of 4 percent by volume. Ten percent of that LEL is 0.4 percent by volume, or 4,000 ppm. This conversion does not establish a safe exposure or permit limit.

Distinguish instantaneous concentration, short-term exposure average, time-weighted exposure and an instrument alarm. They answer different questions. Do not average away a ceiling exceedance or a momentary evacuation trigger. An alarm setting is not automatically the applicable legal limit or the site's work criterion. Jurisdiction, substance, task and employer controls determine the applicable requirements.

Worked case: a technician calls 5 percent LEL “5 percent gas.” For fictional G, 5 percent LEL is 0.2 percent volume, or 2,000 ppm; the original statement is wrong by a factor of twenty-five. Changed case: another gas has a 2 percent volume LEL. The same 5 percent LEL corresponds to 0.1 percent volume, or 1,000 ppm. Never apply a conversion across unknown mixtures or unverified calibration response.

## Q85-04 — Choose sensors and understand their blind spots

Identify the installed sensors, target gases, range, resolution, response, environmental limits and approval for the intended atmosphere. A catalytic combustible sensor depends on oxygen and may under-read after poisoning or in unsuitable atmospheres. An infrared sensor has a different detection mechanism and is not universally responsive to every flammable gas. A photoionization detector responds according to lamp energy and substance characteristics; a displayed number is not automatic chemical identification. Electrochemical toxic sensors may respond to interfering gases. Obtain the actual manufacturer's instructions and approved correction information.

For the screen model D85, the combustible channel is explicitly unsuitable for interpreting oxygen-deficient conditions and has no approved correction factor for an unknown solvent mixture. Those limitations are decisive even if the screen displays zero. Do not invent a correction factor from a different instrument. A diffusion monitor cannot be assumed to accept a remote hose without an approved pump arrangement.

Worked case: the oxygen channel reads low and the combustible channel reads zero. Hold the work and report that the combustible result cannot be used to clear the atmosphere with this sensor. Changed case: oxygen is normal but the hazard is a substance outside the installed sensor capability. The meter still cannot establish its absence. Select a suitable method through competent review rather than adding confidence to an irrelevant reading.

## Q85-05 — Inspect, function-test and manage calibration

Inspect the instrument identity, condition, battery, sensors, filters, inlet, accessories and status before use. Check the manufacturer's required function-test and calibration arrangements. A bump test demonstrates response and alarm function; a calibration check compares response with a known reference within specified tolerance; full calibration adjusts response. They are not interchangeable labels. Use correct unexpired traceable gas, regulator, tubing and adapters under approved instructions. Do not zero a detector in an unknown atmosphere.

In this course actual hazardous calibration gas is not released. The learner examines supplied kit images or equivalent text descriptions and interprets a simulated response sequence. This is evidence of knowledge, not physical instrument handling. Real instrument qualification requires supervised work with the exact equipment and safe gas-handling arrangements. A failed check is a reason to remove the unit from the task and follow the manufacturer's recovery/service process, not to silence the indication and proceed.

Worked case: trainer certificate says a 50-unit reference must read within 45 to 55 units. It reads 44. The check fails by one unit below the lower boundary; rounding it to 45 is not allowed. Changed case: it reads 50 but the reference cylinder is expired. The apparent agreement does not validate the check. Record the defect and obtain a valid kit. A calibration sticker does not override damage or a failed daily function check.

## Q85-06 — Build a representative sampling plan

Plan where the sample comes from, how it reaches the sensor and how long each result needs. Include relevant heights, pockets, adjacent spaces, work locations and changing sources; do not rely only on gas density assumptions because ventilation, temperature and mixing affect distribution. Start from a safe external position and do not cross an entry boundary to obtain a supposedly pre-entry sample. Protect the tester and the escape route.

Remote sampling introduces transport and response delay. Use the actual approved hose type and length, flow verification, manufacturer timing and stabilization criteria. Reactive contaminants can be lost in unsuitable tubing. A blocked line, leak, wet filter or wrong adapter can make a result unrepresentative. A reading at the opening does not establish conditions at the far end.

Worked trainer calculation: approved D85 instructions specify transport allowance 3 seconds per metre plus a 30-second sensor wait, then stable display. With 8 metres, minimum timed allowance is 24 + 30 = 54 seconds per newly positioned sample, plus any further stabilization. Changed case: 12 metres gives 36 + 30 = 66 seconds. These are fictional trainer timings, not universal field rules. In the digital sequence, identify the required wait, record the simulated position and do not accept early readings because they look favorable. Physical sampling speed is not assessed on screen.

## Q85-07 — Conduct pre-entry verification and interpret sequence

The evaluation determines what hazards and methods matter; verification checks the actual atmosphere against that plan. Review oxygen before relying on oxygen-dependent combustible results, then flammability and required toxics. A multi-sensor instrument may display channels simultaneously; the interpretive dependency still matters. Record actual values and units beside the applicable criteria rather than writing only “safe.” Follow the manufacturer's response time at each location.

For scenario V85, sampling points are upper U, middle M, lower L and remote pocket P. All are external probe targets. The plan prohibits entry until every required point and channel is valid and all other permit conditions are satisfied. One failed point holds the whole proposed work scope; averaging readings from different locations is not a release method.

Worked case: U and M meet the exercise bands, but P has an alarm. Report the location-specific result, maintain the hold and activate the prescribed response. Do not discard the pocket as an outlier. Changed case: P cannot be reached by the approved probe. The gap is unresolved, not zero. Ask the competent planner for a safe suitable method. A test record should make an inaccessible point visible to the permit authority rather than hide it in a general check mark.

## Q85-08 — Test for hot work and neighboring hazards

Hot work introduces an ignition source and can release contaminants from coatings, residues or materials being heated. The testing plan must address the actual work and relevant connected or adjacent areas, not merely the operator's breathing zone. Atmospheric testing is one part of isolation, cleaning, ventilation, fire prevention and permit control. A personal monitor alone does not assess every potential ignition location.

In the fictional H85 task, grinding is proposed outside the mock vessel near a drain and a shared wall. The plan requires the work point, drain recess and opposite side of the wall to be evaluated from approved safe positions. The drain recess fails the exercise flammable criterion. Work remains held even when the open-air work point reads zero. The tester reports facts and scope; they do not redefine the permit to exclude the inconvenient location.

Changed application: a process vent is opened nearby after the initial test. Stop the authorized activity according to the plan, notify the permit authority and reassess conditions before restart. Do not assume that a test remains valid for the entire shift because the form has today's date. When a shipyard or other specialist regime requires a particular qualified person or certificate, this training does not substitute for that requirement. Confirm the governing jurisdiction and role before signing.

## Q85-09 — Monitor change and respond to alarms

A pre-work test is a snapshot. Work can alter conditions, ventilation can fail, material can leak and nearby operations can change. Follow the approved plan for continuous monitoring, personal monitoring, periodic recorded tests or a combination; do not invent one universal interval. Place equipment so that it measures the intended exposure or location and can be heard or seen as required. Check that a remote display corresponds to the correct sensor and current time.

The exercise response to an alarm, fault, lost sampling flow or failed ventilation is stop work, withdraw by the planned route without entering a hazard, notify the responsible person and maintain the hold. Do not reset and continue merely because the display later falls. Investigation, restored controls, valid retesting and authorized restart are separate steps. A low battery or sensor fault is not a clean-air indication.

Worked case: the entry team is operating under a valid plan when the pump fault alarm appears. Treat atmospheric assurance as lost, initiate the planned withdrawal and report the fault. Changed case: the instrument still works but ventilation stops. Apply the plan's stop response; a temporarily normal reading does not prove the future atmosphere will remain acceptable. Preserve the event time and trend rather than clearing the history to obtain a tidy record.

## Q85-10 — Communicate results and preserve traceability

A useful report identifies the task and location, sampling points, date/time, instrument and sensor configuration, calibration/function-check status, method and hose, actual readings and units, applicable criteria, abnormalities and the person informed. Explain limitations such as unmeasured substances, unreachable areas or an invalid sensor response. The permit authority needs to know what the test did not establish.

Use repeat-back for a critical result: location, channel/value, action and recipient acknowledgment. Avoid vague messages such as “a little high.” Record changes and corrections transparently. Do not copy yesterday's values, pre-sign a result or silently substitute another instrument number. Data logging helps but does not by itself establish where the inlet was or whether the hose was valid.

Worked case: the record lists 0 on every channel but no units. Zero percent LEL and zero ppm refer to different measures, and zero oxygen would be a severe abnormality. Correct the record using actual evidence; if unavailable, repeat the test under the approved safe plan rather than guessing. Changed case: a colleague asks you to sign their test. Identify the actual tester and use the organization's review process; do not represent an unobserved test as your own. Honest gaps are safer than fabricated assurance.

## Q85-11 — Emergencies, standby duties and rescue boundaries

Know the alarm route, muster location, communications and planned rescue arrangements before work. A person collapsing in a confined space can tempt an unprotected coworker into the same atmosphere. Do not make an impulsive entry. Raise the alarm, prevent further entry and activate the trained rescue response. Use non-entry actions only within the planned system and your training; a rope alone is not a complete rescue plan.

A tester assigned simultaneous standby duties must understand whether those duties are compatible and what continuous attention is required. Do not leave an assigned watch to fetch a meter without an authorized handover. Respiratory protection is not selected by guesswork, and an air-purifying respirator does not supply missing oxygen. This course does not qualify rescue entry, breathing-apparatus use or medical treatment.

Worked scenario: a radio call reports a worker down beyond the mock entry marker. Stay outside, stop others approaching, call the designated rescue channel and report location and last known conditions. Changed scenario: the supervisor says “just lean in and pull.” Refuse the unplanned exposure and continue the emergency plan. In classroom practice, use a paper casualty marker; no learner enters a real vessel, wears a supplied-air system or encounters a deliberately hazardous atmosphere.

## Q85-12 — Transfer training to the specific workplace

Before appointment, demonstrate the actual site's hazards, instrument configuration, sampling accessories, permit conditions, emergency actions and reporting process under competent supervision. Generic knowledge does not establish skill with every meter. If a new sensor, firmware setting, process chemical or work method changes the testing basis, review the task and retrain or reassess as needed.

This package uses a fictional screen model to teach decision making without exposing learners to gases. Its knowledge-assessment record must say exactly that. A workplace assessor needs evidence from the real approved equipment and representative tasks under safe conditions before confirming authorization. The employer defines the appointment and its limits; VNDRLY does not issue an OPITO certificate by using an OPITO source title.

Worked handover: your training result lists successful D85 screen-based sampling decisions and alarm scenarios. The site uses a different pumped instrument with a different hose limit. Bring the record, identify the difference and complete the site-specific demonstration rather than translating the trainer timing by intuition. Changed application: your authorization covers cold-work pre-entry testing, but you are assigned specialist hot-work certification. Refer for the required scope and competence review. Confidence, years of service and an unexpired training record cannot expand a documented remit.

## Q85-13 — Use digital scenarios without confusing them with live measurement

A training screen is a representation, not a gas monitor. Identify whether a value is live operational data, a replay, a teaching example or a saved answer. In this program every reading is a synthetic scenario value. A green border, animation or completion badge cannot certify an atmosphere. Read the location, channel, unit, time and quality flag before making a decision. If any required item is missing, explain the uncertainty instead of supplying an assumed favorable value.

The D85 activity presents one information card at a time. Select a response and write the reason before revealing teaching feedback. A wrong critical decision leads to an explanation and a changed task. During the controlled encounter, the instructor withholds feedback until the attempt ends and records the learner's own response. A mouse click alone does not prove understanding; a short explanation or oral follow-up establishes the decision basis.

Worked case: the displayed oxygen is 20.8 percent, but the timestamp is from the previous shift and the current status is CONNECTION LOST. The value is historical, not current assurance. Under the scenario plan, stop and report the lost monitoring rather than describing the current atmosphere as acceptable. Changed case: an offline training answer was typed but there is no save receipt. Preserve the draft through the approved method, reconnect or contact the instructor and confirm which version was received. Do not confuse “typed,” “saved” and “submitted.”

Use accessible text equivalents for maps, readings and alarms. Color cannot be the only way to distinguish HOLD from ACCEPTABLE. The learner may zoom, use a keyboard or hear a screen reader without losing the same decision challenge. If an interface prevents a response, record a delivery problem and provide an equivalent accessible method. An inaccessible button is not evidence of weak gas-testing knowledge.

## Q85-14 — Maintain independent evidence and transfer digital learning

Before a controlled digital assessment, agree identity verification, permitted materials, assistance, privacy, interruptions and submission rules. Use proportionate identity checks through the approved training system, not unnecessary personal documents sent to an instructor's private inbox. The final is open coursebook but individual: no peer, answer key or generative assistance. Practice feedback is available before the final; it must not leak the controlled answers during the attempt.

A connection failure pauses evidence collection. Preserve responses actually received, log the interruption and agree a fair continuation or changed equivalent task. Do not award completion from time spent logged in. A fully watched video can support participation but cannot demonstrate the person's reasoning. Use the supplied written decisions and an individual oral challenge to check ownership. If authorship is uncertain, seek fresh evidence without making unsupported accusations.

Worked case: a learner submits a correct response identical to a colleague's. That similarity alone does not prove cheating, but independent knowledge remains to be verified if concern is reasonable. Ask the learner to explain a changed hose-length and fault scenario using their own reasoning; document the response. Changed case: the learner successfully explains every scenario but has never operated the site's detector. Issue only the digital knowledge award earned. Arrange supervised instrument practice and workplace assessment before any appointment.

The transition record lists the actual instrument, installed sensors, approved accessories, function/calibration method, sampling timing, permit scope, emergency arrangements and assessor. Each must be verified at the workplace. Digital completion does not mark those rows passed. This prevents a useful fast learning route from becoming a false claim of operational competence.

# VUC085 digital workbook and facilitator guide

## Delivery resources and learner route

All materials needed for the knowledge exercises are printed below. Use a readable PDF/HTML document or instructor screen sharing with accessible text. No custom software build is required to deliver this package. Learners read a chapter, attempt its two checks before viewing explanations, complete the associated scenario and submit their reasoning. The facilitator conducts individual observed screen/voice encounters and a controlled final. A phone, tablet or computer may be used if text, tables and response controls remain accessible; an equivalent paper/oral route can address a documented delivery barrier while preserving the outcomes.

Every map has a text description, every warning has words as well as color, and every table has named channels and units. Do not infer answers from a colored icon. Provide captions or a transcript for any added instructor narration. Permit zoom, keyboard navigation, screen readers and agreed breaks. Confirm the learner can access and respond before beginning the controlled attempt. The facilitator must understand gas-detection principles and assessment practice; this is not an unsupervised click-through attendance certificate.

No real gas, cylinder, monitor configuration change, entry, hot work or rescue occurs. Screens represent knowledge scenarios only. A facilitator may demonstrate an inert device on camera, but watching it is not evidence that the learner operated it. Do not request a learner to improvise hazardous demonstrations at home or at a workplace without a separate authorized safe training arrangement.

## D85 screen instrument card

D85 is a fictional display, not a safety device. Channels: O2 percent volume, combustible percent LEL relative to fictional gas G, H2S ppm, CO ppm. It cannot establish the concentration of an unidentified solvent. Its combustible response is not reliable for interpretation in the supplied oxygen-deficient scenario. No correction factor is authorized for unknown mixtures. Display quality states are VALID, CHANGING, FAULT, OVER, HISTORICAL and CONNECTION LOST. Only VALID stable readings with the required current location/time can contribute to the exercise decision; every required control must also be satisfied.

Remote-sample teaching rule: approved fictional hose up to 12 metres, transport allowance 3 seconds per metre plus 30 seconds response, then stability. This is an arithmetic model, not an instruction for any actual detector. Hose 8 metres gives 54 seconds; 12 gives 66. Learner selects the earliest permissible review point and then checks the quality card. A reading still CHANGING after that time is not ready. A blocked flow or wet filter invalidates the path regardless of elapsed time.

Readiness cards: ID D85-07; battery adequate; casing intact; configuration as stated; status CURRENT. Reference RX is expired; reference RY is in date with a traceable fictional certificate. A generic 50-unit verification requires 45–55 inclusive. RY response 44 fails. Instructor supplies a later simulated service/full-calibration record and response 50 with alarm-function PASS. Only that documented recovery resolves the readiness failure. No actual gas mixture, regulator setting or instrument calibration procedure is supplied or authorized by these cards.

## P85 fictional decision bands

O2 20.0–21.0 percent volume inclusive; combustible less than 1 percent LEL; H2S less than 1 ppm; CO less than 5 ppm. These are original exercise bands, not legal limits or operational alarm settings. Equality fails a less-than criterion. Invalid data, unknown hazards, missing points, lost ventilation or communications and changed work also HOLD. Never round, average locations or substitute a previous favorable result to get a pass. The simulated response is stop work, withdraw through the planned safe route, notify permit issuer Nia Stone and maintain hold until investigation, valid retest and authorized restart.

## Branching activity script — facilitator can deliver without software

1. Present initial V85. Learner chooses PROCEED, HOLD FOR GAPS or IGNORE CLEANER and gives a reason. Correct HOLD leads to EV-25/IV-25; other choices receive teaching feedback that neither a missing isolation nor unknown hazard is resolved by a meter. Then use a changed missing-document card before progression.
2. Present readiness RX/RY and response 44. Correct rejection leads to recovery record; a bypass choice triggers feedback and a changed expired-reference case. During controlled assessment, record the error and withhold coaching until the attempt ends.
3. Ask for sample order/locations and 8-metre timing. Present CHANGING at 54 seconds, VALID at 60. Correct learner waits for VALID; early acceptance is remediated with a 12-metre changed sequence. These are scenario timestamps, not measured physical skill.
4. Reveal U/M/L/P. Correct HOLD at P leads to a report and CR-25. Averaging or deleting P is a critical error. After retest, ask who authorizes the job.
5. Inject one H85/M85/E85 event and one digital-quality card. Require action and reason, then complete F85. Instructor records the exact chosen branch and explanation. Practice branches may repeat with feedback; controlled encounters must be independent and use changed cues.
