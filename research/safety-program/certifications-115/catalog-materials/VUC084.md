# VNDRLY/U Gas Tester Initial Training

VUC084 · original available-source materials · 18 November 2026

Audience: personnel preparing for gas-testing duties. This 32-hour program teaches hazard evaluation interfaces, instrument limitations, safe sampling, interpretation, reporting and response through inert practical simulation. No formal academic prerequisite; accessible numeracy and language support must preserve safety decisions. Actual workplace competence and employer appointment remain required. Not OPITO approval, external certification, confined-space entry or rescue authorization.

## Q84-01 — Role, authorization and the work boundary

Gas testing supplies evidence about an atmosphere at particular places and times using specified equipment. It does not by itself authorize entry, hot work or rescue. Before accepting a task, identify the responsible permit authority, the hazards to measure, the instruments and methods, the acceptance conditions and the action on change. The person signing a gas-test record must know what the signature means. Initial training is preparation for workplace development and assessment, not automatic appointment as an authorized tester.

Our exercise concerns an isolated mock vessel drawn on paper. The tester may sample from outside using an inert trainer and report simulated results. No one crosses a real confined-space boundary. The permit issuer decides whether all controls are satisfied, including mechanical isolation, access and rescue arrangements. A reading within a numerical band cannot compensate for an absent isolation certificate.

Worked case: the supervisor says “The meter is green, so sign the entry permit.” You verify your remit, report the actual channels, locations, time and limitations, and leave the wider authorization to the designated person. Changed case: the same vessel will now be welded instead of visually inspected. Stop the old assessment path; the changed activity can create different hazards and requires the relevant permit and testing plan. Do not reuse a cold-work result as an unrestricted hot-work clearance.

## Q84-02 — Identify atmospheric hazards before selecting a meter

Review process contents, cleaning materials, residues, connected systems, work-generated contaminants and nearby operations. Consider oxygen displacement or enrichment, flammable gases or vapors and specific toxic substances. A familiar four-channel instrument does not measure every possible hazard. Normal oxygen does not exclude a toxic atmosphere. A zero hydrogen-sulfide channel says nothing about an unmeasured solvent.

Use the hazard inventory to build a target list and have unresolved substances or mixtures evaluated by a technically qualified person. Include non-atmospheric hazards in the referral: engulfment, moving equipment, electrical energy and restricted escape remain relevant even when atmospheric results are acceptable. Odor is not a measurement method; hydrogen sulfide can defeat smell as a warning. Do not approach a suspected release to identify it by sniffing.

Worked case: the historical contents were water, but yesterday a contractor used a solvent cleaner. The old target list names only oxygen and methane. Obtain the cleaner identity and competent evaluation before testing for release. Changed case: the cleaner is removed, but a nearby engine exhausts toward the opening. The plan must consider that changing source as well. Record what is known, what is uncertain and who resolves the gap; do not turn unknown into “none.”

## Q84-03 — Read units and limits correctly

Percent by volume, parts per million and percent of lower explosive limit describe different things. One percent by volume equals 10,000 ppm. Percent LEL is relative to a specified flammable substance and calibration basis; it is not percent oxygen or a direct toxic-exposure value. For arithmetic practice only, fictional gas G has an LEL of 4 percent by volume. Ten percent of that LEL is 0.4 percent by volume, or 4,000 ppm. This conversion does not establish a safe exposure or permit limit.

Distinguish instantaneous concentration, short-term exposure average, time-weighted exposure and an instrument alarm. They answer different questions. Do not average away a ceiling exceedance or a momentary evacuation trigger. An alarm setting is not automatically the applicable legal limit or the site's work criterion. Jurisdiction, substance, task and employer controls determine the applicable requirements.

Worked case: a technician calls 5 percent LEL “5 percent gas.” For fictional G, 5 percent LEL is 0.2 percent volume, or 2,000 ppm; the original statement is wrong by a factor of twenty-five. Changed case: another gas has a 2 percent volume LEL. The same 5 percent LEL corresponds to 0.1 percent volume, or 1,000 ppm. Never apply a conversion across unknown mixtures or unverified calibration response.

## Q84-04 — Choose sensors and understand their blind spots

Identify the installed sensors, target gases, range, resolution, response, environmental limits and approval for the intended atmosphere. A catalytic combustible sensor depends on oxygen and may under-read after poisoning or in unsuitable atmospheres. An infrared sensor has a different detection mechanism and is not universally responsive to every flammable gas. A photoionization detector responds according to lamp energy and substance characteristics; a displayed number is not automatic chemical identification. Electrochemical toxic sensors may respond to interfering gases. Obtain the actual manufacturer's instructions and approved correction information.

For the trainer T84, the combustible channel is explicitly unsuitable for interpreting oxygen-deficient conditions and has no approved correction factor for an unknown solvent mixture. Those limitations are decisive even if the screen displays zero. Do not invent a correction factor from a different instrument. A diffusion monitor cannot be assumed to accept a remote hose without an approved pump arrangement.

Worked case: the oxygen channel reads low and the combustible channel reads zero. Hold the work and report that the combustible result cannot be used to clear the atmosphere with this sensor. Changed case: oxygen is normal but the hazard is a substance outside the installed sensor capability. The meter still cannot establish its absence. Select a suitable method through competent review rather than adding confidence to an irrelevant reading.

## Q84-05 — Inspect, function-test and manage calibration

Inspect the instrument identity, condition, battery, sensors, filters, inlet, accessories and status before use. Check the manufacturer's required function-test and calibration arrangements. A bump test demonstrates response and alarm function; a calibration check compares response with a known reference within specified tolerance; full calibration adjusts response. They are not interchangeable labels. Use correct unexpired traceable gas, regulator, tubing and adapters under approved instructions. Do not zero a detector in an unknown atmosphere.

In this course actual hazardous calibration gas is not released. The learner checks an inert kit and follows a trainer-generated response sequence. Real instrument qualification requires supervised work with the exact equipment and safe gas-handling arrangements. A failed check is a reason to remove the unit from the task and follow the manufacturer's recovery/service process, not to silence the indication and proceed.

Worked case: trainer certificate says a 50-unit reference must read within 45 to 55 units. It reads 44. The check fails by one unit below the lower boundary; rounding it to 45 is not allowed. Changed case: it reads 50 but the reference cylinder is expired. The apparent agreement does not validate the check. Record the defect and obtain a valid kit. A calibration sticker does not override damage or a failed daily function check.

## Q84-06 — Build a representative sampling plan

Plan where the sample comes from, how it reaches the sensor and how long each result needs. Include relevant heights, pockets, adjacent spaces, work locations and changing sources; do not rely only on gas density assumptions because ventilation, temperature and mixing affect distribution. Start from a safe external position and do not cross an entry boundary to obtain a supposedly pre-entry sample. Protect the tester and the escape route.

Remote sampling introduces transport and response delay. Use the actual approved hose type and length, flow verification, manufacturer timing and stabilization criteria. Reactive contaminants can be lost in unsuitable tubing. A blocked line, leak, wet filter or wrong adapter can make a result unrepresentative. A reading at the opening does not establish conditions at the far end.

Worked trainer calculation: approved T84 instructions specify transport allowance 3 seconds per metre plus a 30-second sensor wait, then stable display. With 8 metres, minimum timed allowance is 24 + 30 = 54 seconds per newly positioned sample, plus any further stabilization. Changed case: 12 metres gives 36 + 30 = 66 seconds. These are fictional trainer timings, not universal field rules. Use a timer, record the position and do not move to the next point early because the first numbers look favorable.

## Q84-07 — Conduct pre-entry verification and interpret sequence

The evaluation determines what hazards and methods matter; verification checks the actual atmosphere against that plan. Review oxygen before relying on oxygen-dependent combustible results, then flammability and required toxics. A multi-sensor instrument may display channels simultaneously; the interpretive dependency still matters. Record actual values and units beside the applicable criteria rather than writing only “safe.” Follow the manufacturer's response time at each location.

For scenario V84, sampling points are upper U, middle M, lower L and remote pocket P. All are external probe targets. The plan prohibits entry until every required point and channel is valid and all other permit conditions are satisfied. One failed point holds the whole proposed work scope; averaging readings from different locations is not a release method.

Worked case: U and M meet the exercise bands, but P has an alarm. Report the location-specific result, maintain the hold and activate the prescribed response. Do not discard the pocket as an outlier. Changed case: P cannot be reached by the approved probe. The gap is unresolved, not zero. Ask the competent planner for a safe suitable method. A test record should make an inaccessible point visible to the permit authority rather than hide it in a general check mark.

## Q84-08 — Test for hot work and neighboring hazards

Hot work introduces an ignition source and can release contaminants from coatings, residues or materials being heated. The testing plan must address the actual work and relevant connected or adjacent areas, not merely the operator's breathing zone. Atmospheric testing is one part of isolation, cleaning, ventilation, fire prevention and permit control. A personal monitor alone does not assess every potential ignition location.

In the fictional H84 task, grinding is proposed outside the mock vessel near a drain and a shared wall. The plan requires the work point, drain recess and opposite side of the wall to be evaluated from approved safe positions. The drain recess fails the exercise flammable criterion. Work remains held even when the open-air work point reads zero. The tester reports facts and scope; they do not redefine the permit to exclude the inconvenient location.

Changed application: a process vent is opened nearby after the initial test. Stop the authorized activity according to the plan, notify the permit authority and reassess conditions before restart. Do not assume that a test remains valid for the entire shift because the form has today's date. When a shipyard or other specialist regime requires a particular qualified person or certificate, this training does not substitute for that requirement. Confirm the governing jurisdiction and role before signing.

## Q84-09 — Monitor change and respond to alarms

A pre-work test is a snapshot. Work can alter conditions, ventilation can fail, material can leak and nearby operations can change. Follow the approved plan for continuous monitoring, personal monitoring, periodic recorded tests or a combination; do not invent one universal interval. Place equipment so that it measures the intended exposure or location and can be heard or seen as required. Check that a remote display corresponds to the correct sensor and current time.

The exercise response to an alarm, fault, lost sampling flow or failed ventilation is stop work, withdraw by the planned route without entering a hazard, notify the responsible person and maintain the hold. Do not reset and continue merely because the display later falls. Investigation, restored controls, valid retesting and authorized restart are separate steps. A low battery or sensor fault is not a clean-air indication.

Worked case: the entry team is operating under a valid plan when the pump fault alarm appears. Treat atmospheric assurance as lost, initiate the planned withdrawal and report the fault. Changed case: the instrument still works but ventilation stops. Apply the plan's stop response; a temporarily normal reading does not prove the future atmosphere will remain acceptable. Preserve the event time and trend rather than clearing the history to obtain a tidy record.

## Q84-10 — Communicate results and preserve traceability

A useful report identifies the task and location, sampling points, date/time, instrument and sensor configuration, calibration/function-check status, method and hose, actual readings and units, applicable criteria, abnormalities and the person informed. Explain limitations such as unmeasured substances, unreachable areas or an invalid sensor response. The permit authority needs to know what the test did not establish.

Use repeat-back for a critical result: location, channel/value, action and recipient acknowledgment. Avoid vague messages such as “a little high.” Record changes and corrections transparently. Do not copy yesterday's values, pre-sign a result or silently substitute another instrument number. Data logging helps but does not by itself establish where the inlet was or whether the hose was valid.

Worked case: the record lists 0 on every channel but no units. Zero percent LEL and zero ppm refer to different measures, and zero oxygen would be a severe abnormality. Correct the record using actual evidence; if unavailable, repeat the test under the approved safe plan rather than guessing. Changed case: a colleague asks you to sign their test. Identify the actual tester and use the organization's review process; do not represent an unobserved test as your own. Honest gaps are safer than fabricated assurance.

## Q84-11 — Emergencies, standby duties and rescue boundaries

Know the alarm route, muster location, communications and planned rescue arrangements before work. A person collapsing in a confined space can tempt an unprotected coworker into the same atmosphere. Do not make an impulsive entry. Raise the alarm, prevent further entry and activate the trained rescue response. Use non-entry actions only within the planned system and your training; a rope alone is not a complete rescue plan.

A tester assigned simultaneous standby duties must understand whether those duties are compatible and what continuous attention is required. Do not leave an assigned watch to fetch a meter without an authorized handover. Respiratory protection is not selected by guesswork, and an air-purifying respirator does not supply missing oxygen. This course does not qualify rescue entry, breathing-apparatus use or medical treatment.

Worked scenario: a radio call reports a worker down beyond the mock entry marker. Stay outside, stop others approaching, call the designated rescue channel and report location and last known conditions. Changed scenario: the supervisor says “just lean in and pull.” Refuse the unplanned exposure and continue the emergency plan. In classroom practice, use a paper casualty marker; no learner enters a real vessel, wears a supplied-air system or encounters a deliberately hazardous atmosphere.

## Q84-12 — Transfer training to the specific workplace

Before appointment, demonstrate the actual site's hazards, instrument configuration, sampling accessories, permit conditions, emergency actions and reporting process under competent supervision. Generic knowledge does not establish skill with every meter. If a new sensor, firmware setting, process chemical or work method changes the testing basis, review the task and retrain or reassess as needed.

This package uses a fictional trainer to teach decision making without exposing learners to gases. Its practical record must say exactly that. A workplace assessor needs evidence from the real approved equipment and representative tasks under safe conditions before confirming authorization. The employer defines the appointment and its limits; VNDRLY does not issue an OPITO certificate by using an OPITO source title.

Worked handover: your training result lists successful T84 sampling and alarm simulations. The site uses a different pumped instrument with a different hose limit. Bring the record, identify the difference and complete the site-specific demonstration rather than translating the trainer timing by intuition. Changed application: your authorization covers cold-work pre-entry testing, but you are assigned specialist hot-work certification. Refer for the required scope and competence review. Confidence, years of service and an unexpired training record cannot expand a documented remit.

# VUC084 workbook, trainer specification and delivery

## Safe resources and instructor prerequisites

Use an inert non-detecting trainer or an actual instrument only in a manufacturer-approved training/simulation mode in a confirmed safe classroom. A non-detecting trainer must be marked NOT FOR SAFETY USE. It must never be mistaken for a working monitor. Provide a tabletop vessel drawing, four probe sockets open to ordinary room air, inert hose, dry filter, mock regulator/cylinder cards, clock, HOLD tag, blank records and communication role-player. No toxic, flammable, oxygen-enriched or oxygen-displacing gas is released. No live confined space, hot work, pressurized process, rescue entry or respiratory apparatus exercise is part of this program.

Instructor must understand gas detection and the selected real equipment sufficiently to explain the boundary between trainer and workplace use. Classroom groups maximum eight; equipment stations maximum four with individual observed turns. Everyone completes their own task and record. Use accessible display cards and oral explanation where appropriate; learners must still distinguish units and act on critical conditions. If real equipment is demonstrated, its actual manual governs operation. Fictional T84 values must never be loaded into a real operational detector as safety settings.

## T84 fictional instrument instruction card — complete exercise basis

T84 is an inert training interface with four displayed channels: oxygen percent volume, combustible percent LEL on fictional gas G basis, hydrogen sulfide ppm and carbon monoxide ppm. It is not a safety device. It does not measure the unidentified solvent in V84. The training combustible channel must not be used to interpret low-oxygen or unknown-mixture conditions. Display range cards may show numeric readings, OVER, FAULT or LOW BATTERY; any invalid state holds the task. No student may change limits, suppress alarms or invent correction factors.

Approved inert accessory is hose H84, maximum 12 metres, with dry filter F84 and mock pump P84. Check secure connections, damage, kinks and water indication. At startup the mock pump flow card must show FLOW OK. When the instructor covers the external probe socket, the trainer must show BLOCK/FAULT; releasing it restores flow only after the instructed reset in safe classroom conditions. This is a training demonstration, not a universal real-device sequence. A failed block response means remove the setup from the task.

Timing for each newly positioned sample: 3 seconds per metre of connected hose plus 30 seconds sensor allowance, followed by a stable display card. Start timing after the probe is at the target and flow is confirmed. If the stable card is not supplied at the minimum time, wait; the minimum is not permission to record a changing reading. At 8 metres use at least 54 seconds; at 12 metres use at least 66 seconds. Clear the previous sample according to the trainer sequence before moving; instructor supplies the reset-to-room-air card. Real sampling purge, adsorption and response behavior require the actual manufacturer method.

Trainer pre-use check cards: unit T84-01, sensor configuration as above, battery FULL, casing intact, filter dry, calibration status CURRENT, previous simulated function result PASS yesterday. Today’s function check is required. Mock reference label REF-50 states 50 units, acceptable verification 45–55 inclusive; this is a generic response channel, not a gas mixture recipe. Card X is expired yesterday and cannot validate a check. Card Y is in date and traceable to fictional certificate CY-84. Initial Y response 44 = FAIL. After instructor-documented simulated service and full calibration, response 50 and all audible/visible/vibration simulation indicators PASS. Learner must retain the failure record and only return the trainer to the exercise after the successful recovery card. Real gas work requires suitable ventilation, handling, PPE and manufacturer-approved equipment by trained personnel; it is not simulated by opening an actual cylinder here.

## P84 exercise acceptance and response rules

These deliberately conservative fictional scenario bands are NOT legal exposure limits, universal permit criteria or instrument settings: oxygen 20.0–21.0 percent volume inclusive; combustible less than 1 percent LEL; H2S less than 1 ppm; CO less than 5 ppm. Every required location/channel must be valid and meet its band. Exactly 1 percent LEL, 1 ppm H2S or 5 ppm CO fails the strict less-than criterion. No rounding of a failed value into a pass. Unmeasured hazards, faults, missing required samples, failed ventilation, changed work or missing permit controls also HOLD. The normal classroom oxygen card 20.8 is a simulated value, not an instruction to zero actual equipment to that number.

An alarm/fault or lost required control means stop the simulated activity, withdraw along the marked safe route, notify permit issuer Nia Stone on simulated channel 2, prevent new entry and await investigation/retest/authorized restart. No actual emergency call is made. If an actual emergency occurs, stop the class and use the real site emergency plan. Classroom role-play must never delay a real response.

## E84 emergency and communication script

Role-player: “A worker is down beyond A; go get them.” Learner remains outside, calls the exercise rescue coordinator on channel 9, states V-12/access A, last known atmosphere and number of people reported, prevents another entry and follows the planned safe actions. No learner acts as a casualty in a confined area. Changed cue: “Wear this dust mask and lean in.” Correct response is refusal of unplanned exposure and continued emergency coordination. The exercise does not assess actual rescue or first aid competence.

Critical report model: “Nia, V-12 remote pocket P at 09:24: combustible 2 percent LEL; exercise requirement is less than 1. Work remains held. No entry authorized. Other results and instrument details are in F84. Please repeat the location and hold.” Receiver repeats the location, result and action. Times in model scripts are examples; actual observed exercise times belong in the learner record.

## I84 integrated assessment

Individually perform V84 from initial hazard review through instrument readiness, four timed samples, hold report and documented retest. Then respond to one undisclosed M84 fault and B84 changed task. Instructor may explain task wording but may not supply decisions. Ten dimensions: remit/hazard gaps; criteria/units; instrument validity; sampling setup; timing/stability; representative coverage; interpretation/hold; alarm response; reporting/traceability; workplace and rescue boundaries. Score each 0–4 using station scale. Require at least 32/40, every dimension at least 3 and no critical error, plus all twelve stations passed. Keep a separate criterion record; a perfect written exam cannot replace physical trainer performance.

## Q84-01-L1

Does a satisfactory gas test authorize entry?

**Answer:** No; the designated permit authority must confirm all required controls.

**Reason/remediation:** Atmospheric evidence is only one part of authorization. Revisit Q84-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q84-01-L2

Does initial training confer employer appointment?

**Answer:** No; workplace development, competence assessment and appointment remain separate.

**Reason/remediation:** Generic knowledge is not equipment- and site-specific authority. Revisit Q84-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q84-02-L1

Can normal oxygen rule out a solvent hazard?

## Q84-02-L2

Can smell verify absence of H2S?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Odor is unreliable and approaching to sniff creates exposure risk. Revisit Q84-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q84-03-L1

Convert 0.3 percent volume to ppm.

**Answer:** 3,000 ppm.

Is 10 percent LEL the same as 10 percent gas?

**Answer:** No.

## Deliberate boundaries

All T84 timings, G/J/K conversion data, P84 bands, response tolerances, packet identifiers and simulated readings are original teaching assumptions. They cannot replace a real hazard evaluation, exposure limit, manufacturer instruction or permit. No live gas release, entry, ignition, rescue or respiratory-protection assessment occurs. Actual workplace equipment demonstration and appointment remain explicit requirements for operational use. Materials complete does not mean a learner is qualified or a course is published.
