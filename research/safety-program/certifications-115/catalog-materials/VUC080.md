# VNDRLY/U International Industry Safety Foundations

VUC080 · Original materials · 18 November 2026

This program addresses the nine public IMIST topic families for new industry entrants and workers needing a broad safety foundation. It is a VNDRLY award, not an OPITO-approved IMIST certificate or a Vantage registration. No prerequisite qualification is required; accessible reading/arithmetic support is available. Completion does not qualify specialist work, replace site induction or establish offshore medical fitness.

Outcomes: recognize hazards; make a factual observation; participate in risk assessment and control of work; report integrity issues; plan benign handling; interpret substance controls; recognize fall and lifting exposures. Demonstrations are classroom or ground-level only.

## Q80-01 — Recognize the hazardous environment

Energy sites combine process hazards with everyday work hazards. A pressurized hydrocarbon system can release fuel; an ignition source can turn a release into a fire or explosion. A toxic or oxygen-deficient atmosphere may harm people without a visible flame. Vehicles, moving equipment, electrical energy, noise, weather and work near water can cause injury even when the process is operating normally. A major accident can affect many people and systems; a personal injury event can affect one person. Both deserve prevention, but low injury numbers do not prove major-accident barriers are healthy.

Before beginning work, locate the actual work area, access restrictions, emergency signal, reporting route and assembly point. Identify what could reach you from nearby work: a swinging load, a vehicle route, a release, falling objects or another crew's test. Remote sites also require planning for communication and medical response. A familiar job at an unfamiliar installation is not automatically familiar work.

Ask three questions: what energy or substance could cause harm, how could I or someone else be exposed, and what verified control prevents that exposure? 'Be careful' is not a physical barrier. A painted line alone does not stop a vehicle; a warning sign alone does not contain pressure. Controls must fit the actual hazard and remain effective as the work changes.

Worked case: a worker is sent to read a label beside a pipe rack. The route passes below a lifting operation, and a nearby leak alarm has activated. The worker should not treat the quick duration as permission to enter. Remain outside the affected zone, notify the responsible contact and follow the emergency direction. Getting the label can wait; exposure cannot be undone.

Changed case: the alarm clears, but the lifting exclusion remains. Do not assume all restrictions ended together. Confirm the status of each relevant control. This course teaches recognition and safe participation; it does not qualify the learner for rescue, gas testing or process shutdown duties.

## Q80-02 — Work safely and report useful observations

A safety management system links responsibilities, planning, competence, work procedures, emergency arrangements, reporting and improvement. A site-specific system should tell you who controls the work and how to raise a concern. Where a safety-case approach applies, the duty holder demonstrates how major hazards are managed; an individual worker still needs practical instructions for today's task. Different jurisdictions use different legal arrangements. Do not assume a rule from one country is the universal legal requirement everywhere.

You contribute by using the agreed method, checking your understanding, reporting changes and refusing work outside your competence or controls. Supervisors and employers must provide the conditions and resources for safe work; safety is not transferred entirely to the last person holding a tool. Fatigue, language, workload and conflicting instructions can undermine a good procedure. Raise those conditions early rather than hiding them to appear capable.

A useful observation describes the task, location, condition, potential harm, immediate protection and requested follow-up. It focuses on evidence, not blame. Report a near miss even when nobody was injured. Record a positive control when it genuinely prevented exposure, but do not use a quota of positive cards to suppress a serious concern. After reporting, know who owns the action and how closure will be verified.

Worked observation: 'At access lane B at 09:10, stored pallets obscured the marked pedestrian crossing. A forklift was approaching. I stayed outside the lane and contacted the area supervisor. Please restore a protected pedestrian route before access resumes.' This is more actionable than 'operator careless.' It identifies the condition and the control needed.

Changed case: a supervisor says the pallets have always been there. Explain the present exposure and seek the site escalation route if unresolved. Do not cross to prove the hazard. An observation card is not a substitute for an immediate warning when someone is in danger. Preserve factual records, including disagreement and acknowledgment, without adding accusations.

## Q80-03 — Assess risk and verify controls

Hazard identification asks what can harm people; risk assessment considers the exposure, possible consequence and likelihood under actual conditions. Break a job into steps, identify who may be affected, select controls, assign responsibility and verify that controls exist before starting. Include maintenance staff, contractors, visitors and nearby crews rather than only the person performing the central task.

Prefer eliminating the hazardous task or exposure where feasible. If it remains, consider safer methods or substances, engineered separation, administrative arrangements and suitable personal protective equipment. PPE can be necessary but does not make a failed primary control acceptable. Controls can interact: a screen that contains debris may obstruct an escape route, and ventilation can carry a contaminant toward another workgroup.

A risk matrix is a decision aid, not a measurement of safety. This course's fictional matrix uses likelihood 1 unlikely, 2 possible, 3 likely, and severity 1 minor, 2 serious, 3 potentially fatal. Multiply to prioritize discussion only. For the classroom, any missing critical control means HOLD regardless of score. A reduction in likelihood must have a reason supported by an implemented control, not simply a lower number written to obtain approval.

Worked case: a person could be struck by a moving load, likelihood 2 and severity 3: score 6. A verified exclusion and controlled route may reduce the fictional likelihood to 1, score 3; the potential severity remains 3. If the exclusion is removed, the lower score no longer has its basis. The job must be reassessed and the exposure controlled, not continued because the form was signed earlier.

Changed case: weather reduces visibility during the lift. Stop the affected activity through the work-control process and reassess with the responsible team. A last-minute personal check is useful but cannot replace a formal assessment, competent lift plan or permit where required. Record residual concerns and the person authorized to accept them.

## Q80-04 — Understand permits and the control of work

A permit-to-work system coordinates specified work under defined conditions. It identifies scope, location, equipment, responsible people, hazards, precautions, validity and handback. It is communication and authorization within the site's system, not a guarantee that every hazard has disappeared. Some work needs a permit because of its interfaces or energy exposure; the site's rules determine the categories and authorities.

Read the actual scope and limits before signing acceptance. Confirm that the work area and equipment identifiers match. Understand required isolations, tests, barriers and other linked permits. Do not sign for a check you did not perform or are not qualified to verify. A permit receiver and an isolation authority may have different responsibilities. A padlock or tag is not a complete explanation of the isolation boundary.

Work can become invalid when the task, crew, equipment, conditions or adjacent operations change. Follow suspension and revalidation rules. An alarm may require stopping and leaving the work area; a shift change may require a formal handover. Do not extend a validity time yourself or borrow a neighboring crew's permit. At completion, account for people, tools and condition, communicate incomplete work and obtain the required handback before equipment is returned to service.

Worked case: permit P80 covers cold inspection of pump A from 08:00 to 12:00. At 11:40 the crew proposes grinding a seized fastener. Grinding introduces a different activity and possible ignition source. The existing permit does not automatically cover it. Suspend the changed scope and refer to the issuing authority for assessment and authorization before proceeding.

Changed case: no grinding is needed, but the job continues past 12:00. The scope may be unchanged while authorization has expired. Stop and follow extension/reissue rules. A verbal 'nearly finished' does not alter the written control. Site-specific training is required before acting as an actual permit issuer, receiver or isolation specialist.

## Q80-05 — Protect asset integrity

Asset integrity means equipment and supporting systems can perform their intended functions without unacceptable harm. It includes design, installation, operation, inspection, maintenance and controlled change. A safety-critical function may prevent an event or limit its consequences. Examples include containment, detection, shutdown, fire protection and escape arrangements. Some failures remain hidden until a demand occurs; apparently normal production is not evidence that every protective system works.

Workers support integrity by identifying equipment correctly, staying within operating instructions and reporting deterioration or abnormal behavior. A missing guard, damaged support, leak, blocked escape, overdue inspection or inhibited detector should not disappear into an informal assumption. Describe the condition and its possible effect, then use the designated defect process. Competent personnel decide repair, isolation, compensating measures and any permitted continued operation.

An impairment is not controlled merely because it is known. It needs an authorized decision, defined restrictions or alternative protection where appropriate, a responsible owner and a time/review condition. Do not bypass an alarm to stop nuisance messages or reset a trip repeatedly without understanding the cause. Unauthorized modification can remove a barrier for people who do not know it changed.

Worked case: detector D80 is shown as inhibited in the control record while hot work is proposed nearby. An observer cannot approve the combination. Report the impairment to the work-control authority and hold the affected work until the responsible team evaluates the combined condition. 'Maintenance already knows' does not resolve the permit interface.

Changed case: a repair work order is marked closed, but the physical guard is still missing. Report the mismatch and retain the restriction. Administrative closure and restored function are not identical. A completion record should reflect the actual equipment and evidence, not a desire to reduce the backlog.

## Q80-06 — Plan manual handling rather than proving strength

Manual handling includes carrying, lifting, lowering, pushing, pulling and supporting a load. Injury risk depends on the task, load, person and environment, not only mass. A light awkward object carried repeatedly through a narrow route can be difficult. A wet grip, sharp edge, unstable contents, poor visibility or twisted posture can change the risk. There is no single kilogram value that makes every lift safe for everyone.

First ask whether the handling can be avoided or redesigned. Deliver closer to the point of use, reduce package size where appropriate, use a suitable handling aid, or change storage height and route. A team lift is not automatically safe: people need a coordinated method, adequate space and a stable load. Each participant must be able to stop. Do not infer safe team capacity by adding individual lifting limits.

For the benign training object, inspect the route and destination, assess the grip and balance, keep the object close where practicable, move smoothly and turn with the feet instead of twisting under load. Set down before adjusting an insecure grip. Stop if pain, loss of balance or unexpected weight occurs. Technique supplements risk reduction; it does not justify a poorly designed task.

Worked case: a sealed box weighs 18 kg, must be carried 40 m and blocks the worker's view. Its mass alone cannot establish safety. Consider a suitable trolley and clear route, delivery closer to use or smaller packages after assessing the contents. Do not ask the learner to prove the lift by trying it.

Changed case: the box is only 5 kg but has a hot surface and no secure handhold. The reduced mass does not remove those hazards. Arrange a safe method and appropriate equipment. Physical practice in this package uses only an empty lightweight training box, with individual consent and adjustments; no maximum-strength test is permitted.

## Q80-07 — Control hazardous substances and exposure

Hazardous substances include purchased products and materials generated by work, such as dust, fume, mist or gas. Exposure can occur by breathing, skin or eye contact, swallowing contamination, or other task-specific routes. Harm can be immediate or develop after repeated exposure. A product's smell, familiar brand or small quantity cannot establish that its use is safe.

Read the correct label and current safety data sheet, then relate the information to the actual task. Identification, hazards, first aid, handling/storage, exposure controls and incompatibilities help inform the assessment. An SDS alone is not the task risk assessment. Spraying, heating or using a product in a confined area can create a different exposure from a small wipe application. Process-generated contaminants may have no purchased-product label.

Control exposure at source where practicable: avoid the substance or process, use a suitable safer alternative, enclose or capture emissions, and apply the assessed work method. PPE selection must match the substance and exposure; not every glove protects against every chemical, and a generic dust mask is not a universal respirator. Respiratory protection requires the appropriate program, selection, fit and training. Do not improvise chemical mixing or neutralization.

Worked case: an unlabeled bottle is offered as a cleaning product. The worker should not sniff or test it. Segregate/report it through the site's procedure and obtain correct identification and assessment before use. If a release occurs, follow the emergency instructions and only undertake cleanup within training and authorization.

Changed case: the correct product is identified, but local extraction is not working and the task has changed from wiping to spraying. The previous control assumptions no longer apply. Hold the changed task, report the failed control and obtain reassessment. The correct label does not make an ineffective exposure-control system acceptable.

## Q80-08 — Recognize work-at-height and dropped-object risks

A fall can injure a person even when the work is brief or relatively low. Edges, openings, fragile surfaces and access equipment all need consideration. The first question is whether the task can be done without exposure to a fall. If not, select suitable prevention and protection through competent planning. Collective protection such as a suitable guarded platform protects without relying solely on each person's attachment action.

A ladder, scaffold, mobile platform and personal fall system are not interchangeable. Selection depends on task, duration, access, surface, weather and rescue needs. Training in this awareness course does not authorize scaffold alteration, platform operation, anchorage selection or fall-arrest use. A harness alone is not a complete system; compatibility, clearance, anchorage, inspection and rescue arrangements matter.

Protect people below as well as those above. Tools and materials can fall or be displaced. Establish the required exclusion and secure items using an approved arrangement; do not improvise attachments that create other hazards. Maintain safe access and escape. A closed access gate or missing scaffold tag must be resolved through the site's competent process, not bypassed because the work will take a minute.

Worked case: a label can be photographed from ground level using a suitable remote method. This avoids climbing solely to read it. If a later task requires hands-on work, that is a new assessment, not permission inherited from the photograph job.

Changed case: a worker proposes standing on a box beside an unprotected opening. Stop the unsafe approach and obtain a suitable planned method. Do not teach a universal height threshold below which no precautions are needed; the possibility and consequences of a fall govern the assessment. In this course all diagrams and demonstrations remain at floor level.

## Q80-09 — Stay safe around mechanical lifting

Mechanical lifting moves a load with equipment such as a crane or hoist. Safe execution depends on a competent plan, suitable equipment, known load information, appropriate rigging, ground or support conditions, communication and exclusion of people from danger. An awareness learner should recognize these dependencies without pretending to be a lift planner, rigger or crane operator.

A suspended load can move, rotate or fall. Keep clear of the load path, pinch points and the space between a load and a fixed object. Never walk under a suspended load to save time. Do not catch or steady a moving load with your body. Tagline use, signaling and rigging tasks require specific instruction and authorization; a nearby worker should not spontaneously take over a signaler's role.

Inspection tags and rated-capacity markings provide information but do not prove suitability for every arrangement. Sling angle, hitch, load distribution, center of gravity and attachment condition can affect the lift. Missing identification, damage or changed configuration must be referred to the competent lifting team. The absence of visible damage is not a complete inspection.

Worked case: a planned 900 kg load will use 100 kg of lifting accessories. For this fictional planning check, total suspended mass is 1,000 kg before any other items the actual plan must include. A worker who reports only the 900 kg item has omitted part of the load. That calculation alone does not approve a crane or sling; configuration and other requirements remain.

Changed case: the route is moved closer to a pedestrian walkway after the briefing. The old exclusion arrangement may no longer protect people. Stop the affected activity through the designated communication process and have the competent team reassess. Everyone can raise an immediate danger, but routine lifting signals and restart authority must remain clear. The integrated course exercise combines this interface with permits, chemicals and asset condition so learners practice recognizing conflicting controls.

# VUC080 workbook and instructor delivery

## Scope, schedule and safe resources

Plan 20 contact hours over at least three days: 6 hours instruction/demonstration, 4 guided labs, 4 individual stations, 2 integrated assessment/debrief, 1.5 final exam, 2.5 feedback and correction. Breaks are additional; do not exceed 8 contact hours/day or 2 consecutive contact hours without a break. These are this program's delivery arrangements, not a claim of OPITO approval or identical duration. Additional reassessment time is allowed when needed.

Instructor must be competent in general energy-industry safety and control of work, and competent to supervise the benign handling exercise. Seek a specialist's input when adapting examples; do not replace specialist competence with this course. Print all cards and forms below, provide pens/calculators, a floor-level map, a stable bench and an empty lightweight box with sound handles. Verify the room, route and individual suitability before physical practice. No pressurized systems, hazardous chemicals, suspended loads, real heights, gas testing, isolation of working plant or genuine emergency signals are used. Label every mock permit and substance sheet TRAINING ONLY.

One instructor observes one physical handling learner at a time; other learners remain outside the movement area. Participants may stop without penalty for discomfort. Adjust object, method, route and access to individual needs. If a safe physical demonstration cannot be offered, record theory completion and arrange an appropriate observed alternative before awarding the complete program; do not record a paper answer as physical performance. Accessible oral/digital versions may assess knowledge without changing safety criteria. The basic demonstration is not certification for all handling tasks.

## W80-03 — Redesign and demonstrate handling

Discuss the 18 kg, 40 m, vision-blocking case without lifting it. List alternatives: closer delivery, suitable aid, redesigned package/method and clear route. Then use only the empty training box for an individually suitable short move to the stable bench. Learner plans, checks grip and destination, moves smoothly without twisting under load and sets down safely. Instructor observes each person. Add paper obstruction before the changed move; learner must pause and resolve it. Do not teach a universal maximum mass or use team capacity addition. Retain a record of actual demonstration and any adjustment.

## W80-04 — Controls across substances, heights and lifting

Divide the paper task into three activities: cleaner use, label access and incoming equipment lift. Use S80/H80/L80. For each, identify what can be eliminated, what competent decision is needed and what would stop the task. Changed case combines unreadable sling identity with a route across B; both must be resolved. Every learner submits their own control table and explains one changed condition from each activity.

## I80 — Integrated workday decision exercise

Learner is a general worker joining a maintenance task, not a permit issuer, rigger, chemist or isolation specialist. Instructor plays area authority and issues the cards in order. Resources are M80, P80, D80, S80, H80, L80 and blank O80/R80 forms. All activity is tabletop except the separately assessed benign handling station.

Practice sequence: arrival briefing; blocked crossing report; pump permit with missing verification; grinding change; detector impairment; cleaner method change; label access decision; changed lifting route; alarm and muster; truthful handback. At each step learner states hold/proceed-within-scope, evidence and responsible decision. 'Proceed' is acceptable only for a safe preparatory activity explicitly supported by the cards, such as completing a report outside restrictions. No live task is approved by this exercise.

Independent assessment A: equipment A180, cold inspection 08:00–10:00, verification blank; at 09:30 grinding requested; detector D180 inhibited without disposition; spray task proposed with failed extraction; item 750 kg plus 80 kg accessories; route crosses blocked B; remote label reading available. Alarm occurs with south route clear. Expected: resolve identity/scope/verification, hold grinding/chemical/lift work, use remote reading only when safe, report 830 kg as a partial planning total not lift approval, muster A and preserve incomplete work in handback.

Changed reassessment B: equipment A280, cold inspection 13:00–15:00; at 15:10 same work remains; documented impairment review time has expired; generated dust from unknown base material replaces cleaner task; hands-on label replacement replaces remote reading; item 640 kg plus 60 kg accessories but sling ID missing; south route blocked so west/C applies. Expected: expired authorization requires hold/reissue, review impairment, obtain material/exposure assessment, competent height plan, 700 kg does not resolve sling suitability, muster C and truthful unresolved-status handover.

Individual rubric: six dimensions scored 0 unsafe/missing, 1 heavily prompted, 2 safe but incomplete, 3 independent/correct, 4 independent/correct with clear reasoning: hazard/interfaces; reporting; risk/control verification; permit/integrity boundaries; substance/height/lifting decisions; emergency/handback. Require 20/24 and every dimension at least 3, no critical error. Critical errors include knowingly entering a release/lift/fall exposure, starting under missing critical control, bypassing protection, unsafe chemical experimentation, falsifying verification or ignoring emergency direction. ## Controlled final and completion

Nine written cases, three decisions each, 54 points, 90 minutes. Pass at least 44/54, at least 4/6 in every domain and no unresolved critical error. Score each decision 2 correct with reason, 1 safe but incomplete, 0 wrong/missing/unsafe. Coursebook/calculator allowed; private keys, collaboration and outside assistance prohibited. Form B changes each case and includes enough original context; score only its changed decisions. Keep forms/keys in the private instructor directory, not learner handouts.

Completion requires all nine chapters, all eighteen formative checks corrected, four labs, nine individual stations including the benign demonstration, integrated exercise and final. Written performance alone is not the full award. For deficiencies, identify the misconception, reteach the relevant section, require a changed application, and reassess failed practical work; after a final failure use the entire changed form. Repeated failures require a newly reviewed equivalent form rather than recycling exposed answers. Preserve attempt history and accommodations. A second competent assessor handles appeals against retained evidence.

Issue VNDRLY/U International Industry Safety Foundations with version/hash, learner identity, date, assessor and scope. Record instruction, station observations, actual demonstration, integrated scores, final, remediation and source version. It is not an OPITO IMIST certificate, Vantage record, offshore medical clearance, permit-role appointment or specialist authorization. Employer/site induction and task-specific training remain required. VNDRLY review interval is two years or sooner after significant change, incident or demonstrated gap; this internal rule is not the external IMIST validity rule. Publication and actual learner delivery are separate from creation of this package.

# VUC080 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q80-01-L1

A brief label-reading task crosses a lifting exclusion. May duration justify entry?

**Answer:** No. Remain outside and obtain a safe authorized access method.

**Reason/remediation:** Short exposure can still have severe consequences; the barrier applies until released. Revisit Q80-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-01-L2

Does a low personal-injury count prove major-accident protection is healthy?

**Answer:** No. Protective systems may have hidden failures despite normal daily work.

**Reason/remediation:** Different indicators describe different aspects of safety. Revisit Q80-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-02-L1

Rewrite operator careless as a useful observation for pallets obscuring a crossing.

**Answer:** Identify lane/time, obscured crossing, approaching vehicle, immediate safe position and requested restoration of a protected route.

**Reason/remediation:** Evidence and an actionable control are more useful than blame. Revisit Q80-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-02-L2

A worker struggles to understand a briefing. What should happen?

**Answer:** Pause for an accessible explanation and confirm understanding before work.

**Reason/remediation:** Signing without understanding defeats the communication control. Revisit Q80-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-03-L1

In the fictional matrix likelihood 2 and severity 3 give what score?

**Answer:** 6.

**Reason/remediation:** The product prioritizes discussion; it does not itself authorize work. Revisit Q80-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-03-L2

Likelihood is reduced to 1 after an exclusion is verified, then the exclusion is removed. What changes?

**Answer:** The basis for score 3 is lost; hold/reassess the affected work.

**Reason/remediation:** A lower written number cannot replace the missing critical control. Revisit Q80-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-04-L1

Cold inspection permit changes to grinding. Does the old scope cover it?

**Answer:** Not automatically. Suspend the changed work and refer to the issuing authority.

**Reason/remediation:** The new ignition/activity hazards require assessment and authorization. Revisit Q80-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-04-L2

An unchanged task passes its permit end time. May nearly finished justify continuation?

**Answer:** No. Follow suspension and extension/reissue rules before continuing.

**Reason/remediation:** Validity is part of authorization even when physical scope is unchanged. Revisit Q80-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-05-L1

A detector is inhibited near proposed hot work. What must an observer do?

**Answer:** Report the impairment and interface; hold affected work for the responsible control-of-work decision.

**Reason/remediation:** Known impairment is not equivalent to controlled risk. Revisit Q80-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-05-L2

Work order closed but guard absent: what is the status?

**Answer:** A physical/record mismatch remains and must be reported; do not assume restored protection.

**Reason/remediation:** Administrative closure is not functional restoration. Revisit Q80-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-06-L1

An 18 kg box blocks vision over a 40 m carry. What determines the method?

**Answer:** Task, load, person and route; consider avoiding/redesigning or a suitable aid.

**Reason/remediation:** Mass alone is not a universal safe-lift criterion. Revisit Q80-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-06-L2

A 5 kg item is hot with no secure grip. Is lighter enough?

**Answer:** No. Control heat, grip and handling conditions before any movement.

**Reason/remediation:** Reduced mass does not remove other exposure. Revisit Q80-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-07-L1

What should be done with an unidentified cleaning bottle?

**Answer:** Do not sniff/use it; segregate/report under procedure and obtain correct identification and assessment.

**Reason/remediation:** Testing by exposure creates risk rather than reliable identification. Revisit Q80-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-07-L2

Correct product, failed extraction and new spraying method: proceed?

**Answer:** No. Hold the changed task and reassess effective exposure controls.

**Reason/remediation:** Label accuracy does not establish control of the actual process. Revisit Q80-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-08-L1

A remote ground-level photograph can read the required label. Why consider it first?

**Answer:** It can eliminate the need for fall exposure for that task.

**Reason/remediation:** Avoiding exposure is preferable to adding fall protection to an unnecessary climb. Revisit Q80-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-08-L2

Does a harness alone authorize work beside an opening?

**Answer:** No. A planned compatible system, competence, inspection and rescue arrangements are required.

**Reason/remediation:** An isolated item is not a complete protection system. Revisit Q80-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-09-L1

Fictional load 900 kg plus accessories 100 kg: what total must not be omitted?

**Answer:** 1,000 kg, before other items required by the actual plan.

**Reason/remediation:** The item alone understates suspended mass; total alone still does not approve the lift. Revisit Q80-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q80-09-L2

The lift route moves toward pedestrians after briefing. What is needed?

**Answer:** Stop affected work and have the competent team reassess route, exclusion and communication.

**Reason/remediation:** The old controls may not protect the new interface. Revisit Q80-09 and perform its changed application.
