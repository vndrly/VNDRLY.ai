# VNDRLY/U HLO for Normally Unattended Installations — Further Training

VUC097. Original available-source refresher for previously prepared personnel. Focus: changed procedures and emergency skills rarely practiced at work. No external renewal, workplace appointment or unrestricted specialist authority is conferred.

## Q97-1 — Further-training entry and performance review

Further training refreshes established NUI HLO preparation and exercises events rarely encountered in normal work. It is not an accelerated initial course for an unprepared learner. Verify the relevant initial/further history, current role and local competence arrangements before entry. Review recent practice, incidents, near misses and individual gaps without treating years of service as proof that emergency skills remain current.

Agree the functions to be refreshed and the limits of the outcome. Current DG, radio, weather, refuelling and other specialist preparation remain separately governed. Check fitness for the planned safe simulations. A candidate who lacks the prerequisite foundation needs appropriate development rather than an unsupported renewal label. Record independent performance, prompts and remediation; attendance alone is not the outcome.

Worked and changed applications: A candidate has current routine work but has never practiced loss of remote support. Include that gap in assessed simulation. Changed case: the prior certificate cannot be authenticated. Verify it through the authorized process; do not issue a further-training outcome based on an unverified photograph.

## Q97-2 — Change review and replacing outdated habits

Compare the current controlled manual, emergency plan, equipment arrangements and communications with those used at the learner’s last training. Record what changed, its operational consequence and the required action. Updates may affect role boundaries, equipment alternatives, status reporting or final-departure handover. Do not merely list new document titles; teach how a changed control alters decisions.

The current CAP 437 amendment includes a structured equipment-unserviceability reference, but local application still requires its actual conditions and authority. A newly documented alternative is not automatic permission to operate with any defect. Verify the applicable current revision and local procedure. Use a change register linking old practice, new requirement, affected roles and demonstrated understanding. Never invent a regulatory change to make a refresher appear current.

Worked and changed applications: Old practice treated a sent status message as completed handover; the current local exercise procedure requires acknowledged receipt. Demonstrate the new confirmation step. Changed case: a colleague insists the old shortcut worked last year. Explain and apply the current controlled requirement, then record the learning gap.

## Q97-3 — Reassessing the NUI operating concept

Revisit the responsibilities before first arrival, during occupancy and at final departure. Ask which capabilities are automatic, remote or dependent on visiting personnel, and how their availability is established. A staffed-installation assumption can creep into routine NUI work: someone imagines a resident team will handle a defect or account for a missing person. Expose those assumptions during planning.

Evaluate changes in visit duration, staffing, work scope and external support. If the planned resource or route no longer exists, the old plan is not adequate simply because the flight is booked. Define the hold, escalation owner and evidence needed to resume. A refresher should require the learner to explain how a changed operating concept affects both normal and emergency phases, not merely recite a checklist.

Worked and changed applications: The return flight is delayed while the planned remote support shift ends. Confirm replacement coverage and actual contingency arrangements rather than assuming the old contact remains available. Changed case: one trained team member is unavailable; reassess assigned duties and emergency capability before proceeding.

## Q97-4 — Information quality under degraded conditions

Experienced personnel may trust familiar dashboards too readily. Distinguish a current valid reading from stale data, a frozen display, a fault flag or a missing required field. Report source, time, units and uncertainty. A recent timestamp does not establish validity if the sensor is faulty, and absence of an alarm does not prove protection is available.

Use approved alternatives only with every required condition satisfied. If an alternate communication or measurement path fails, reassess the operating status immediately. Do not average conflicting safety-critical reports to produce a reassuring number. Determine the authoritative source and notify responsible parties. Assessment should challenge the learner with multiple individually plausible records and require a traceable decision.

Worked and changed applications: A display refreshed at 11:00 shows a measurement last captured at 10:40. At 11:03 the measurement is 23 minutes old, not three. Changed case: the alternative report is current but its location is wrong. Reject its use for the intended site and obtain appropriate information or preserve the restriction.

## Q97-5 — Team leadership and closed-loop communication

Refresh clear task allocation, briefing and confirmation under time pressure. State who owns each action and what completed status must be reported. Acknowledgment that a message was heard is not evidence that a valve, route, roster or remote action is in the required state. Invite challenge and keep unresolved conditions visible rather than allowing confidence or seniority to silence concerns.

During communication loss, follow the approved fallback and its limits. Do not combine fragments from different channels into an assumed instruction. A readback that changes the location or action needs correction before execution. Maintain a concise event log and handover so incoming personnel can reconstruct actual status. The exercise should test both sending an accurate message and detecting an inaccurate response.

Worked and changed applications: HLO requests confirmation that route R2 is clear; reply says R1 clear. Correct the mismatch rather than treating any clear message as success. Changed case: relief staff accepts the handover but repeats the wrong manifest version. Resolve the version and confirm understanding.

## Q97-6 — First arrival and normal-operation drift

Refresh the approved first-arrival transition and local establishment of duties. Routine familiarity can lead to premature movement, skipped inspection or retrospective checklist completion. Observe actual conditions when access is safe, compare them with remote information and communicate discrepancies. Do not let a successful landing be treated as proof that every ground control is in place.

Maintain aircraft-specific access controls, passenger identity and item reconciliation, briefing and crew interface during attended operations. A late change or dropped object can create a new hazard after earlier checks passed. The learner must stop and update rather than rely on momentum. Assessment should use safe mock conditions that challenge habits without exposing anyone to rotors, fuel or live aircraft movement.

Worked and changed applications: A familiar route is blocked after arrival. Use the approved alternative and rebrief, not memory. Changed case: the final headcount matches but an unfamiliar substitute is present. Resolve identity and current manifest; equal count is not enough.

## Q97-7 — Emergency preplan refresh and resource decisions

Review credible events against actual available people, protection, routes, communications and outside support. Identify tasks the team can safely perform and tasks requiring specialist resources. An emergency plan must remain achievable if a team member becomes a casualty or a route closes. Do not assign one person simultaneously to incompatible duties simply to make the staffing table balance.

Prioritize alarm, protection of people, accountability and appropriate support within the actual plan. Reassess when the situation changes. A preplanned action may become unsafe after loss of protection or blocked access. Maintain a route out and refuse unsupported entry or rescue demands. Refreshing rarely practiced skills means testing these decisions, not creating a more hazardous exercise.

Worked and changed applications: Plan assigns the same person to remote communications and a task that prevents them communicating. Identify the conflict and obtain an authorized revised arrangement. Changed case: external support estimate increases; maintain the approved safe contingency rather than attempting an untrained rescue to meet the earlier estimate.

## Q97-8 — Protection-system failure and authorized intervention

Know the current installed-system indications and approved response to activation, fault and loss of remote control. The objective is to manage the condition within authority, not to make every display green. A stopped discharge or cleared alarm does not necessarily establish that the hazard is gone. Confirm actual status through the responsible process.

Do not reset, isolate or bypass protection without the required authority and verified conditions. An approved alternative remains valid only while all its conditions hold. If a conditional relay or resource fails, withdraw the assumed capability and update restrictions. Simulated control panels allow safe practice of these decisions; they do not qualify maintenance or unrestricted firefighting.

Worked and changed applications: Remote support authorizes a specific action only after condition C is confirmed, but C remains unknown. Do not execute early. Changed case: the alternative initially works and then its link fails. Reassess immediately rather than continuing under the old approval.

## Q97-9 — Emergency command interface and evolving response

Refresh the transition from normal operations to the approved emergency structure. Send a clear initial report with location, event, people at risk, known restrictions and assistance needed. Keep facts separate from estimates. Coordinate with flight crew, remote support and the responsible incident authority; do not assume the HLO role includes all command or technical decisions.

Update priorities as routes, capability and casualty information change. Give concise task instructions, obtain confirmation and monitor effects. Avoid tunnel vision on equipment while people remain unaccounted for. If an instruction would exceed trained capability or contradict a known safety condition, clarify and escalate. The exercise should reward safe challenge and accurate uncertainty, not a theatrical rapid response.

Worked and changed applications: A controller asks for a casualty number before identities are reconciled. Report confirmed information and the unknowns, then update. Changed case: a previously safe route becomes blocked. Redirect through the approved alternative and confirm the team received the change.

## Q97-10 — Accountability, casualty support and evacuation

Reconcile expected identities with verified safe locations, not raw tally marks. Track visitors, crew interfaces and any person moved for assistance. A duplicate entry cannot compensate for someone missing. Report last confirmed information to the authorized response process and do not initiate unprotected searches. Preserve the history of uncertainty and later verification.

Provide assistance within actual training and resources; medical treatment, hazardous entry and aircraft evacuation require the applicable competent direction. Use preplanned safe routes and muster or evacuation arrangements. A support delay does not create new rescue competence. In exercises, casualty cards and safe roleplay test communication and prioritization without real hazardous movement or untrained lifting.

Worked and changed applications: Expected eight people; muster has eight entries but two are duplicates, leaving six unique and two missing. State both missing identities and seek verified information. Changed case: one is confirmed at an alternate safe point; update to seven accounted for and one missing, not all clear.

## Q97-11 — Final departure, recovery and handover after disruption

After an abnormal event, final departure requires more than the ordinary checklist. Establish the current protection state, unresolved defects, people/items, records and remote ownership of follow-up. Do not leave a system disabled or an unresolved restriction unreported because the aircraft is waiting. Restoration must be verified within the approved process.

Handover should include what happened, what is confirmed now, what remains unknown, controls in effect and the responsible next owner. Confirm receipt and understanding of the current revision. Preserve event records and failed or superseded information for review. A later correction must not erase evidence of the original condition. If required unattended status cannot be achieved, escalate through the actual contingency rather than sign a false completion.

Worked and changed applications: All people are ready but a protection isolation remains open. Obtain authorized resolution and handover before declaring the required state. Changed case: remote staff acknowledges an obsolete status. Correct the version and confirm current restrictions, not just successful message delivery.

## Q97-12 — Independent refresher evidence and continued development

Further-training completion requires current knowledge and independent performance in changed, rarely practiced conditions. Do not reuse an earlier initial-course score as proof of today’s skill. Record prompts, missed controls and how the learner handled new information. A critical unsafe or dishonest action prevents a pass even if most routine tasks are fluent.

Use targeted remediation followed by a changed independent scenario, retaining the first result. The original VNDRLY outcome records refresher scope and does not renew an OPITO certificate or replace workplace competence assessment and employer appointment. Identify local follow-up and changes requiring additional preparation. An effective debrief produces specific actions with owners and verification, not merely a statement that communication should improve.

Worked and changed applications: The learner performs routine checks smoothly but invents a missing-person location. Record critical failure and reassess evidence-based accountability after practice. Changed case: learner correctly preserves a hold under pressure. Credit that decision and assess the remaining domains; delay alone is not failure.

## I97 independent further-training assessment

Each candidate leads both complete cycles, including changed emergency phases. Controller releases facts at their stated point, records actions and avoids coaching. All required communication, system-condition, accountability and final-handover controls must be demonstrated. No live-fire or hazardous rescue capability is claimed from these simulations.

Twelve dimensions match the stations. Score 0 absent/unsafe, 1 major coaching, 2 safe incomplete/prompted, 3 independently acceptable, 4 accurate independent performance with explanation. Require at least 39/48 in each cycle, every dimension at least 3, all stations passed and no critical error. Critical: false readiness, invented location/event, unsafe uncontrolled movement, unauthorized reset/isolation, unsupported rescue or DG authority, or fabricated evidence. Preserve failed attempts, teach/practice the gap, then use changed independent reassessment. Correct holds are credited despite delay.
