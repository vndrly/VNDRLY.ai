# VUC075 — Lift Director

## Purpose and boundaries

This original VNDRLY/U program teaches the person coordinating a lift to turn a verified plan into controlled work. It includes mobile and tower routes: telescopic rough-terrain and lattice-boom mobile planning, and hammerhead and luffing-tower planning. The director coordinates people, equipment, information and changing conditions; the role is not permission to overrule an operator's safety refusal, design lifting attachments, approve ground engineering or operate a crane without separate qualifications. The package is not a CCO credential or a claim of complete proprietary ASME coverage. Applicable law, current manufacturer instructions and employer responsibilities govern actual work.

Learning outcomes: allocate responsibilities; resolve missing evidence before release; establish load weight, center of gravity and rigging demand; read configuration-specific charts through the complete route; arrange competent ground and electrical-hazard controls; conduct a closed-loop briefing; direct ordinary single-crane lifts through trained personnel; control changes, stops and handover; recognize when specialist planning is required. Both actual mobile and tower routes are required for the combined VNDRLY scope. Paper cases do not establish actual direction competence.

Entry requires documented rigging/signal knowledge, experience around the requested crane families, chart-reading ability and basic arithmetic. The instructor evaluates gaps before live work. Complete the checks after each chapter and the practical route under qualified supervision. Every numerical training card below is fictional; never take it to a job as a capacity chart, engineering calculation or clearance plan.

## Q01 — Authority, roles and the decision to proceed

A safe lift needs explicit owners for distinct decisions. The crane owner supplies an appropriate maintained machine and its records. The user arranges its use in the work. The site supervisor coordinates surrounding activities and access. The controlling entity has construction ground-preparation and underground-hazard duties. The operator verifies configuration and operates within limitations. Qualified riggers select and attach rigging within their scope; qualified signal personnel communicate movement. The director confirms that these contributions form one workable plan. One person may hold multiple roles only when genuinely qualified and able to perform them without divided-attention conflict.

Start a responsibility sheet with names, qualifications relevant to this lift, authority and alternates. Ask each role holder to explain the assigned task back. A title, employer badge or attendance certificate is not proof of every needed qualification. Resolve an absent ground decision or disputed rigging arrangement before motion. Document who can approve plan revisions and who must be consulted.

**Worked situation:** a scheduler asks the director to start while the operator questions an outrigger location. The correct instruction is to hold, retain the operator's stop authority, and obtain a competent resolution from the responsible people. A schedule signature is not evidence of stable support. **Changed application:** the director is also the only power-line spotter; identify the conflict, assign a trained dedicated spotter, and rebrief before proceeding. Do not silently leave either function uncovered.

## Q02 — Defining a lift rather than just a pick

Define the object, pick point, complete route, set point and safe fallback landing. Include unload, initial tension, clearance from supports, travel or swing, landing, de-rigging and return of the unloaded crane. A favorable pick radius does not establish capacity at the farthest set radius. An empty return can still strike a structure or enter an electrical boundary. Draw the route and plan where people can stand without being trapped by the load, counterweight or a reversing carrier.

Gather crane identity and configuration, load identity and weight evidence, rigging drawings/ratings, inspection status, ground information, site restrictions, weather limits, crew and communication arrangements. Assign each unresolved item an owner and hold point. A plan marked draft stays draft until its prerequisites are verified. A missing document cannot be replaced by a guessed value just because the arithmetic would then fit.

**Worked situation:** a lift travels from radius 8 m to 12 m and then to 10 m. Check all three and intervening route conditions; 12 m may control. **Changed application:** a delivery truck blocks the planned set point and someone suggests another location at 14 m. Stop and replan the changed radius, support and exclusion arrangements. The lift is not unchanged merely because its weight is unchanged.

## Q03 — Establishing weight, units and uncertainty

Use reliable evidence for the actual load in its present condition: manufacturer data matched to identity, reliable weighing, or a justified engineering calculation. Account for contents, retained liquid, packaging, loose components and lifting attachments. Distinguish mass units from force units and do not mix pounds, kilograms and tonnes in one sum. A shipping label for an empty vessel is not its field weight when partly full. Obtain a defensible upper bound or resolve uncertainty before release.

Training load L75 is a steel assembly with verified mass 4, 800 kg. Loose items fixed to it add 200 kg. Rigging adds 300 kg and the hook block adds 500 kg. In the fictional M75 chart convention, all these consume gross capacity: total demand is 5, 800 kg. Payload alone is 5, 000 kg. Use the manufacturer's actual deduction convention in real work; do not double-subtract a component already included in a chart.

**Worked calculation:** an original teaching block measures 2 m × 0.5 m × 0.1 m at an assigned density of 7, 850 kg/m³. Volume is 0.1 m³ and calculated mass is 785 kg. The assigned density applies only to the stated solid material; hollows, deposits or attached parts change the model. **Changed application:** 0.12 m thickness gives 0.12 m³ and 942 kg, a 157 kg increase. Record dimensions, units, evidence and uncertainty rather than only a final number.

## Q04 — Center of gravity and controlled balance

The center of gravity determines how the suspended object seeks balance. Before rigging, locate weight concentrations and verify attachment points are rated for the actual force directions. The hook must be positioned so the load can lift without an unintended roll or side pull. The geometric center of a skid may not be its mass center when a motor sits at one end. Do not use a trial lift to discover an unknown attachment rating.

For a simplified rigid training beam, two vertical lift reactions separated by 4 m support 6, 000 kg with the center of gravity 1.5 m from support A. Moment balance gives reaction B = 6, 000 × 1.5 / 4 = 2, 250 kg-equivalent and reaction A = 3, 750 kg-equivalent. These are static vertical shares, not final sling tensions and not a tandem-lift approval. Inclined legs and motion require further analysis.

A controlled initial lift under the approved plan checks balance, secure attachment and brake response with people clear. If tilt or movement differs from the plan, lower safely and revise; never have a worker pull the object level under suspension. **Changed application:** move the center of gravity to 2.5 m from A. B becomes 3, 750 and A 2, 250. A seemingly modest equipment relocation reverses the heavier end. Update rigging and the entire lift plan rather than reusing the previous assignment.

## Q05 — Rigging selection and angle effects

Trace the load path from object and attachment points through slings, hardware, lifting beam and hook. Every element needs suitable identification, rating, configuration and condition. Consider hitch type, sling angle, edge protection, bend radius, temperature, chemical exposure, side loading and connection fit. A shackle that physically fits a hole is not necessarily rated for the resulting force direction. Obtain the manufacturer's reduction rules rather than improvising a percentage.

For two equal legs sharing a centered static load, with each leg at angle theta above horizontal, tension per leg is W/(2 sin theta). For a 4, 000 kg-equivalent teaching load: at 60° tension is 2, 309.40; at 45° it is 2, 828.43; at 30° it is 4, 000. A flatter sling increases tension. This simplified equation does not establish that a real load is equally shared or that four legs each carry one quarter. Uncertain sharing needs qualified analysis.

**Worked decision:** a tagged leg rated 3, 000 in the relevant configuration passes the simplified 45° demand but not 30°. Change geometry or select a properly engineered arrangement; never round 4, 000 down. **Changed application:** at 45° with 5, 000 load, demand is 3, 535.53 per leg, so the same leg fails. Inspect rigging before use and as necessary during use; missing identification, damage or incompatible components require segregation and qualified disposition. Never repair a damaged sling with a knot or unapproved clamp.

## Q06 — Chart selection and gross-to-net capacity

Match the actual machine, boom/jib, counterweight, support extension, operating sector, reeving and stationary/travel condition to its chart. Read notes before using numbers. Identify structural versus stability limitations where the chart distinguishes them. A load indicator helps the operator but does not replace chart interpretation or correct setup. Verify boom clearance and maximum/minimum radius as well as capacity.

Fictional M75 is a stationary mobile-crane teaching configuration on fully extended supports, approved level ground, specified counterweight, no jib. At radii 8, 10, 12 and 14 m its gross ratings are respectively 9, 000, 7, 500, 6, 000 and 4, 500 kg. For this teaching card only, use the next larger listed radius between entries; do not interpolate. The 5, 800 kg demand from Q03 uses 64.44%, 77.33%, 96.67% and 128.89% respectively. At 14 m it exceeds rating by 1, 300 kg. At 12 m, net payload allowance after 800 kg of block/rigging deductions is 5, 200 kg, leaving 200 kg against the 5, 000 kg payload. This small residual is not a universal acceptable planning margin.

**Changed application:** measured route radius is 12.2 m. The fictional rule requires the 14 m entry, so the lift is not allowed. Obtain a redesigned route/configuration with valid evidence. Real charts may prescribe other rules; never transfer the fictional rule to them.

## Q07 — Ground, supports and hidden load paths

Walk the complete setup and travel area with responsible site personnel. Request ground-bearing evidence, underground tanks/utilities/voids, recent excavation, backfill, drainage and slopes. A surface that supports a pickup may fail under a concentrated outrigger. The controlling entity's information and preparation do not remove the operator's duty to report unsuitable conditions. Resolve uncertain support before setup.

Use actual worst support reactions from an appropriate manufacturer or engineering source, not crane total weight divided equally among supports. For a simplified training reaction of 240 kN over an effectively supported 2 m² mat area, average pressure is 120 kPa. If the assigned allowable average pressure is 100 kPa, arithmetic minimum area is 2.4 m². That quotient alone does not design a mat: stiffness, bending, punching, load distribution, edge effects, underlying layers and settlement must also be addressed by the responsible competent/qualified people.

**Worked decision:** a 3 m² mat would give 80 kPa average only if its effective supported area and structural capacity are valid. A corner spanning a void invalidates the assumption. **Changed application:** reaction rises to 300 kN after configuration change; the same 3 m² gives 100 kPa. Reassess the changed reaction and real support, not just the average. Watch settlement and level during work; visible movement requires a controlled stop and responsible reassessment.

## Q08 — Electrical hazards and work-zone control

Identify lines before positioning. Treat them as energized until the utility confirms deenergization and visible grounding at the site. Assess the full defined work zone, including load, rigging and maximum reach, rather than today's intended centerline. For construction operations up to 350 kV, the 20 ft work-zone trigger leads to the prescribed options and controls; it is not a universal substitute for voltage-specific planning. Above 350 through 1, 000 kV the corresponding trigger is 50 ft. Unknown voltage means obtain reliable utility information, not guess from the number of wires.

OSHA Table A lists minimum distances of 10 ft up to 50 kV; 15 ft over 50 through 200; 20 ft over 200 through 350; 25 ft over 350 through 500; 35 ft over 500 through 750; and 45 ft over 750 through 1, 000. Above 1, 000 kV, the utility or appropriately qualified registered engineer establishes clearance. These distances do not by themselves satisfy required meetings, visible boundaries, nonconductive taglines and encroachment measures. If the operator cannot see the warning boundary, the specific additional spotter/control requirements apply.

**Worked situation:** confirmed 230 kV gives Table A 20 ft, but a planned 18 ft approach cannot be approved by a director's waiver. Reposition or use the applicable qualified process. Closer-than-Table-A work has separate stringent conditions under 1926.1410. Travel without a load uses a separate rule; loaded travel does not inherit that exception. Transmitter-induced charge is another hazard even without line contact. **Changed application:** a 500 kV line uses 25 ft Table A and the 50 ft assessment trigger. Confirm all controls, not just the smaller number.

## Q09 — People, exclusion and access

Plan where workers, vehicles and the public can be during every phase. Include counterweight swing, carrier travel, load fall zone, crush points at landing and access to emergency equipment. A painted circle is not effective if delivery traffic repeatedly crosses it. Establish barriers and a responsible access controller, with a way to stop movement when exclusion fails. Workers receiving or guiding a load must meet applicable requirements and cannot be used as human restraints against a swinging mass.

Choose a load route minimizing exposure. Prepare stable landing supports and confirm capacity and access before lifting. Use tools/taglines where appropriate to keep hands and bodies out of pinch points. Taglines can snag, whip or pull a person; select safe lengths and positions, do not wrap them around a hand or body, and stop if control is lost. Never place someone beneath a suspended load for convenience.

**Worked situation:** the shortest path crosses an occupied walkway; the longer route stays within capacity and can be isolated. Choose and brief the controlled route rather than assuming pedestrians will notice. **Changed application:** the receiver is absent and the landing area fills with materials. Hold the lift until the set point and qualified receiving arrangement are restored. A director cannot compensate for an unsafe landing by urging the operator to move faster.

## Q10 — Signals and communication discipline

Use a qualified signal person when required by visibility, travel obstruction or other safety concerns. Select the communication method for actual site conditions, test it at the site before the lift and agree on how control transfers between signal people. Use standard hand signals where appropriate; any necessary nonstandard signals must be agreed before operation. Radio direction must identify the crane and motion clearly. For voice signals, agree the language and direction reference, then use function/direction, distance or speed, and stop as appropriate. Do not let two people issue competing movement instructions.

An emergency stop may come from anyone who detects danger. The operator must obey it. When a required signal is lost or unclear, stop safely until communication is restored. A dedicated radio channel and hands-free reception at the operator protect attention. Do not test radio failure by creating an uncontrolled suspended-load situation during training; use a planned safe pause and simulated inject.

**Worked exchange:** signaler identifies Crane A, calls the agreed hoist movement and small distance, operator responds using the agreed procedure, and signaler calls stop at the planned position. The director checks comprehension without creating a second competing signal stream. **Changed application:** a replacement signaler uses an ambiguous left/right reference. Stop, establish common references and verify understanding before resuming; do not guess from the load's apparent movement.

## Q11 — Weather, visibility and changing conditions

Obtain the actual crane and load-specific limits and site stopping criteria. Wind effects depend on load area and shape as well as mass; a light wall panel can be harder to control than a compact heavier load. Gusts at load height may differ from readings at ground level. Rain can change ground support; ice can change mass, footing and rigging behavior. Lightning, poor light and lost visibility can invalidate an otherwise sound plan.

Name who monitors weather, where readings are obtained, who calls the stop, and how the load will be landed and equipment secured. Establish sufficient lead time to stop safely before a limit is crossed. Do not use the rated-capacity indicator as a wind sensor or a forecast as proof of current conditions. The competent person addresses weather effects and applicable manufacturer securing recommendations.

**Training-only example:** plan W75 establishes a 10 m/s operating ceiling and an 8 m/s pre-stop trigger for its selected load. A 9 m/s reading requires the prescribed controlled landing even though it has not reached 10. These are invented exercise values, not manufacturer limits. **Changed application:** the load is replaced by a larger-area panel at the same mass. The previous W75 approval does not automatically apply; obtain its approved wind conditions and handling method. If none is available, hold.

## Q12 — Mobile configurations and loaded travel

Mobile-crane planning distinguishes telescopic versus lattice boom, tire/crawler support, outrigger sectors, installed counterweight, boom extensions, reeving and ground route. A stationary fully supported rating cannot justify a pick-and-carry maneuver. Loaded travel needs manufacturer permission and appropriate configuration/rating, competent supervision, route/support assessment, controlled load position and speed, and tire conditions where applicable. Dynamic changes can increase radius or shift reactions.

Use assembly/disassembly plans and qualified personnel for changes requiring them. The lift director verifies readiness but does not become the assembly/disassembly director by default. With multiple load lines, account for both hooks, attachments, possible simultaneous loading and interference under the actual instructions. Duty-cycle work, demolition, magnet/clamshell service and operations on water introduce different restrictions; ordinary lifting charts do not automatically authorize them.

**Worked situation:** M75 has no travel rating. A request to move 2 m while carrying the load is refused until an authorized travel configuration and plan exist, or the load is landed and the crane repositioned unloaded under its procedures. **Changed application:** a lattice crawler has an approved travel chart but the route crosses a buried tank. Rating alone does not resolve support; obtain ground and route clearance. Inspect the route before lifting, not with a suspended load waiting for a decision.

## Q13 — Tower arrangements and overlapping operations

Tower planning includes foundation and ties, erected configuration, jib/boom geometry, trolley or luffing movement, hoist limitations, slew clearance, adjacent cranes and public space. The director verifies required erection/inspection evidence and operating arrangements with responsible personnel. A hammerhead changes radius through trolley travel; a luffing crane changes geometry through boom angle. Both can have critical route positions beyond the initial pick.

Fictional T75 hammerhead configuration rates 5, 000 kg at 20 m, 3, 500 at 30 m and 2, 500 at 40 m, with next larger listed radius used between entries only for this card. A 2, 800 kg object plus 200 kg rigging and 300 kg block creates 3, 300 kg demand: 66% at 20 m, 94.29% at 30 m and 132% at 40 m. A 34 m set cannot use the favorable 30 m entry. Fictional U75 luffing configuration rates 4, 000 kg at 18 m and 2, 700 at 28 m; a 2, 600 kg total uses 65% and 96.30%. Neither chart includes erection, climbing or out-of-service instructions.

**Worked decision:** two cranes have overlapping slewing envelopes. Establish a coordinated, approved interference arrangement; do not rely on both operators noticing each other. **Changed application:** the out-of-service weather-vane path conflicts with another structure. Escalate for the actual manufacturer/site solution; a director must not invent a locked-slew remedy. Climbing, ties and dismantling remain separately controlled specialist operations.

## Q14 — Briefing and controlled execution

At the lift point, use the current plan revision and walk the sequence with all affected people. Confirm roles, object identity, rigging, configuration, route, exclusion, signals, hold points, set-down, weather and emergency response. Ask the operator and rigger to explain limiting conditions and the signaler to demonstrate communication. Record attendance and resolve questions; a signature without comprehension is insufficient.

Proceed only when prerequisites are satisfied. During initial tension and the approved low lift, observe balance and support from safe locations. Maintain the planned sequence and communication. Keep the director's coordination role distinct from the designated signaler's movement commands. If something changes, stop at a safe condition, identify what invalidated the plan, obtain the correct revision and rebrief. Do not authorize adjustment of supports or rigging beneath a suspended load.

**Worked situation:** a shackle substitution has the same nominal capacity but a different allowable connection geometry. Hold for qualified compatibility review before release. **Changed application:** a substitute operator arrives mid-shift. Confirm competence and machine evaluation as applicable, hand over current restrictions and rebrief the plan. Familiarity with a different crane is not automatic acceptance of this configuration. The director records the actual handover and confirms who now has each responsibility.

## Q15 — Multiple-crane and critical lift escalation

A multiple-crane lift requires a plan developed by a qualified person, engineering expertise where needed, the required direction competence and a meeting of all involved workers. Reactions can change with rotation, unequal motion, deflection, changing geometry and center-of-gravity uncertainty. Two cranes each rated for half the object's mass do not automatically make a safe tandem arrangement. Rigging, individual gross loads, route, communication and safe recovery must be analyzed together.

For a simplified static paper case only, a 10, 000 kg beam supported at two vertical points 5 m apart has its center of gravity 2 m from A. B carries 4, 000 kg-equivalent and A 6, 000 before their own rigging/block loads. With 600 added at each hook, demands are 4, 600 and 6, 600. At a changed center 3 m from A these become 6, 600 and 4, 600. This is a learning illustration, not sufficient tandem-lift engineering.

Critical-lift classification depends on applicable rules and employer/project criteria. Do not treat a percentage from steel-erection rules or one company as a universal definition. High utilization, irreplaceable loads, public exposure or unusual configuration may require enhanced planning even below a numerical threshold. **Changed application:** an ordinary single-crane plan becomes a two-crane plan after access changes. Suspend release and obtain the qualified plan; do not amend only the number of cranes on the cover sheet. This program assesses specialist-plan recognition and review, not actual tandem execution authorization.

## Q16 — Personnel hoisting and other specialist boundaries

Hoisting people is not ordinary material lifting with a basket substituted. Under construction rules, the employer must satisfy the applicable justification and all equipment, platform, rigging, trial, proof-test, work-practice and briefing requirements. Conventional access must be evaluated first. A platform's presence or a crane's spare capacity is not enough. Specific exceptions and activities have additional provisions; apply the exact current requirements rather than treating a general summary as a complete plan.

For a suspended-platform teaching calculation, a 900 kg total including loaded platform, hook, line and rigging against 2, 000 kg crane capacity is 45%. Although below the usual 50% personnel-hoisting capacity ceiling, this does not establish legal or safe authorization. The qualified design, appropriate devices, setup, inspections and personnel procedures still need proof. A prescribed unoccupied trial follows the actual route and is not the same as a platform/rigging proof test. The latter's 125% of platform rated capacity requirement is not a blanket instruction to overload every crane by 25%.

**Changed application:** total becomes 1, 100 kg at the same crane capacity, or 55%; hold even if every other document exists. No occupied platform lifting occurs in this course's routine practical route. Barge stability, marine transfers, demolition and special duty-cycle work likewise require separate specialist arrangements. The director identifies the missing discipline, obtains its approved plan and communicates boundaries instead of manufacturing expertise.

## Q17 — Abnormal events and safe recovery

Plan credible stops before starting: lost signals, unexpected load shift, support settlement, equipment alarm, person entering exclusion, deteriorating weather and electrical contact. State the safe landing options and emergency contacts. In an abnormal event, communicate stop, keep people clear and let the operator implement the applicable safe response. Do not demand an improvised motion that could worsen instability. A damaged or stuck load does not justify pulling sideways or bypassing a safety device.

For suspected electrical contact, keep others away from equipment/load and the potentially energized ground, call the utility/emergency response, and follow the trained equipment-specific emergency procedure. Operators normally remain in the cab unless an imminent emergency requires escape; nobody should rush to touch the crane or victim while energization may persist. Course exercises use tabletop scenarios, never live electrical exposure.

**Worked situation:** a mat settles during initial hoisting. The crew stops, controls access and follows the operator/qualified response to reach a safe condition. Support is reassessed before reuse. **Changed application:** the load is already over an obstructed set point and communication fails. Use the preplanned lost-signal response and safe hold/landing provisions; do not invite anyone under the load to clear the obstruction. Recovery planning may require specialist engineering. Log what happened, preserve relevant evidence and prevent unauthorized restart.

## Q18 — Closure, evidence and handover

After landing, verify stable support and release of tension before authorized de-rigging. Restore the crane and site according to the plan, secure equipment appropriately and communicate residual hazards. Close the lift record with actual configuration, load/route, personnel, conditions, deviations and observations. An incomplete lift or a correct hold is recorded honestly, not converted into a successful movement for a performance target.

Review what changed and why. Carry unresolved defects, restrictions, altered ground or pending approvals to the next shift with named recipients. Keep the original plan and revision history so a later reviewer can distinguish intended from performed work. The director's signature documents the coordination actually done; it is not an engineering seal, crane inspection certificate or substitute for the employer's designation of qualified personnel.

**Worked handover:** the object is safely landed, but the crane remains restricted because of a reported operational aid fault. Record the lift outcome and continuing restriction separately. **Changed application:** another crew asks to reuse the rigging plan for an apparently identical item. Verify identity, actual weight, attachments, configuration and site conditions before reuse. Similar appearance does not establish equivalence. Finish with a clear statement of what may proceed, what must remain on hold, who owns the next action and what evidence will close it.

# VUC075 — Workbook, instructor delivery and evaluation

## Delivery plan and resources

Plan 72 hours: 24 instruction/worked cases, 12 laboratory planning, 20 supervised field practice, 12 independent practical evaluation/reporting, and 4 controlled examination/remediation review. Extend practice for learner need; elapsed attendance does not establish competence. Allocate roughly 1–2 instructional hours to each domain, combining Q03–Q06 calculation teaching and Q15–Q17 specialist decision cases. Begin each lesson with its outcome, demonstrate the worked example, have learners solve the changed application independently, discuss reasoning, then complete both formative checks. Correct errors before the corresponding field task.

Instructor must have demonstrated competence in lift planning/direction, relevant mobile and tower families, rigging/signals and safe training delivery. Qualified operators, riggers and signalers perform their actual roles. A separate competent evaluator, when practicable, observes independent decisions without coaching. Record instructor/evaluator qualifications and conflicts. Entry diagnostic asks learners to identify a chart configuration, add gross load, calculate a two-leg tension, recognize a damaged rigging specimen and explain stop authority. Teach prerequisite gaps before live participation; do not award qualification by waiving them.

Provide current applicable law and actual manufacturer instructions, crane charts, inspection/maintenance readiness records, actual ground/site information and approved training lift plans. Provide mobile and tower equipment, suitable verified training loads and attachments, rated rigging with legible identification, safe isolated reject specimens, tape/laser measurement tools as appropriate, radios, barriers, weather measurement, PPE and clear landing supports. Qualified personnel validate equipment, ground, load, route, weather and emergency arrangements before learners enter the exercise. Classroom M75/T75/U75/W75 values never authorize actual lifting.

For mobile practice include actual direction on the chosen mobile family plus planning interpretation of both telescopic and lattice examples. For tower practice include actual direction on the chosen tower type plus hammerhead and luffing chart/route interpretation. Record the exact machines actually used; theoretical family coverage is not an actual endorsement on every model. A combined mobile-and-tower award requires both observed routes. If only one route is delivered, issue an explicitly limited route award and leave the other incomplete; do not count a video as actual direction.

Use legible diagrams, enlarged charts, language support and extra reading time as appropriate without relaxing safety communication or independent decision requirements. Validate shared signal language before field work. Calculators are allowed; the learner must state units, assumptions and disposition. Reading an answer aloud is not demonstrating the physical or coordination skill.

## Application laboratories

For every lab submit inputs/sources, assumptions, calculations or annotated sketch, go/hold decision, missing evidence and a short explanation. Instructor marks each required outcome, not just attendance. Changes below are distinct from the controlled final.

### W01 — Responsibilities and complete route

Create a role sheet for director Dana, operator Lee, rigger Sam, signaler Alex, site supervisor Jo and controlling-entity contact Pat. Plot pick 8 m, swing through 12 m and land 10 m, with a walkway crossing the original route. Assign load evidence to the appropriate supplier, ground information to responsible site/controlling personnel, rigging to qualified support, signals to Alex and final coordination to Dana. Explain why Dana cannot override Lee's safety refusal. Expected product includes a controlled walkway solution, alternate landing and unresolved-item hold list. Change: Alex leaves and Pat cannot confirm a buried service. Expected response is to hold affected work and restore both prerequisites, not transfer dedicated signaling casually to a distracted director.

### W02 — Load and center of gravity

Use L75 and the solid block example. Show 5, 000 kg payload, 800 deductions and 5, 800 gross; block volumes/masses 0.1/785 and 0.12/942. Solve the 6, 000 kg beam at center positions 1.5 and 2.5 m on a 4 m support span. Expected pairs A/B are 3, 750/2, 250 and 2, 250/3, 750. State why these static reactions do not authorize rigging or a tandem lift. Change: attach an unweighed motor. Correct outcome is unresolved mass/center and hold, not assume it is included in a label.

### W03 — Rigging geometry and condition

Calculate 4, 000 kg two-leg tensions at 60°, 45° and 30° above horizontal: 2, 309.40, 2, 828.43 and 4, 000 kg-equivalent. Compare a tagged 3, 000 rating in the stated configuration; 30° fails. For changed 5, 000 at 45°, 3, 535.53 also fails. Select inspected compatible sample components and mark a load-path drawing including attachments and edge protection. Explain why four-leg equal sharing and a missing tag cannot be assumed. Use isolated defective specimens for recognition only; never install them in the operational training rig.

### W04 — Mobile and tower chart decisions

Complete M75 route table with total 5, 800: utilization 64.44%, 77.33%, 96.67%, 128.89% at 8/10/12/14 m. At 12 m net payload is 5, 200 and spare allowance 200; at 12.2 m the fictional 14 m rule fails. T75 total 3, 300 uses 66%, 94.29%, 132% at 20/30/40 m; 34 m fails. U75 total 2, 600 uses 65% and 96.30% at 18/28 m. Attach a separate actual manufacturer-chart interpretation for the site's approved safe configuration; the evaluator verifies the selected notes and units. Change: support extension differs from the chart heading. Correct response is to obtain the matching chart/setup before calculation, not retain the previous rating.

### W05 — Ground and electrical planning

Calculate 240 kN over 2 m² = 120 kPa; area quotient at 100 kPa = 2.4 m²; 3 m² average = 80 kPa. Changed 300 kN over 3 m² = 100 kPa. List why mat stiffness, actual effective area and underground support remain unresolved by these divisions. Sketch a confirmed 230 kV site and identify Table A 20 ft plus required controls; compare confirmed 500 kV, Table A 25 ft and assessment trigger 50 ft. A closer-than-minimum proposal stays on hold pending the actual qualified process. Do not physically set up training equipment near energized lines to demonstrate this lab.

### W06 — Briefing, signaling and controlled change

Role-play the prelift briefing, with one signaler and agreed radio identity/directions. Each member explains their role and stop response. Inject a radio failure while the mock load is represented on a floor plan; demonstrate stop and restoration, not continued movement from memory. Change: different shackle geometry and substitute operator. Expected record identifies revised compatibility/competence checks and rebriefing. Evaluate the director's coordination and the signaler's distinct function. Misunderstood signals are corrected before any actual lifting.

### W07 — Specialist review and refusal

Solve the static two-support 10, 000 kg beam with 5 m span and center at 2 m, then 3 m: hook demands including 600 each are A/B 6, 600/4, 600 then 4, 600/6, 600. Identify qualified engineering/planning still needed. Review a personnel scenario of 900/2, 000 = 45%, changed 1, 100/2, 000 = 55%; explain why passing a percentage is insufficient. Separate platform/rigging proof test from trial route and crane rating. Add a barge operation request; identify marine stability and applicable qualified procedures. No actual tandem/personnel/marine operation is authorized by this lab.

### W08 — Integrated plan and recovery

Prepare I75 teaching plan using an approved actual mobile or tower route. Include fallback landing, exclusion, weather, communications and release criteria. Respond to W75 9 m/s after an 8 m/s pre-stop trigger by controlled landing under the plan. Inject support settlement, receiver absence and an unresolved equipment defect as paper events at safe pauses. Produce stop/change/restart decision records and a shift handover distinguishing completed movement from continuing restrictions. Changed case uses a different valid route, verified load and crew arrangement. Do not transplant classroom wind numbers onto real equipment.

## Practical stations and common scoring

All stations use a task record: learner/date___ task/version___ machine or paper evidence___ inputs___ observed actions___ results___ assessor___ remediation/retest___. Score five dimensions 0–2: preparation/source choice; correct technical judgment; safe sequencing; communication; traceable evidence. Two means independent correct performance with reasons, one safe but incomplete, zero missing/incorrect/unsafe. Pass each station at least 8/10, no zero in safety and no critical error. Also meet every listed essential outcome. A high total cannot compensate for entering exclusion, overriding stop, fabricated evidence or unsupported capacity.

## I75 — Independent lift direction on mobile and tower routes

The training team prepares teaching set T and independent sets A/B. Each contains two actual approved single-crane lifts, one mobile and one tower, using known safe loads and actual equipment documents. The responsible qualified team validates the plan before use; learner work is checked before release so an incorrect learner answer never becomes a hazardous experiment. Provide complete real chart/rigging/ground/inspection evidence, site sketch, crew qualifications, weather limits and emergency contacts. Include telescopic/lattice and hammerhead/luffing paper variants to assess planning range, but identify which machines are actually used.

Learner independently develops or verifies the plan, identifies and closes prerequisites, traces total demand and all route limitations, allocates roles, establishes exclusions, tests communications, conducts a comprehensible briefing and gives the coordinated readiness decision. A qualified operator retains operating control and stop authority. Qualified riggers/signalers retain their roles. Perform approved initial tension/low lift, travel through the planned stationary-crane lift path, land on prepared supports, confirm stability, de-rig safely and close the record. Crane travel with load is excluded unless separately authorized and evaluated outside the routine route; no simulated chart authorizes it.

At predetermined safe pauses introduce two paper changes: a missing replacement-component compatibility record before lift, and a changed set location after the load has been safely landed. The learner must stop/revise/rebrief rather than improvise. Also conduct a radio-loss tabletop event and a worsening-weather paper event with equipment in safe condition. Never disable a safety device, overload, energize a hazard, destabilize a support or create a real communication failure under load to test recognition. Real hazards stop the evaluation immediately.

Required evidence for EACH route: actual identity/configuration; verified load and attachment basis; correct full-route chart analysis; ground/site readiness; qualified roles; exclusion and signals; observed briefing; safe actual coordinated lift and landing; correct change handling; complete closure/handover. Assess each with the common five dimensions and record specific observed behavior. Pass all essential outcomes, all applicable stations and both routes without critical error. A correct hold on an injected missing prerequisite earns decision credit, but eventual actual safe lift evidence is still required; do not award the whole route for only declining to lift.

Set B changes verified load, valid route, roles or configuration under a separately approved safe plan. Reassess failed domains plus the affected whole route after documented remediation. Preserve original failures. Equipment/weather interruptions are not failures unless the learner's response is unsafe; resume only after renewed readiness and repeat interrupted portions sufficiently to observe the complete sequence. Allow four hours per route plus four for planning/reporting in the evaluation block, extending for safe operations rather than rushing. No time bonus offsets missed controls.

## Controlled examinations and award

Private Form A and changed Form B each contain 18 original cases with three scored decisions: 108 points, 180 minutes, pass at least 87/108 and at least 4/6 in every domain, no unresolved critical error. Each decision earns 2 for supported correctness, 1 for safe incomplete reasoning, 0 for incorrect/missing/unsafe. Calculator and coursebook allowed; answer keys, collaboration and outside assistance prohibited. Changed forms include original context and new facts; assess new decisions rather than memorized wording. Keep keys in the private instructor directory and record version, assessor and accommodations.

Award requires all 36 corrected formative checks, eight labs, 18 stations, applicable actual routes, I75 and final. Record mobile/tower scope and exact equipment demonstrated. Knowledge-only learners receive a study record, not the full practical award. The certificate is VNDRLY/U Lift Director — assessed scope; it does not grant CCO certification, engineering authority, operator qualification, power-line proximity exception, tandem/personnel/marine specialization or employer appointment. A one-year VNDRLY review reminder is administrative, not a regulatory qualification interval. Reevaluate when duties/equipment change or performance indicates a gap.

## Deliberate limits and delivery controls

The materials cover a combined mobile/tower lift-director curriculum using original instruction and current public sources. They do not certify a learner, establish statutory competence by text alone, approve an actual engineering design, authorize electrical exceptions, or replace manufacturer charts.
