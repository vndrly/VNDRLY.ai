# VNDRLY/U HLO for Normally Unattended Installations — Initial Training

VUC096. Original available-source initial instruction and simulated skills package. Full external specification unavailable. No OPITO award, workplace appointment, unrestricted firefighting, rescue, refuelling or aircraft authority is conferred.

## Q96-1 — NUI role, preparation and limits

A normally unattended installation creates a different support problem from a permanently staffed helideck. During first arrival and final departure there may be no resident deck team waiting to intervene. The approved operating concept must explain who provides information, what automated or remote safeguards exist, how the visiting team assumes duties and how the site is left. Never fill those transitions by assuming that someone else must be present.

Initial training builds knowledge and simulated skills. Employer appointment requires local preparation, experience and workplace assessment. Verify function-specific dangerous-goods preparation and required radio qualification for the jurisdiction, plus fitness for practical training. Weather reporting, refuelling, rescue and other specialist duties require their own competence. The HLO coordinates within a defined plan; the pilot retains aircraft decisions and the duty holder retains installation responsibilities.

Worked and changed applications: A first-time visitor is called HLO because they hold a generic safety certificate. Do not treat that as sufficient role preparation. Changed case: an experienced HLO transfers from a staffed deck to a NUI. Review the unattended transitions and local emergency concept rather than assuming the old staffing model still exists.

## Q96-2 — Rules, manuals and the installation operating concept

Identify the controlled helideck manual, aircraft-operator arrangements, emergency plan, equipment requirements and jurisdiction-specific rules. Record revisions and the actual assigned functions. Public standards provide context, but local criteria such as deck compatibility, required staffing, communication links and permitted operating modes must come from authorized sources. A course example is not a waiver or operating procedure.

Map responsibility across remote support, flight crew and visiting personnel for each phase. Before arrival, establish who confirms status and how uncertainty is reported. During occupancy, define who controls the helideck and emergency response. Before leaving, identify who accepts the final unattended status. If responsibilities conflict or required information is missing, hold dependent actions and obtain an authorized resolution.

Worked and changed applications: The remote contact expects the visiting team to inspect before the first landing, which is impossible. Escalate the gap in the operating concept; do not claim an inspection occurred. Changed case: a new manual revision changes the final-departure checks. Brief and apply the current revision rather than using a familiar old checklist.

## Q96-3 — Visit planning and remote readiness

Plan the visit with current flight/sector information, personnel and equipment, communications, expected duration, rescue arrangements and contingencies for delayed return. Obtain the approved current remote status, restrictions and inspection information. A green screen without source time or fault status is not enough to establish current capability. The pilot and operator need accurate information, not an unsupported all-clear.

Consider loss of communications, worsening weather, equipment defects and inability to depart as planned. Identify approved shelter, accountability, emergency contacts and escalation triggers. Do not assume a rescue resource will arrive within a fictional classroom time. Confirm what the visiting team must carry and what is available on the installation. Resolve missing safety-critical arrangements before the visit proceeds through the authorized process.

Worked and changed applications: A remote report is old and one required status field is unknown. Obtain approved current information or report the restriction; do not infer serviceability from silence. Changed case: return is delayed beyond the planned work window. Use the approved contingency and welfare arrangements while maintaining communications and accountability.

## Q96-4 — Helicopter and helideck hazards

Recognize rotor, tail-rotor, exhaust, noise, visibility, downwash, loose-object, slip and edge hazards. Aircraft-specific movement routes and crew indications govern approach and departure from the aircraft. Do not teach one compass direction as safe for every aircraft. Keep people controlled and prevent retrieval of dropped objects into dangerous areas. Use appropriate PPE and secure loose clothing or equipment.

An unattended period can allow contamination, debris or other deterioration to develop. Report observations accurately and inspect when access is authorized and safe. Do not create exposure by attempting correction while rotors or other hazards make access unsafe. Maintain clear escape and passenger routes. Assessment uses ground-level marked hazards and harmless props; real hazards are not needed to test recognition and control.

Worked and changed applications: A dropped glove moves toward the marked mock tail-rotor area. Stop the person who starts after it and arrange safe recovery. Changed case: debris is removed but contamination still affects the route. Keep that independent issue open; a partial cleanup is not complete readiness.

## Q96-5 — Status systems, weather and equipment information

Understand the actual status indications, remote monitoring and failure modes supplied by the installation. Distinguish confirmed serviceability from an unavailable signal, stale report or sensor fault. Weather and motion information must come from approved sources with units and time. Do not convert an absent alarm into proof that every system is functional.

Required equipment and approved alternatives are conditional. A defect must be assessed through the applicable unserviceability process, including authority and notifications. Never bypass or reset a safety system simply to remove a red indication. The HLO communicates current facts and limitations; specialist personnel diagnose or repair outside the HLO’s authorization. A changed condition after a status report requires an update.

Worked and changed applications: A fictional exercise requires a report within eight minutes; at 10:12 a 10:00 report is twelve minutes old and fails that exercise rule. Obtain current approved information. Changed case: a new report is only two minutes old but carries a sensor-fault flag. Freshness does not cure invalid data.

## Q96-6 — First arrival and transfer to attended operations

Follow the approved NUI first-arrival procedure and flight-crew directions. Recognize that the visiting HLO may arrive aboard the aircraft rather than already controlling the deck. The operating concept must address this without pretending a resident team is present. Initial access and establishment of local control occur only through the authorized sequence.

Once safely established, confirm the local condition and required resources, team roles, communications and access controls. Report discrepancies discovered after arrival and restrict dependent activity. Do not retrospectively describe a remote assumption as a completed physical inspection. The transition to attended operation is a deliberate handover of information and duties, not an automatic consequence of one person stepping off the aircraft.

Worked and changed applications: The team arrives and finds an item unavailable despite the remote record. Report the actual finding and apply the restriction before later movements. Changed case: the aircraft is still in a controlled phase and a team member wants to inspect behind it. Defer unsafe access and follow crew/HLO controls rather than prioritizing the checklist.

## Q96-7 — Team briefing and communications

Brief roles, current hazards, movement routes, normal and emergency communications, accountability and stop criteria. Check understanding and make it clear who is coordinating each phase. A small visiting team must not silently assume additional specialist roles for which members are unprepared. Confirm required communication links to remote support and flight crew through approved means.

Use concise messages with location, condition, restriction and requested action. Read back critical information and distinguish receipt from completed action. When a link fails, use only the approved alternative with its conditions satisfied; otherwise hold or escalate as the plan requires. Never use an operational frequency for an unannounced training emergency. Practical exercises use isolated training communications.

Worked and changed applications: Remote support acknowledges a message but has not confirmed the required action. Obtain the actual status rather than treating acknowledgment as completion. Changed case: a team member cannot repeat the emergency muster location. Rebrief and check understanding before the team disperses.

## Q96-8 — Passenger, baggage and freight control

Coordinate passenger movement with crew readiness, actual routes, briefing and assistance arrangements. Reconcile identities and current manifest through the approved process. Equal counts do not establish correct passengers. Keep bags and freight controlled so they do not obstruct movement or change recorded mass after weighing. Refer loading suitability and unusual items to authorized personnel.

Recognize possible dangerous goods and stay within assigned function-based preparation. Hold uncertain items, protect people and seek authorized decisions; do not invent capacity, packaging or classification. A late item is not exempt because the return aircraft is waiting. Maintain the link between person, bag, destination and sector through additions, removals and diversions.

Worked and changed applications: Expected P1/P2/P3/P4, actual P1/P2/P3/P9: four people but the wrong identity. Hold and reconcile before movement. Changed case: a toolbox is added after weighing; verify and revise through the required process rather than accepting the unchanged total.

## Q96-9 — Emergency preplanning and available capability

Before the visit, understand the credible helicopter and installation emergencies, alarms, escape routes, muster arrangements, casualty support and remote assistance. Identify what the installed automatic systems can do and what the visiting team is actually trained and equipped to do. An automatic system does not eliminate accountability, communication or evacuation duties.

Plan for blocked access, failed equipment, delayed external response and a team member becoming unavailable. Preserve a safe escape route and do not commit personnel to a task beyond available protection or competence. Emergency actions must follow the actual plan and crew/incident-command interfaces. A course can rehearse decisions, but cannot invent a rescue capability that the installation does not possess.

Worked and changed applications: The preplan assumes a four-person response team but only two appropriately trained people are available. Escalate the mismatch before accepting the plan. Changed case: an escape route becomes unavailable. Use the approved alternative and update accountability rather than sending people toward the original route by habit.

## Q96-10 — Installed fire systems and limits of intervention

Know the location, indications, activation arrangements and authorized controls of the actual NUI fire-protection system. Understand its relationship to automatic detection and remote support. Do not memorize one generic control sequence for every installation. A reported activation does not prove the hazard is fully controlled; a fault or loss of coverage must be communicated.

Manual intervention, reset or isolation is permitted only under the approved procedure by authorized personnel. Never disable protection merely because activation seems inconvenient. Initial training uses an inert simulator to practice indication recognition, alarm, communication and authorized control decisions. Hands-on firefighting, breathing apparatus, entry or casualty rescue beyond the defined training requires separate competent facilities and qualification; this course does not silently grant them.

Worked and changed applications: The simulator shows an automatic activation while the condition remains uncertain. Raise the alarm, protect people and obtain the required status; do not reset to restore a green display. Changed case: the system reports a fault before movement. Apply the approved restriction/alternative process rather than claiming the team can replace missing protection with enthusiasm.

## Q96-11 — Initial response, casualty information and evacuation

On recognizing an emergency, raise the alarm through the approved route, state location and known facts, protect people and initiate the preplanned actions within your role. Avoid unsupported diagnoses or casualty counts. Coordinate with flight crew, remote support and the responsible incident authority. Keep communications clear and preserve access for responders where safe.

Account for people by identity and last confirmed location. A duplicate name cannot offset a missing person. Use approved muster or evacuation arrangements and do not send an unprotected searcher into a hazardous area. Provide casualty information and assistance within training; medical treatment, rescue entry and aircraft-specific evacuation actions require the applicable competent direction. Reassess as conditions change rather than completing an obsolete plan.

Worked and changed applications: Roster has six people; muster sheet has six entries because one person is listed twice and another is absent. Report five unique accounted for and one missing, with last known information. Changed case: the missing person is later verified at an alternate safe location. Update the record with source/time; do not erase the earlier uncertainty.

## Q96-12 — Final departure and return to unattended state

The final departure has a special transition: the visiting team leaves and local human response may no longer be available. Follow the approved shutdown, equipment restoration, access, accountability and remote handover sequence. Confirm required automatic/remote protection remains in the specified state and record unresolved defects. Do not switch off a system merely because work is finished.

Reconcile all departing people and items, current manifest and crew interface. Verify no one is left unaccounted for and no tool or equipment remains in a prohibited location. Remote acknowledgment must reflect the actual unattended status, restrictions and follow-up. If the required state cannot be established, escalate through the authorized plan rather than falsely signing completion to catch the flight.

Worked and changed applications: The team is ready to leave but a protection-status field is unknown. Hold the handover decision and obtain authorized resolution; do not fill ready by default. Changed case: all people are accounted for but one tool is missing. Resolve its location safely before declaring the relevant area clear.

## Q96-13 — Integrated initial assessment and development

The learner must demonstrate a coherent visit from planning through first arrival, attended operations, abnormal condition, emergency response and final departure. The scenario must make responsibility transitions explicit and require the learner to preserve unresolved holds. Performance is judged on safe decisions, communication, accountability and accurate records, not on how quickly the mock aircraft leaves.

Initial completion requires instruction, corrected checks, applied tasks, observed simulated performance and the separate final. It does not establish independent workplace competence or external accreditation. Record prompting and remediate specific gaps before a changed independent assessment. Employer-led local training, experience and workplace assessment follow before appointment. Preserve source limitations and revisit the package when actual procedures or assigned roles change.

Worked and changed applications: A learner passes calculations but resets an uncertain fire indication without authority. Record critical failure and reassess changed system-control decisions after remediation. Changed case: a learner correctly holds departure because accountability is unresolved. Credit the safety decision; schedule pressure is not a reason to invent completion.

Instructor must understand NUI helicopter operations, emergency planning and assessment; a competent safety controller supervises physical mock work. Verify function-appropriate DG preparation, jurisdiction-required radio qualification, fitness and the learner's intended duties. Actual appointment requires further local training, experience and workplace assessment. Do not use this course as a substitute for specialist weather, refuelling, firefighting, breathing-apparatus, rescue or medical qualifications.

## I96 integrated initial assessment

Each learner leads both A and B through all visit phases; rotate supporting roleplayers without assigning candidate decisions to them. Controller releases only the specified facts, logs responses and avoids coaching. Safety controller can stop at any time. Both emergency exercises must include alarm, communication, safe movement/accountability and an authorized system-status decision. There is no live-fire or unrestricted rescue claim.

Thirteen dimensions match P96-1 through P96-13. Score 0 absent/unsafe, 1 major coaching, 2 safe incomplete/prompted, 3 independently acceptable, 4 accurate independent performance with explanation. Require at least 42/52 in each integrated cycle, every dimension at least 3, all stations passed and no critical error. Critical errors: false readiness, unsafe uncontrolled movement, unauthorized protection reset/isolation, unprotected improvised rescue, invented accountability or operational data, unsupported DG/specialist authority, or fabricated evidence. Retain failed attempts and use targeted practice followed by changed independent assessment. Safe holds receive credit even when they delay the exercise flight.
