# VNDRLY/U Source Inspection — Fixed Equipment

VUC056 · Original available-source course · 2026-11-18

## Program definition

This course teaches supplier surveillance of fixed equipment from purchase requirements through manufacturing records and shipment release. It covers vessels, exchangers, piping, valves, relief devices and associated structural work at the level of inspection planning, evidence review, benign measurements and escalation. It does not qualify pressure-vessel design, code stamping, specialized NDT, welding, pressure testing or operation. The public API SIFE outline and effectivity sheet establish subject context for November 2025–July 2027; full listed standards were not reviewed. This is original VNDRLY training, not API SIFE certification or a claim of complete proprietary-code equivalence. [S01–02]

Prerequisites are industrial safety, reading technical drawings, units and basic arithmetic. Diagnose gaps with the opening work-package exercise and provide instruction before assessment. All F56 criteria are fictional classroom requirements. Use actual approved contractual/code criteria on a real order. Practical tasks use inert models, safe samples, ordinary measurement tools and synthetic records, never live pressure, radiation, hot work or energized equipment.

## Q01 — Source inspection and accountability

Source inspection checks evidence while equipment is being made, when defects or missing information can still be addressed before shipment. The inspector works to an assigned scope rather than taking over the manufacturer's quality system. Identify the purchaser, supplier, sub-supplier and technical decision makers. Clarify who can approve a deviation, release a hold point, authorize shipment and accept equipment at destination. These may be different people.

An inspection visit does not prove every manufacturing activity was observed. State the time, items and records examined. If an unobserved stage is important, determine what evidence the approved plan requires; do not sign a retrospective witness statement. A supplier's responsibility for making conforming equipment does not disappear because a purchaser's inspector attended. Likewise, the inspector must not conceal a known defect by calling the visit a courtesy.

Use a three-part statement: requirement, evidence and conclusion within scope. For example, “Order F56 requires identified heat records for all six plates; four are linked; two remain unresolved.” That is more useful than “material looks good.” Keep technical acceptance separate from commercial schedule discussion.

**Worked case:** supplier asks for an unconditional release after a short visit to one of three fabrication bays. Record the actual bay/items inspected and remaining scope; do not issue a broader conclusion. **Changed case:** records for the other bays arrive. Review their applicability and completeness under the plan, labeling record review accurately rather than claiming personal observation.

## Q02 — Purchase requirements and document hierarchy

Read the purchase order, specification, drawings, data sheets and approved inspection plan as a connected package. Record revisions and any stated order of precedence. A later email does not automatically supersede a controlled drawing. If requirements conflict, raise a technical query to the authorized owner before the affected activity. Preserve the original conflict and the disposition so later inspectors can understand the basis used.

Create a requirement register with the equipment tag, clause or drawing reference, evidence needed, stage and responsible reviewer. Distinguish required from optional documents, but do not downgrade a requirement because it is difficult to obtain. Where a code is named, establish the contractual edition and applicable scope; this course does not supply every code provision. A public examination reference list is not a project specification.

Check units and terminology early. “Test pressure” and “design pressure” are different fields, and a value without units can be ambiguous. Do not repair an ambiguous order by assuming customary units. Resolve it and record the approved interpretation. Changes may affect already completed work as well as future activity.

**Worked case:** drawing revision D specifies nozzle orientation 90°, while the shop traveler still uses revision C at 0°. Hold the affected configuration for controlled resolution. **Changed case:** the revised traveler is issued after fabrication. Future documentation is corrected, but the existing nozzle still needs dimensional verification and authorized disposition.

## Q03 — Inspection plans and notification

Translate the requirements into stages that can actually be inspected. Material identity may need checking before cutting; weld preparation before welding; internal cleanliness before closure. Define hold, witness, review and surveillance points according to the contract's definitions. Record notification timing and the authority required to waive or release a point. Silence is not automatically a waiver unless the governing agreement explicitly makes it so.

Discuss the plan at a pre-inspection meeting with relevant parties. Confirm scope, schedule, document status, sub-supplier controls, test readiness and communication routes. Write actions and owners. The meeting should expose missing prerequisites, not merely produce attendance signatures. A schedule change may require revised notification so the inspector can attend an irreversible stage.

Maintain a stage register rather than relying on memory. If a required hold was bypassed, preserve the actual condition and seek an authorized recovery or disposition. A photograph can be useful evidence, but it may not replace a specific required observation. Do not manufacture a release date after the fact.

**Worked case:** ten internal-inspection holds are required; eight have evidence and two units were closed early. Eight of ten are evidenced, with two missed holds. **Changed case:** supplier offers a final external photograph for each closed unit. The photographs do not establish the internal condition; obtain the designated disposition instead of changing the inspection plan retrospectively.

## Q04 — Supplier quality controls and sub-suppliers

Assess whether the controls needed for this order are actually being used. Look for current work instructions, identification, calibrated equipment, personnel qualifications, inspection records and control of nonconforming items. A quality certificate or polished manual is not proof that a specific traveler was followed. Sample actual jobs and preserve the limits of the sample.

Sub-supplier activities can carry important requirements, such as heat treatment, material testing or coating. Determine how the main supplier transfers requirements and verifies returned evidence. A subcontracted test report should identify the correct item, method, date and acceptance basis. The main order's traceability must survive the handoff. Do not assume a sub-supplier's generic certificate covers all delivered items.

Use findings to separate isolated record errors from potential common-cause issues. If several records use the same invalid tool, the affected population may be larger than the sample. Escalate through the approved quality process; the inspector must not invent a statistical sampling plan or infer lot acceptance from a convenient small sample.

**Worked case:** three of twelve reviewed travelers lack the required tool identity. The sample defect rate is 25%; this does not prove exactly 25% of all travelers are defective. **Changed case:** the same unidentified tool appears across another production lot. Identify the potentially affected scope and obtain an authorized review rather than limit the finding to the first twelve records.

## Q05 — Material receipt and traceability

Match physical items to their specified material and supporting records before fabrication obscures the identity. Check heat/lot marks, product form, dimensions and the required certificate fields. An authentic material report may still belong to the wrong plate. Record how identity transfers when an item is cut, machined or split into multiple parts. Unmarked remnants need controlled disposition, not guessed identity.

Separate material identity, mechanical properties, chemical composition and physical condition. One test may support one of these without proving all the others. Corrosion or damage found at receipt remains a condition issue even if the certificate is correct. If the order requires additional testing, confirm the sample traceability and authorized laboratory/method rather than accepting a result detached from the delivered item.

Use a receipt map tied to the bill of material. For each piece, record tag/heat, certificate, dimension check, visual condition and open issue. Do not multiply a certificate's coverage simply by attaching it to several unmatched pieces. Where markings cannot be read safely, record the limitation and arrange approved access or verification.

**Worked case:** six plates are required; four physical heat marks match reports, one is unreadable and one report belongs to another heat. Four of six have supported identity, 66.6667%; two are unresolved. **Changed case:** the unreadable mark is recovered but that plate has edge damage. Identity coverage improves; physical condition remains a separate decision.

## Q06 — Alloy verification and method limits

A material-verification plan defines which items, elements, locations and methods need checking, and how results are evaluated. The inspector verifies that the plan and records correspond to the actual equipment. A displayed alloy name is not a universal guarantee of all specified properties. Surface condition, contamination, geometry and the instrument's capability can affect what is established.

Handheld XRF cannot establish carbon content, so it cannot by itself resolve a grade distinction that depends on carbon. The manufacturer's public guidance identifies light-element limitations. [S03] Select the actual approved method through qualified personnel rather than inventing a workaround. Chemical identity also does not prove heat treatment, strength, toughness or weld quality.

For this course, use supplied synthetic reports and reference labels; do not operate X-ray or spark equipment. Review instrument identity, reference checks, item/location mapping, units, results and acceptance basis. An unexpected result requires segregation or the applicable hold and technical review. Never relabel a component to make it match a favored reading.

**Worked case:** an order requires a carbon-dependent distinction, but the packet contains only XRF grade matches. State the unverified property and request suitable evidence under the approved plan. **Changed case:** appropriate chemistry evidence arrives, while required heat-treatment records are missing. Close the chemistry issue only; the treatment requirement remains.

## Q07 — Manufacturing routes and inspection timing

Different manufacturing routes create different evidence needs. Casting, forging, rolling, forming, machining and welding do not leave the same features or records. Identify the route for each component and place inspection where relevant characteristics can be checked. A final dimensional check cannot establish every earlier treatment or hidden condition.

For a casting, examine the specified surface/volumetric examination and repair records rather than assuming a smooth exterior proves internal soundness. For a forging or formed plate, correlate the required material condition and treatment with the actual process record. For machining, verify critical dimensions and preservation of identification. This course supplies no universal acceptance limits for these processes; the actual specification controls.

Changes in process can affect qualification and inspection requirements. Ask whether the revised route has authorized approval and whether prior work is affected. Record the change before continuing the affected assessment. The inspector is not a process designer and should not prescribe heat-treatment cycles or repair methods from memory.

**Worked case:** an item specified as forged arrives with a casting route record. Hold the material/process discrepancy for authorized review. **Changed case:** a substitution is approved, but the approval requires additional examination not yet documented. The substitution decision alone does not complete its conditions.

## Q08 — Welding procedures and personnel evidence

Review the welding instruction, its supporting qualification basis and the welder/operator evidence as different records. TWI distinguishes procedure qualification from personnel qualification. [S04] A qualified person still needs the applicable approved production procedure; an approved procedure does not prove every person is qualified to use it. Match process, material, joint, thickness and other applicable variables through the governing requirements, without inventing qualification ranges.

Check actual production records against the approved instruction. Consumable identity/storage, preparation, preheat/interpass controls, sequence and any specified treatment should have appropriate evidence. If a required parameter was not recorded, do not fill it from the target value. A target is not proof of what happened. Raise the gap and preserve the affected weld IDs.

A weld map connects location, welder, procedure, examination and any repair. Keep it current when a weld is repaired or an item is renumbered. A successful examination does not automatically cure a qualification deficiency. The responsible technical authority determines disposition under the actual code and contract.

**Worked case:** WPS revision 4 is approved, but production used revision 2. Identify the changed variables and affected welds for authorized assessment; do not declare all welds good or bad without that basis. **Changed case:** the revisions are technically reconciled, but one welder's applicable qualification evidence is missing. That separate issue remains.

## Q09 — Fabrication fit-up and visual records

Before joining, inspect the specified preparation, alignment and identification using suitable tools and the approved drawing. Record actual gap, offset or dimensions at defined locations. After joining, record visible condition and any required dimensions under the applicable criteria. A visual observation must not be presented as volumetric examination or a complete fitness-for-service conclusion.

Use a repeatable reference: equipment tag, weld number, clock position or drawing datum. Photograph relevant observations when permitted, with scale and orientation where useful. Avoid ambiguous descriptions such as “small defect near end.” Describe the measurable feature and limitation. Do not classify a discontinuity as acceptable without the governing criterion and a valid method.

On inert training samples, practice gauge placement and reading without grinding, welding or entering operating equipment. If a measurement tool cannot access the feature, record that limitation and seek an approved method. Do not force the tool, alter the sample or choose only favorable locations. Retain repeat readings and their reason.

**Worked case:** a fictional maximum offset is 1.0 mm; valid readings are 0.8, 1.1 and 0.9. The 1.1 reading exceeds by 0.1, so averaging does not make the feature conforming. **Changed case:** corrected readings are all 0.9, but required surface examination remains pending. The dimensional correction does not close that examination.

## Q10 — NDT personnel and procedure review

Verify the examination method, written procedure, technique, personnel authorization and equipment evidence required by the order. A training certificate is not automatically an employer's method-specific qualification. ASNT's current public advisory emphasizes the employer's written practice and warns against independent training certificates presented as employer-based certification. [S05] Record the actual scope and validity of the evidence, not just a person's highest title.

The source inspector reviews and witnesses within their competence. Performing or interpreting specialized NDT requires the appropriate qualification and procedure. Do not sign as the examiner because the examiner is unavailable. If a report lacks an acceptance basis, coverage or item identity, request correction supported by actual records; do not supply guessed values.

Examination coverage matters. Ten reports may concern eight unique welds, and a partial scan may not cover the required volume. Reconcile unique locations and the prescribed extent. Where the technique cannot inspect a region, preserve the limitation for technical disposition. “No indication reported” does not necessarily mean the full required examination was completed.

**Worked case:** twenty welds require examination; eighteen unique welds have valid reports and two files are duplicates. Coverage is 90%, not 100%. **Changed case:** reports for the last two arrive, but one lacks applicable personnel authorization. The evidence gap remains until resolved through the proper process.

## Q11 — Examination methods and detection boundaries

Method selection depends on the material, geometry, expected discontinuity, access and governing requirements. Visual examination addresses accessible surfaces. Liquid penetrant relies on surface-connected openings in suitable materials; magnetic methods require appropriate magnetic behavior. Ultrasonic and radiographic methods have their own geometry, orientation and technique limitations. A source inspector must ask whether the prescribed method and coverage were used, not assume all NDT methods are interchangeable.

Review the actual report for technique, reference checks, sensitivity or other required setup evidence, coverage and interpretation. A simple pass stamp without supporting scope may be inadequate for the order. If a method substitution is proposed, obtain designated technical approval and the required qualified procedure. Do not replace a specified examination with a cheaper method on personal judgment.

This course teaches report and readiness review, not live radiography or independent specialist interpretation. Training uses synthetic result maps and safe visual specimens. Unresolved indications go to the qualified examiner and designated acceptance authority. Keep the distinction between detecting a feature, classifying it and deciding acceptance.

**Worked case:** a required volumetric examination is replaced by a surface-only report without approval. State the coverage/method gap and hold acceptance. **Changed case:** a substitution is formally approved, but the report does not show the approved coverage. Authorization of a method does not prove it was performed completely.

## Q12 — Heat-treatment evidence

A treatment record must relate to the actual item and approved cycle. Review identification, thermocouple/location mapping, equipment/reference status, time-temperature trace and required acceptance evidence. The source inspector compares against the supplied approved requirements; this course does not prescribe metallurgical cycles. A chart with an attractive curve is not adequate if it belongs to another batch.

Check the relevant intervals and all required locations. An average temperature can hide a location outside a specified band. A hold period needs the required conditions for the required duration, according to the actual procedure. Missing data, sensor failure or an interrupted cycle requires technical review; do not draw a line through a gap and call it measured.

Preserve original records when re-treatment occurs, and link the approved disposition and subsequent evidence. Re-treatment can have implications that require engineering/metallurgical judgment. The inspector must not order repeated heating simply to obtain a passing chart. Record the limits of what the available trace establishes.

**Worked case:** fictional hold band 600–620°C for 60 continuous minutes at every required channel. One channel remains at 598°C for ten minutes, then meets the band for fifty minutes. The required continuous hold is not established. **Changed case:** a new authorized cycle shows sixty-five continuous minutes in band, but one channel identity is unverified. The time comparison passes; traceability remains unresolved.

## Q13 — Pressure-vessel fabrication surveillance

Identify the vessel tag, construction basis, drawing revision, material map and required inspection stages. Review shell/head dimensions, nozzle orientation, attachments and internal components against the approved documents. The source inspector verifies specified evidence rather than calculating a new allowable pressure or changing the design. Missing design approval is a prerequisite gap, not a request for the inspector to improvise one.

Distinguish the purchaser's surveillance from code-required inspection. ASME's certification program and public data-report forms show manufacturer and authorized-inspection roles. [S06] A VNDRLY course does not provide an Authorized Inspector commission or authority to apply a code mark. Verify required signatures and identity through the applicable process without signing in someone else's role.

Arrange safe access to the necessary surfaces before closure. Real vessel entry requires its own confined-space controls; no entry occurs in this course. Use an open model or drawing for internal mapping. If an internal attachment was not inspected before closure, record the missed stage and seek authorized disposition. External appearance does not establish the hidden configuration.

**Worked case:** vessel drawing D locates nozzle N2 at 90°, while a valid measurement gives 95°; fictional tolerance is ±2°. Difference 5° exceeds tolerance by 3°. **Changed case:** approved correction achieves 91°, but a required internal attachment record remains absent. Close orientation only; retain the internal-evidence issue.

## Q14 — Heat exchangers and separated pressure boundaries

An exchanger can contain distinct sides with different materials, requirements and test records. Map shell side, tube side, tubesheets, channel/heads and specified internals to the approved documents. Do not treat one side's test as proof of the other side or of all possible leakage paths. Identify the exact configuration and boundaries for each required examination or test.

Review tube identity, tube-to-tubesheet joint method, required examination, dimensional evidence and cleanliness through the order's plan. A tube count is not proof of correct material or joint quality. If a repair or plugging proposal changes the supplied configuration, it needs the designated technical disposition and updated records. The inspector does not decide how many tubes may be removed from service based on a classroom percentage.

Preservation matters because openings and internal passages can be contaminated after fabrication. Verify the specified protective measures and their condition at the inspection point. Report inaccessible or unverified areas rather than assuming cleanliness. No actual exchanger entry, pressure application or tube repair is part of the course.

**Worked case:** synthetic exchanger E1 requires separate shell- and tube-side records. Only the shell-side record is present. One required side remains unsupported. **Changed case:** the tube-side record arrives but identifies E2. Two files still do not establish two valid sides for E1; resolve identity before acceptance.

## Q15 — Piping spools and tubular products

Use the isometric or fabrication drawing to map each spool, fitting, flange, weld and material record. Verify dimensions from the stated datum, including orientation and attachment details required by the order. A spool that fits a shipping rack is not necessarily dimensionally correct. Preserve identification when pieces are cut or repackaged.

Review weld maps and required examinations against the actual spool configuration. Repairs, added branches or revised weld numbers must flow into the final dossier. A report associated with a removed weld cannot prove the replacement weld was examined. For tubular products, confirm the specified product identity, dimensions, condition and required manufacturing/testing records; do not assume all pipe standards or grades are interchangeable.

Separate shop completion from field installation. Field supports, fit-up, system testing and operating release may remain outside the source inspection. A shipment statement should say which shop requirements were verified and which conditions remain. Do not authorize force-fitting a spool to compensate for an unresolved dimension.

**Worked case:** fictional spool length 2000±3 mm; actual 2004. Deviation is +4 mm, one millimetre beyond the allowed upper limit. **Changed case:** corrected length 2002 meets that comparison, but flange orientation is wrong. A passing length does not resolve orientation.

## Q16 — Valves and test evidence

Confirm valve tag, type, size, material, pressure/class basis, end connections and specified orientation. Review the actual test requirements for the ordered valve, including which parts or sealing directions are covered. Shell integrity, seat leakage and functional movement are different questions. A general test certificate may omit a required direction, medium, duration or acceptance basis.

The inspector reviews approved test procedures and actual records, or witnesses under the supplier's safe test system where authorized. This course uses records only and does not energize a valve test stand. Do not copy a leak limit from another valve type or derive a test pressure from an unrelated class designation. The actual order and applicable standard govern.

After testing, verify required drainage, cleanliness, preservation, locking or shipping position as specified. A test result can be valid while shipping condition is unacceptable. Record any change or disassembly after the test and determine whether required reinspection or retesting has been addressed. Do not assume a prior pass covers a changed configuration.

**Worked case:** an order requires two seat directions and a shell test; records show shell and only direction A. Direction B remains missing. **Changed case:** B's report arrives, but the valve was disassembled afterward with no required follow-up evidence. The record set must address the final configuration.

## Q17 — Pressure-relief devices

Verify the device identity and approved data sheet, including required set conditions, materials, connections and associated certification records. A set-pressure entry alone does not establish every required performance or capacity characteristic. Keep the device matched to its tag and records through testing, sealing and shipment. A swapped tag can make an otherwise valid certificate irrelevant.

Source inspection does not authorize changing a relief setting or selecting a replacement protection scheme. Such changes require the designated engineering/operating authority and applicable certification process. Review whether required settings, seals and test evidence are intact, without breaking a seal or operating the device casually. Use inert models and synthetic reports in training.

Document the final shipping configuration and any specified protective items. Transport damage or missing protection can require further action even after a valid test. If a discrepancy concerns a safety function, preserve the hold and escalate clearly. Do not approve shipment merely because the device looks similar to another accepted unit.

**Worked case:** device R1's plate and certificate identify different serial numbers. Hold identity unresolved; do not copy one onto the other. **Changed case:** identity is corrected by valid records, but a required seal is broken. The identity issue closes while seal/configuration verification remains.

## Q18 — Structural fabrications and attachments

Review the specified structural drawing, material identities, weld/bolt requirements, dimensions and inspection records. Associated platforms, supports, lifting attachments and frames may have different governing requirements from the pressure boundary. A vessel weld procedure is not automatically suitable for every structural joint. Match the actual approved basis and qualification scope.

Dimensional inspection should use known datums and suitable tools. Check the features required by the drawing, not only overall appearance. Squareness, hole spacing and alignment can affect installation even where individual member lengths look correct. Record actual measurements and limitations. Do not approve an unengineered alteration to a lifting attachment or treat a classroom inspection as a load test.

If a structural item is repaired, determine required approval, examination and revised evidence. Keep coating or insulation from concealing unresolved inspection points. Access for inspection must be safe; this course uses bench-scale models and no lifting operation or work at height.

**Worked case:** rectangular model diagonals are 1000 and 1008 mm; fictional maximum difference 5. Difference 8 exceeds by 3. **Changed case:** corrected diagonals differ by 4, but a specified weld examination is missing. Geometry passes; weld evidence does not.

## Q19 — Coating, cleanliness and preservation

Identify the ordered surface preparation, coating system, environmental limits, application records and final inspection requirements. A uniform color is not proof of correct preparation or film thickness. Use the specified method, instrument and sampling plan; a single favorable reading cannot replace required coverage. Coating applied over an unresolved inspection point can obstruct later verification.

In training, use safe coated coupons and a suitable checked thickness gauge under its instructions. Record substrate/reference, units, actual readings and the supplied fictional decision rule. Do not perform abrasive blasting, solvent application or hazardous surface preparation. Actual coating chemistry and cure conditions must come from approved product/project documents.

Preservation extends beyond paint. Verify specified caps, plugs, desiccants, corrosion protection, support and storage conditions as applicable. Identify who maintains them during transport and storage. A shipment release should not imply indefinite preservation or remove the receiving party's inspection responsibilities. Record damage or a broken barrier before shipment.

**Worked case:** fictional coupon band 200–300 µm; valid readings 220, 245 and 190. The low reading is 10 below minimum, despite average 218.3333. **Changed case:** all required corrected readings meet the band, but a shipping cap is missing. Coating closure does not close preservation.

## Q20 — Pressure-test readiness and safe witnessing

Pressure testing stores energy and can eject components or release fluid. HSE GS4 emphasizes risk assessment, a written safe system, segregation and control of test connectors. [S07] The inspector must not enter a danger zone or approach a suspected leak to obtain a better view. Follow the test authority's controls and stop-work route. This course conducts no pressure test, including supposedly harmless low-pressure demonstrations.

Before an authorized real test, review the required procedure, item/boundary identity, readiness evidence, instruments, controls and witness arrangements within assigned scope. Do not create the test pressure, duration or medium from a generic training formula. Hydrostatic and pneumatic tests have different hazards; a proposed substitution requires the actual approved process. A gauge reading alone does not prove the test setup or record is valid.

After a test, review actual results and any anomalies against its acceptance basis. A repaired or altered configuration may require additional evidence. Do not sign for a test you did not witness; label record review accurately. The test crew retains responsibility for safe depressurization and access. No leaking connection should be tightened at the inspector's casual request.

**Worked case:** required test record lacks instrument serial and actual hold time. A signature alone does not establish those facts. **Changed case:** supporting records resolve them, but the tested serial differs from the ordered equipment. Identity remains a separate prerequisite.

## Q21 — Measurement reliability and arithmetic

Choose a tool and method capable of resolving the required characteristic. Verify range, condition, reference checks and identification. Record the datum, units and actual readings. If the tool is found invalid afterward, identify potentially affected work rather than replace it silently. A calibration sticker without the matching tool identity is incomplete evidence.

Use units consistently and show comparisons. Percent error needs a stated denominator; dimensional tolerance needs the actual nominal and allowed range. An average can hide an out-of-limit individual result when the requirement applies to each point. Uncertainty near a boundary must follow the specified decision rule, not a rule selected to obtain acceptance.

Training uses verified reference specimens and benign gauges. The instructor records known reference values before scoring, while learners record their actual measurements. If a suitable tool or reference is missing, the practical outcome remains pending. Discussion can teach the decision but cannot establish that the learner can make a valid physical measurement.

**Worked case:** indicator 50.2 mm, reference 50.0, allowed error ±0.1. Error +0.2 fails by 0.1. **Changed case:** indicator 50.05 is within that comparison, but the instrument range does not cover the actual job's 300 mm feature. Passing one reference does not establish suitability for every task.

## Q22 — Nonconformance and corrective action

Write findings as specific requirement/evidence gaps tied to item identity. Describe the actual condition, affected scope and hold or restriction. Avoid unsupported blame or speculative root cause. The designated authority decides disposition; the inspector verifies required implementation and evidence within assigned scope. “Use as is” needs actual authorization and conditions, not an inspector's convenience.

Correction fixes the immediate issue; corrective action addresses why it occurred and whether recurrence is controlled. A rewritten form may correct a clerical defect while leaving an invalid process unchanged. Conversely, a process improvement does not automatically accept already affected products. Track both populations where applicable.

Close a finding only when the required disposition and verification are supported. Preserve original observations and reopening history. If the same tool, lot or procedure affects other items, communicate the possible extent for authorized investigation. Do not conceal a serious issue in a general progress percentage.

**Worked case:** fifteen findings: nine verified closed, three corrected awaiting verification, three awaiting disposition. Verified closure is 60%, six open. **Changed case:** two verified findings reopen and one pending closes. Eight are now verified closed, seven open; closure is 53.3333%. Record the actual identities and restrictions.

## Q23 — Final records and shipment release

Reconcile the order's required dossier against unique equipment identities and the final configuration. Check approved drawings, materials, fabrication, examinations, treatment, tests, deviations, preservation and required formal records as applicable. A large file count does not demonstrate completeness. Duplicate reports and superseded revisions should not be counted as new evidence.

State the release scope precisely. Shipment release may authorize transport under conditions while leaving destination checks, installation or operating acceptance outstanding. A code-required data report must be handled by its designated manufacturer/authorized-inspection process; the purchaser's source inspector does not fill another authority's signature. [S06] If required evidence is missing, record the appropriate hold or conditional decision under actual authority.

Before release, verify that open conditions have named owners and that the recipient can identify them. A verbal promise to send missing records later is not automatically an approved concession. Preserve what was reviewed, by whom and when. Report actual status rather than equating “almost complete” with complete.

**Worked case:** twenty dossier items required, eighteen valid, one duplicate and one wrong revision. Completeness is 90%, with two gaps. **Changed case:** the wrong revision is replaced validly, but the duplicate remains; 19/20=95%, not complete. Any conditional shipment needs the authorized decision and its restrictions.

## Q24 — Reporting, handover and continuing competence

A useful visit report allows another inspector to continue without guessing. Identify order, supplier, equipment, date, stage, documents used, work observed, records reviewed, findings and next actions. Separate fact from interpretation. If a result is pending, say what evidence will resolve it and who owns it. Do not use a generic “satisfactory” conclusion that hides known limitations.

Give urgent findings through the required immediate route, then preserve the written record. Routine reporting should be timely enough to prevent an irreversible next stage from proceeding on false assumptions. Maintain professional independence while explaining the issue clearly. If a dispute persists, use the defined technical escalation rather than invent authority or quietly erase the finding.

Keep competence and records current for the actual methods and equipment inspected. A general source-inspection course does not replace specialized NDT qualifications, code commissions or employer authorization. Changes in manufacturing route, requirements or demonstrated performance can require additional training and assessment. An accurate limited conclusion is more valuable than a broad unsupported one.

**Worked case:** successor inspector receives only “all good” although two holds are unresolved. Replace it with actual item IDs, evidence, restrictions and owners. **Changed case:** one hold closes before handover. Update that item with the new evidence while retaining the second; do not repeat stale status or erase the original history.

# VUC056 workbook, practical delivery and award

## Delivery and safe equipment

Plan 48 hours: 16 instruction/checks, 8 application labs, 12 stations, 6 integrated supplier case/remediation and 6 final. Breaks and extra remediation are additional. Record actual attendance. Diagnose reading, drawing, unit and arithmetic prerequisites, then teach missing skills before scored assessment. Instructor must be competent in source-inspection planning, fixed-equipment records and the actual benign measurement tools; obtain specialist support for metallurgical/code questions outside that competence.

Provide synthetic F56 purchase/drawing/traveler/test packets, uniquely tagged inert metal coupons, known reference dimensions, bench-scale spool/structure models, suitable verified ruler/caliper/offset gauge, safely coated coupons and a suitable checked film-thickness gauge. Instructor records actual reference values/tool serials before assessment. Supply no energized process equipment, pressure test, radiation source, welding, chemicals, abrasive preparation, lifting operation or vessel entry. Photographs and synthetic charts cover hazardous processes. Actual measurement stations cannot be replaced by reading case numbers.

P05, P09, P19 and P21 require observed physical performance. I56 includes those actual tasks plus a coherent dossier. If equipment or safe supervision is missing, the affected learner outcome remains pending. Accessible fixtures/response methods are acceptable if equivalent outcomes remain observable. Instructor uses safe labels/cards for seeded defects; never create a physical hazard to test recognition.

## F56 supplier dossier

Order F56-01 contains vessel V1, exchanger E1, spool S1, valve A1, relief device R1 and frame F1. Each has an identity register. Approved classroom drawings and criteria below are fictional. The packet includes: revision D nozzle at 90±2°; spool 2000±3 mm; frame diagonal difference maximum 5 mm; coupon offset maximum 1.0 mm; coating 200–300 µm at every supplied point; measurement error maximum ±0.1 mm; treatment 600–620°C for 60 continuous minutes at each required channel. These are exercise criteria, not code values.

Instructor issues the specific current record cards and preserves a reference copy. Missing records are intentionally missing; learners must report them rather than invent them. An authorized-disposition card closes only the conditions stated on that card. Use distinct issue IDs to prevent one correction from silently closing unrelated gaps. Mark all documents synthetic and never insert them into business records.

## W01 — Requirements, plan and supplier controls

Packet: drawing D and traveler C conflict on nozzle orientation; ten internal holds, eight releases, two units already closed; three of twelve sampled travelers omit tool identity. Task: create requirement/stage matrix and supplier finding register with owners.

## I56 — Supplier surveillance from receipt to shipment

Learner receives F56 dossier, models, coupons and tools. Build the inspection plan, map requirements/identities, perform the four actual measurement/identity tasks, review fabricated equipment records, issue findings and reconcile final shipment evidence. Instructor supplies controlled disposition cards only when requested with a supported issue; never reward a guessed approval. Learner gives a written and spoken handover with equipment-specific restrictions.

Score six dimensions 0–2: planning/identity, physical measurement, process/examination evidence, equipment-specific comparisons, finding closure, handover/safety. Pass 10/12, no zero/critical error, all four actual stations passed. Changed case uses different safe coupon/reference and document IDs with revised findings. Preserve initial and changed records. No actual pressure, NDT radiation, heat treatment or fabrication is performed.

## Controlled final and remediation

Twenty-four cases, three decisions each, two points each: 144 total, 360 minutes. Calculator/coursebook allowed; keys, collaboration and external assistance prohibited. Pass 116/144 and at least 4/6 in every domain, with no unresolved critical error. Per decision: 2 correct supported response, 1 safe incomplete, 0 wrong/missing/unsafe. Separate changed form tests changed facts; do not issue keys before scored attempts.

After a failure, teach the specific gap, require corrected reasoning and a changed case. Reobserve failed actual tasks. After two unsuccessful changed attempts, provide individualized instruction and a fresh equivalent supervised assessment. Allow documented appeal with criterion/evidence to another competent assessor where feasible. Keep unsuccessful attempts; do not overwrite them with the passing score.

## Credential and records

Employer qualification and applicable statutory/code responsibilities remain separate. Review at 24 months under VNDRLY policy and sooner after changes or demonstrated gaps; this is not an external credential renewal rule.
