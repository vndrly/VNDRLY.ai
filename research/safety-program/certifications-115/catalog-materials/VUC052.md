# VNDRLY/U Pipeline Facility Construction Inspection

VUC052 · Original available-source materials · 2026-11-18

## Scope and learning route

This course prepares construction quality personnel to inspect evidence for onshore pipeline facilities, including civil works, structures, packaged machinery, piping and instrumentation interfaces. It incorporates the separately identified pipeline-construction foundation Q01–24, then adds facility instruction Q25–40. It is not an API1184 credential, an engineering license, an electrical qualification or permission to energize equipment. API's public program and forthcoming December2026–August2028 outline establish the external subject; full proprietary standards were not available for clause-by-clause comparison.[F01–03]

Read the foundation first, complete its W01–08 applications, then work through the following facility sequence with W09–16. All F52 dimensions, tolerances, strengths, times and acceptance rules are fictional teaching specifications. They are not extracted from API, ASME, ACI, AWS or manufacturer acceptance tables. Obtain the actual approved drawings, procedures and governing requirements for real work. Distinguish a measured condition from the engineer's disposition and from the operator's permission to commission.

## Q25 — Facility boundaries and inspection planning

A facility comprises interacting systems rather than a single continuous line. A pump package may arrive with a vendor inspection dossier, sit on a separately constructed foundation, connect to contractor piping and depend on another contractor's power and controls. A satisfactory document for one boundary does not accept the others. Start with the system list, plot plan, piping and instrumentation diagrams, equipment list and contractual inspection matrix. Identify ownership at each interface and establish who witnesses, who accepts technical deviations and who authorizes operations.

Build the inspection plan around irreversible or concealed work: underground services before cover, reinforcement before concrete, foundation readiness before placement, alignment before and after connections, and isolated test boundaries before testing. Define a hold as a requirement for release by the designated authority; do not reinterpret a missed hold as a normal witness point. Record actual notification, attendance and disposition. If the specified inspector is unavailable, seek the documented alternative; do not sign attendance after the fact.

Review bid and construction documents for exclusions, ambiguous interfaces and conflicting revisions before work starts. A lower-priced package may exclude a test that the overall project still requires; record who will perform it rather than silently omit it. Check trade qualifications against actual tasks and operator-qualification applicability, validity and scope, rather than assuming every construction task has the same qualification requirement. A welding credential does not establish electrical competence. Review calibration identity, validity and range for the actual measurement. Site orientation, task risk review and work permits are prerequisites to the relevant work, not interchangeable certificates.

**Worked case:** F52 has four work packages: civil, steel, mechanical and electrical. A vendor signs mechanical factory acceptance while foundation anchor evidence is missing. Record the factory evidence as one completed interface and retain the civil hold. **Changed case:** a civil report arrives without the equipment tag. Resolve its applicability before treating the hold as closed. The mistake is counting signatures instead of testing what each signature establishes.

## Q26 — Survey, grading and underground coordination

Facility coordinates include horizontal location, elevation and equipment orientation. A plot plan dimension can be correct while the elevation datum is wrong. Read the benchmark identifier, units, survey control and drawing revision together. Record measurements as observations against named reference points, not as a guessed adjustment to make the numbers agree. Compare final grading with drainage requirements and buried-service information; settlement, ponding or an unexplained crossing warrants the responsible technical review.

Sequence locates and authorized exposure before excavation. Keep construction survey evidence separate from clearance to dig. During backfill, associate compaction reports with lift, material, test location and method. A passing nearby test does not necessarily cover the actual pad or changed material. For a supplied density comparison, relative compaction is field dry density divided by the specified laboratory reference density, multiplied by 100. Both densities must use the same basis; wet density is not interchangeable with dry density.

If blasting is proposed, verify the competent specialist's approved plan, applicable permissions, protected boundaries and coordination with nearby facilities through the responsible authority. This course does not teach charge design or authorize explosives handling. Missing notification or a changed nearby facility is a reason to stop the affected authorization path, not to estimate a safe distance. Check erosion and sediment controls against the actual environmental plan before disturbing the site, then record maintenance and changed runoff conditions. A landowner's permission does not replace regulatory or facility-owner requirements.

**Worked case:** F52 pad reference is 2000 kg/m³, measured dry density 1880 kg/m³, fictional minimum 95%. The result 94% is one percentage point short. Record the location/lift and request disposition; do not average it with an unrelated good lift. **Changed case:**1900 gives 95%, meeting only that supplied comparison. Missing drainage approval remains open. At target elevation 100.000 m, measured 99.982 m is 18 mm low; a fictional ±10 mm criterion is exceeded by 8 mm.

## Q27 — Reinforcement, embeds and pre-pour evidence

Before concrete conceals the work, reconcile foundation identity, dimensions, reinforcement arrangement, cover, embedded plates, anchor template and openings with the approved revision. Trace required material evidence and inspect stability and cleanliness from a safe position. A photograph can support the record, but an unscaled image usually cannot establish a precise cover or anchor dimension. Record the actual measuring method and locations. Never move reinforcement or anchors merely to make a checklist pass without the responsible approval.

In US construction, protruding reinforcement that creates an impalement hazard needs protection, and construction loads require the specified structural-capacity determination.[F04] Inspection access must not create exposure to unprotected reinforcement, unsupported forms or suspended loads. Observe from an approved safe location; refer unsafe conditions immediately through site controls.

**Worked case:** fictional required cover is 50±5 mm. Readings48, 51, 43 mm produce one low result: 43 is 2 mm below the 45 mm lower bound. The mean 47.33 mm does not clear the local deficiency. Anchor pitch target 600±3 mm with 606 measured is 3 mm beyond the 603 maximum. Record two separate findings and keep the pour hold. **Changed case:** corrected pitch 602 mm does not resolve cover. Verify corrected cover before the responsible authority releases the pour. The common error is treating a corrected visible item as closure of the whole foundation.

## Q28 — Concrete placement, specimens and curing records

Inspect the approved mix identification, delivery record, placement location and specified sampling plan together. The inspection record should connect the batch to the actual foundation, specimen identification, sampling time, curing history and laboratory report. Unapproved water addition or another mix substitution is a recorded deviation for authorized assessment, not an inspector's opportunity to invent a compensating strength factor. Document interruptions and observed segregation or consolidation concerns without declaring an unsupported structural conclusion.

Separate placement acceptance, curing protection, form removal and load release. They may depend on different evidence. OSHA's cast-in-place requirements address formwork support, available plans and relevant removal conditions; a visually firm surface is not a universal release criterion.[F05] The approved project procedure determines testing and technical acceptance. A low or ambiguous result requires investigation of the specimen and structure through the responsible process, not automatic demolition or automatic acceptance.

**Worked case:** F52 supplies a teaching rule requiring mean of three named 28-day specimens≥30 MPa and none below 27 MPa. Results31, 29, 32 have mean 30.67 and minimum 29, satisfying that rule if identity and curing are valid. **Changed case:**32, 32, 26 average 30, but 26 is 1 MPa below the individual minimum. Record the failure despite the passing mean. A missing specimen identity prevents confident application of either result to foundation F2. These rules are fictional, not concrete-code acceptance criteria.

## Q29 — Grout, baseplates and load-transfer evidence

An equipment base transfers load through a designed support arrangement. Grout identity, substrate preparation, placement, curing, anchor condition and the specified checks form one evidence chain. Inspect against the actual installation documents, including environmental limits and hold times. Record void indications and damaged areas for disposition rather than invent a repair by drilling or adding material. Quantities help reconcile deliveries but do not prove adequate contact or load transfer.

Read the geometry carefully. Gross envelope volume includes spaces occupied by pockets, rails or other exclusions. Convert all dimensions to consistent units before subtracting those exclusions. Apply the exercise allowance only after calculating net volume, and label it procurement allowance, not an acceptance tolerance.

**Worked case:** a fictional 2 m×1 m base with 0.04 m grout depth has 0.08 m³ gross volume. Subtract0.01 m³ specified exclusions to get 0.07 m³ net. A10% ordering allowance yields 0.077 m³, or 77 L. This says nothing by itself about actual cured quality. **Changed case:** depth 0.05 m yields 0.09 m³ net and 99 L with allowance. If an anchor is altered during placement, retain its engineering disposition separately. A model-specific Goulds manual describes alignment rechecks after grout and piping connections; use the actual equipment manual rather than generalizing its tolerances to every machine.[F06]

## Q30 — Structural steel, connections and protective systems

Structural inspection starts with member and connection identity. Match erection drawings, marks, material records and approved revisions. Verify the required connection inspection records and the competence of the personnel doing the work. A bolt head marking is one piece of identification, not proof of installation method, pretension or acceptance. Do not prescribe a universal torque from diameter alone. Welding evidence similarly requires applicable procedure, personnel and examination information rather than a generic credential.

Temporary stability and lifting remain controlled construction functions. Do not step onto an incomplete structure to obtain an inspection photograph or direct removal of temporary bracing. Report unexpected deformation, missing connections or altered holes through the responsible route. Separate structural disposition from coating or fireproofing restoration; a repaired finish cannot establish structural adequacy.

**Worked case:** F52 has 24 specified connections. Twenty-two have valid final records, one failed and one has an incorrect location ID. Valid coverage is 22/24=91.67%, with two unresolved. **Changed case:** the incorrect ID is reconciled by controlled evidence; 23/24=95.83%, one remains. Report the actual failure rather than rounding coverage to “complete.” If the failed connection is repaired, require the specified verification before closure; a work order marked done is not a passing inspection record.

## Q31 — Packaged equipment receipt and preservation

Factory acceptance is evidence about a defined factory configuration and test. Transport, storage and site assembly can change the condition. At receipt, compare tag, serial, packing list, shipping restraints, preservation instructions and visible damage. Record discrepancies without operating or opening equipment beyond authorized instructions. Keep preservation activities traceable by equipment, date, condition and responsible person. Protect stored instruments, machined surfaces and openings according to the actual vendor plan.

Check the lift plan and designated lifting interfaces through competent personnel before movement; the construction inspector's course is not rigging qualification. A shipping bracket is not automatically an approved lifting point. Confirm foundation release and access arrangements before scheduling placement so production pressure does not turn a missing prerequisite into a retrospective signature.

**Worked case:** a synthetic preservation plan requires review every 14 elapsed days. Last review was day 4; next due day 18. At day 20 it is 2 days overdue. Record the gap and seek condition assessment, not a backdated check. **Changed case:** a shipping cap is missing on receipt even though the interval is current. Contamination/preservation condition is independently unresolved. A vendor's later statement must identify what was examined and what disposition was authorized. Preserve the original observation and subsequent resolution as distinct records.

## Q32 — Machinery alignment, soft foot and pipe strain

A machine can have correct shaft alignment before connections and move when its supports or piping change. Capture the setup, reference, instrument status, measured positions and applicable vendor criteria. Check support condition and the required sequence before interpreting an alignment number. Soft foot concerns uneven support or movement at a foot; its correction is a controlled installation task, not permission to loosen live machinery. Distinguish parallel offset from angular misalignment and retain the actual measurement method.

In classroom work, use a stable, unpowered model with no pinch or stored-energy hazard. Read a dial or equivalent reference under assessor supervision. Field work requires isolation, competent personnel and the approved method. A final “within tolerance” statement must identify which criterion and equipment state it covers. Do not force piping into position with bolts as a shortcut to an acceptable indicator reading.

**Worked case:** F52 parallel offset is 0.04 mm before piping and 0.16 mm after, with fictional maximum 0.10 mm. Change is 0.12 mm; the final reading is 0.06 mm high. Record the condition and seek support/piping investigation before release. **Changed case:** after an approved correction the reading is 0.08 mm, but angular evidence is missing. The offset comparison passes; overall alignment remains unverified. Separate the measured change from any unproven diagnosis about its cause.[F06]

## Q33 — Facility piping, supports and bolted joints

Follow the line list and piping class through spools, valves, gaskets, bolting, supports and instruments. Similar appearance does not prove pressure rating, material grade or compatibility. Trace required records to the installed identity. Inspect orientation, access, flow direction and support arrangement against approved drawings. Field rerouting may change loads or stress assumptions even when the line connects physically. Obtain the authorized change disposition and preserve the actual as-built information.

A bolted joint is a system: correct components, condition, alignment, assembly method and recorded verification. A completed tightening checklist cannot correct a wrong gasket. A piping support's installed condition must match its intended service state, including any required temporary restraint removal or setting verification. Do not remove shipping or test restraints without the approved sequence and authorization.

**Worked case:** a twelve-joint dossier has ten valid assembly records, one missing record and one wrong gasket. Document two unresolved joints. The wrong gasket needs physical correction and verification; the missing record requires an evidence-based disposition and cannot be reconstructed by invented initials. **Changed case:** the gasket is corrected, but the associated tightening record belongs to an earlier configuration. Verify the final assembly. This is configuration control, not an invitation to copy a previous signature.

## Q34 — Test boundaries, cleanliness and reinstatement

Pressure testing begins with an engineered and approved boundary, not a target pressure chosen by the inspector. Check the documented system limits, component compatibility, exclusions, temporary items, instruments, personnel and emergency controls. Verification is not authorization to conduct the test. Never approach or tighten a leaking pressurized joint. Preserve the test record's actual conditions and the disposition of anomalies; a flat chart alone does not prove every acceptance requirement.

After testing, the facility must be restored to its intended configuration. Reinstatement is a separate controlled stage: account for blinds, removed instruments, vents, drains, temporary supports, test connections and protective devices. Match the register to the physical system under the approved safe process. Cleaning, drying or preservation evidence must identify the system and method; a clean sample from another line proves nothing about the target boundary.

**Worked case:** F52 register lists 18 temporary blinds. Sixteen have signed removal evidence, one is still installed and one has unknown status. Two remain unresolved; do not assume the unknown one was removed. **Changed case:** physical verification closes the unknown item. The installed blind still blocks the specified readiness condition. Report17/18 verified removals, not operational readiness. Handover must preserve remaining restrictions even if the pressure-test report itself is accepted.

## Q35 — Instrument installation, tubing and identification

An instrument tag connects process function, location, range, materials, wiring and software configuration. Trace the installed device to its datasheet and the loop drawing. Inspect mount, accessible orientation, tubing identification and protection under the approved documents. A transmitter with the right model but wrong range can produce plausible yet incorrect control information. A local display and the control-room indication may use different units or scaling; compare both against the same approved basis.

Tubing and fittings require the applicable manufacturer's assembly and verification procedure. This course does not supply universal turns, torque or leak-test pressure. Protect openings and preserve cleanliness. An inspector should recognize mixed or unidentified components, unsupported tubing or a revision mismatch and route the finding; do not disturb a live connection for a better look.

**Worked case:** PT52 is specified 0–100 bar, but its configuration sheet says 0–160 bar. A50% signal corresponds to 50 bar in the required range and 80 bar in the configured range. The30 bar difference can be a scaling error even if the wiring has continuity. **Changed case:** configuration is corrected, but the installed device's process-material compatibility remains undocumented. Keep that issue open. Range correction is not universal acceptance of the instrument. Record actual tag, serial, revision and verified final configuration.

## Q36 — Loop checks, calibration and functional boundaries

Calibration compares response with a reference; a loop check follows a signal through the configured path. Neither automatically proves the whole protective function. Identify the test point, reference, expected response, observed response and tolerance. Preserve as-found results before adjustments and as-left results afterward. A selected output test may bypass the sensing element, so state exactly which portion was exercised. The Rosemount manual provides a model-specific loop-test example and cautions; apply only the correct equipment instructions.[F07]

For a fictional linear 4–20 mA signal, fraction of span=(mA−4)/16. Engineering value=lower range+fraction×span. A signal below or above the normal range may indicate a fault or configured alarm; do not extrapolate it into a valid process value without the device basis. Testing that can move valves, trip machinery or bypass protection requires the authorized isolation and test plan. Classroom work uses only a current-limited benign simulator.

**Worked case:**0–100 bar at 12 mA means 50 bar. A display reading 52 bar has+2 bar error, or 2% of span, exceeding a fictional ±1% span limit by 1 percentage point. **Changed case:**16 mA means 75 bar; 74.5 bar has−0.5% span error and passes that numerical comparison. Missing final restoration of a test override still prevents readiness. Record scaling performance and protection status independently.

## Q37 — Electrical classification and installation evidence

Electrical inspection at a facility includes equipment identity, installation details and the actual hazardous-area basis. OSHA's construction rule addresses equipment and wiring for classified locations; area conditions and suitability cannot be inferred from a generic enclosure rating alone.[F08] Match the equipment markings and installation documentation to the approved area classification, including relevant service conditions and protection method. Do not translate one certification system into another from visual similarity.

The course does not qualify a learner for energized testing or to certify a hazardous-area installation. Inspect records and de-energized teaching samples only. In actual work, qualified electrical personnel perform the applicable examinations and tests under site controls. Glands, seals, unused openings and alterations can matter to the protection system; an appropriate nameplate does not excuse an incorrect installation. Record exact missing evidence rather than inventing a code acceptance limit.

Trace cables from their schedule through trays, conduits, junction boxes and terminations. Compare routing, supports, separation, labels and enclosure entries with the approved installation drawings and manufacturer instructions. Do not invent universal tray fill, bend radius, separation or pulling limits. A cable-end label proves neither the route nor the correct terminal. Where a conductor is moved, preserve the revised termination record and required competent verification before energization. Unidentified spare conductors or open entries require disposition under the actual installation basis.

**Worked case:** the equipment file shows an outdoor ingress rating but no evidence of suitability for the specified classified area. Record the suitability gap and refer it; “weatherproof” is not a substitute. **Changed case:** a suitable device certificate arrives, but the cable entry arrangement differs from its approved installation. Keep the installation issue unresolved. Avoid an overall electrical pass based on a single certificate. Maintain the tag-to-certificate-to-installation traceability through handover.

## Q38 — Grounding, bonding and corrosion interfaces

Grounding, protective bonding and cathodic protection serve different purposes and may interact. Identify the design function of each connection before judging a measurement. A low resistance reading does not by itself prove fault-clearing performance, lightning protection or correct CP isolation. A high resistance at a deliberately insulating feature is not automatically a defect. Compare the approved schematic and actual installation, then obtain the competent specialist's specified tests and disposition.

Record test method, instrument, lead compensation, location and system state. Never disconnect a live protective or corrosion-control connection as a classroom-inspired experiment. Classroom measurements use only an isolated low-voltage model. Treat unknown parallel paths as a limitation rather than evidence that the intended connection exists. Preserve CP references and electrical-safety records separately, with documented interface review where the designs interact.

**Worked case:** F52 teaching continuity maximum is 0.10 Ω after lead correction. Observed0.18 Ω with lead resistance 0.03 Ω gives 0.15 Ω, which is 0.05 Ω high. **Changed case:** observed 0.11 minus 0.03 gives 0.08 Ω and passes only that supplied criterion. If a parallel temporary bond was present, the intended installed path still needs verification. A passing arithmetic result cannot resolve an unknown test configuration or establish full electrical safety.

## Q39 — Simultaneous operations and controlled completion

Facility trades overlap spatially and in time. Welding beside coating work, lifting over installed equipment or testing while another crew modifies an adjacent boundary creates coordination risks. Review the actual permit and work-control interfaces before inspection. A drawing change can invalidate a previously cleared boundary or access arrangement. Communicate the affected system and current restriction promptly; a daily report issued after exposure is not a substitute for immediate coordination.

Classify punch items using the project's approved definitions. Do not downgrade a safety-critical item because the completion percentage looks good. Track each item to evidence of correction, verification and authorized closure. Where conditional handover is permitted, record precise restrictions, owner, due date and acknowledgment. This does not let an inspector authorize operation beyond their role.

**Worked case:** eight punch items include two defined by the fictional project as startup-blocking. Six minor items close; overall closure is 75%, but both startup blockers remain. **Changed case:** one blocker closes and one minor item reopens. Six of eight remain closed, still 75%, but the risk picture changed. Report item identity and consequence, not percentage alone. If a contractor marks all work complete while a protection override remains, retain the override as a separate controlled readiness issue.

## Q40 — Facility turnover and defensible final reporting

Turnover is a traceable account of what was built, checked, changed and left restricted. Assemble system-specific records: approved/as-built drawings, material and joining evidence, civil releases, machinery installation checks, test/reinstatement records, electrical/instrument evidence, NCR dispositions, preservation status and operating handover acknowledgments. The exact dossier depends on the actual project. A folder full of files is not a verified index; check that each file belongs to the system and final configuration.

Write conclusions at the level the evidence supports. “Civil foundation records complete” differs from “ready for energization.” Identify missing and superseded evidence, the responsible decision maker and any condition that prevents handover. Do not erase failed records after repairs; preserve the path from finding to disposition and verification. Assign each closure a date and accountable person so later reviewers can reconstruct the decision.

**Worked case:** F52 requires 20 named dossier items. Nineteen files exist, but one is the wrong revision and another belongs to a different system. Only17/20=85% are valid; three items remain unsupported. **Changed case:** corrected documents raise valid coverage to 19/20=95%, but the remaining item is the reinstatement register. Do not issue an unconditional operational release. Deliver a factual readiness statement and route the unresolved boundary to the authorized owner. This final exercise integrates the course's central distinction between completion claims and evidence.

# VUC052 retained pipeline-construction foundation

Original VUC042 Q01–24 retained with source references S01–07. VUC052 combined delivery and award rules govern.

## Q01 — Inspector role and objective evidence

The inspector observes specified work, compares it with the applicable approved basis, records facts and routes deviations through assigned authority. Observation, interpretation and acceptance are different acts. A contractor's statement may be useful evidence but is not the same as witnessing the activity. Identify the inspected location, time, method, result and limitation so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

Understand stop-work and escalation responsibilities before work begins. Do not direct a hazardous operation beyond your competence. Where a condition creates imminent risk, follow the established immediate protection/escalation process; do not continue exposure merely to obtain better photographs. Production pressure cannot justify inventing a pass.

**Worked case.** A foreman asks for a witnessed weld entry while the inspector was at another spread. Record the actual evidence source and required follow-up, not a false witness signature. **Changed case:** a valid NDT report later arrives; it may establish its own result, not retroactive personal observation.

## Q02 — Jurisdiction, specifications and change control

Identify pipeline service, location, regulatory jurisdiction, project approvals and governing specification editions. Gas and hazardous-liquid construction provisions are not interchangeable, and rules for one location or facility type may not apply elsewhere.[S02–S03] The owner establishes the controlled requirements; the inspector verifies against them and escalates conflicts.

Keep drawings and procedures at the correct revision. A field change may affect route, stress, environment, permits, coating or testing. Record who proposed, technically reviewed and authorized it, and what was actually installed. “The new drawing is coming” is not approval, and a redline alone does not prove an approved change.

**Worked case.** A liquid-line project copies a gas-line cover table without checking applicability. Obtain the correct basis rather than use whichever is less restrictive. **Changed case:** a project requirement is more conservative than the applicable minimum; do not silently waive the project requirement.

## Q03 — Preconstruction readiness and inspection plan

Before mobilization verify available permits, route/access limits, drawings, materials/procedures, qualified personnel, inspection resources and communication paths. The inspection plan should identify stages that will become hidden, hold/witness points and records required for release. An inspector cannot recover an unrecorded buried condition by signing a final checklist.

Coordinate interfaces among civil, welding, coating, NDT, environmental, survey and commissioning teams. Define how incomplete work is handed over between shifts and spreads. Verify actual readiness rather than treat a planned appointment or ordered instrument as available capacity.

**Worked case.** Lowering is scheduled before coating examination resources arrive. Hold the dependent stage under the plan and resolve resources. **Changed case:** an inspector arrives but lacks the required equipment/procedure; attendance alone does not satisfy readiness.

## Q04 — Route, survey, stationing and as-built control

Use the project's coordinate system, datum, station convention and units. A station identifies position along an alignment, not necessarily straight-line distance. Check benchmarks and survey references before comparing elevations or cover. Preserve bends, tie-ins, crossings, weld locations and exceptions in the as-built record.

A coordinate without datum or units can be dangerously ambiguous. Redlines should distinguish approved design changes from observations requiring review. Do not move a route stake or alter a survey value to make installed work appear compliant. Query discrepancies and keep the original evidence.

**Worked case.** In a synthetic metric station system,1+250 means1,250 m;1+430−1+250 is180 m of station difference. **Changed case:** a station equation or different alignment exists; simple subtraction may no longer give actual installed length without reconciliation.

## Q05 — Right-of-way and environmental controls

Construction authorization includes limits on access, clearing, soil handling, water management, crossings and restoration. The environmental plan must be available and understood by relevant parties; CER guidance explicitly addresses such coordination for its regulated projects.[S04] Do not treat a landowner's informal permission as a substitute for required project/environmental authorization.

Inspect boundaries and protective measures before disturbance. Keep topsoil handling, drainage protection, waste and spill controls traceable to the actual plan. Unexpected water, cultural material or sensitive habitat can require a defined stop/notification response. Record the condition without undertaking unauthorized sampling, removal or relocation.

**Worked case.** Equipment uses an unapproved shortcut through a wet area. Shorter travel does not establish permitted access. **Changed case:** the owner authorizes a route change but the environmental approval has not been checked; both interfaces must be resolved.

## Q06 — Existing facilities and damage prevention

Plans and surface marks are inputs to a controlled excavation/crossing process, not guarantees of exact buried location. Establish required notifications, current locates, permissions, owner information and safe exposure methods. CER's construction-near-pipelines guidance distinguishes written consent, locating and operator safety information.[S05] Apply the actual local system elsewhere.

When records, marks and exposed facilities disagree, stop the affected work through the established process and reconcile with responsible parties. Do not guess depth or assume an abandoned-looking pipe is harmless. Protect exposed services against loads and damage as required; crossing equipment can create hazards even without digging.

**Worked case.** A marked line is not found at the expected location. That does not prove the area clear. **Changed case:** another unmarked pipe is exposed; identify/control it before continuing affected work rather than assigning it the nearest drawing tag.

## Q07 — Pipe and component receipt

Verify identity, grade, dimensions, wall, manufacturing/heat records, coating and condition against approved procurement/design requirements. Preserve marking through cutting and installation. A certificate in a folder must link to the actual pipe or component; matching diameter does not establish grade or wall.

Inspect handling damage and report dents, gouges, coating damage, contamination or distorted ends for appropriate disposition. Do not grind away evidence or use an invented acceptance threshold. Separate quarantine status from available stock and record substituted components through actual approval.

**Worked case.** A cut piece has no transferred identity and a nearby pipe has a valid certificate. The certificate cannot simply be assigned to the cut piece. **Changed case:** chemistry matches a grade family, but required mechanical properties and traceability remain unresolved.

## Q08 — Transportation, storage and stringing

Supports, restraints and lifting arrangements should protect pipe geometry, coating and personnel. Observe the approved handling plan and component condition before/after movement. Keep workers clear of suspended loads and pinch zones; inspection does not require standing under a pipe. Stringing sequence should retain joint identity and prevent unintended stress or contamination.

PHMSA's construction issue guidance describes damage that can arise during handling and installation.[S06] Record actual location and extent, not just “minor handling mark.” A later coating repair cannot automatically resolve metal damage beneath it.

**Worked case.** A sling damages coating and leaves a visible gouge. Raise separate coating and base-metal findings. **Changed case:** the coating is restored; the gouge still needs its own technical disposition.

## Q09 — Excavation and safe observation

Trench hazards include collapse, water, atmosphere, falling loads, adjacent structures and equipment. A construction-inspection credential does not itself appoint the learner as the excavation competent person. Establish that the responsible competent person and applicable protective/access systems are in place. Observe from safe positions; do not enter an unsafe excavation to measure it.

As a US federal example, OSHA1926.651 requires safe egress for trench excavations4 ft or deeper with no more than25 ft lateral travel; protective-system requirements must also be assessed under1926.652.[S07] Shallower depth is not a universal safe-entry exemption. Reinspection after relevant changes is part of the excavation-control system.

**Worked case.** Rain introduces water and sloughing after the morning inspection. Request removal/protection and competent reassessment under the applicable process. **Changed case:** a ladder is added; egress does not by itself resolve collapse or water hazards.

## Q10 — Bending and fit-up geometry

Bends must preserve acceptable pipe shape, wall and coating under the approved procedure/design. Examine identity, bend location, geometry and observations such as wrinkles or flattening using the actual acceptance basis. Do not improvise allowable ovality or bend radius. Cold bending, manufactured bends and other configurations have different qualification/design considerations.

Fit-up must address alignment, bevel, gap, wall transitions and cleanliness before joining. Excessive force to fit an alignment can introduce stress; it is not automatically acceptable because the weld can bridge the gap. Document the condition before it is concealed by joining.

**Worked case.** A synthetic ovality measure uses(Dmax−Dmin)/Dnom×100. Values510,500 and508 mm give1.969%. That is only the stated geometric measure, not a code pass. **Changed case:** a wrinkle exists despite the number meeting a supplied limit; the wrinkle remains a separate finding.

## Q11 — Welding procedure and personnel evidence

Verify applicable qualified procedure, personnel scope and actual production parameters through the project welding system. Procedure qualification, welder qualification and production acceptance address different questions. Changes in process, material, thickness or other applicable variables require the actual governing review; an inspector should not invent qualification ranges.

Keep weld identity linked to pipe ends, personnel, date and procedure. Check preparation and environmental/thermal conditions at the required stage. PHMSA construction findings include problems at fit-up and thickness transitions; these must not be hidden by a completed bead.[S06]

**Worked case.** A qualified welder uses an unsupported procedure for the actual wall transition. Welder credentials do not qualify the procedure. **Changed case:** procedure support is corrected, but completed welds made under the unsupported conditions still require disposition.

## Q12 — NDT, repairs and weld maps

Examination personnel, procedures, method, extent and acceptance must match the actual project requirements. Link each result to the correct weld, repair cycle and final disposition. A report marked acceptable for weld17 cannot clear weld71 because their numbers resemble one another. No learner qualification here replaces required NDT certification or interpretation authority.

Repairs require authorized method and follow-up examination. Keep the original indication/result and each repair/retest record so the final status can be reconstructed. Do not erase a failure once a later report passes, or count a scheduled retest as completed.

**Worked case.** Twenty welds are listed; eighteen have valid final reports, one failed and one lacks a report. Evidence-backed final coverage is90%, not100%. **Changed case:** the failed weld is repaired but retest pending; it remains unresolved for the required final examination.

## Q13 — Coating preparation and application

Coating quality depends on actual surface condition, preparation, environment, materials, application and cure under the approved system. Check instrument suitability and record the measured location/time. Product shelf life and batch identity matter; so do contamination and conditions that differ from the procedure.

A thicker coating is not automatically better, and a favorable average can hide local low/high readings where individual limits apply. Dry-film thickness, cure and adhesion/discontinuity checks are distinct evidence. Do not mix different coating systems or repair methods without approval.

## What completion establishes

Materials completion establishes an original instructional package with exercises, controlled assessments, instructor requirements and a declared available-source basis. It does not establish comprehensive technical-code coverage, external accreditation, a student's demonstrated competence, actual project acceptance or production publication. Actual learner award requires the documented practical and examination gates. Employer-specific requirements, current approved engineering documents and specialist qualifications remain necessary for real work. Review this package annually and when relevant guidance or identified defects change; record affected outcomes and revised version.
