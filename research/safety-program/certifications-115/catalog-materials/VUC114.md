# VUC114 — Merchant Mariner Credential Preparation and Entry-Level Shipboard Readiness

Original VNDRLY/U instruction. USCG issuance, approved STCW training and advanced endorsement qualifications remain separate.

## Q114-01 — Credential architecture and selecting a lawful role

An MMC is a Coast Guard credential carrying specific endorsements, not a universal license to perform every shipboard job. National endorsements and STCW endorsements serve different regulatory purposes. A medical certificate and a TWIC are separate documents. A valid card in one category does not cure a missing endorsement, expired medical authority or employer familiarization. Begin with the actual vessel, route, tonnage, propulsion, position and duties; then choose the current NMC checklist for that exact capacity.

Entry-level roles include Ordinary Seafarer, Wiper and Steward's Department, with Food Handler identified separately. Older records may use Ordinary Seaman; preserve the document's wording and check the current terminology. Qualified ratings, tank-vessel responsibilities and officer positions require their own evidence and evaluations. An entry-level endorsement does not authorize independent navigation, engineering watch responsibility or cargo-transfer person-in-charge duties.

Worked example: a new hire holds Wiper but the dispatch sheet says independent engine-room watch. The correct response is to hold that assignment and have the operator verify required qualified rating/STCW and safe manning. Do not relabel the job as cleaning while expecting watch duties. This course teaches credential preparation and common entry-level shipboard readiness; it does not award an MMC or substitute for every advanced endorsement curriculum. Build an individual progression plan naming the exact next endorsement rather than promising a generic master-mariner qualification.

## Q114-02 — Application package, identity and truthful declarations

Use the current NMC application route and forms. CG-719B identifies the requested transaction and endorsements. Supporting forms and evidence depend on the checklist: conviction disclosure where applicable, sea service where required, drug-testing evidence, identity/citizenship and TWIC or an applicable documented exemption. Do not add fictional sea time to an entry-level application that does not require it. Sign only truthful statements and check names, dates and requested capacities across documents.

The current NMC page directs most MMC submissions through its ASAP portal, with special handling for First Class Pilot and Local Limited applicants. Medical submissions are separate; the MMC portal warns that medical applications sent there will not be processed. Older PDFs still describe REC-email workflows. Follow the current official route for the actual transaction rather than a stale checklist's mailing paragraph. Retain the submission receipt, complete package and request-for-information history securely.

Worked review: CG-719B requests Wiper, the cover sheet requests Master, the fee receipt is for another person and one page is unsigned. Stop submission, resolve the intended endorsement and identity mismatch, obtain the applicant's actual signature and reconcile the fee. An administrator must not sign the applicant's declarations or guess an answer. A complete packet is ready for evaluation, not automatically approved.

## Q114-03 — Medical fitness, drug evidence and privacy

Medical fitness is evaluated through the Coast Guard process, not by the course instructor. Use the appropriate current CG-719K or entry-level CG-719K/E route and qualified medical practitioner. Check the issued certificate's applicable dates and restrictions. National and STCW validity can differ; maximum periods are not a personal guarantee. An applicant under 18 may have a shorter STCW medical period. An otherwise unexpired MMC does not make expired medical authority valid for the intended service.

For transactions requiring drug evidence, use a currently accepted NMC option. An approved test, qualifying random-program letter or qualifying pre-employment letter has specific timing, laboratory/MRO and content conditions. A generic employer drug screen is not necessarily acceptable. The current test pathway uses a 185-day application window and DOT-compliant evidence. Do not change collection dates, omit a refusal or treat a negative-dilute result as a routine accepted negative.

Worked example: medical certificate shows national validity through a later date but STCW validity already expired; the assignment is on a vessel to which STCW applies. Refer the person for correct renewal/evaluation and hold affected service. Store health and drug records in restricted channels, not the general crew chat. The supervisor normally needs fitness/restriction status, not unnecessary clinical details. If health changes affect safe duty, report through the applicable process rather than relying only on the printed expiry.

## Q114-04 — Sea-service evidence and arithmetic

Sea service records describe actual service in an assigned vessel position. Passenger days and guessed dates are not interchangeable with qualifying service. Record vessel identity, tonnage, propulsion/power when relevant, waters/routes, capacity, dates, hours and authorized attestation. Use CG-719S where appropriate or an acceptable letter containing required information. Keep originals/copies because future endorsements may use the same history while imposing their own recency and type-of-service requirements.

NMC guidance describes the usual day as eight hours of watchstanding/day work, excluding overtime. The under-100-GRT provision can recognize an appropriate day of at least four hours under its conditions; it is not a universal four-hour rule. Time-and-a-half credit depends on an authorized and practiced two-watch arrangement and supporting evidence, not simply working twelve hours. The Coast Guard makes the credit determination. Do not double count simultaneous dates or turn transit as a passenger into deck service.

Synthetic review: 24 documented eight-hour duty days plus 6 passenger days gives 24 candidate service days, not 30. If 20 separate days are documented under an actually qualifying 1.5-credit arrangement, the arithmetic is 30 credited days, subject to evaluation. Ten ordinary overtime days remain ten under the base assumption. A spreadsheet must retain raw days and the claimed rule separately so an evaluator can audit the result. Missing signatures or vessel details are unresolved evidence, not zero-risk clerical issues.

## Q114-05 — STCW, approved training and competence boundaries

Determine whether STCW applies to the intended service and which endorsement/competence standards are required. Basic Training includes personal survival, fire prevention/firefighting, elementary first aid, and personal safety/social responsibilities. Security awareness, designated security duties, survival craft, watch ratings and advanced functions are distinct requirements where applicable. Attendance at a general online safety course does not establish completion of approved practical components.

Check the exact provider/course approval and what it satisfies, the certificate identity/date, assessment evidence and applicable continued-competence route. Renewal or revalidation requirements are not identical to original qualification. Maintain a gap matrix: requirement, accepted evidence, issuing authority, currency, missing action and owner. This course's mock exercises prepare the learner; they are not Coast Guard-approved STCW assessments and must never be signed as such.

Worked case: a worker has a VNDRLY orientation certificate and an expired training record but is scheduled as a designated security-duty crew member. Do not mark the gap closed because both documents mention security. Verify the exact endorsement/training requirement and arrange approved training/evidence. Include respectful workplace and prevention/reporting of violence, bullying and harassment in readiness. Verify current flag-state implementation for formal PSSR requirements rather than assuming another country's transition policy applies to a US credential.

## Q114-06 — Vessel familiarization, alarms and personal survival

Before duty, locate assigned muster station, alarm meanings, escape routes, lifejacket/immersion equipment, emergency communications and immediate supervisor. Read the vessel's station bill and emergency instructions; alarm signals and duties must be learned on the actual vessel. Find alternate routes because fire, flooding or cargo can block the usual path. Report missing or damaged lifesaving equipment immediately.

Practice inspecting and donning an appropriate lifejacket on dry land using its instructions: check condition, correct size, straps/closures, attachments and fit, then explain safe use. Do not jump into water, deploy survival craft or activate distress devices in an uncontrolled class. Real survival/boat-launch drills need qualified instructors, approved arrangements and safety controls. In a person-overboard event, raise the alarm, point continuously and follow the vessel's recovery plan; do not create another casualty by jumping in unassigned.

Worked case: the primary muster route is smoke-filled. Do not enter it to preserve attendance. Use the designated safe alternate, report your location and account for others using verified information. A missing-person report is not permission for untrained enclosed-space or smoke rescue. Prepare for cold stress and exposure using the vessel's equipment/instructions; never treat a classroom certificate as proof of water-survival performance.

## Q114-07 — Deck seamanship and stored-energy hazards

A line can move suddenly under tension, and the whole working area can be dangerous when its path changes or it parts. Keep out of bights, pinch points and potential recoil paths; painted zones alone do not define every hazard. Never straddle a loaded line, wrap it around a hand or place a foot in a coil. Maintain clear communication with the person directing the operation and know the stop signal. Use task-appropriate PPE and footing, and keep escape paths clear.

For a training rope with no load, inspect abrasion, cuts, contamination and fittings, coil safely, and practice a bowline and clove hitch under instructor observation. A bowline forms a fixed loop when properly tied/dressed for the intended benign practice; it is not authorization for personnel lifting or every load. A clove hitch can slip or need additional securing depending on use. Actual mooring/rigging follows approved equipment limits and vessel procedures. Do not improvise knots to substitute for certified lifting attachments.

Worked scene: a crew member steps inside a slack bight as the winch operator prepares to heave. Call stop, prevent tensioning and clear personnel before a new brief. Slack now does not mean safe when load is applied. Mooring plans, weather, vessel movement and coordinated commands determine the safe sequence. Do not demonstrate powered winch recovery in an unguarded classroom.

## Q114-08 — Lookout, communications and watch handover

A lookout observes and listens continuously, reports promptly and avoids distractions. State relative direction/bearing, object type, approximate range/trend and uncertainty. A steady bearing with decreasing range indicates collision risk requiring immediate report; it is not an instruction for an entry-level lookout to independently maneuver. The responsible officer applies the navigation rules and vessel procedures using all available information. Maintain lookout even when electronic equipment is operating.

Use closed-loop communications: receive the instruction, repeat it accurately, carry out only authorized actions and report completion or inability. Ask when uncertain. Distinguish routine calls, urgent safety information and distress; do not transmit false distress or practice on live emergency channels. In simulation, identify the vessel/station, nature and location of the problem, people at risk and assistance needed. Use the vessel's actual emergency radio procedure during operations.

Handover includes position/duty, hazards, equipment defects, traffic, work permits, communications and pending actions. Do not leave a watch until properly relieved under the vessel's procedure, and do not accept a duty beyond your endorsement/training. Worked exercise: target remains 030 degrees relative while range falls from 2.0 to 1.5 nautical miles. Report constant bearing/decreasing range promptly; do not assume radar or the officer has already noticed. Fatigue or impairment in a relief must be raised before the handover is treated as complete.

## Q114-09 — Engine-space work, isolation and fire prevention

Engine spaces combine heat, noise, moving machinery, pressure, electricity, fuels and poor escape conditions. Entry-level assistance does not authorize opening energized equipment or operating unfamiliar valves. Identify the responsible engineer, boundaries, communications and emergency exits. Report leaks, abnormal smells, vibration, temperature and alarms promptly without touching hazardous equipment to investigate.

Before maintenance, the vessel's isolation/permit procedure must control electrical, mechanical, pressure, thermal and stored energy. A stopped motor can restart; a closed valve can leak; a gauge at zero may not prove every space safe. Only trained authorized personnel isolate and verify. The entry-level worker must recognize a missing isolation and stop, not perform a test beyond competence. Enclosed spaces require the applicable entry/rescue arrangements; never enter to retrieve a coworker without authorization and safeguards.

Worked case: an oil mist leak is near a hot surface and a supervisor asks the Wiper to tighten a fitting while machinery runs. Withdraw to safety, alert the engineer and stop the unsafe task. Do not turn a small leak into an injection/burn/fire casualty. Housekeeping reduces ignition and slip hazards, but cleaning must not bypass guards or mix incompatible chemicals. For fire, raise alarm and follow assigned duties; extinguisher use is limited to trained, safe conditions with an escape route, not a duty to fight an escalating fire.

## Q114-10 — Pollution prevention, cargo boundaries and security

Keep oils, chemicals and waste out of the water through the vessel's approved containment, segregation and disposal procedures. Identify drains and spill pathways before work. A small leak can spread quickly; report promptly and use only authorized safe containment actions. Do not wash a spill overboard, falsify an oil record or bypass pollution-control equipment. Cargo transfer requires competent authorized personnel, communications, checks and emergency shutdown arrangements; entry-level familiarity is not a tank-vessel PIC endorsement.

Security duties depend on the vessel's plan and assigned role. Verify access through established channels, protect keys/credentials and report suspicious activity without unsafe confrontation. Do not disclose restricted security details in public training records. A badge is not proof of authorization for every space. Cyber precautions include protecting accounts, checking unexpected attachments and reporting compromised devices; never connect unknown media to control systems.

Worked scenario: an unfamiliar contractor asks an entry-level worker to open a restricted machinery space and later to sign the transfer checklist. Verify the request with the responsible officer and decline an unsupported signature. If a spill is seen, alert the designated response chain with location/material if known, exposure and safe containment status. Do not guess a substance from color or enter vapor to identify it.

## Q114-11 — Fatigue, respectful work and incident records

Shipboard work depends on reliable rest, clear roles and a crew able to report danger. Record actual work/rest and follow the applicable vessel/flag requirements; never rewrite hours to make a schedule appear compliant. A person can be fatigued despite a compliant-looking record. Raise fitness concerns early and arrange safe relief. Medication, illness, alcohol or other impairment must be handled through the appropriate medical/supervisory process.

Violence, harassment, bullying and sexual misconduct are safety and welfare issues. Seek immediate safety, support the affected person, use the designated reporting routes and preserve relevant facts without gossip, retaliation or promises of absolute confidentiality. Do not require the victim to confront the alleged offender. Instructors must offer a private alternative to sensitive role-play and avoid recreating abuse. Formal reporting/legal duties are governed by current policy and law; this course does not replace them.

Incident notes should distinguish observation, reported information and inference: time, location, people, event, immediate action and unresolved questions. Worked example: you hear a bang and then see a person on deck. Record that sequence, not an invented fall from a ladder. Protect records against alteration and share only with authorized recipients. A near miss deserves learning and corrective action even when no injury occurred.

## Q114-12 — Renewal, progression and joining-vessel release

Maintain a personal credential matrix listing each endorsement, medical scope/date, TWIC status, training/competence currency, limitations and employer familiarization. Review upcoming expirations before accepting a voyage, not at the gangway. Renewal, raise of grade, increase of scope, replacement and continuity are different transactions. A document of continuity is not authority to sail; do not treat a pending application as an issued credential.

Progression begins with the desired job and current checklist, then verified service, training, assessments and examinations where required. Keep raw evidence and identify gaps honestly. Do not purchase a course solely because its title resembles an endorsement; verify current approval and exact credit. Fees and processing routes change, so use official current information and keep receipts. The Coast Guard evaluates the application and may request more evidence or deny it; course completion cannot guarantee approval.

Worked joining review: the MMC is current, national medical date is current, STCW medical date is expired and the voyage requires STCW. Hold affected joining duties until valid authority is restored; do not erase the earlier date. On release, confirm actual role, vessel familiarization and emergency assignments. VNDRLY's award documents training and assessed readiness only. It must list the scope and observed methods and must never use a Coast Guard seal or claim to be an MMC.

# VUC114 practical workbook and delivery guide

## Audience, outcomes and schedule
For prospective entry-level mariners and coordinators preparing an MMC packet. This complete original preparation course teaches credential selection, document integrity and common shipboard readiness. It is not every officer/rating specialty, an approved STCW course or an MMC issuer. Prerequisites: basic literacy/numeracy, ability to participate safely with accommodations; actual eligibility/medical decisions remain with the competent authority. Outcomes: produce a truthful gap-checked packet, interpret role limits, evaluate service records, demonstrate safe dry-land routines and report emergencies accurately.
Forty-eight planned hours: 12 teaching, 8 document workshops, 10 dry-land stations, 8 scenarios, 6 assessment, 4 coached remediation. Original course hours, not statutory sea service or approved-course credit. Instructor needs relevant maritime operational/credential competence; use qualified safety instructors for equipment demonstrations. No live vessel navigation, loaded mooring, water entry, fire, enclosed-space entry or energized machinery is authorized by classroom exercises.

Coverage: Q01–12 each has two explained checks, a final case, changed retake case and matching station. W1 covers Q01/02, W2 Q03, W3 Q04, W4 Q06, W5 Q07, W6 Q08/11, W7 Q09/10, W8 Q05/12 and integrated gap review. Two integrated cases test documentary and operational judgment.

Boundary: MMC is a container for many national/STCW endorsements, not a single universal syllabus. This package fully supplies original credential-preparation and common entry-level readiness materials. It does not claim to teach all officer engineering/navigation, tankerman, survival-craft or other advanced endorsement curricula, nor supply approved STCW practical signoffs. Those are identified explicitly as role-dependent external requirements. No inaccessible proprietary text or official exam bank was copied. Synthetic service arithmetic does not determine actual Coast Guard credit.
