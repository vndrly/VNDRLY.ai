# VNDRLY/U OIM — Controlling Emergencies

VUC104 • Original employer-readiness and simulated-assessment package •18November 2026

Audience: employer-nominated OIMs, deputies or prospective appointees supported as ready for formal emergency assessment. Requires documented readiness, essential knowledge, asset-type knowledge and safe participation. Not OPITO approval, statutory appointment or independent specialist operating authority. Full proprietary criteria unavailable; public scope and regulator guidance form the source basis.

Planned preparation/assessment allocation 48 hours: 16 instruction/refresher, 8 labs, 4 asset/readiness/interface review, 16 individual scenarios/debrief, 2 written exam, 2 development planning. Adjust time for valid evidence; no time-served award. Objectives: maintain readiness, activate command, assess uncertainty, delegate safely, communicate, account for people, preserve evacuation options, manage team/stress and hand over/recover within authority.

## Q104-01 — Employer readiness and asset-specific command

An OIM emergency assessment sits within the employer's selection, training, experience and workplace appraisal process. It cannot establish readiness from a short simulation alone. Before formal assessment, the employer must provide a supported declaration covering readiness, essential knowledge and the relevant asset type. The assessor reviews the evidence and identifies gaps; a signed statement without supporting records is not a substitute for demonstrated preparation.

Understand the installation's major hazards, safety case or equivalent basis, operating configuration, emergency plan, command hierarchy and external interfaces. Fixed and mobile installations have different marine, structural and stability considerations. The OIM coordinates with competent marine, drilling, aviation and technical authorities rather than inventing their specialist operating actions. Identify exactly which simulated asset and role the assessment covers.

Worked case: a candidate experienced on a fixed platform is assessed on a mobile installation without evidence of the relevant asset knowledge. Do not infer competence from job title. Complete the employer-supported preparation and appropriate assessment model. The original VNDRLY certificate must describe the assessed scope and must not claim an external OIM qualification or employer appointment.

## Q104-02 — Maintaining readiness and managing degraded protection

Readiness includes current people, capabilities, protection systems, routes, refuge, evacuation means, communications and concurrent work. Review impairments and their compensating controls with responsible specialists. Confirm that the actual shift can deliver the emergency plan. An unavailable team, isolated firewater component or failed communication channel changes feasible options and may require restrictions before an incident occurs.

Maintain a clear record of configuration and outstanding decisions. Review changing weather, marine activity, maintenance and contractor exposure. Make sure deputies and team members know their roles and succession arrangements. Preparation should reveal conflicts between planned tasks and emergency responsibilities. Do not count the same person or resource twice when simultaneous demands could occur.

Worked case: two evacuation means are shown on the plan but one is under maintenance. The OIM must know the actual available capacity and authorized compensating arrangements. Restating the nominal total does not satisfy readiness. If the planned protection cannot be maintained, escalate and restrict affected operations through the established authority rather than normalizing the impairment.

## Q104-03 — Recognition, command activation and priorities

On receiving a credible emergency report, establish location, people at risk, hazard, source and time. Activate the emergency organization and protective measures according to the plan without waiting for perfect diagnosis. Announce command and use the designated succession process if required. Set immediate objectives that protect life, control or limit escalation and preserve viable evacuation/escape options.

Distinguish strategic objectives from specialist tactics. The OIM must understand the consequences of a proposed action and authorize it within the plan, but should not replace a competent response leader's technical method with an improvised instruction. Identify the next decision point and what evidence will trigger a change. Production and asset protection never justify concealing uncertainty about people or continuing an untenable tactic.

Worked case: a pipeline release report is credible but the failed component is unknown. Initiate the applicable emergency response, obtain control-system and field evidence, protect exposed people and request specialist support. A demand for the exact flange number before activation delays the response without making it safer.

## Q104-04 — Assessing uncertainty and choosing effective action

Maintain a decision picture that distinguishes confirmed facts, reports, assumptions and unknowns. Evaluate evidence quality and time, including stale displays and unconfirmed verbal reports. Ask what could happen next, how quickly options might close, and which controls are verified effective. A protective command sent is not proof of its result. Reassess when feedback is missing or a condition changes.

Compare feasible options using consequences, capability, time, access and uncertainty. State the reason for the selected action and assign a responsible owner. Establish feedback and review triggers. Avoid repeating a failed strategy because effort has already been invested in it. Invite challenge from specialists and team members with contrary evidence; the command role includes resolving the evidence rather than suppressing disagreement.

Worked case: the CRO reports isolation commanded with continuing flow. Treat source control as unconfirmed and consider continued release in the strategy. A plan assuming the leak has stopped is not supported. Require the prescribed failed-action response and reassess the threat to refuge and evacuation routes.

## Q104-05 — Delegating authority and coordinating specialists

Delegate a clear task, authority boundary, constraints, required report and review time to a named role. Confirm understanding. Retain oversight of interactions: a ventilation action, vessel movement or response-team deployment may affect another part of the emergency plan. Ask specialists to identify prerequisites, consequences and failure indicators before relying on an option.

Marine masters, aviation crews, well-control personnel, response leaders and medical staff retain their specialist responsibilities. The OIM coordinates the overall incident and interfaces through the plan. Do not issue an unsafe instruction because a resource appears under organizational control. If advice conflicts, clarify the evidence and authority, record the decision and obtain the required higher or specialist support.

Worked case: a vessel offers assistance but weather and approach suitability are not confirmed. Request the master's capability and constraints; do not order an approach from a control-room map alone. A resource offer is provisional until the responsible operator confirms its safe availability and the command plan integrates it.

## Q104-06 — Communications under escalation

Establish reliable internal and external communication with named roles and a shared update rhythm. Critical instructions need exact readback and acknowledgment. Keep urgent operational messages separate from routine requests and media inquiries. Use the authorized external communicator for shore support and relevant agencies, supplying identity, location, incident, people status, hazards, needs and contact arrangements.

Monitor communication failures as operational impairments. Test the approved fallback and re-establish the current instruction, not an obsolete one. A sent alert or a resource request is not confirmation of receipt or dispatch. Correct mistaken reports openly and promptly. Ensure people at muster receive appropriate updates through the designated roles so silence does not create rumor or unsafe self-directed movement.

Worked case: shore repeats forty accounted when the OIM reported forty on board and thirty-nine confirmed. Correct the distinction immediately and verify the correction. If the channel fails, retain the unresolved acknowledgment and use the approved alternative. The incorrect all-accounted statement must not remain the assumed common picture.

## Q104-07 — People, responders and medical coordination

Require identity-based accountability through muster, response duties, medical treatment and transfers. Counts alone can conceal duplicates or missing people. Ask the coordinator to state confirmed identities, locations, unresolved people and source times. Provide missing-person evidence to the response decision process without automatically ordering hazardous entry. Track response teams and their withdrawal arrangements as part of the people picture.

Medical personnel direct clinical care; command arranges resources, access and external support. Verify transport and receiving arrangements before calling a medical transfer complete. Consider assistance needs and welfare during prolonged refuge or evacuation. A planned helper, seat or medical destination must be confirmed as available. Protect sensitive information while providing the facts needed for the response.

Worked case: a medic verifies a previously missing person, but a response team has not reported back. The first discrepancy may close; the installation is not automatically fully accounted for. The OIM must retain and act on the outstanding team-status uncertainty rather than allow one reassuring report to erase another problem.

## Q104-08 — Refuge, evacuation and loss of control

Recognize when controlling the source is no longer a credible basis for keeping people on the installation. Use the emergency plan's escalation and evacuation criteria, current refuge condition, route availability, capacity and time. Endurance estimates depend on the incident and functioning protection; they are not guaranteed countdowns. Decisions must allow the required communication, preparation and movement with the plan's uncertainty allowances.

Confirm actual available capacity and operator suitability. Do not overload or assume an unconfirmed external rescue asset will arrive in time. Distinguish evacuation by planned means from escape arrangements when planned options fail; use the installation's procedures and competent roles rather than inventing techniques. Continue accounting for people through departure and receipt. Preserve alternative options early instead of waiting until the preferred one is lost.

Worked case: forty-eight people need evacuation and one of two thirty-place means is unavailable. Available capacity is thirty, leaving eighteen without that option. Report and address the shortfall immediately through confirmed alternatives. A nominal sixty-place design does not solve an actual thirty-place emergency capability.

## Q104-09 — Asset-specific escalation and degraded command locations

An incident can affect structural integrity, stability, well control, pipelines, marine collision exposure or aviation operations. The OIM must recognize which specialist information and authority are needed and how those hazards threaten the overall emergency plan. This course does not teach ballast sequences, well-kill calculations, aviation control or structural acceptance limits. Use approved asset procedures and competent specialists; do not extrapolate from an unrelated installation.

Assess whether the command location remains habitable and functional. An alternate room may have different communications, information or control capability. Arrange transfer according to the plan with explicit task acceptance and current records. If life is threatened, protective movement takes precedence over perfect paperwork. Report what can no longer be controlled or verified and adapt the strategy accordingly.

Worked case: a mobile installation reports abnormal inclination while the primary control room loses displays. Obtain competent marine assessment and use the asset's emergency arrangements. Do not improvise ballast operations. State the loss of reliable control information and confirm what the alternate location can actually support.

## Q104-10 — Stress, challenge and sustained team performance

Emergency command creates high workload and pressure to appear certain. Watch for fixation, rushed speech, repeated unanswered requests, missed reports or inability to delegate. Use short structured reviews to re-establish the shared picture and priorities. Allocate support and relief before fatigue undermines decisions. A team member should be able to challenge an unsafe assumption with evidence regardless of rank.

Manage your own response to bad news. A failed action is information, not disloyalty. Acknowledge corrections, revise the plan and make the new instruction clear. Avoid humiliation or blame during the response. Record what matters, delegate routine tasks and retain attention on the next time-critical decision. After the event, support welfare and a learning-focused debrief.

Worked case: a junior communicator identifies that a rescue ETA was never confirmed. The OIM should verify the claim, correct the planning assumption and reassess local options. Dismissing the challenge because the plan has already been announced converts an uncertain estimate into a dangerous false guarantee.

## Q104-11 — Stabilization, command handover and recovery

Define what stabilized means in the current incident and identify conditions that could reverse it. Maintain restrictions while protection, plant integrity, people status or evidence remain unresolved. Command handover requires current objectives, verified and failed actions, team/people status, external commitments, time-sensitive decisions, impairments and recovery constraints. The incoming commander accepts and critical roles are informed.

Recovery and restart require authorized technical checks, permit/isolation disposition and management release. Do not restore operation merely because alarms clear or external attention has faded. Preserve records and evidence with traceable corrections. Debrief decisions in the context of the information available at the time; identify training, interface and system changes with owners and verification evidence.

Worked case: isolation is now confirmed but a refuge damper remains faulted. The immediate source may be controlled, yet readiness for continued occupation or restart is not automatically restored. Include the fault and required decision in the handover and recovery plan.

## Q104-12 — Independent assessment and continuing employer judgment

Formal assessment requires independent performance across different emergencies, supported by prior employer readiness evidence. The assessor observes command, decision quality, delegation, communication, team performance and stress management through changing conditions. Familiarization teaches the simulator interface, not the hidden solution to the final scenario. Coaching during a run must be recorded and cannot be presented as independent evidence.

The original package requires three satisfactory emergency scenarios within a maximum of four assessed attempts. Critical unsafe decisions cannot be averaged away by strong performance elsewhere. Remediate the cause and use a changed scenario for any permitted reassessment. When attempts are exhausted, return to a documented development plan before a new assessment cycle. The employer uses the result alongside asset knowledge, workplace appraisal and experience; the simulation certificate does not replace that judgment.

Worked case: the learner manages a controllable incident and an escalation well but delays evacuation after the scenario's loss-of-control criteria are met. The third scenario is not passed because the first two were strong. Preserve the evidence, remediate the decision failure and assess a changed loss-of-control scenario within the permitted process.

## Scoring, attempts and credential boundary

Each of 12 dimensions 0–4: 0 unsafe/absent, 1 major assistance, 2 safe but incomplete/prompted, 3 independent accurate traceable, 4 independent anticipatory with clear reasoning. Require 39/48, every dimension at least 3, no critical error, and three satisfactory independent scenarios. Each scenario must pass its relevant command criteria; readiness review applies to the entire assessment. Maximum 4 assessed attempts for 3 successful scenarios; after exhaustion require further development and a new justified plan. Do not relabel a failed assessed run as practice after seeing the result.

Critical errors include unsupported hazardous deployment, deliberate overload, false all-accounted or received declaration, unauthorized bypass/restart, concealed loss of protection, unjustified delay after explicit evacuation trigger, or fabricated readiness/evidence. Freeze unsafe simulated action, retain trace and remediate. Independent competent review handles disputed evidence against unchanged criteria.

Complete 24 explained checks and 6 labs. Controlled final: 12 cases/36 decisions, 72 points, pass 58, at least 4/6 each, no critical error, 120 minutes. Changed final and keys remain instructor-only. What to do with overload signs?

**Answer:** Reprioritize, delegate support and arrange relief with monitoring intact.

**Reason/remediation:** Unmanaged workload degrades decisions. Revisit Q104-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q104-11-L1

Source controlled but refuge protection faulted: fully recovered?

**Answer:** No. Retain impairment and authorized recovery decision.

**Reason/remediation:** One successful action is not complete readiness. Revisit Q104-11 and perform its changed application.

## Q104-11-L2

What completes command transfer?

**Answer:** Explicit accepted handover and notification of affected roles.

**Reason/remediation:** Leaving the room is not transfer. Revisit Q104-11 and perform its changed application.

## Q104-12-L1

Two strong scenarios cancel critical third failure?

**Answer:** No. Each required scenario must pass independently.

**Reason/remediation:** Critical decisions cannot be averaged away. Revisit Q104-12 and perform its changed application.

## Q104-12-L2

Four attempts exhausted with only two passes: award?

**Answer:** No. Further development and a new documented assessment plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Attempt limits cannot be bypassed by relabeling failures. Revisit Q104-12 and perform its changed application.
