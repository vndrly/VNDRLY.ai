# VNDRLY/U Corrosion Technician Preparation

VUC050 · Original learning materials · 2026-11-18

## Purpose and route

Prepare a technician to collect trustworthy corrosion evidence, perform bounded calculations, recognize damage and support an approved corrosion-control plan under competent direction. The program includes instruction, supplied investigations, instrument tasks, explained checks, a separate controlled final and changed reassessment. Its award is VNDRLY's training-and-assessment record, not AMPP certification, employment experience, pipeline operator qualification or engineering authority. Current AMPP requirements and the dated public scope guide are identified in the source register.[S01, S02]

Learners need basic arithmetic, unit conversion and safe laboratory practice. Instructors provide a numeracy diagnostic and remedial teaching before assessment; accessibility arrangements may change presentation, not supply answers. No learner may open pressurized coupon fittings, enter confined spaces, adjust live rectifiers, handle unknown process samples or conduct hazardous chemical cleaning through this course. Practical work uses safe identified specimens and current instrument instructions. Missing physical demonstrations remain incomplete.

## Q01 — Technician responsibility and the evidence chain

A reading becomes useful only when its asset, location, time, method and conditions are known. Start a job by identifying the approved scope and supervisor, the decision the evidence will support, and the conditions requiring stop/escalation. Confirm access, permits and instrumentation before mobilizing. A routine measurement assignment does not authorize changing chemical dosage, opening process equipment or declaring pressure integrity.

Separate observation, calculation and interpretation in a report. “Pit depth measured 1.2 mm at grid B4” is an observation; “20% of an assumed 6 mm wall” is a calculation with an assumption; “fit for service” is a separate engineering decision requiring more information. Keep those distinctions even when the same person requests all three.

**Worked:** a supervisor asks for a coupon mass-loss report, but its exposure dates are missing. Report the measured masses and missing dates; do not invent a rate . **Changed:** dates arrive from a different coupon. Identity still fails. Correct evidence must match the specimen and exposure.

## Q02 — Reactions and the corrosion cell

In aqueous metal corrosion, oxidation releases electrons at anodic sites; reduction consumes them at cathodic sites. A conducting metallic connection and an ionic path through electrolyte complete the cell. For iron dissolving as Fe²⁺, Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻. One possible cathodic reaction in aerated neutral water is O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻. Conditions determine the reactions; do not assume oxygen is necessary for every corrosion process.[S03]

Anode and cathode can be different microscopic regions of one component. A visibly single-metal pipe does not eliminate electrochemical corrosion. Likewise, dry appearance at the inspection time does not prove the surface was dry throughout its exposure. Deposits can retain liquid after exposed surfaces dry.

Draw the paths before proposing control. A barrier may interrupt electrolyte contact; electrical isolation can interrupt a dissimilar-metal circuit; chemical treatment may affect reaction rates. Each approach must be compatible with the complete system and verified after installation.

**Worked:** carbon steel and copper are connected in wet service. Identify both electronic and ionic paths before discussing galvanic effects. **Changed:** an insulating joint is installed but a bonding wire bypasses it; the intended isolation is not established merely by seeing the joint.

## Q03 — Electricity, current density and units

Use V = IR only with consistent units and the applicable circuit model. Voltage is potential difference, current is charge flow and resistance relates them in an ohmic element. One volt across 100 ohms gives 0.01 A, or 10 mA. Current density divides current by the relevant area; confusing square metres with square centimetres changes the result by ten thousand.

A corrosion circuit also contains electrochemical interfaces and polarization; it is not generally a fixed resistor. Use simple circuits for instrument familiarization and bounded calculations, not an unvalidated field corrosion-rate model. A current meter connected directly across a source can short it. Confirm meter jacks, range, fuse condition and the instrument procedure before energizing a current-limited training circuit.

**Worked:** 20 mA distributed over 2 m² is 10 mA/m². If it concentrates at 0.01 m², density becomes 2,000 mA/m². **Changed:** area was actually 2 cm²; first convert to 0.0002 m². Do not “correct” units after accepting the original conclusion.

## Q04 — Faraday's law and rate conversion

For a specified reaction and current efficiency, metal mass associated with charge is m = I t M η/(n F). Use current I in amperes, time t in seconds, molar mass M in g/mol, electrons n per metal atom, efficiency η as a fraction, and F = 96,485 C/mol for these exercises. The result is grams. For Fe → Fe²⁺ use M = 55.845 and n = 2. This is a charge balance, not evidence that a measured external current equals all corrosion current.

Convert mass to volume using density, then volume to average thickness using exposed area. Average loss can conceal deep local attack. A calculated uniform penetration rate is not a pit growth law or permission to extrapolate indefinitely. State exposure conditions, assumptions and the significance of current efficiency.

**Worked:** 0.010 A for one day at 100% efficiency gives 0.250039 g iron loss. At 50% efficiency the modeled mass halves. **Changed:** a recorder reports 10 mA, not 10 A. Convert before multiplying; a thousand-fold error should be caught by a dimensional and magnitude check.

## Q05 — Potential, equilibrium and polarization

An electrode potential is measured relative to a reference; the number alone is incomplete. A galvanic series is environment-dependent, while standard electrode potentials refer to specified equilibrium conditions. Neither list alone predicts the actual coupled corrosion rate . Passivation, electrolyte, temperature, area and kinetics matter.

A Pourbaix diagram organizes thermodynamic stability by potential and pH for stated species activities and temperature. Regions commonly described as immunity, corrosion or passivity express possible equilibrium states; they do not give an operating corrosion rate or guarantee a sound protective film. A point in a passive region does not rule out localized breakdown under an actual complex environment. Read axes, reference scale and assumptions first.

Polarization is a change from the freely established potential as current flows. Activation, concentration and solution-resistance effects influence measured response. A more negative reading can include voltage drop through electrolyte and is not automatically proof of adequate protection. The technician records setup and conditions for competent interpretation.

**Worked:** compare −0.70 V versus CSE with −0.70 V versus another reference only after a valid conversion basis. **Changed:** the reference type is unknown; do not assume interchangeability. Repeat with a known suitable reference and retain the original limitation.

## Q06 — General, localized and differential-environment attack

Describe the distribution before assigning a mechanism. Broad rough thinning differs from isolated cavities, attack at a gasket crevice or narrow corrosion under a deposit. Map accessible and inaccessible areas, use scale in photographs and record cleaning that changes what can be seen. A small affected surface area can still contain a deep penetration.[S04]

Differences in oxygen access or local chemistry can establish cells. A crevice or deposit can create conditions different from the bulk fluid. A clean bulk-water result therefore does not fully describe the metal surface beneath deposits. Avoid scraping unknown deposits simply to get a photograph; safe sampling and preservation may be needed.

**Worked:** average coupon loss is low but a deep pit is present. Report both average loss and maximum measured pit with method limitations. **Changed:** the pit base cannot be reached by the gauge tip. Record the measurement as limited, not the true maximum, and seek a suitable method.

## Q07 — Galvanic effects and area relationships

Galvanic attack requires electrically connected materials with a driving potential and an electrolyte path. The more anodic member can experience accelerated dissolution. A small anodic area coupled to a large cathodic area can be unfavorable, but actual rate still depends on the environment and kinetics.[S05]

Inspection should identify material identity, contact, wetted area, isolation and damage location. Do not assume a coating defect exposes equal areas on both materials. Coating only an anodic member can concentrate attack at its defects when the cathodic area remains large; mitigation requires a system-level design rather than an unreviewed patch choice.

**Worked:** a small exposed steel fastener is electrically connected to a large wetted noble-metal surface. Flag the area relationship and verify alloy/environment before recommending material changes. **Changed:** the metal that appeared noble is passive only in a different environment; update the interpretation rather than treating a memorized series as universal.

## Q08 — Mechanical effects, cracking and selective attack

Flow-assisted damage, erosion-corrosion and cavitation can produce distinct patterns, but visual shape alone is not a definitive diagnosis. Record location relative to bends, restrictions, impingement and pressure changes. Fretting involves small relative movements at contacts. A change in process velocity or solids may help explain a trend without proving the entire mechanism.

Cracking can involve stress, environment and susceptible material. The technician must escalate crack-like indications rather than substitute a uniform wall-loss calculation. Intergranular attack follows susceptible grain-boundary regions; selective leaching can leave a weak remnant that retains apparent shape. Brass dezincification is one example.[S06]

**Worked:** a pink porous brass fitting looks dimensionally intact. Do not infer strength from its outline; preserve evidence and request competent evaluation. **Changed:** a crack-like indication appears next to corrosion. Add the crack concern immediately; do not average it away in a thickness report.

## Q09 — Materials and metallurgy in field records

Material selection balances environment, strength, temperature, fabrication and economics. Carbon steel, stainless steels, aluminium, copper alloys and nonmetals have different limitations; “stainless” means neither universally immune nor automatically compatible. Heat treatment, welding and cold work can change local behavior. An alloy label cannot replace an actual material record.

Trace item identity through markings, certificates, drawings and approved identification tests. Positive material identification can support composition verification but has method-specific limits; it does not alone establish every mechanical property, heat treatment or weld condition. Keep unknowns visible and involve the responsible materials specialist for substitutions.

**Worked:** two similar-looking elbows have different heat identifiers. Preserve the distinction until records establish grade and specification. **Changed:** composition matches but heat treatment is unverified. Do not close the whole material requirement from composition alone.

## Q10 — Environment and representative sampling

Temperature, wetting, dissolved species, oxygen, flow, deposits and microbial activity can influence corrosion. Sample location and timing matter. A stagnant branch, water bottom or shutdown period may differ from a well-mixed main stream. Define the question before selecting a sample point; record whether the sample represents bulk fluid, deposit, surface or another population.

pH describes hydrogen-ion activity logarithmically. A decrease from pH7 to pH5 corresponds to a hundred-fold increase in activity, not automatically a hundred-fold corrosion rate . Conductivity reflects the solution's ability to carry current and depends on composition and temperature. Neither measurement independently identifies all corrosive species.[S07]

Use suitable containers, preservation and holding instructions from the actual laboratory method. Gas loss, temperature change and contamination can alter results. Prepare chain-of-custody records before sampling. Do not collect unknown sour or pressurized process fluid as a classroom exercise.

**Worked:** sample pH changes after transport; investigate handling and timing rather than silently replacing the field value. **Changed:** the instrument verification failed before sampling. Both specimen handling and instrument validity must be resolved.

## Q11 — Internal corrosion and microbial evidence

Internal corrosion evaluation connects materials, fluid chemistry, water availability, temperature and operating history. A dry-gas assumption must be checked against actual water accumulation and transients. CO₂, H₂S, oxygen ingress and deposits can matter in different ways; safe sampling and specialist interpretation are essential. Do not infer safety from absence of odor.

Microorganisms can modify surface conditions and corrosion behavior. A positive bulk-fluid result does not by itself establish the cause of a particular pit, and a negative bulk result does not prove the absence of a surface-associated community. Integrate morphology, deposit/surface evidence, operating history and suitable laboratory work.[S08]

**Worked:** pits occur under deposits while the bulk microbial screen is negative. Preserve the discrepancy and request a representative investigation rather than closing MIC. **Changed:** surface evidence is positive but another mechanism is plausible; retain competing explanations until the evidence distinguishes them.

## Q12 — Monitoring plan and method selection

A monitoring plan specifies asset/location, expected mechanism, measurement method, interval, quality checks, action thresholds and responsible reviewer. Select methods that can detect the kind and timing of damage that matters. Coupon averages, probe trends, wall-thickness maps and process chemistry answer different questions. Agreement strengthens a conclusion only when scope and time actually match.

Define what triggers an investigation before seeing a favorable number. A failed sensor, missing record or changed operating condition can require action even without a threshold exceedance. An installed instrument is not proof that valid data are being collected. Verify clocks, units, location tags and data continuity.

**Worked:** a monthly coupon cannot establish the exact hour a short upset began. Use it as exposure-average evidence alongside valid shorter-interval records. **Changed:** the online probe was out of service during the upset; report that gap rather than drawing a smooth line through it.

## Q13 — Coupons: handling and mass loss

Record coupon material, serial number, initial mass, exposed area, installation/removal times, orientation and location. Preserve as-received appearance and deposits as directed. Cleaning must remove corrosion products without unaccounted base-metal loss; the laboratory procedure and blank/correction method matter. Unknown cleaning history makes a precise rate suspect. Legacy instructions containing hazardous solvents are not classroom cleaning recipes.[S09]

For these exercises, average penetration in mm/year is 87.6W/(ρAt), where W is corrected loss in mg, ρ density in g/cm³, A exposed area in cm² and t hours. The constant follows from unit conversion and a 365-day year. Include exposed edges when the specified area definition requires them. Do not switch grams and milligrams or days and hours without changing the equation.

**Worked:** corrected loss 100 mg, density 7.85, area 20 cm²,720 h gives 0.07749 mm/year. **Changed:** cleaning blank indicates 10 mg of the original 110 mg loss was cleaning-related; use 100 mg only where the validated correction method supports that subtraction. Unknown correction cannot be invented.

## Q14 — Pits, thickness and trends

Measure pit depth from an appropriate reference surface and document whether general thinning affects that reference. A bridge spanning corrosion products can give a false datum. Thickness measurements require suitable method, calibration/reference checks, surface condition and location repeatability. An isolated minimum cannot be compared with an unrelated historical average as if they were the same point.

For matched readings, rate = (earlier thickness − later thickness)/elapsed years. A simplified time-to-limit = (current thickness − supplied limit)/assumed rate is only an exercise or screening calculation. It does not establish remaining life, inspection interval or fitness for service when mechanisms, uncertainty, geometry or loading are unresolved.

**Worked:** matched 6.0 mm then 5.6 mm over 2 years gives 0.2 mm/year. A supplied 4.8 mm screening limit gives 4 years algebraically. **Changed:** the newer measurement was at a different grid position; the matched-point rate is no longer established. Obtain comparable evidence before using the projection.

## Q15 — Electrical-resistance and polarization monitoring

An electrical-resistance probe follows change in a sensing element as metal is lost, with temperature compensation and design limitations. Interpret the supplied instrument conversion and trend, not raw counts as universal thickness. Damage to the element, deposits, noise and changes in conditions can complicate interpretation. A short interval dominated by resolution/noise should not be annualized into a dramatic unsupported rate .

Linear polarization resistance uses a small electrochemical perturbation around the corrosion potential and an appropriate model to estimate rate . Conductive electrolyte contact and valid assumptions are necessary; multiphase exposure, deposits and solution resistance may compromise results. A displayed number does not prove the method was applicable.[S10]

**Worked:** a calibrated ER record shows 0.012 mm loss in 30 days; annualized trend is 0.146 mm/year using 365 days. **Changed:** the element was replaced halfway through and the baseline reset was omitted. Segment the records correctly; do not interpret the reset as loss or recovery.

## Q16 — Cathodic protection systems and boundaries

Cathodic protection makes the protected metal the cathodic member of an electrochemical system. Galvanic systems use a suitable sacrificial anode; impressed-current systems use an external DC supply and anodes. Coatings can reduce current demand, while defects and shielding affect distribution. Neither system restores metal already lost.[S11]

The technician identifies components, records authorized measurements and reports changes. Design, criterion selection, interference evaluation and adjustments require the appropriate competence and authority. Excessive or poorly distributed current can create problems; “more negative is always better” is not an acceptable control rule.

**Worked:** rectifier output changes sharply from its established trend. Check identity, instrument validity and operating records; report to the responsible CP specialist. **Changed:** a neighboring system also changed. Include potential interference and timing, without independently changing either installation.

## Q17 — Structure-to-electrolyte measurements

Record reference-electrode type, condition, contact, position, polarity, meter, structure connection, time and current state. A structure-to-electrolyte voltage includes the measurement geometry and may include IR drop. On, instant-off and depolarized readings are different observations; interruption must be authorized and technically valid, not improvised by a learner.[S12]

Use an appropriate high-input-impedance instrument and a verified reference according to the actual procedure. A reversed connection reverses sign; dry contact or a defective lead may produce unstable or misleading values. Do not average obviously invalid readings into a compliance result. Apply only the governing criterion with its qualifications, material/reference basis and voltage-drop treatment.

**Worked:** on −1.10 V and properly obtained off −0.82 V differ by 0.28 V. This difference alone does not establish all protection criteria. **Changed:** one influencing source was not interrupted. Record that limitation and obtain specialist direction rather than calling the off reading IR-free.

## Q18 — Coatings as a control system

A coating system includes surface condition, preparation, material compatibility, application, thickness, cure and service exposure. A specified product on an inadequately prepared surface is not the specified system. Inspect approved requirements, environmental records and defects before accepting the apparent color or coverage.

Wet film and dry film are related through solids and application conditions, but actual dry measurement remains important. In a simple unthinned model, requiredWFT = targetDFT/volume-solids fraction. Too much thickness can be a defect, not an extra safety margin. Holiday testing must use the correct method and safe settings for the actual system; this course uses low-voltage training equipment only.

**Worked:**150 µm target at 60%solids implies 250 µm wet film in the simplified model. **Changed:** unapproved thinner was added. Do not retain the original solids assumption or accept the material change without its governing instruction.

## Q19 — Inhibitors and treatment calculations

Chemical treatment can alter corrosion reactions or form protective films, but performance depends on chemistry, delivery, concentration and operating conditions. Distinguish product concentration from active ingredient concentration. Confirm whether a specification uses mass or volume basis and what flow the dose is based on. A tank level change does not alone prove delivery to the intended process point.

Calculate the approved setpoint from actual units; do not select a chemical or dose experimentally on production equipment. Check pump calibration, injection availability, compatibility and monitoring under the responsible specialist. An underdose or interrupted delivery can matter even when inventory is available. Changes require authorization and a documented verification plan.

**Worked:**20 mg/L active treatment at 10,000 L/h needs 200,000 mg/h=200 g/h. A200 g/L active concentrate implies 1 L/h, subject to actual approved conditions. **Changed:** the supplied concentrate is 100 g/L active; the arithmetic becomes 2 L/h, but the technician must obtain authorization before changing the pump.

## Q20 — Treatment evaluation and false reassurance

Evaluate treatment with a defined baseline, exposure conditions and valid follow-up evidence. A lower average corrosion rate may reflect a temperature or water-content change rather than treatment alone. Where conditions are suitably comparable, calculated efficiency =100(untreated rate − treated rate)/untreated rate . Report the comparison basis and any confounding change.

Treatment that reduces average loss may leave local attack, deposits or cracking unresolved. Do not declare all corrosion controlled from one favorable metric. Retain adverse observations and escalation thresholds. Review missed doses, pump failures and sampling gaps alongside chemical residuals and damage measurements.

**Worked:** comparable rates 0.50 and 0.10 mm/year give 80%reduction. **Changed:** follow-up temperature was substantially lower; report observed reduction, but do not attribute the full change to inhibitor without further evidence.

## Q21 — Design, maintenance and environmental controls

Good control can include appropriate materials, drainage, access, electrical isolation, coatings, chemical treatment and changes to operating conditions. Each has interactions. Removing a water trap may reduce one risk, but modifying piping requires design and operating approval. Isolation must account for electrical safety and the complete circuit, not just a gasket.

The technician contributes observations: persistent wetting, blocked drains, inaccessible inspection points, damaged insulation/weather barriers and locations where deposits accumulate. Record actual condition and consequence, then route a specific recommendation. Do not remove insulation or alter equipment without the applicable safe-work authorization.

**Worked:** repeated corrosion occurs beneath a leaking weather seal. Document the moisture pathway and affected inspection scope, not just a repaint request. **Changed:** the seal is repaired; existing hidden damage still needs appropriate evaluation before closure.

## Q22 — Instrument quality and uncertainty

Distinguish calibration, adjustment, field verification and functional checks. Confirm range, resolution, suitable reference and acceptance limits before use, and repeat required checks afterward. Retain failed checks; replacing them with a favorable value conceals the reliability of intervening results. Trace affected records back to the last known valid state.

Consider uncertainty when readings approach a decision limit. Do not invent a universal guard band; apply the supplied decision rule or seek clarification. Repeatability shows consistency, not necessarily accuracy. Multiple identical readings from the wrong mode or reference can all be wrong.

**Worked:** reference 100 units, allowed 98–102, post-check 104. Quarantine affected readings and investigate; do not simply subtract 4 from every result without a validated correction. **Changed:** records establish a discrete change after the third specimen. Narrow the affected set only to the extent the evidence supports.

## Q23 — Records, visualization and escalation

Keep raw values, units, method, location, conditions, instrument checks and corrections linked. Plot like quantities against actual time. Mark missing data and replacements, avoid lines implying measurements where none exist, and retain source records behind summaries. A mean cannot erase a known local exceedance.

An escalation report should state asset/location, observed concern, evidence quality, immediate safe action, unresolved question, required reviewer and due action. Avoid both unsupported alarm and unsupported reassurance. The owner of a production decision must be explicit.

**Worked:** seven of ten planned locations were measured and one failed the supplied threshold. Report 70%coverage and the failure; “mostly good” is inadequate. **Changed:** the remaining three pass. Coverage becomes 100%, but the original failure still needs disposition.

## Q24 — Integrated technician handover

Build a coherent handover from coupon, thickness, chemistry, probe and control-system evidence. Ask whether dates, assets and conditions match. Reconcile differences by investigating method and exposure before selecting the most favorable number. State what is established, what remains uncertain and which authorized action can resolve it.

The technician's final product is not merely a table. It is a traceable record that another competent person can use without guessing how results were obtained. Preserve limitations, rejected data and changed conclusions. An unresolved concern may be the correct outcome; hiding it to obtain a clean certificate is not.

**Worked:** coupon average is acceptable under the fictional plan but a mapped pit exceeds its action threshold. Escalate the pit and preserve both results. **Changed:** the pit measurement is invalid due to datum error. Correct the status to “unresolved pending valid measurement,” not “passed,” until adequate evidence exists.

# VUC050 instructor and practical guide

## Delivery sequence

Plan 48 hours:16 instruction with worked examples,8 workbook laboratories,16 supervised practical/portfolio work,4 remediation and 4 controlled final. Extend practice as needed; hours are planning allowances, not an attendance-only award. Begin with units, percentages and safe instrument use. TeachQ01–05 before calculations, Q06–11 before mechanism interpretations, Q12–20 before monitoring/control work, andQ21–24 before the integrated dossier. Follow each chapter with its two formative checks; require explanation and corrected reasoning, not answer copying.

Instructor must demonstrate competence in corrosion monitoring and the actual instruments used. Arrange a qualified specialist for methods outside that competence. Use current equipment instructions and a controlled safe laboratory. Explain that source certifications have their own eligibility and experience conditions. A preparation course cannot create them.

## Equipment and safety

Provide a current-limited source no greater than 12VDC, protected leads and suitable multimeter; safe metal coupons and labeled photos; balance with appropriate check masses and 0.1 mg resolution for the supplied mass exercise; caliper and known area specimens; safe pH/conductivity standards and meters; pit-depth gauge and known recessed specimens; UT thickness instrument with suitable reference blocks and known training plates; ER/LPR instrument demonstration or an approved manufacturer training simulator; reference electrode and safe potential simulator; DFT/WFT equipment, known coated panels and low-voltage holiday training apparatus; water-only pump calibration rig, graduated vessel and timer; recording tools.

Assessor holds actual specimen reference findings separately and checks instruments before learners start. Use benign materials with reviewed safety information. No pressurized retrieval, hazardous chemical cleaning, live process fluids, confined-space entry, live rectifier adjustment or mains electrical work. Stop if safety controls or instrument suitability are absent. The dose calculation is not permission to inject chemicals.

Mandatory actual demonstrations: P03, P10, P13, P14, P15, P17, P18, P19, P22. P15 permits a validated manufacturer training simulator because no production probe handling is authorized; record simulator identity and its limitation. Other physical stations require the actual safe apparatus. Reading a case does not satisfy a physical requirement. If equipment is unavailable, record knowledge work completed and practical pending.

## Scoring and remediation

Each station has four listed criteria scored 0–2:2 independent correct performance with supporting record,1 safe incomplete performance,0 missing/incorrect/unsafe. Require 6/8 each plus correction of every deficient safety/identity/decision element. Critical failures include unsafe energization, unauthorized process access, chemical/CP changes without authority, fabricated readings or concealed known defects. Stop the attempt, teach the deficiency and assess a changed specimen/case; retain both results. Do not award based only on aggregate points.

## P01 — Work authorization

Given L50 and a request to retrieve a live coupon, produce the bounded work plan. Score scope/decision owner, safe access boundary, required records and correct escalation. The learner must decline unauthorized retrieval while proceeding with safe supplied analysis.

## P02 — Cell explanation

Draw and explain a wet dissimilar-metal assembly, including anode, cathode, metallic and ionic paths. Score correct reactions/roles, complete paths, environmental dependence and changed bypass-wire interpretation.

## P03 — Low-voltage measurement

On the protected training rig, inspect meter/leads, select correct voltage/current connections under instructor procedure, measure a known resistor circuit and record units. Score setup safety, correct connection/range, valid reading/reference comparison and unit conversion. Never connect the current input directly across the source.

## P04 — Charge calculation

Solve W01 and its changed efficiency/resistance case without coaching. Score dimensional setup, charge/time conversion, correct mass and bounded interpretation. A numerical answer without units is incomplete.

## P05 — Potential and diagram reasoning

Assessor supplies a schematic equilibrium map with explicitly labeled regions: atpH7, below−0.8Vmetal stable;−0.8to 0.2Voxide stable; above 0.2Vdissolved species stable. These invented boundaries are only a reading exercise. Classify−0.9,−0.4and 0.3 V, then explain why the map gives neither corrosion rate nor localized-damage immunity. Score axes/reference awareness, correct region reading, assumptions and kinetic limitation.

## P06 — Morphology record

Use labeled safe specimens/photos showing broad thinning, isolated pits and under-gasket attack. Record observed shape and extent before proposing mechanisms. Score morphology distinction, scaled location record, uncertainty and appropriate follow-up without unsupported diagnosis.

## P07 — Galvanic assembly

Trace an actual safe disconnected metal assembly and its proposed isolation/bypass. Score contact/electrolyte paths, material/environment limits, area consideration and bounded mitigation recommendation. Do not energize or modify production equipment.

## P08 — Crack/selective attack escalation

Given a pink porous fitting image and a crack-like indication, prepare an escalation. Score observation versus inference, recognition of strength uncertainty, correct specialist route and refusal to use average thinning as crack acceptance.

## P09 — Material traceability

Reconcile two heat numbers, one certificate and a composition-only result. Score item identity, unresolved certificate link, method limitations and controlled disposition. Assessor supplies a corrected certificate as the changed case.

## P10 — Benign water measurements

Verify pH/conductivity meters against their appropriate standards, measure identified benign samples and record temperature/time. Score verification, contamination control, actual raw readings and interpretation limits. A failed check requires correction and actual repeat, not a handwritten expected result.

## P11 — Internal/MIC investigation plan

Use a pit/deposit case with negative bulk microbial result. Score representative evidence selection, competing explanations, safe sampling boundary and no premature proof/exclusion of MIC.

## P12 — Monitoring plan

Build L50's location/method/interval/action-owner table. Score mechanism-method match, quality checks, missing-data handling and usable escalation thresholds. Actual production intervals require the governing program, not the fictional plan.

## P13 — Coupon measurement record

Handle an identified safe precleaned coupon with appropriate clean gloves, verify balance using check masses, weigh and measure the supplied exposed-area geometry. Link to supplied exposure/cleaning records and calculate rate . Score traceability, actual measurement quality, unit/correction reasoning and pit/average distinction. No learner chemical cleaning.

## P14 — Pit and thickness measurement

Use known recessed and thickness specimens. Verify instruments, establish datum/grid and obtain repeat readings at specified locations. Score reference/mode suitability, actual location control, correct measurements within the instructor's documented instrument/specimen tolerance and bounded trend calculation. Record inaccessible pit bases as limited.

## P15 — Probe trend demonstration

On a safe instrument or validated simulator, identify probe/baseline, read a trend and recognize a supplied replacement/reset. Score actual setup identification, units/baseline, segmented calculation and LPR applicability explanation. Record simulator use explicitly; do not claim field probe installation competence.

## P16 — CP component identification

On a de-energized schematic/training assembly, identify protected structure, anodes, reference/test connections and supply where applicable. Score galvanic/impressed distinction, current-path explanation, authority boundary and response to abnormal trend.

## P17 — Potential measurement

Use a safe simulator and verified reference arrangement per instructor procedure. Make actual measurements with recorded polarity and reference identity, then diagnose a deliberately disconnected lead in the safe rig. Score setup/reference check, actual reading, invalid-condition recognition and no automatic compliance claim. No production interruption.

## P18 — Coating measurements

Verify the gauge on known training references, measure coated panels and demonstrate WFT on safe training material. Demonstrate low-voltage holiday equipment only on the designated safe panel following its instructions. Score preparation/reference checks, actual values/units, appropriate method and interpretation of limits. Record actual apparatus used.

## P19 — Water-only dosing calibration

Collect a timed discharge from the benign pump rig and calculate L/h; compare with a supplied target. Calculate active-dose requirement separately. Score safe setup, timing/volume measurement, correct concentration/flow arithmetic and distinction between calculation and production authorization.

## P20 — Treatment evidence

Compare W07 rates and a changed-temperature follow-up. Score percentage calculation, comparability assessment, local-damage limitations and specific monitoring recommendation.

## P21 — Maintenance recommendation

Use a leaking insulation-weather-seal case. Score moisture-path observation, safe access boundary, existing-damage follow-up and clear authorized owner/action. Repair of the seal alone is not inspection of hidden metal.

## P22 — Quality-control failure

Assessor supplies an actual safe instrument with a detectable unsuitable setting or failed reference check. Learner checks it before unknown measurements and produces an affected-record plan. Score check execution, failure recognition, nonfabricated records and justified retest scope.

## P23 — Corrected report

Produce W08 report and changed-record trail. Score unique-location coverage, validity versus pass distinction, preserved originals and actionable escalation. Duplicate rows cannot increase inspection coverage.

## P24 — Dossier defense

Submit I50 with actual practical records. Explain one disagreement between methods and revise after changed evidence. Score traceability, technical reconciliation, authority/uncertainty and specific next action. A rehearsed generic answer without the supporting dossier is incomplete.

## Controlled final and award

Separate instructor form contains 24 original cases with three scored decisions each:144 points. Allow 240 minutes, calculator and coursebook; no keys, collaboration or coaching. Require 116/144 and at least 4/6 per domain, with no unresolved critical error. Each decision scores 0–2 using the station meanings. Keys explain expected reasoning. After remediation use the distinct changed case; retain original results. Two unsuccessful changed attempts require individualized teaching and a fresh equivalent assessment.

Award VNDRLY/U Corrosion Technician Preparation only after 48 corrected formative responses, all 8 workbooks, I50, all 24 stations including actual mandatory demonstrations, and controlled final pass. Record identity, material hash/version, actual dates/hours, instrument/specimen IDs, assessor competence, raw scores, remediation and scope. An incomplete practical record cannot be presented as full qualification. No actual learner has been assessed through the authoring of this package.

Recommend VNDRLY review at 24months or sooner after material method changes, extended nonuse or performance concerns; external credentials follow their own current renewal rules. Appeals receive competent review independent of the disputed assessment where practicable. Employer authorization and external experience remain separate .

# VUC050 formative checks

Attempt before reading the response. Explain every correction, revisit its chapter/station and apply the changed case. These checks support instruction and do not replace the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Can a measured mass establish a rate without exposure time?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Rate requires valid matched duration and other inputs. Revisit Q01 and explain its changed case.

## Q01-L2

Does a routine reading assignment authorize process changes?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Preserve assigned scope and decision authority. Revisit Q01 and explain its changed case.

## Q02-L1

Where does metal oxidation occur?

**Answer:** At anodic sites.

**Reason/remediation:** Oxidation releases electrons. Revisit Q02 and explain its changed case.

## Q02-L2

Can one metal contain both anodic and cathodic regions?

**Answer:** Yes.

**Reason/remediation:** Different local conditions can support a cell. Revisit Q02 and explain its changed case.

What current flows through 100Ω at 1 V?

**Answer:** 0.01 A or 10 mA.

**Reason/remediation:** I=V/R. Revisit Q03 and explain its changed case.

Is a field corrosion circuit always a fixed resistor?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Electrochemical interface behavior also matters. Revisit Q03 and explain its changed case.

What time unit is used with current inA andF inC/mol?

**Answer:** Seconds.

**Reason/remediation:** Ampere means coulomb per second. Revisit Q04 and explain its changed case.

Does Faraday mass establish pit depth?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Mass loss does not establish its spatial distribution. Revisit Q04 and explain its changed case.

**Answer:** No.
