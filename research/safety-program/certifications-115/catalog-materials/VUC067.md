# VUC067 — Tower Crane Operation

## Program boundary and learning outcome

This original VNDRLY/U program teaches planning, inspection, controlled load handling and safe securing of tower cranes. Completion records the exact crane type and observed activities. It is not CCO certification, a license, an erection-director qualification or an employer's equipment/task evaluation. A written or simulated result alone earns a knowledge-only record. Actual operating assessment requires suitable equipment, authorized supervision and the performance evidence in the workbook.

Read each chapter, work its example and complete the corresponding two checks before its application. Bring uncertainty to the instructor rather than filling missing load, configuration or procedure information by assumption. The governing operating information is the actual manufacturer's current instructions for the installed configuration, together with applicable rules and authorized site procedures. All numerical charts and limits labeled T67 are fictional classroom data, never operating instructions for a real crane.

## Q01 — Roles, readiness and stop authority

A tower operator controls machine motion but depends on several independently competent people: the assembly/disassembly director manages that operation, qualified technical personnel resolve design and configuration questions, riggers arrange the load, signal personnel communicate movements, and management establishes site control. Certification, training and employer evaluation are different evidence. A course completion cannot substitute for demonstrated competence on the equipment and work assigned.

Before accepting a shift identify your authorization, crane identity, task, supervisor, signal channel and means of safe access. Review previous defects and restrictions. Fatigue, impairment, a missing familiarization or inability to understand the signal system can make an otherwise trained person unready. Report the condition and obtain relief or training; do not begin merely because a schedule is urgent. Anyone's emergency stop must receive an appropriate safe stop, not an argument about who normally signals.

Worked case: a learner has passed this written course and is assigned alone to a luffing crane after practice only on a simulator. The missing evidence is actual competence and required employer authorization/evaluation, not another signature on the written score. Arrange supervised training on the actual type. Changed application: an experienced operator receives an unfamiliar control layout. Experience does not remove familiarization and evaluation needs.

## Q02 — Crane types, components and power flow

A hammerhead crane generally changes radius with a trolley on a substantially horizontal jib. A luffing crane changes jib angle to alter radius; geometry and out-of-service configuration differ. A self-erecting crane may use a distinct base, outrigger and unfolding arrangement. Identify mast, slewing assembly, jib, counterjib where fitted, trolley or luffing machinery, hoist, reeving, hook block, ballast and electrical supply on the specific machine rather than assuming every type has every component.

Trace operator command through control, drive, transmission and brake to load movement. Electrical, hydraulic and mechanical systems can retain energy after normal controls stop. A brake is not a substitute for isolation, and an emergency stop is not proof of zero energy. Operator observation and permitted maintenance stay within the manual and employer authorization; electrical repair belongs to authorized personnel. Never bridge a limit device to diagnose a nuisance trip.

Worked case: a control produces no motion, but the drive cabinet is energized and the load is suspended. Treat this as a malfunction with stored energy, control exposure and summon qualified help. Do not enter the cabinet or release a brake. Changed application: power returns unexpectedly. Prevent an unplanned restart using the approved recovery procedure before resuming.

## Q03 — Site and supporting system

The site plan must address the entire operating and out-of-service envelope: load path, counterjib, other cranes, structures, roads, public access, underground services, overhead conductors and space needed during erection. Air rights and property boundaries are constraints, not clearance assumptions. Foundation and supporting structure adequacy require the prescribed manufacturer/engineering basis. Operators identify damage, flooding, excavation, settlement or unauthorized alterations and report them; they do not certify foundation design by visual judgment.

For self-erecting equipment, verify the specified setup geometry, support and outrigger arrangements through the approved procedure. A large-looking mat does not prove capacity. Keep foundations, anchors, drainage and access protected from site changes. Electrical supply, isolation, grounding and lightning provisions must be established by appropriate authorized personnel. Do not infer their adequacy from the crane powering on.

Worked case: yesterday's accepted foundation now has an excavation immediately beside it and water collecting around anchors. Hold operations and refer the changed support condition to the responsible authority. Yesterday's approval describes yesterday's condition. Changed application: no visible movement exists. Lack of visible movement is not proof that support capacity is unchanged.

## Q04 — Erection and dismantling interfaces

Erection and dismantling are planned sequences with changing centers of gravity and support conditions. The assembly/disassembly director must have the required competence and qualifications. Crew members need their task, hazards and communication arrangements before movement. The operator follows the authorized sequence and does not improvise a shortcut because a component appears nearly balanced. Pins or bolts may be carrying loads that are not visually obvious.

Identify temporary supports, assist-crane capacity at the actual radius, rigging, connection specifications and the point at which each component becomes stable. Keep people clear of crush zones and suspended components. If a worker moves out of view into an exposure area, establish communication and stop the relevant motion until their position and safety are known. Structural connections use the specified components, installation and verification process; generic torque values or substitute bolts are unacceptable.

Worked case: a connection pin resists removal while the crew says the section is supported. Stop and verify the engineered support/load-transfer state; do not add force or move the hoist experimentally. Changed application: the pin frees suddenly. That does not validate the sequence; evaluate movement and stability before continuing under the director.

## Q05 — Climbing, ties, ballast and plumb

Climbing changes the support arrangement of the crane. Internal climbing frames, collars, beams and wedges, or an external climbing unit and ties, must match the specified configuration and sequence. The operator must understand the commanded balance condition and communication plan, but this course does not qualify the learner to design ties or direct climbing. Do not use a remembered balance load, tie spacing or permissible free-standing height from another crane.

Verify the approved mast height, tie locations, supported height above ties, ballast arrangement and post-climb position of the climbing equipment. Both insufficient and excessive ballast can be unsafe. Host-structure attachments require the appropriate engineering basis. Plumb is checked by the prescribed method and tolerance. A generic arithmetic exercise cannot establish the real crane's acceptance. After erection or relevant changes, the specified inspection and testing must be completed before ordinary lifting.

Worked T67 exercise: an assigned fictional plumb limit is 1: 500 over 40 m. The displacement limit is 40, 000/500 = 80 mm; a 92 mm displacement exceeds it by 12 mm. The decision is a hold for correction/technical review, not to move ballast until it looks straight. Changed application: the manufacturer specifies 60 mm. That stricter applicable instruction governs the exercise instead of the generic value.

## Q06 — Shift inspection and defect disposition

An inspection is an organized search for conditions affecting safety, not a form completed from memory. Follow the actual inspection scope: structure and connections visible from safe access, support changes, fluids and leaks, controls, brakes, guards, hoist system, rope path, hook and latch, electrical condition, operational aids, safety devices and access. Check required records and outstanding restrictions. Use safe functional checks specified by the manufacturer; do not create an overload or disable a device to prove it operates.

Distinguish a shift inspection from periodic or annual inspections, post-assembly inspection and special inspection after a change or event. The operator cannot extend an expired inspection merely by finding no obvious defect. Record what was observed, where, when and the restriction communicated. A defect that creates a safety hazard requires the appropriate removal from service and repair process. A replacement part does not establish readiness until the required checks and authorized release are complete.

Worked case: the checklist is signed, but the hook latch is damaged and the rope appears to have jumped a sheave. Suspend the lift and report the actual conditions. A signed checklist does not override the equipment. Changed application: a colleague says it ran this morning. Previous movement does not prove continued safe condition.

## Q07 — Wire rope, reeving and hooks

Inspect the rope where deterioration can concentrate: sheaves, drums, repetitive bending locations, terminations and sections exposed to abrasion or heat. Look for distortion, broken wires, corrosion, diameter change, damaged terminations and evidence of crushing or kinking. Use the applicable rope type and removal criteria; a single remembered broken-wire count cannot cover all ropes or all defects. Do not handle moving rope, run fingers along damaged strands, or climb into an unprotected reeving area.

Reeving determines both load capability and available hook travel. Confirm line parts, permitted line pull, block and hook capacity, drum capacity and required remaining wraps against the actual manual. More parts do not automatically make every chart rating larger. Check that rope is seated and spooled correctly and that the hook/block has the appropriate identity and inspection status. Never straighten a hook, weld a fitting or substitute a rope because its diameter is similar.

Worked T67 example: permitted line pull is 2.0 t and four parts give a simplified ideal 8.0 t force ceiling, but the configuration chart permits only 6.5 t. The 6.5 t limit still governs before all other applicable deductions/limits. This simplified multiplication is a classroom comparison, not a reeving design. Changed application: only two parts are installed. Reassess the actual configuration rather than retaining the four-part rating.

## Q08 — Capacity charts and configuration

A valid chart decision begins with identity: crane model, jib length/type, mast/support configuration, reeving and chart revision. Determine the operating radius from the correct reference, the full planned path and the governing worst condition. Do not substitute jib length for load radius. Follow the actual chart's conventions for intermediate values, deductions and gross/net load. Never interpolate or extrapolate unless explicitly permitted by the governing information.

T67 fictional chart: at radii 15, 20, 25 and 30 m, gross suspended-load capacities are respectively 8.0, 6.5, 5.0 and 4.0 t. The classroom instruction requires the next larger listed radius for intermediate radii and includes hook, rigging and attachments in gross suspended load. These conventions belong only to this chart. For a 23 m path use 25 m and 5.0 t, not an average between columns. An unavailable or mismatched chart is a stop condition.

Worked: payload 4.2 t, hook 0.3 t, rigging 0.2 t and beam 0.4 t total 5.1 t. At 23 m the permitted T67 gross load is 5.0 t, so the lift fails by 0.1 t. Moving slowly does not repair insufficient capacity. Changed: the path extends to 28 m; the 30 m column gives 4.0 t and the deficiency increases. Replan with authorized information before lifting.

## Q09 — Load weight, center of gravity and rigging

Obtain reliable weight information for the actual object, including retained contents and attached equipment. A shipping weight for an empty vessel may not describe a wet or partly filled vessel. Determine center of gravity and approved lifting points. Rigging capacity depends on its identity, configuration, angle, condition and how the load is distributed. A qualified rigger must resolve the actual arrangement. The operator verifies the information needed for the lift rather than assuming a crane's load display will safely discover an unknown weight.

For classroom understanding only, two equal symmetric sling legs carrying a static load W at angle theta above horizontal have ideal tension W/(2 sin theta) in each leg. As the angle becomes shallower, tension rises. Real rigging selection also depends on component ratings, connection factors, unequal loading and manufacturer instructions. Never use this simplified equation to authorize an unreviewed arrangement.

Worked: a 4.0 t load with ideal equal legs at 30 degrees above horizontal produces 4.0 t tension per leg, not 2.0 t. At 60 degrees it is about 2.31 t. Changed: the center of gravity shifts. Equal load sharing is no longer justified. Stop to correct the plan before the load is suspended, and perform only the approved initial check lift to confirm balance and rigging behavior.

## Q10 — Signals and blind lifts

Agree on the signal system before movement. The designated signal person needs the required qualification for the signals used. The operator and signaler must share the meaning of directions, identify the crane when more than one can hear the channel, and test communications. Use standardized hand signals as applicable; agree on any necessary nonstandard signal beforehand. Stop if a signal is unclear or communication needed for safe movement is lost.

Blind lifting needs planned communication and control of the hidden area, not merely a radio. Confirm who sees the load, travel path and landing area, how a transfer between signalers works, and who controls exclusion zones. Only one person normally directs motion at a time; emergency stop signals from others remain valid. Describe directions from the agreed operator perspective and use an unambiguous command sequence. A camera or anti-collision device supplements rather than automatically replaces required signaling.

Worked: the radio breaks up after a command to lower a load behind a wall. Stop safely; do not finish the remembered command while waiting for the signal to return. Re-establish communication and confirm conditions before resuming. Changed: two voices give conflicting directions. Resolve control of signaling with the load secure instead of choosing the louder voice.

## Q11 — Controlled motion and load placement

Start with familiarization inside the permitted practice envelope and no unsupported assumptions about control response. Lift vertically over the load's center of gravity, take slack in a controlled way and make the prescribed initial balance/brake check close to the support surface. Keep the load and hook under control through hoisting, radius change and slewing. Avoid abrupt acceleration and reversal that amplify swing. A load's momentum does not stop when the control returns to neutral.

Plan a clear route, adequate clearance and a prepared landing area. Never side-pull or drag a load with the crane. Use tag lines only under an appropriate plan where they can be handled without entanglement, pinch or electrical exposure; do not wrap a line around the body. Keep people out from under the load and prevent hands entering crush points during placement. Slack and disconnect only after the load is supported and stable and the rigger directs the next step.

Worked: the hook is two metres to the side of a load snagged behind a pallet. Pulling would apply side force and might suddenly free the load. Stop, reposition and resolve the obstruction through an authorized plan. Changed: the load begins to swing. Use the taught machine-specific controlled recovery, protect the path and land when safe; do not chase swing with aggressive commands.

## Q12 — Devices, zoning and multiple cranes

Know which installed devices are safety devices and which are operational aids under the governing requirements. A failure does not create permission to invent a workaround. Safety-device failures require the prescribed stop/removal-from-service response; some operational-aid provisions allow specified temporary alternatives and repair periods, but only under the applicable rule and authorized implementation. The operator must know the particular provision, condition and restrictions before any continued use.

Confirm the configured limits match the actual crane and site. Anti-collision or zoning systems depend on correct geometry, settings and communications; they cannot justify an otherwise unsafe overlapping envelope. Multiple cranes require coordination for jibs, towers, ropes and loads, including out-of-service positions and wind effects. Keep changes controlled. Do not silence alarms or increase a limit because another crane appears farther away on a screen.

Worked case: the indicator shows a 40 m jib, while the installed configuration is 50 m. Stop and have the configuration corrected and verified. A plausible displayed capacity is not proof of a valid model. Changed: an anti-collision alarm repeatedly trips at the same location. Investigate the actual envelope and settings through authorized personnel; do not bypass the system to maintain output.

## Q13 — Wind, weather and out-of-service position

Obtain the specific limits and procedures for operation, erection, climbing, dismantling and parking. These can differ. Wind at the crane and gusts may differ from a ground weather report. Large surface-area loads can become uncontrollable before a nominal machine limit is reached. Lightning, poor visibility, ice and temperature can require additional restrictions. Monitor conditions and plan enough time to land and secure before a hazardous threshold is reached.

Parking is an engineered configuration, not a universal instruction to lock or release slew. Follow the exact crane's out-of-service jib angle/radius, hook position, slew/brake arrangement and restraints. Some cranes require weather-vaning; preventing it can create dangerous loads. Others have different arrangements. Never use a practical-test instruction to set a brake as the complete real-world overnight parking procedure. Verify the manufacturer's instructions for the installed machine and surrounding structures.

Worked T67 case: a fictional lift plan stops lifting at 12 m/s, and observed gusts reach 13 m/s although the average is 9. Stop the exercise and secure under its plan. Do not average away the exceedance. Changed: a broad panel becomes difficult to control at 8 m/s. Stop for loss of control even though the numeric limit has not been reached.

## Q14 — Electrical hazards and exclusion zones

Identify overhead lines and establish the required work-zone assessment, voltage information and protective measures before operations. Treat lines as energized unless the utility owner/operator confirms the required de-energized and grounding condition. An observer saying a line looks unused is insufficient. The OSHA assessment triggers, clearance tables and special arrangements are not interchangeable; do not teach one distance as safe for every voltage and circumstance.

Plan for every part of the equipment, rigging and load, including swing and wind movement. Barriers, warning lines and designated observers or other measures must match the selected compliant method. If line contact occurs, stop movements, warn people away and get emergency/utility assistance through the site response plan. Do not approach or touch the equipment to help someone while it may be energized. The operator follows the emergency procedure; evacuation when fire or other immediate danger requires it needs trained action, not experimentation.

Worked: the lift plan assesses the boom but ignores the long suspended steel load extending toward a line. The plan is incomplete. Reassess the complete envelope before the lift. Changed: a spotter leaves. If that measure is required for the selected method, suspend the operation until compliant protection is restored. All course electrical scenarios are tabletop; no energized-line approach is used for practice.

## Q15 — Special lifts and travel

Personnel lifting, underwater loads, traveling with suspended loads and multiple-crane lifts introduce additional requirements and may be prohibited for the configuration. They are not authorized by passing ordinary load-placement practice. Obtain the specific applicable procedure, equipment suitability, planning and approvals. For personnel platforms, ordinary cargo rigging and a confident operator are not enough; applicable personnel-hoisting requirements must be satisfied. This program uses planning cases only for personnel lifting.

An underwater load may include suction, trapped material, changing buoyancy or snagging. Do not use crane capacity as an experimental breakout force. Rail travel requires the specified track condition, stops, clearances and control arrangements. Self-erecting setup or travel must follow the actual manufacturer's limits. A suspended load can shift the effective envelope and ground/support demand; evaluate the whole operation rather than only the lifting position.

Worked: a submerged object has a recorded dry weight below chart capacity, but its release force and contents are unknown. Hold for a qualified plan; the dry weight alone is not adequate. Changed: a supervisor proposes using a cargo basket to lift a worker for a quick repair. Refuse the improvised personnel lift and use an authorized access method or properly planned compliant operation.

## Q16 — Malfunctions and emergencies

Know how to stop and secure under the actual crane's emergency procedures, who to call and how the site protects the affected zone. A malfunction with a suspended load needs controlled isolation of the hazard and qualified recovery, not an improvised repair by the operator. Do not leave controls with a suspended load except as required by the emergency situation and approved response. Communicate the load's location and condition so responders do not enter blindly.

Fire, smoke, a failing brake, loss of power or a medical emergency in the cab may require distinct actions. Establish a rescue plan for elevated access before the shift; calling emergency services without crane location, height or access information is incomplete preparation. Never perform live fault creation or an unplanned rescue drill to make training realistic. Use a simulator or tabletop for failures and an authorized separate rescue program for actual rescue techniques.

Worked: a load drifts down with the hoist control neutral. Warn and protect the area and execute the trained equipment-specific safe response; report the brake/control fault and remove the equipment from operation until appropriately repaired and released. Changed: the fault disappears on restart. Intermittent failure still requires investigation; absence of a repeated symptom is not proof of repair.

## Q17 — Maintenance, access and handover

Before access or maintenance, identify gravity, electrical, hydraulic and mechanical energy and use the authorized isolation procedure. Controls turned off do not demonstrate isolation. Fall protection and access arrangements must match the actual equipment and work; no climbing practice occurs without the separately established access/rescue controls and competent supervision. Operator maintenance is limited to authorized tasks in the instructions, not structural repair or limit calibration by intuition.

A handover must preserve what the next shift needs: crane/configuration, work status, weather restrictions, defects, repairs and release evidence, inspection status, unusual events and parked condition. Clearly distinguish observed facts from diagnosis. A verbal note alone may not satisfy required reporting and records. Store the defect record where the incoming operator and responsible management can actually retrieve it. Never delete an unresolved restriction because a new shift begins.

Worked: the outgoing operator reports a recurring trolley fault but the log says normal. Record the discrepancy and maintain the appropriate hold while it is resolved. Changed: a technician replaced a component but release checks are not documented. Obtain the required verification before use; the repair action and return-to-service decision are separate.

## Q18 — Integrated lift decision

A sound lift decision integrates authority, site, configuration, condition, load, route, communication and recovery. Passing each topic on a different hypothetical crane is not proof that today's combined lift is ready. Use a repeatable pre-lift briefing: identify equipment and controlling documents; confirm inspection and restrictions; calculate the governing capacity; verify rigging and exclusion zone; agree signals and weather limits; identify stop conditions; establish landing and securing sequence.

Worked T67 case: at a maximum radius of 24 m, payload 3.8 t plus hook 0.3, rigging 0.2 and beam 0.4 gives 4.7 t against the 5.0 t fictional gross rating. Capacity passes by 0.3 t, but a failed required device and missing landing signaler still prevent the lift. A passing arithmetic result is only one condition. Changed case: after those issues are resolved the planned radius changes to 27 m. Now the 4.0 t rating is insufficient. Reassess every material change; never carry forward a stale approval.

The final practical requires controlled actual operation, not just this analysis. Record the observed crane type and limits. If actual equipment or safe supervision is unavailable, finish the knowledge record honestly and leave operational performance unawarded until it can be demonstrated.

# VUC067 — Delivery and observed performance

## Instructor preparation

Plan 64 hours: 14 taught instruction and formative checks, 8 application labs, 24 supervised familiarization and stations, 10 integrated practice/independent assessment, 4 controlled written assessment/review, and 4 remediation planning and records. Additional practice and reassessment are scheduled as necessary; attendance alone never earns completion. Breaks are separate. Record actual hours and accommodations. The instructor must be competent and authorized for the selected tower-crane type, actual equipment, training tasks and applicable rules. Use separately qualified riggers, signal personnel and technical authorities for their roles. Do not treat a curriculum author as the site's equipment or engineering approver.

Entry diagnostic: interpret a simple chart, add weights with consistent units, distinguish radius from jib length, identify stop signals and explain an unsafe-act stop. Correct gaps before machinery operation. Teach with a known worked example, demonstrate safely, let the learner practice a changed task, then assess independently without hints. Explain the grading and critical failures before the attempt.

Provide a suitable inspected crane with its actual manual, current configuration/load chart and inspection records; approved training site and exclusion barriers; verified test loads, rated rigging and suitable ground supports; radios and official signal reference; weather monitoring; safe access and rescue plan; checklists; measuring tools for safe ground-course layout; simulator or tabletop packets for hazardous events; and a controlled assessor record. Validate foundation/support, configuration, operating envelope, weather criteria, load path, overhead hazards, public protection, supervision and emergency response before delivery. No unsafe condition may be introduced into a real crane for a test. Fault photographs, locked-out demonstration components or simulator scenarios replace live defects.

## Original T67 training information

The fictional chart in Q08 is for paper calculation only: gross capacity at 15/20/25/30 m is 8.0/6.5/5.0/4.0 t; intermediate radii use the next larger listed radius. Include all listed suspended components. No extrapolation beyond 30 m. Worked wind and plumb limits are also fictional assigned scenarios. Replace none of the actual crane instructions with these numbers. Each actual lift packet contains the real governing chart and conventions, configuration, load weight evidence, rigging and task-specific limits, validated by the responsible instructor before use.

### W01 — Authority and machine identity

Packet: simulator certificate, unfamiliar luffing crane, expired equipment familiarization record and conflicting signal channel. Worked decision: hold independent operation, verify required authorization/evaluation and undertake supervised familiarization. Changed: experienced operator but different control system. Learner produces a task/authority matrix and traces the control-to-drive-to-brake system on safe diagrams. Instructor asks which actions require additional qualifications and rejects a claim that this certificate alone permits operation.

### W02 — Site and erection interface

Packet: site drawing with adjacent excavation, public walkway under the proposed load route, nearby crane and one component scheduled for removal before its temporary support is shown. Worked: four unresolved hazards; foundation review, route protection, crane coordination and verified support sequence are required. Changed: walkway closed but excavation remains. Do not approve all conditions from one fix. Learner marks exclusion/access/communication arrangements and explains safe A/D handoff. No actual assembly or pin removal occurs in this lab.

### W03 — Climbing and inspection

Packet: fictional 35 m tower, assigned plumb limit 1: 500, measured displacement 78 mm, unverified post-climb record. Worked: limit 70 mm, exceedance 8 mm, and independent missing-release condition. Changed: displacement 65 mm but tie arrangement differs from approved plan. Still hold. Learner compares specified and observed configuration, then conducts the safe actual shift inspection under supervision. Simulated faults are presented in records/photos, not installed in the crane.

Learner locates the actual device-failure and parking procedures and explains why real overnight slewing arrangements cannot be copied from a test-course instruction. Actual device checks are only those authorized for normal safe testing.

### W07 — Special conditions and emergency response

Packet: unknown submerged load, proposed improvised personnel basket, broken signal radio and a tabletop power-line contact. Worked: no authorized lift from the first two proposals, stop for required communication loss, isolate exposure and activate the emergency plan for contact. Changed: power is supposedly off according to an unaffiliated observer. Maintain the energized assumption until the required confirmation/protection is established. Submit a response sequence and missing-information list; no live personnel, underwater or electrical exposure exercise is conducted.

### W08 — Handover and integrated planning

Packet: T67 total 4.6 t at 24 m, real-site record of unresolved brake drift, and an incomplete incoming-shift log. Worked: paper capacity margin 0.4 t, but the equipment hold remains and must be handed over. Changed: authorized repair/release is obtained, then radius becomes 29 m; the 4.0 t paper capacity fails by 0.6 t. Learner completes a lift plan and defect/shift record, distinguishes repair from release, and retrieves the actual securing instructions.

## P67 station requirements

Score each station on five dimensions: correct basis/identity, method/preparation, result/control, safety/limits, and reproducible record. Each receives 0 unsafe/absent, 1 safe but incomplete, or 2 complete and supported. Require at least 8/10, no zero, and no unresolved critical failure at every station. A high total cannot offset unsafe performance. Critical failures include unauthorized movement, capacity violation, bypass of a safety requirement, person under an uncontrolled suspended load, disregarded stop/lost required communication, fabrication, or unsupported equipment-release authority. Stop actual unsafe performance immediately. Numerical paper answers allow 0.01 stated unit; never round an insufficient capacity upward into acceptance.

## I67 independent practical: preparation through securing

The instructor prepares distinct practice set T and assessment sets A/B. Each assessment uses at least two independently verified, stable test loads and two different safe pickup/landing paths, within an explicitly validated operating envelope and with generous capacity margin. Document crane make/model/type/configuration, manual/chart revisions, measured route radii, loads including all components, rigging, ground supports, personnel roles, weather limits and exclusion plan. No candidate is required to discover an unknown load weight by lifting it. Establish actual safe task heights from the site plan; do not copy CCO course dimensions or timing rules.

Before assessment the technical/instructor team validates the actual course and test load plan. Use clearly marked landing rectangles with at least 0.5 m clearance around each actual test-load footprint and an obstacle-free route wide enough for the planned load and its controlled movement. Course markers are lightweight visual references, never people or rigid impact hazards. No marked obstacle is placed where avoiding it would demand an unsafe maneuver. Assessment is untimed for scoring; schedule about two hours plus safe familiarization and record elapsed time for coaching only.

Learner independently reviews records, conducts inspection, establishes actual capacity over the route, briefs the signaler, confirms exclusion and weather, and demonstrates unloaded control. Then perform two controlled load transfers to distinct landing points, including one planned direction/radius change and one return transfer. A blind portion may be used only with a validated protected site and qualified signaling; otherwise assess blind-lift decisions in simulation and record that limitation. No person stands under the load. The assessor can stop at any time; intervening to prevent an unsafe act is a failed independent attempt, not a successful coached completion.

For each actual transfer require: no capacity/configuration violation; load stays within the approved envelope and clear of obstacles; no side pulling, dragging, unintended ground contact or uncontrolled swing; correct response to every signal and stop; load placed fully inside the designated landing area without collision; stable support before rigging release; and continuous protection of people. All three transfers must meet these conditions. Safe voluntary pauses to restore control are permitted. The learner then secures the crane using its actual out-of-service instructions and completes a legible handover. Rate all five station dimensions for P18 and confirm every critical condition. Written plans without these observed transfers do not pass I67.

Changed set B uses different verified loads and route/landing locations, recalculated under the actual chart. It must not simply repeat memorized joystick movements. If equipment, wind or other conditions interrupt a test, record the interruption separately from learner error, restore safe conditions and restart the affected complete transfer after appropriate familiarization. A change of machine requires new identity/configuration/familiarization and a fresh full integrated attempt.

## Controlled final, remediation and credential

The private instructor directory holds Form A, changed Form B and separate keys. Each has 18 original cases with three scored decisions: 108 points total, pass at least 87 and at least 4/6 in every domain, no unresolved critical error. Allow 180 minutes, calculator and coursebook, but not keys or assistance. The format is VNDRLY's assessment, not CCO's 55-question timed examination. Use the protected practical truth/approved plans independently from learner submissions. Keep answer keys out of learner distribution.

On failure identify the exact knowledge or performance gap, reteach using the relevant worked example, require a changed formative application, and reassess with Form B or changed physical set B. Do not replace an unsafe physical attempt with a written explanation. Retain both attempts and their outcomes. Accommodations may change presentation or communication access without removing the required operational evidence.

Award the materials-based VNDRLY training credential only after all checks are corrected, all labs submitted, every station passed, I67 passed and the written final passed. Record the actual crane type/model, tasks and limitations. If actual performance is missing, issue only a knowledge-completion record clearly identifying that limitation, not the operating credential. Employer evaluation, external certification/licensure and site authorization remain separate. VNDRLY sets a one-year review reminder for this training record; it is not a declaration that external certification expires in one year. Earlier review follows equipment/task change, unsafe performance, incident or lost competence. Do not auto-extend a credential from attendance or an old score.

## Reusable records

**Enrollment/readiness:** learner, language/accommodations, prior training, diagnostic results, intended crane type, instructor qualifications, supervision and restrictions, dates/hours.

**Equipment/course validation:** site, crane ID/type/configuration, manual/chart revisions, support and inspection evidence, load IDs/verified masses, rigging IDs, route/radii, ground supports, device checks, weather criteria, exclusion/signals, access/rescue plan, validator/date.

**Lift calculation:** load/components and units, gross/net convention, chart cell/path worst radius, other limiting factors, result/margin, unresolved information, authorization and changed-condition review.

**Observation:** learner, assessor, set/station, actual actions and results, five dimension scores, critical condition, pause/intervention, evidence reference, pass/fail and reason, changed reassessment.

**Defect/handover:** time, crane/location, observed condition, current load/park state, restriction, person informed, repair action, required release evidence, incoming acknowledgment.

**Credential decision:** corrected formative/lab evidence, station/I67 results, final/retake score, critical-error closure, actual type/tasks, knowledge-only limitations if applicable, external authority exclusion, reviewer/date, next review trigger. Do not populate these forms with invented learner results.

# VUC067 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Does a written VNDRLY pass alone authorize independent tower operation?

**Answer:** No. Actual demonstrated competence and required employer evaluation/authorization remain separate.

**Reason/remediation:** A knowledge result cannot establish equipment-specific operating performance. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

An experienced operator cannot understand the assigned signal channel. What happens first?

**Answer:** Hold movement and establish an understood, tested signal system.

**Reason/remediation:** Experience does not remove the need for reliable communication. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

How does a typical hammerhead crane change radius differently from a luffing crane?

**Answer:** The hammerhead uses trolley position; a luffing crane changes jib angle.

**Reason/remediation:** Different geometry and controls require the correct equipment instructions. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

Does pressing emergency stop establish zero energy for maintenance?

**Answer:** No. Follow authorized isolation and verification for all relevant energy sources.

**Reason/remediation:** Gravity, electrical and other stored energy can remain. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

Yesterday the foundation was accepted; today adjacent excavation appears. Is the prior approval sufficient?

**Answer:** No. Hold and obtain appropriate review of the changed support condition.

**Reason/remediation:** The original basis may no longer describe the site. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

Does a functioning electrical supply prove proper grounding and protection?

**Answer:** No. Authorized installation/verification evidence is needed.

**Reason/remediation:** Normal movement is not a test of every protective function. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

A structural pin resists removal. Should the operator increase force to finish the sequence?

**Answer:** No. Stop and verify support/load transfer under the A/D director.

**Reason/remediation:** The pin may still carry load; forcing it can release unstable components. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L2

A crew member enters an out-of-view exposure area. What must precede relevant motion?

**Answer:** Communicate and establish that the worker is safely clear under the plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Lost visibility cannot be replaced with an assumption. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L1

A fictional 45 m tower uses an assigned 1: 500 displacement limit. Calculate it.

**Answer:** 90 mm.

**Reason/remediation:** 45, 000 mm divided by 500 equals 90 mm; actual acceptance uses the actual governing instruction. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L2

Can extra ballast be added as a general remedy for a configuration concern?

**Answer:** No. Use the specified ballast and engineering/manufacturer configuration.

**Reason/remediation:** Both excessive and insufficient ballast can be hazardous. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L1

Why does a signed inspection form not override a newly observed defect?

**Answer:** The actual condition governs; stop/report and follow defect disposition.

**Reason/remediation:** A record describes an inspection, not perpetual safety. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L2

Does a routine shift check replace a required post-assembly inspection?

**Answer:** No. Complete the applicable distinct inspection/release requirements.

**Reason/remediation:** Different inspections address different events and responsibilities. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L1

Why is a single broken-wire number not a complete rope acceptance rule?

**Answer:** Rope type, location, other damage and applicable criteria matter.

**Reason/remediation:** Distortion, heat damage or terminations can require action independent of a remembered count. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L2

Ideal four-part pull is 7.2 t but chart capacity is 5.8 t. Which of those two ceilings controls?

**Answer:** 5.8 t, with all other applicable constraints still checked.

**Reason/remediation:** A force multiplication cannot override the configuration chart. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L1

T67 at 21 m uses which chart cell and capacity?

**Answer:** 25 m and 5.0 t gross.

**Reason/remediation:** The fictional rule requires the next larger listed radius, not interpolation. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L2

Convert 2, 500 kg to metric tonnes.

**Answer:** 2.5 t.

**Reason/remediation:** One metric tonne is 1, 000 kg; retain consistent units. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L1

Why is dry shipping weight insufficient for a vessel that may retain fluid?

**Answer:** Actual contents and attachments must be accounted for.

**Reason/remediation:** Unknown additional weight invalidates the lift calculation. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L2

Ideal symmetric two-leg lift: W=6 t, angle 30 degrees above horizontal. Tension per leg?

**Answer:** 6 t per leg.

**Reason/remediation:** 6/(2×0.5)=6; shallow angles increase tension. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L1

Two signalers give conflicting commands. What should the operator do?

**Answer:** Stop safely and resolve the signal authority/meaning before moving.

**Reason/remediation:** Guessing can create an unexpected movement. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L2

Can an emergency stop from a person other than the designated signaler be ignored?

**Answer:** No. Respond with the appropriate safe stop.

**Reason/remediation:** Emergency stop authority is not limited to the normal motion signaler. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L1

Why not use the hoist to drag a snagged load sideways?

**Answer:** Side pull and sudden release can destabilize/control the load and equipment.

**Reason/remediation:** Reposition and resolve the obstruction under an approved plan. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L2

When may the rigging be slackened at landing?

**Answer:** When the load is supported and stable and the coordinated procedure permits it.

**Reason/remediation:** Premature slack can shift or drop the load. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L1

Can a safety-device failure be treated as permission to invent a workaround?

**Answer:** No. Follow the required stop/removal-from-service response.

**Reason/remediation:** Specified operational-aid alternatives are not general safety-device exceptions. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L2

Why must an anti-collision configuration match the installed crane?

**Answer:** Its predictions depend on actual geometry and settings.

**Reason/remediation:** A displayed safe condition is unreliable with the wrong model. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L1

Why is a ground average wind reading insufficient by itself?

**Answer:** Gusts, crane-height conditions and load sail area can govern control.

**Reason/remediation:** Use the actual monitoring and limits, not a favorable average alone. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L2

Should every tower crane be parked with slew locked?

**Answer:** No. Follow the actual out-of-service instructions; some require weather-vaning.

**Reason/remediation:** A universal lock instruction can create dangerous wind loading. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L1

An observer says a line is unused. May it be treated as de-energized?

**Answer:** No. Obtain required utility confirmation and protective arrangements.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance or informal opinion does not establish electrical safety. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L2

Does the line assessment include only the jib?

**Answer:** No. Include equipment, rigging and load throughout the planned envelope.

**Reason/remediation:** Long loads, movement and swing can reduce clearance. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L1

Why does a submerged object need more than its dry weight for a lift plan?

**Answer:** Suction, contents, buoyancy changes and snags can alter demands.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not discover breakout force by pulling experimentally. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L2

Does an ordinary cargo-lift pass authorize lifting people?

**Answer:** No. Personnel hoisting has separate equipment, planning and regulatory requirements.

**Reason/remediation:** This course uses planning cases rather than granting unrestricted special-lift authority. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L1

A brake fault disappears on restart. Is that a repair?

**Answer:** No. Intermittent failure still needs qualified resolution and required verification.

**Reason/remediation:** A temporary absence of symptoms is not proof of readiness. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L2

Should training create a live electrical contact or brake failure?

**Answer:** No. Use controlled simulation/tabletop scenarios.

**Reason/remediation:** Real faults can expose people and equipment to uncontrolled hazards. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q17-L1

Does a new shift erase an unresolved restriction?

**Answer:** No. Preserve and hand over the restriction and supporting records.

**Reason/remediation:** A change of personnel does not correct equipment condition. Revisit Q17 and perform its changed application.

## Q17-L2

What distinguishes repair evidence from release evidence?

**Answer:** Repair states what changed; release establishes required checks and authorized readiness.

**Reason/remediation:** A replaced part may still need inspection/function verification. Revisit Q17 and perform its changed application.

## Q18-L1

Can a passing capacity calculation override missing signaling or failed equipment?

**Answer:** No. All necessary conditions must be satisfied.

**Reason/remediation:** Lift readiness is a conjunction of requirements, not an averaged score. Revisit Q18 and perform its changed application.

## Q18-L2

A load path changes after approval. What must be reconsidered?

**Answer:** All materially affected capacities, envelope, exposures, signals and plan conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Approval of one route does not automatically apply to another. Revisit Q18 and perform its changed application.
