# VNDRLY/U Ultrasonic Thickness Measurement

VUC065. Original training and assessment materials. This course develops manual straight-beam thickness measurement, damage interpretation and defensible reporting. Completion is a VNDRLY training award, not API certification, employer NDT qualification, fitness-for-service approval or authorization to examine live equipment. The practical work requires real instruments and validated safe specimens. Written exercises alone cannot establish that competence.

Learners need basic UT knowledge, units and arithmetic. The instructor uses the diagnostic in the delivery guide to provide missing foundations before independent assessment. Learning objectives: establish a valid measurement system; distinguish material thickness from an intermediate reflector; find and record a defensible local minimum; characterize supported damage without overclaiming; preserve coverage, calibration and reporting evidence. Follow the chapter sequence, attempt each check before reading its explanation, complete the labs, then demonstrate the stations and independent examination.

## Q01 — Define the assignment before taking readings

A thickness value answers a local measurement question. It does not by itself answer whether a component is safe to operate. Establish component identity, material, accessible surface, thickness range, coordinate system, inspection area and required outputs. Identify the procedure, its revision and the person authorized to resolve exceptions. A prior inspection sketch is useful only after matching it to the actual component and orientation.

Separate measured remaining wall, nominal supplied wall, depth to an embedded response and a calculated required thickness. They are different quantities. If the assignment asks for a minimum in a region, a single convenient point is insufficient. If it asks for corrosion characterization, a number without waveform and spatial evidence may be insufficient. Record inaccessible areas rather than quietly shortening the assignment.

Worked: a four-region assignment has only three accessible regions. Report those examined and the fourth as unexamined; do not call the component fully surveyed. Changed: the fourth region is visible but a support blocks probe contact. Visibility does not establish ultrasonic coverage. P01 requires a signed scope and limitation sketch. A training certificate does not grant permission to remove a support or change an operating system.

## Q02 — Understand the time measurement

For an ideal normal pulse-echo path, thickness is material velocity multiplied by round-trip material transit time, divided by two. Instrument delay, probe delay and coating transit must not be mistaken for metal transit. The formula is a model, not proof that the selected echo is the backwall. State the velocity units and whether the displayed time is already corrected.

Using a velocity appropriate to another material creates a proportional error. A precise screen can display the wrong answer consistently. Check a known reference of appropriate material and thickness before relying on the setting. Do not adjust velocity until an unknown component matches the nominal drawing: that would force the desired result rather than measure it.

Worked: a fictional verified velocity of 6.0 mm/µs and corrected round-trip time of 4.0 µs yield 12.0 mm. Changed: the displayed time includes 0.4 µs of nonmaterial delay. Corrected time is 3.6 µs and thickness 10.8 mm; ignoring delay adds 1.2 mm. P02 requires a labeled time diagram and a calculation with units. Actual dual-element geometry needs the system's validated compensation rather than this simplified straight-path formula alone.

## Q03 — Select a probe for the actual problem

A single-element contact probe, a dual-element probe and a delay-line probe do not offer interchangeable performance. Inspect their identity, frequency, contact dimensions, cables and compatible instrument modes. Select against the material, smallest expected remaining wall, surface condition, curvature and need to resolve near-surface or intermediate responses. Manufacturer range claims are a starting point; demonstrate performance on relevant references.

A smaller footprint can improve contact on curvature, but it does not guarantee detection of a narrow pit. Resolution, beam interaction, coupling and the scan path also matter. A probe that reads a thick smooth block may fail on thin rough wall. Retain the failed condition and select an approved alternative rather than entering the nearest stable value.

Worked: the planned probe verifies 8–30 mm but the assignment can include 5 mm wall. Its demonstrated range does not cover the task. Changed: an alternate verifies 5 mm yet cannot maintain contact on the curved specimen. The alternate still needs an appropriate demonstrated arrangement. P03 compares actual approved probe types on known references and records both successful and limiting conditions.

## Q04 — Verify the complete measuring system

Inventory instrument model, serial, software, probe, cable, operating mode and reference blocks. An instrument service certificate does not verify today's probe, settings or coupling. Conversely, a good local reference check does not erase an overdue required instrument verification. Both obligations must be resolved under the governing practice.

Check connectors, cable condition, probe face and power before use. Save a training configuration separately from production settings. Build the setup from the approved technique, then independently confirm it. Do not assume a stored file is suitable because its name resembles the specimen. An unnoticed units setting can defeat otherwise correct measurements.

Worked: file T4 names probe D1, while the connected probe is D2. Reconcile the configuration and redo the applicable checks. Changed: D1 is restored but a damaged cable causes intermittent signal loss. Restoring identity did not restore reliable performance. P04 requires an observed inventory and fault response; never create a hazardous electrical fault to test the learner.

## Q05 — Establish zero and velocity across the range

Zero/delay correction and velocity influence measurements differently. Use known references spanning the assigned range, with suitable material and documented dimensions. Follow the instrument-specific calibration sequence. Check additional thicknesses after adjustment so one successful point does not conceal nonlinear or wrong-echo behavior. Preserve actual before/after results.

For dual-element work, the sound path between separate transmit and receive elements can produce a thickness-dependent effect. A single offset cannot always correct the entire range. Use the instrument's approved compensation and demonstrate its adequacy across the range. If it cannot meet the exercise's stated tolerance, select another qualified setup or report the limitation.

Worked: references 5, 15 and 30 mm read 5.0, 15.1 and 30.8 mm. Under fictional ±0.2 mm reference criteria the third fails; the first two do not validate the range. Changed: adjustment fixes 30 but makes 5 read 5.5. The range remains invalid. P05 requires actual multi-point verification, not a calculation-only answer or a promise to calibrate later.

## Q06 — Read the waveform rather than trusting a digit

Use the A-scan to identify the signal selected for measurement. Check time base, gates, gain, blanking, detection edge and the expected sequence of echoes. A gate can lock onto noise, an interface, a repeated backwall or another reflector. A stable number is not evidence of correct identification. Compare the waveform while moving and reapplying the probe.

The applicable instrument procedure determines whether leading edge, peak or another timing feature is used. Do not change that feature without checking the reference again. Increasing gain may reveal a weak response but may also change triggering or amplify noise. Reducing gain to hide an inconvenient early response is not legitimate interpretation.

Worked: an early response produces 7 mm and a later one 14 mm. Neither is automatically the wall; investigate echo order and spatial behavior on a suitable known reference. Changed: the later response disappears while the early one persists across a bounded region. Record the evidence and investigate an intermediate reflector; do not double the early value by habit. P06 includes actual examples of wrong gate and wrong echo selection on safe teaching specimens.

## Q07 — Obtain reliable contact without altering the component

Inspect the surface and obtain authorization for preparation. Loose material, roughness and poor contact can prevent reliable transmission. Preparation must not remove unknown wall thickness, damage a coating or release hazardous contamination. On production equipment, access and surface work remain governed by the owner's rules. Training uses cool clean specimens with no hazardous residue.

Apply compatible couplant and establish repeatable contact. Reapply and compare the waveform rather than recording a transient low digit. Keep the probe aligned with the local surface. A coupling failure can look like signal loss from damage, so compare neighboring known regions and reference response before interpreting it. Record areas where contact could not be established.

Worked: three readings at one location are 9.8, 9.9 and a momentary 2.1 mm during loss of contact. Do not select 2.1 merely because the task asks for a minimum; verify a real echo. Changed: 2.1 repeats with a supported waveform after valid recoupling. Now investigate it as potentially real, within the setup's demonstrated range. P07 requires both recovery from poor coupling and preservation of a genuine low result.

## Q08 — Handle coatings with a demonstrated technique

Sound passing through a coating contributes time. If the system treats that time as metal, the displayed thickness can be too large. Do not simply subtract the physical paint thickness from an apparent metal reading. The velocities and timing mode matter. An authorized coating-removal approach or a validated coating-compensating measurement can resolve the issue.

Echo-to-echo timing measures the interval between appropriate successive metal backwall responses. It needs those responses to exist and be correctly identified. Severe roughness or attenuation may prevent that. Proprietary coating modes require the compatible probe, instrument setup and demonstrated operating conditions; they are not generic capabilities of every gauge.

Worked: in an ideal illustrative model, 0.3 mm coating with velocity 2.0 mm/µs is interpreted at metal velocity 6.0 mm/µs. Its apparent contribution is 0.9 mm, not 0.3 mm. Changed: the second metal backwall response is not resolvable. Do not claim echo-to-echo compensation worked. P08 requires actual coated and uncoated reference comparisons and an honest unresolved result when the selected method cannot support a value.

## Q09 — Establish reproducible coordinates and coverage

Mark an authorized origin and direction and identify each reporting region. Record how the sketch relates to physical landmarks. A grid labels where work belongs; it does not prove the spaces between isolated points were scanned. Plan coverage appropriate to the region and the demonstrated detection performance of the probe and technique.

A minimum can lie between convenient grid centers. Move systematically, overlap as required by the validated procedure and preserve interruptions. The technical authority establishes scan speed and track spacing by demonstrating the relevant target response, not by selecting a round number that is easy to remember. Record the actual minimum location and its supporting signal.

Worked: a teaching region spans 0–100 mm, with prescribed tracks every 10 mm including both boundaries. There are eleven tracks. Changed: the validated plan requires 5 mm spacing: twenty-one tracks. These arithmetic counts do not establish that either spacing is adequate. P09 requires actual planned coverage and a changed origin; a reverse sketch must still locate the same physical region correctly.

## Q10 — Find a minimum that is both real and local

Scan the assigned region, investigate low responses, and repeat the valid measurement at the location of the supported minimum. Preserve the waveform or equivalent evidence and location. Do not average away a localized thin area when the task requires the minimum. Do not substitute a nominal wall value when no reliable reading is obtained.

Distinguish the lowest displayed number from the lowest valid measured wall. An echo outside the calibrated range, an unstable coupling artifact or an intermediate interface is not automatically remaining wall. If uncertainty or access prevents a defensible minimum, state that limitation. A conservative-sounding fabricated value is still fabricated.

Worked: supported local measurements are 8.3, 7.9 and 8.1 mm. Minimum measured is 7.9 mm; mean 8.1 mm is not the requested output. Changed: further valid coverage establishes 7.4 mm. Update the minimum and location while retaining the prior record. P10 requires a real mapped search on a specimen whose minimum is independently established, with a changed specimen for reassessment.

## Q11 — Interpret wall-loss patterns without inventing a cause

Map the distribution and supporting responses before assigning a description. Broad thinning, localized loss and discrete pits have different spatial patterns. The same thickness number can occur in different damage conditions. A narrow pit may interact with only part of the beam; a strong response from surrounding wall can mask the deepest local loss.

Record what the method supports. A UT pattern can support a wall-loss description without proving the chemical mechanism, process cause or remaining life. Use permitted surface observations and inspection history as corroboration, preserving disagreement. An unfamiliar or mixed pattern should be escalated rather than forced into a convenient category.

Worked: one region contains broad 9–10 mm wall with a reproducible local 6 mm feature. Reporting only broad thinning misses the localized feature. Changed: the 6 mm feature is confined to one track and cannot be reproduced with a suitable alternate setup. Resolve the signal and coverage uncertainty before treating it as established pit depth. P11 uses known teaching damage patterns and records descriptive evidence separately from inferred mechanism.

## Q12 — Distinguish laminations and inclusions from remaining wall

An internal reflector can return sound before the true back surface. If the instrument locks onto it, the display represents depth to that reflector under the assumed path, not necessarily total wall. Map its extent and compare surrounding sound material and any supported backwall response. Do not force an early reflector to be corrosion merely because the assignment is a thickness survey.

Large planar internal features can obscure the backwall. Smaller dispersed responses can coexist with it. Those patterns guide investigation but do not uniquely identify composition or manufacturing origin. Record observed depths, extent, waveform behavior, supported interpretation and unresolved wall thickness. A missing backwall is not itself proof of zero remaining metal.

Worked: an intermediate response at 6 mm extends across a region while neighboring validated wall is 18 mm. Report the intermediate reflector and unresolved total wall where the backwall is hidden, not 6 mm remaining wall by default. Changed: a valid backwall at 18 mm is separately established. Preserve both quantities with distinct labels. P12 requires actual known internal-reflector specimens and a blind changed interpretation.

## Q13 — Assess overlay interfaces and external loss

Layered construction introduces interfaces that may reflect sound. Determine whether the reporting question concerns substrate wall, overlay thickness, total section or interface condition. A response at the expected interface may be normal or may indicate a discontinuity; compare the validated method and reference behavior. Do not convert an interface echo automatically into a disbond declaration.

External loss also changes the local entry surface and reference geometry. A mechanical depth measurement uses a stated datum and cannot be combined with an ultrasonic value indiscriminately. Record which surface each measurement starts from. If UT already measures local remaining wall at a depression, subtracting the depression again can double-count loss.

Worked: local UT remaining wall is 10 mm, and an external depression is 2 mm below a surrounding datum. Reporting remaining wall as 8 mm without a justified geometry model double-counts the depression. Changed: UT was taken on the surrounding surface, not the depression. The local remaining wall at the depression still needs a defensible measurement or approved method. P13 requires a labeled section sketch, actual datum checks and separate observed versus inferred results.

## Q14 — Manage curvature, fittings and temperature

Curved surfaces, bends and changing section geometry make probe alignment and echo interpretation harder. Establish local contact and the expected surface relationship. Demonstrate the selected setup on representative geometry. A flat-block check is necessary for its purpose but may not establish performance on a tight curve or taper.

Temperature can change material sound velocity and probe delay. Equipment temperature ratings and contact-time restrictions must be respected. This course uses ambient specimens; a discussion of hot measurement does not qualify the learner to perform it. An actual hot-service assignment needs an approved procedure, appropriate references, equipment and access controls.

Worked: a setup passes on a flat plate but loses a stable backwall at the elbow intrados. Do not transfer the flat result or treat the loss as a hole. Changed: an approved smaller contact arrangement resolves the waveform and passes representative checks. Document the changed system and scope before measuring. P14 observes safe curved-surface work and uses a temperature-change scenario without exposing learners to hot equipment.

## Q15 — Quantify limits without pretending precision is certainty

Display resolution, repeatability and accuracy differ. A hundredth-millimeter digit does not establish hundredth-millimeter accuracy. Consider reference uncertainty, setup behavior, surface condition, waveform selection and location repeatability. Use the exercise's predeclared decision rule and preserve raw values before rounding.

If a result is near a limit, report the measured value and uncertainty information required by the authority. Do not move the decimal or round favorably to obtain a pass. A training tolerance describes performance against protected truth; it is not a production acceptance threshold. A procedure without a usable decision rule needs clarification before a boundary decision.

Worked: measured 7.85 mm versus reference truth 8.00 mm gives signed error −0.15 mm. Under fictional ±0.20 mm tolerance with suitable established truth uncertainty and the announced rule, this lies inside the band. Changed: measured 7.79 gives −0.21 and is outside; rounding to one decimal must not rescue it. P15 requires signed errors, units and a distinction between measurement performance and component acceptability.

## Q16 — Detect drift and recover affected work

Perform the checks at the intervals and events required by the procedure. Changing a probe, cable, mode or temperature condition can invalidate prior setup assumptions. Record actual check results and times. When a check fails, stop relying on the system and identify the potentially affected interval since the last valid verification.

Correct the cause, reverify and determine the necessary repeat work under the responsible authority. A later passing check does not prove earlier measurements were valid. Preserve the failed check, affected component/region IDs, disposition and repeat evidence. Do not overwrite the failure with the corrected number or quietly repeat only the most recent point.

Worked: valid check 08:00, failed check 10:00, readings 08:10–09:55. All potentially affected readings since 08:00 need assessment, not just those at 09:55. Changed: the operator corrects at 10:15 and repeats one of four regions. The remaining affected regions are unresolved. P16 uses a controlled training discrepancy; it does not require damaging equipment or altering production data.

## Q17 — Preserve units and measurement meaning in the report

Record component/region, coordinate, actual thickness or reflector depth, units, method, configuration, check reference, condition description and limitations. Distinguish unavailable, not examined and not applicable from zero. A blank can be mistaken for an omission; state the reason explicitly. Preserve actual measured precision while formatting according to the authorized report convention.

Convert units with a declared factor and retain the original. For this course, one inch equals 25.4 mm. Do not label a millimeter value as inches or confuse decimal places with significant figures. Inspect the exported report because spreadsheet formatting, hidden rows and truncated limitations can alter its meaning.

Worked: 0.400 inch converts to 10.16 mm. Changed: a report prints 10.16 under an inch heading. That is a serious unit error, not a harmless label issue. Correct it against source evidence and issue a traceable revision. P17 requires actual entry and inspection of an exported training report, including unresolved regions and separate internal-reflector depths.

## Q18 — Use trends only when measurements are comparable

Two thickness values support a change calculation only after confirming the same physical location, comparable method and valid measurement conditions. A difference can reflect relocation, a changed coating mode or setup error rather than metal loss. Keep those possibilities visible. Do not extrapolate remaining life from a convenient pair of numbers without the authorized engineering basis.

A calculated rate is descriptive under its assumptions. Negative apparent loss requires investigation rather than automatic treatment as growth of metal. Large differences demand timely communication according to the inspection plan, but the examiner should not invent operating decisions. Preserve evidence needed by the authorized reviewer.

Worked: comparable validated values 12.0 and 11.4 mm separated by three years imply 0.6 mm change and 0.2 mm/year average loss. Changed: the second reading is from an adjacent region. The arithmetic remains possible, but the trend claim is unsupported. P18 asks the learner to identify comparability conditions, calculate only justified differences and hand off uncertainties without inventing a shutdown or service-life conclusion.

## Q19 — Respond to anomalies and maintain examination integrity

Follow the assignment's communication path for a credible severe low reading, unexpected damage or inability to complete coverage. Give the actual location, method, evidence, uncertainty and extent examined. Request the authorized next step. Do not reassure an operator that a component is safe because most points look normal, or declare a failure mechanism beyond the evidence.

Independent assessment requires genuine measurements. Copying a missing value, uncovering protected specimen truth or altering a setup record after seeing the answer destroys the evidential basis. Honest noncompletion is preferable to fabricated completion. Keep original attempts for review and use changed specimens after teaching.

Worked: one validated region is substantially thinner than its neighbors. Preserve the data and notify the designated authority under the exercise communication plan. Changed: the assessor asks why a missing cell suddenly has a value with no scan record. The learner must identify and correct the unsupported entry; it cannot count as demonstrated work. P19 uses a simulated escalation only, with no external messages sent.

## Q20 — Complete an independent examination and handover

Bring the whole task together: identity, setup, verification, systematic coverage, supported minima, damage descriptions, internal-reflector distinctions and a usable final report. Evaluate these independently. A high numerical score cannot compensate for fabricated data, unsafe behavior or a whole region left unexamined. An instrument screenshot alone does not establish setup competence or coverage.

The assessor uses protected validated specimens unfamiliar to the learner. The learner performs without coaching; the assessor records actual actions and results. After grading, teach the underlying failure and reassess with different geometry or hidden features. Memorizing disclosed values is not a demonstration of transferable competence.

Worked: 22 of 24 readings meet tolerance, but two regions were never scanned and were filled with nominal wall. The numerical fraction does not establish completion; the entries are invalid and the integrity condition fails. Changed: all regions are genuinely examined, but a legitimate internal reflector prevents total-wall measurement in a designated ambiguity station. Honest limitation can pass that interpretation station; final thickness-performance targets must be resolvable by the validated setup. I65 separates those tasks so learners are never rewarded for inventing an answer.

# VUC065 delivery, application and performance assessment

## Instructor preparation

## S65 original training procedure and specimen specification

Prepare known teaching set T and independent protected sets A/B. Each final set has six specimens with four defined examination regions each: 24 regions total. Include flat plate, curved pipe and fitting geometry; coated and uncoated conditions; broad loss, localized loss and pitting; and known sound regions. Include exactly eight predesignated sound regions for the false-call denominator. At least eight other regions have significant hidden local loss, designated before assessment as critical to detect. Thickness targets span the actual approved setup range, selected approximately 5–40 mm for the ambient training bench. These are VNDRLY choices, not external exam replication.

Separately provide three interpretation specimens: a known internal planar reflector, a dispersed/internal feature example and an overlay/interface example. The authority establishes the actual truth and supported interpretive scope; do not invent defect metallurgy from unvalidated echoes. These interpretation tasks are separate from the 24 resolvable remaining-wall targets so an obscured backwall is not graded as a demand to fabricate thickness.

Before use, document material, geometry, dimensions, coating, independent truth method, uncertainty, probe suitability and detectability. Use reference documentation or adequate independent qualified characterization. The author has not procured or validated physical specimens. If truth or method cannot support the rubric, replace or validate the specimen before assessment, not after seeing a learner's score. Mask hidden features and keep truth private without concealing needed safe-handling information.

The authority supplies actual instrument button instructions from its manual and validates a technique sheet: identity/range; probe/mode; reference dimensions; zero/velocity and compensation; screen/gates/timing feature; coupling; scan tracks/speed demonstrated on relevant targets; coating method; minimum search/reconfirmation; verification interval/events; damage descriptions; reporting and recovery. No generic velocity or gate setting is imposed on every instrument.

Original S65 criteria: multi-point reference error within ±0.20 mm across the assigned range; check before and after each specimen and after any relevant system change; no failed check unresolved. Record a supported minimum and location for all 24 regions. At least 22/24 thickness results must have absolute error ≤0.30 mm, and every one of the eight designated critical low-wall regions must be detected and measured within that band. At least 22/24 descriptive region classifications must agree with protected validated truth; no more than one false damage call in the designated sound regions. All three separate interpretation tasks must distinguish reflector depth from total wall and correctly state limitations. These are training performance rules, not production acceptance limits or API scoring.

Truth uncertainty must be documented and no greater than 0.05 mm for the thickness targets; use a conservative training decision rule: absolute measured error plus declared truth uncertainty must be ≤0.30 mm to pass that target. For reference checks, use corresponding reference uncertainty and require absolute error plus uncertainty ≤0.20 mm. If those rules cannot be supported, do not use the specimen for scoring. Report actual errors before rounding. All required coverage, records and safety/integrity conditions must pass independently of the numerical threshold.

## Source register and limits

T01 — [API current QUTE-TM program](https://www.api.org/products-and-services/individual-certification-programs/certifications/apiqute-tm). Confirms manual thickness/damage performance context, separate external qualification and links to examination documents. The webpage states an eight-hour exam day, while its linked orientation describes six hours. Neither schedule is silently adopted as a VNDRLY credential condition.

T02 — [Currently linked API-UT-21](https://apidam.widen.net/content/54volvmqgt/pdf/=c&quality=80&x.app=portals), revision 0, March 2019. Supports the scope of straight-beam manual measurement and characterization. Document issues include a duplicate category letter, an incomplete block sentence, and wording that confuses decimal formatting with significant digits. This package uses descriptive condition names, explicit units and independent training rules rather than propagating those defects. It is not a production procedure or a substitute for an approved employer technique.

T03 — [Currently linked candidate orientation](https://apidam.widen.net/content/zrtou1utkq/pdf/=c&quality=80&x.app=portals). Supports observed independent performance, specimen security and separation of thickness and damage-description outcomes. It refers to API-UT-30 while the current webpage links API-UT-21. The external candidate must obtain controlling clarification; this course does not invent a reconciliation. Its external grading details are not represented as the S65 rubric.

T04 — [Evident: factors affecting thickness measurement](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/thickness-gauge/factors). Conceptual support for geometry, material, contact and calibration limitations. Instrument/manufacturer instructions govern actual operation. The course's specific cases and numerical errors are original, not manufacturer performance guarantees.

T05 — [Evident: measuring through paint](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/applications/measuring-metal-thickness-paint). Supports distinguishing coating transit from metal transit and the need for suitable echoes or a compatible specialized mode. The ideal velocity-ratio exercise is a teaching model, not a universal field correction. No proprietary coating algorithm or button sequence is reproduced.

T06 — [Evident: dual-element setup](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/flaw-detection/dual-element-setup). Supports checking timing feature, system configuration and thickness-dependent behavior of dual-element paths. Actual equipment and references must demonstrate suitability. No generalized gain setting is imposed as a universal rule.

The training award states completion of the VNDRLY assessments only. Production decisions, employer qualification, external examination eligibility and specialist authorization remain separate.
