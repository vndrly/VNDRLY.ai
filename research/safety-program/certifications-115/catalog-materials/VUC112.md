# VUC112 — Refrigerant Handling and Section 608 Preparation

Original VNDRLY/U core, TypeI, TypeII andTypeIII training. EPA certification requires an EPA-approved test; no EPA credential is issued here.

## Q112-01 — Certification, law and scope

Section 608 technician certification is earned through an EPA-approved testing organization, not by completing an unaffiliated course. TypeI covers small appliances; TypeII covers high/very-high-pressure appliances except small appliances and MVACs; TypeIII covers low-pressure appliances; Universal requires all types with the required core examination. Section 609 motor-vehicle air-conditioning requirements are a separate path. Identify the appliance and task before selecting the credential. This original VNDRLY course prepares learners and assesses safe practice; it does not issue EPA certification.

The venting prohibition and recovery obligations are not limited to ozone-depleting refrigerants; determine the actual refrigerant and applicable exemptions rather than assuming an HFC is freely ventable. De minimis releases associated with good-faith recovery are not permission for intentional discharge. Do not use a recovery machine as a reason to ignore leaks, cylinder limits or safety. Apprentice work must follow applicable supervision requirements; a training record alone does not authorize independent service.

Worked decision: a technician has TypeI and is asked to open a large high-pressure chiller. The small-appliance credential is not enough. Arrange appropriately certified and authorized personnel. Preserve certification records and verify the certifying organization. EPA credentials do not replace employer safety training, equipment-specific competence or state/local licensing.

## Q112-02 — Environmental effects and refrigerant identity

CFCs and HCFCs can deplete stratospheric ozone; many refrigerants also contribute to climate warming. Ozone-depletion potential and global-warming potential describe different effects. A refrigerant with no ozone-depletion potential can still have significant climate impact or other hazards. Regulatory phaseouts, phasedowns and equipment transition rules are distinct from whether an existing appliance may be safely serviced.

Read the appliance label and service history, then verify refrigerant identity when uncertain using an appropriate method. Blends can behave differently from single-component fluids; temperature glide and fractionation affect pressure-temperature interpretation and charging method. Follow the manufacturer and refrigerant instructions, including liquid charging where specified to preserve blend composition. Never mix unknown refrigerants into a clean recovery cylinder.

Recovery removes refrigerant and stores it externally; recycling cleans recovered refrigerant for appropriate reuse; reclamation processes it to the applicable purity specification through an authorized reclaimer. These are not synonyms. Worked case: recovered fluid from an unlabeled system is offered for another customer's appliance. Quarantine and identify it; do not treat filtering as proof of reclaimed purity or legal resale. Keep source, mass, container and disposition traceable.

## Q112-03 — Refrigeration cycle and pressure interpretation

A basic vapor-compression cycle moves heat using evaporation at lower pressure, compression, condensation at higher pressure and expansion. Identify compressor, suction/discharge, condenser, receiver where present, metering device and evaporator before connecting equipment. Refrigerant can remain dissolved in oil or trapped behind valves; an apparently empty segment may release more vapor after warming or equalization.

Gauge pressure is relative to local atmosphere; absolute pressure is referenced to vacuum. At a supplied atmosphere 14.7 psia, a reading 0 psig is 14.7 psia, not empty space. Vacuum readings in inchesHg relative to atmosphere differ from mmHg absolute and microns.25 mmHg absolute equals 25000 microns; it is a recovery endpoint for the specified low-pressure rule, not a universal dehydration target.

Use the correct refrigerant pressure-temperature data and distinguish saturated conditions from superheated vapor or subcooled liquid. Example with supplied saturation 40°F and suction-line 55°F gives 15°F superheat; supplied condensing 110°F and liquid 95°F gives 15°F subcooling. These calculations do not diagnose charge alone; airflow, load, metering and measurement placement matter. Never use a generic pressure as proof of identity or safe opening.

## Q112-04 — Hazards, tools and cylinders

Refrigerants can displace oxygen, cause frostbite or pressure injury, and some are flammable or toxic. Heating or contact with flame can create dangerous decomposition products. Use ventilation, PPE, compatible equipment and ignition controls according to the refrigerant and task. Do not use oxygen or compressed air to pressure-test a refrigerant circuit. Dry nitrogen testing, when authorized, requires a regulator, relief protection and pressure limits from the manufacturer/procedure.

Use recovery equipment suitable for the refrigerant and certified where required. Inspect hoses, low-loss fittings, scales, gauges and cylinders. A disposable supply cylinder must not be refilled as a recovery vessel. Verify approved refillable cylinder identity, condition, test status, pressure rating, compatibility and allowable fill by the actual refrigerant/temperature/cylinder rules. A familiar 80% volume concept is not permission to guess mass from total cylinder weight.

Worked scale card: cylinder tare 18 lb and approved maximum gross 48 lb. Current gross 42 lb leaves 6 lb permitted mass capacity under that card. A planned 8 lb recovery needs another suitable cylinder or approved transfer plan before starting. Do not bypass overfill controls or heat a cylinder with a flame. Secure and label containers, protect valves, and follow transport requirements. Unknown contents remain segregated.

## Q112-05 — Recovery setup and verification

Plan recovery from appliance identity, charge, pressure class, equipment date/capability, service scope and required endpoint. Isolate only as allowed; establish a path that captures trapped refrigerant without creating a hydraulic or electrical hazard. Confirm recovery-machine, cylinder and hose compatibility and purge/manage connections according to approved instructions without unnecessary venting. Monitor mass, pressure and temperature throughout.

Liquid recovery may reduce time when equipment and appliance configuration permit it; vapor recovery completes remaining removal. Do not pull liquid into equipment not designed for it or run an appliance compressor under conditions that damage it. System-dependent equipment has limitations and relies on appliance pressure or compressor operation; it is not interchangeable with self-contained recovery. Follow the actual equipment instructions.

After reaching the applicable endpoint, allow the specified stabilization/check and investigate pressure rebound. Refrigerant dissolved in oil, trapped liquid or incomplete isolation can raise pressure again. A recovery endpoint does not prove a leak-free dry system ready for charging. Worked case: the gauge reaches the target while the cylinder approaches its approved maximum; stop at the cylinder limit, change safely and complete recovery. Neither the desired schedule nor a momentary gauge value waives either requirement.

## Q112-06 — Type I small appliances

A small appliance is not merely any device with a small-looking compressor. EPA's definition includes factory manufacture, charge and hermetic sealing with five pounds or less refrigerant. Confirm the actual definition and service/disposal task. For recovery equipment made before November 15, 1993, the small-appliance requirement is 80% recovery. For equipment made on/after that date, it is 90% with a functioning appliance compressor or 80% without one; an alternative is evacuation to 4 inchesHg vacuum under the rule.

Use actual equipment instructions to establish access and recovery from both sides when needed. A nonfunctioning compressor may trap refrigerant; pressure on one side does not prove the other is empty. Avoid permanent reliance on temporary piercing access devices where the service procedure requires proper repair. Do not energize an unsafe compressor just to use a different recovery percentage.

Worked arithmetic with a known 4 lb training charge: 90% is 3.6 lb; 80% is 3.2 lb. A mass estimate alone may be unreliable if original charge is unknown or refrigerant remains in oil; follow certified-equipment practice and permitted endpoint. Record method, equipment date, compressor condition and result. Disposal arrangements require the appropriate recovery/documentation responsibilities; a sticker claiming empty is not a substitute for the required evidence.

## Q112-07 — Type II pressure categories and service endpoints

Determine pressure category from the regulatory definition and refrigerant/application, not a momentary gauge reading. The required evacuation table distinguishes recovery-equipment manufacture/import date and appliance charge. For equipment on/after November 15, 1993: very-high-pressure appliances require 0 inchesHg vacuum; high-pressure below 200 lb require 0, high-pressure 200 lb or more 10; medium-pressure below 200 lb 10, medium-pressure 200 lb or more 15. For earlier equipment: very-high and high below 200 require 0; high 200 or more 4; medium below/at-or-above 200 require 4. Values are inchesHg vacuum relative to the stated standard atmosphere; do not confuse them with absolute pressure.

The appliance full-charge category is not the amount recovered today. A300 lb appliance temporarily containing 50 lb does not become a below 200 lb category. An isolated component uses the rule's applicable treatment; verify exact conditions rather than inventing an exemption. Special nonmajor-repair or leaking-appliance provisions have defined conditions and cannot be invoked for convenience; consult the current rule and document applicability before using them.

Worked selection: a qualifying medium-pressure 150 lb appliance using modern recovery equipment requires 10 inchesHg vacuum under the normal table. A high-pressure 250 lb appliance using the same equipment also requires 10. A medium-pressure 250 lb appliance requires 15. Complete stabilization and safe isolation before opening. Service dehydration and leak testing follow the appliance procedure after repair; meeting recovery law is not the whole commissioning process.

## Q112-08 — Type III low-pressure appliances

Low-pressure systems can operate below atmospheric pressure, so leaks may admit air and moisture rather than visibly release refrigerant. Noncondensables can increase condensing pressure and reduce performance; moisture can cause corrosion or other damage. Purge systems must be operated/maintained to minimize refrigerant loss under the applicable requirements. Excessive purge activity is evidence to investigate, not a normal condition to ignore.

The normal low-pressure recovery endpoint is 25 mmHg absolute for both older and newer recovery equipment. Use an appropriate absolute-pressure instrument and understand its units. Do not read 25 inchesHg vacuum as the same endpoint. Follow chiller manufacturer procedures for water circulation, freeze prevention, pressure limits and refrigerant removal. Recovery can cool water and risk freezing tubes; arbitrary heating or pressurization is unsafe.

Worked case: a chiller reaches 25 mmHg absolute then rebounds. Investigate residual refrigerant/trapped regions and procedure-required stabilization rather than immediately opening. A pressure test must respect the actual appliance rating and approved method; never apply a memorized high-pressure-system test value to a low-pressure shell. Separate recovery, leak repair, evacuation/dehydration and charging steps. Confirm safe operating condition and records before return to service.

## Q112-09 — Leak repair and the 2026 HFC rules

Keep legal regimes distinct. Long-standing Section 608 leak-repair requirements concern specified appliances with 50 lb or more ozone-depleting refrigerant. The AIM Act emissions-reduction/reclamation rules add requirements beginning 2026 for certain appliances with 15 lb or more containing HFCs or specified substitutes. Refrigerant composition, GWP, appliance category and exclusions matter. Do not assume the old 50 lb threshold is the only rule or that a proposed exemption is already effective.

Leak-rate calculations require full charge, amount added and the applicable calculation method. For a simple annualizing exercise, rate=(added/full charge)×(365/days since relevant addition)×100. With 10 lb added to 100 lb over 90 days, the result is 40.56%/year. This is not the same as saying 40.56 lb physically leaked that day. The actual rule controls first-use periods, exclusions, method consistency, thresholds and records.

When an applicable threshold is exceeded, follow the current rule's repair, verification, inspection, retrofit/retirement and reporting requirements. Do not merely top off and erase the event. Record discovery, full-charge basis, calculation, repair and verification. The owner/operator has duties that cannot be assumed fulfilled by the technician's invoice. Worked decision: a 20 lb HFC appliance is dismissed as below 50 lb. Reassess under the current AIM applicability rules; being outside one regime does not establish exemption from another.

For the reviewed EPA summaries, trigger rates are 30% for industrial process refrigeration, 20% for commercial refrigeration and 10% for comfort cooling/other covered categories. Normal repair timing is 30 days, with 120 days where the specified industrial shutdown condition applies; initial and follow-up verification are required. Under the 2026 AIM summary, residential/light-commercial AC and heat-pump subsector equipment and qualifying substitutes atGWP53 or below are excluded from that leak-repair provision; this does not erase venting/recovery duties. Extensions and retrofit/retirement routes require their actual conditions and records, not a verbal promise to repair later. Covered commercial/IPR appliances at 500 lb or more have quarterly post-trigger inspections; smaller covered commercial/IPR and covered comfort/other equipment have annual inspections under the stated rule conditions. Verify current applicability and reporting requirements before acting.

## Q112-10 — Reclamation, disposal, records and final service decision

Maintain a refrigerant chain of custody: appliance/source, refrigerant identity, mass, cylinder ID, recovery date, technician, equipment and destination. Separate clean known refrigerant from contaminated or mixed material. Determine when reuse is permitted and when reclamation by an EPA-certified reclaimer is required; do not sell mixed recovered material as clean product. Record disposal/recovery responsibilities for the appliance category and retain required documentation.

Service closeout includes safe electrical/mechanical state, leak testing and repair evidence, manufacturer-required evacuation/charging, final operating checks and remaining restrictions. Never charge by guesswork or substitute a refrigerant without an approved retrofit basis and legal compatibility. For blends, use the specified charging method and correct composition. Document additions/removals accurately; scale tare and units matter.

A training completion record lists core and type-specific learning/assessment. EPA certification still requires the approved examination and certifying body; this course cannot print an EPA credential. Existing certification does not remove the need to learn changed refrigerants, flammability controls or regulations. Review current EPA pages and actual rules before delivery and before a compliance decision. Authoring completion means these original materials are prepared, not that a learner or business has satisfied legal certification or reporting duties.

# VUC112 practical workbook and instructor guide

## Delivery

Original 40-hour course: 12 core instruction, 6 TypeI, 8 TypeII, 6 TypeIII, 4 integrated practice, 2 controlled final, 2 feedback/remediation. Practical work is embedded in type sessions; extend until safe independent performance. Entry: basic units/arithmetic, ability to read labels/procedures and follow stop instructions. Instructor must hold appropriate EPA certification and equipment/refrigerant competence; actual refrigerant work uses authorized facilities and supervision. Classroom rigs may use inert/nonrelease simulation, but actual learner practical scope must be recorded honestly.

Provide approved compatible recovery machine, refillable cylinders, calibrated scale, gauges/absolute vacuum instrument, low-loss fittings, known training appliance circuits, manufacturer procedures, ventilation/PPE, labels, SDS and current EPA reference pages. Flammable-refrigerant practice requires explicitly suitable equipment/facility/controls; never use an ordinary rig by assumption. No intentional refrigerant venting, no oxygen/air pressure testing, no live electrical bypass. Inert models may demonstrate connection logic before supervised real recovery. Missing equipment defers the practical endorsement.

Before each station, instructor verifies refrigerant identity, appliance class/full charge, equipment date/rating, maximum cylinder mass, endpoint and safe work limits. Learner checks independently. Actual manufacturer values govern physical work; numerical cards below are synthetic. Current regulation controls any exception or compliance decision.

### W112-01 — Core identity and pressure

Cards: factory-sealed 4 lb refrigerator; large 250 lb high-pressure appliance; low-pressure chiller; MVAC; unknown blend cylinder. Learner selects credential path, identifies unknown hold and distinguishes 608/609. Calculate at 14.7 psia atmosphere: 0 psig=14.7 psia;-5 psig=9.7 psia. Supplied saturation 40°F, line 55°F gives 15°F superheat; condensing 110°F, liquid 95°F gives 15°F subcooling. Explain why these do not alone determine charge or identity. Changed values 42/60°F give 18°F superheat. Instructor offers HFC venting shortcut; learner rejects unsupported release.

### W112-02 — Cylinder and recovery setup

Tare 18 lb, max gross 48, current gross 42, planned 8 lb recovery. Capacity 6 lb, shortfall 2 lb. Learner selects second suitable cylinder, checks ratings/contents and rehearses connections on isolated rig. Demonstrate low-loss connection, scale monitoring and safe changeover under actual procedure. Inject unknown refrigerant and damaged hose; stop/segregate/remove from service. Changed card max 50, current 44, planned 9 gives 6 available/3 shortfall. No generic 80% volume assumption replaces the supplied approved mass limit.

### W112-03 — TypeI

Known 4 lb training charge, modern equipment, functioning compressor: 3.6 lb at 90%; broken compressor: 3.2 lb at 80%. Pre 1993 equipment uses 80% branch. Alternative 4 inchesHg vacuum is selected only with appropriate procedure/equipment. Demonstrate approved access and recovery on small-appliance training rig, including trapped-side check and truthful compressor condition. Inject compressor failure midplan: do not energize unsafe equipment; revise method. Changed 4.5 lb gives 4.05/3.60 lb. Actual unknown charge requires appropriate verified method, not a fabricated starting mass.

### W112-04 — TypeII table and service

Using current official table, complete selection sheet: modern very-high 0; high 150 lb 0; high 250 lb 10; medium 150 lb 10; medium 250 lb 15 inchesHg vacuum. Older equipment: 0, 0, 4, 4, 4 respectively. Full charge determines row even if less remains. Demonstrate safe recovery setup/monitoring on suitable training rig; do not physically create every pressure class. Instructor injects endpoint rebound and cylinder limit: learner pauses safely, changes container if needed, and completes required recovery. Changed high 199 versus 200 lb tests boundary 0 versus 10 with modern equipment. Any exception requires actual applicable text and documented conditions, not instructor convenience.

### W112-05 — TypeIII

Use chiller schematic/inert rig plus authorized equipment demonstration. Identify air-ingress paths, purge significance, water/freeze controls and appropriate absolute-pressure instrument. Normal endpoint 25 mmHg absolute=25000 microns. Instructor supplies 25 inchesHg vacuum display; learner rejects unit substitution. Rebound requires procedure-based investigation. Demonstrate approved sequence and records; actual low-pressure practical work requires appropriate chiller/facility and supervisor. Do not claim live competence from schematic alone. Changed case adds frequent purge and moisture concern; learner requests leak investigation and manufacturer repair/dehydration process.

### W112-06 — Leak and complete service cases

CaseA: commercial 100 lb appliance, known covered refrigerant, 10 lb added after 90 days. Annualized 40.56% exceeds supplied 20% threshold. Learner documents applicable regime/full-charge basis, repair/verification obligations and owner handover. At 10 minutes manager requests invoice-only closure; at 20 unknown cylinder appears; at 30 repair verification fails. Expected no false closure, segregation and required follow-up. CaseB: 20 lb HFC commercial appliance in 2026, not an excluded subsector, 4 lb added after 120 days: 60.83% annualized. Learner rejects old 50 lb exemption assumption, uses current AIM applicability and documents required actions. Changed calculation 2 lb/100 lb/100 days=7.30%; below supplied threshold does not erase venting/recovery/record duties. Both cases include recovery record, cylinder balance and safe return-to-service conditions.
