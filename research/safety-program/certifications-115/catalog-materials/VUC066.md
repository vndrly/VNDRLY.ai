# VNDRLY/U Long Seam Pipeline Examination

VUC066. Original advanced training and assessment package for long-seam feature detection, characterization, sizing and traceable handover. Its practical pathway uses a declared validated UT/PAUT method with suitable supplementary observations. It does not award API LSP, employer NDT qualification, excavation competence or permission to operate or repair a pipeline. Actual demonstrated methods and limitations appear on the training record.

Prerequisites: established UT fundamentals, ability to configure and verify the selected instrument, dimensional arithmetic and interpretation of section drawings. The instructor diagnoses gaps before advanced work. This course is not a substitute for missing basic method training. Learning outcomes are to establish a defensible seam examination plan, demonstrate setup, distinguish feature evidence from geometry, measure supported dimensions, reconcile field/ILI coordinates and produce an independent report. Complete teaching, checks, labs, observed stations and the separate final assessments.

## Q01 — Define what the examination can decide

The examiner supplies trustworthy observations and measurements to the operator's integrity process. Detection, characterization and sizing are distinct tasks. A successful indication search does not prove accurate height sizing; a correct height on one feature does not establish coverage of the entire seam. Identify the required volume, methods, reporting rules and decision authority before starting.

Record pipe identity, seam evidence, material and geometry information, accessible area and the actual procedure revision. If the supplied history is uncertain, keep that uncertainty visible. Do not turn a guessed manufacturing process into a fact. An examination finding is not itself an engineering acceptance calculation or repair instruction.

Worked: the assignment requests 600 mm of seam, but only 450 mm is accessible. The examined fraction is 75%, with 150 mm unresolved. Changed: a different method reaches part of the gap but has no demonstrated sensitivity to the required feature. Access alone does not complete the assignment. P01 requires a scope matrix that identifies what each method can and cannot establish.

## Q02 — Understand seam manufacture without overdiagnosis

Electric-resistance joining and filler-metal seam welding produce different local geometry and material histories. Examine the available manufacturing records and the actual seam profile. Distinguish the central joining region, adjacent affected material and surrounding pipe body. Avoid assuming that a visible ridge marks every relevant subsurface feature.

Historical process information helps identify plausible threats but does not prove a particular defect exists. Nor does newer manufacture guarantee absence of relevant features. A technician should describe the observed location, orientation and response, then assign a supported category with stated confidence. Mixed or uncertain evidence remains unresolved pending appropriate investigation.

Worked: records identify ERW, but the exposed surface shows an unexpected deposited weld profile. Verify identity and repair history before using the original seam model. Changed: records cannot resolve the discrepancy. Develop an authorized examination plan for the actual demonstrated geometry and label the history uncertainty. P02 uses original section sketches and record cards, not a claim that visual appearance alone identifies manufacturing process.

## Q03 — Establish safe access and preparation boundaries

An in-ditch examination depends on safe access arranged by the operator. This course does not authorize excavation entry, pressure changes, grinding, coating removal or contact with an unsafe line. Confirm the site's access controls, competent-person decisions and permitted surface preparation. If conditions change, stop the affected work and use the designated communication path.

Training uses supported cool nonpressurized coupons. Control sharp edges, lifting, unstable curved pieces, cables, couplant and chemicals. Preserve specimen identification and protected truth. Do not remove material to make an indication easier to interpret; a production removal could change both integrity and evidence.

Worked: a seam region is covered by a coating whose removal is not authorized. Record the restriction and obtain the approved method or preparation instruction. Changed: a supervisor verbally suggests using a grinder without the required controls. The examination assignment does not supply that authority. P03 is a simulated access/preparation decision and safe bench setup, with no hazardous field task performed.

## Q04 — Choose a primary method and its supporting evidence

Select a method whose performance has been demonstrated for the assigned feature types, orientations, depths and geometry. Define what supplementary methods contribute: surface connection, local profile, thickness or an independent response. Combining methods can improve evidence, but two unsuitable methods do not make a suitable examination.

Use the learner's actual qualified method scope. A person unfamiliar with a technique cannot add it to the certificate by mentioning it in a report. Inventory and verify each used system. If a supplementary method is unavailable, evaluate the lost evidence and state the resulting limitation rather than crediting an observation that was never made.

Worked: UT detects a response, and a surface method is proposed to establish external connection. Until the supplementary examination is validly performed, external connection remains unconfirmed. Changed: the surface method is negative but lacks suitable orientation/sensitivity. That result cannot rule out a connected feature. P04 produces a method-to-output matrix and demonstrates the selected primary setup.

## Q05 — Model the curved section correctly

Long-seam scanning crosses curved inner and outer surfaces. The geometry changes beam paths and displayed position. Establish actual diameter/radius, local thickness, probe position and surface contour within the technique's supported range. A flat-plate display can misplace a response when applied without suitable correction to curved pipe.

Software correction is only as credible as its inputs and validation. Check the actual probe/wedge and relevant reference targets. Do not treat a visually plausible image as a calibrated section. Preserve the coordinate convention and distinguish sound path, normal depth and circumferential offset.

Worked: a setup file assumes 300 mm outside diameter, while the specimen is 250 mm. Correct the input and perform the applicable checks before interpretation. Changed: the diameter is corrected but the wedge is still the wrong contour. The configuration remains unverified. P05 requires an actual section/setup comparison and demonstrated location of known references on representative curvature.

## Q06 — Calibrate the system for detection and sizing

Identify the instrument, software, probe, wedge, cable and reference blocks. Establish the required range/position and sensitivity checks for the actual method. A strong known reflector does not verify a height-sizing method; include suitable depth/height references and the required response states. Record achieved values, not merely checked boxes.

Use the applicable approved equipment instructions rather than a universal button recipe. If the instrument or technique cannot demonstrate required performance, do not use it for that assigned output. Replacing a probe or changing relevant laws/settings triggers the prescribed revalidation. A stored file does not substitute for independent setup competence.

Worked: known normal depths 3 and 8 mm display 3.0 and 8.7. Under fictional ±0.3 mm reference tolerance, the second fails. Changed: an adjustment gives 3.5 and 8.0. The lower reference now fails; the range is still unverified. P06 requires actual setup and reference evidence for every method used in the integrated practical.

## Q07 — Locate the seam and preserve the coordinate convention

Establish a reproducible axial origin, positive direction, circumferential reference and viewing direction. State whether clock position is viewed upstream or downstream. Verify the seam location with suitable evidence rather than relying on an arbitrary surface mark. Keep the examination coordinates linked to the operator's reference system.

Reversing a coupon or viewing direction can invert positions without changing the feature. Record transformations explicitly. A result cannot be compared to an ILI feature merely because both numbers are near each other. Match identity, origin, units and direction first.

Worked: on a 600 mm segment, a feature extends 120–170 mm from the left reference. Its length is 50 mm; from the opposite end it extends 430–480 mm. Changed: the report preserves 120–170 while labeling the opposite origin. The dimensions are numerically plausible but the location is wrong. P07 requires a physical relocation from the report alone by another observer.

## Q08 — Cover the seam volume rather than the predicted target

Plan the required scan directions, offsets, tracks and supplementary views against the actual feature orientations and access. An ILI indication can guide correlation, but it must not cause the examiner to ignore the rest of the assigned region. Record contact loss, obstructed paths and excluded volume.

Validate the plan on representative references/targets. One-sided access may leave important orientations poorly interrogated; do not assume an opposite surface response proves all intervening features would be detected. A coverage map should show actual acquisition and limitations, not just planned tracks.

Worked: an assigned 500 mm length has coupling loss from 210 to 235 mm. The 25 mm interval needs valid recovery under the technique. Changed: it is rescanned at a different offset that does not interrogate the same volume. The original gap remains. P08 demonstrates coverage and recovery, preserving both failed and corrected evidence.

## Q09 — Separate geometry from a reportable discontinuity

Offset, residual trim and surface profile can generate responses. Compare the response to actual geometry, movement with probe position and permitted corroborating views. Do not label every strong signal a crack. Equally, a known geometric reflector does not explain away a separate nearby response.

Map evidence before assigning a name. Keep observed signal behavior separate from interpretation. A response that cannot be resolved should be reported with its uncertainty and required follow-up, not deleted to reduce false calls. False calls and missed relevant features are separate error dimensions.

Worked: a response follows a known ridge, while a second persists away from the ridge under suitable alternate views. Investigate and record both; the ridge does not dismiss the second. Changed: the second disappears when a setup error is corrected. Retain the interpretation revision and evidence rather than silently erasing the original call. P09 uses known geometry and adjacent-feature specimens.

## Q10 — Characterize bondline and planar indications

A planar response at the joining region may support a bondline discontinuity interpretation. Establish its position relative to the seam, orientation, extent and connection evidence. Similar response patterns can arise from different conditions. Do not claim metallurgy or fabrication cause solely from a screen image.

Check the full assigned feature and preserve intermittent or stacked responses. A single strong segment does not establish the maximum extent. Sensitivity and orientation influence visibility, so absence under one view is not proof of absence. Use the validated interpretation procedure and record unresolved boundaries.

Worked: a response follows the joining plane in two separated axial intervals. Record both intervals before applying an explicit interaction rule. Changed: a third shallow response is seen only in an unverified law. Do not add it as established height until that state is verified. P10 requires actual known planar-feature interpretation with supporting views and a clear confidence limit.

## Q11 — Examine hook-shaped and laminar features

A laminar feature near a formed seam can change orientation through the wall. A hook-like feature may therefore require more than one view to establish its geometry. Do not expect every feature to produce a textbook hook image. Distinguish a supported shape description from proof of an active crack or propagation mechanism.

Map normal depths and lateral extent using validated geometry. An intermediate reflector can block or redirect sound and hide another region. Retain unresolved volume. When multiple apparent tips exist, establish which are real boundaries and which are geometry or mode artifacts before calculating maximum height.

Worked: a planar feature is nearly parallel to the surface away from the seam and turns toward it near the joining region. Describe the supported orientation change and investigate its connection. Changed: the apparent turn occurs only in an incorrect flat-geometry display. Correct the model and reassess before calling a hook feature. P11 uses independently characterized teaching examples and changed orientations.

## Q12 — Distinguish inclusions, surface marks and contact effects

Volumetric responses, manufacturing surface marks and localized contact-related features require evidence appropriate to their scale and position. Record whether a feature is visible, acoustically detected or both. A familiar appearance does not establish harmlessness. Nearby planar evidence must be assessed separately.

Surface examination can help describe a mark, but it does not automatically establish its subsurface extent. Do not remove or polish a feature without authorization. Where the method cannot establish composition or origin, use a descriptive category and state the uncertainty rather than a confident unsupported label.

Worked: several short surface marks lie beside the seam and one has a separate supported subsurface response. Report the visible marks and the subsurface finding distinctly. Changed: an operator requests “normal manufacturing marks” as the report description without supporting evidence. Preserve the actual findings and refer disposition to the authorized reviewer. P12 combines safe visual/profile observations with actual UT corroboration.

## Q13 — Assess metal loss and trim without double counting

Local thinning, raised trim and edge offset have different physical meanings. Establish the actual local section and datum before computing loss. A deeper surface depression and a crack-like response can coexist; record the basis for each dimension and avoid assuming their depths can simply be added.

Measure remaining wall with a suitable validated technique and distinguish it from depth to an internal reflector. A nominal manufacturing thickness may differ from actual nearby sound wall. State which baseline is used in a percentage calculation. Do not convert percentage loss into an operating decision without the governing engineering criteria.

Worked: local reference wall 10 mm and supported remaining wall 8 mm imply 2 mm loss, or 20% of that reference. Changed: a separate crack height is measured from a different surface datum. Do not add 2 mm and the crack height until the geometry and required engineering convention are established. P13 requires a section sketch with all measured and inferred quantities labeled.

## Q14 — Establish endpoints and apply only the assigned interaction rule

Measure axial start and stop under the validated endpoint technique and preserve the origin. Length is stop minus start only when both share the same coordinate system. Nearby features may need to be reported separately and also grouped under a governing interaction rule. Grouping does not erase individual observations.

Use the actual assigned rule, including whether equality is included. A training interaction threshold is not automatically a production rule. A gap between features must itself be adequately examined before being treated as clear material. Report ambiguous separation rather than choosing the grouping that looks more favorable.

Worked: features 40–70 and 85–110 mm have lengths 30 and 25, a 15 mm gap and 70 mm overall span. Under an explicitly fictional rule to group gaps less than 20 mm, group and retain the two components. Changed: the gap is exactly 20 mm. The strict “less than” rule does not group equality. P14 measures actual endpoints and performs a separate paper rule comparison.

## Q15 — Measure height, ligament and surface connection

Establish normal-depth coordinates and validated feature boundaries before arithmetic. For a far-surface-connected feature in a locally flat section, height equals local wall minus depth to its established near tip. For an embedded feature, height is the difference between its established boundary depths; remaining ligament to the scanning surface is the shallower depth. Curvature and other geometry must be handled by the validated method.

Surface connection is an evidence-based classification. A tip near a surface is not automatically connected. An amplitude image extent is not necessarily physical height. If a boundary cannot be resolved, report the limitation rather than entering zero or a convenient estimate.

Worked: local wall 12 mm and established far-surface-connected tip at normal depth 8 mm give height 4 mm and near-surface ligament 8 mm. Changed: two embedded boundaries at 4 and 8 mm give height 4, near ligament 4 and far ligament 4 mm. Identical heights can represent different geometry. P15 requires actual supported sizing and section sketches for both cases.

## Q16 — Search for maximum extent and control uncertainty

Scan the full feature and pair each valid boundary measurement with its local section. Maximum measured height is not automatically the full-feature maximum when an interval is unexamined. Verify candidate tips with the approved method and preserve rejected interpretations. Report the largest supported value and limitations honestly.

Use declared truth uncertainty and a preannounced decision rule in training. Do not let a highly precise cursor hide poor boundary identification. Signed error, absolute error and uncertainty serve different purposes. Reassessment must test a new feature, not memorization of the disclosed maximum.

Worked: supported heights 3.0, 3.8 and 3.4 mm give a measured maximum 3.8 mm. Changed: a section between those positions is missing. The whole-feature maximum remains unresolved. A separate grading example has error 0.4 mm plus truth uncertainty 0.1; against fictional guarded tolerance 0.6 it passes at 0.5. An error 0.55 plus 0.1 fails at 0.65. P16 records raw evidence and the conservative decision.

## Q17 — Reconcile field findings with ILI without changing them to match

Match pipe identity, weld reference, axial offset, clock convention and units before pairing an in-ditch finding with an ILI call. Keep the original ILI result and the independent field result. Differences may reflect location mismatch, different measured quantities or method uncertainty, not necessarily a defect in one system.

Do not adjust the field number to agree with the predicted value. Document the matching rationale, actual difference and unresolved alternatives. Operator specialists determine the implications for ILI performance and integrity assessment. One matched feature is not a complete statistical validation of a tool population.

Worked: ILI height estimate 5.0 mm and independently measured field height 4.2 yield field-minus-ILI difference −0.8 mm, if they describe the same feature and quantity. Changed: the field location is one joint away. Withdraw the match; the subtraction no longer supports a sizing comparison. P17 uses synthetic records and a traceable correlation table, with no live operational records altered.

## Q18 — Detect verification failure and recover data

Perform prescribed reference checks and event-based revalidation. Record the last valid state, failure time and affected acquisition IDs. Stop using an invalid configuration. Correct the cause, reverify and determine the repeat or disposition required by the responsible authority.

A successful later check does not retroactively validate earlier scans. Preserve failed and original data, even if a revised report is issued. Recover all potentially affected regions, not just the last convenient specimen. If recovery cannot be completed, make the remaining limitation visible at handover.

Worked: valid 09:00, failed 12:00, four seam regions examined in between. All four may be affected. Changed: one is repeated after correction at 12:30. Three remain unresolved unless an authorized supported disposition addresses them. P18 uses harmless controlled training discrepancies and separate files; no production configurations or records are erased.

## Q19 — Produce an actionable examination record

For each finding preserve unique ID, specimen/pipe reference, start/stop/length, circumferential position, normal depths/height/ligament as applicable, supported type/connection, method/setup and limitations. Link the evidence used for detection separately from evidence used for sizing when they differ. Record the required geometric anomalies as well as flaws under the assignment rules.

Inspect the actual exported report for missing rows, incorrect units, truncated limitations and accidental default setup IDs. A complete-looking form can still contain unsupported measurements. Communicate credible significant findings through the designated path without inventing a repair or pressure decision.

Worked: five findings use setup S2, but two rows default to S1. Correct them against actual acquisition evidence. Changed: the corrected export drops the longest limitation. Repair and inspect again; a save message is not proof of a usable handover. P19 requires actual report entry/export and a simulated communication record, with no outside alert sent.

## Q20 — Demonstrate independent competence in the declared method

The final performance combines valid setup, full coverage, feature detection, characterization, dimensions, false-call control and reporting. Score each dimension independently. A high detection percentage cannot compensate for invented tip measurements or omitted areas. The training award lists only methods actually demonstrated under observation.

Use unfamiliar validated specimens with protected truth. Teach failures after the attempt and reassess on changed specimens of comparable scope. Preserve original scores and critical issues. A written pass or prior certificate cannot replace the actual integrated demonstration required by this course.

Worked: nine of ten relevant features found, but a designated critical planar feature is the one missed. The critical condition fails despite 90% detection. Changed: all ten are found, but three false calls are entered among twenty sound regions when the training maximum is one. Detection is 100%; false-call performance still fails. I66 requires both conditions and all assigned dimensions before a completion record is issued.

# VUC066 delivery, application and assessment

## Preparation and schedule

Plan 64 hours: 16 instruction/checks, 10 application labs, 20 observed stations, 12 integrated practice/independent performance and remediation, 6 written final/review. Breaks/additional remediation separate; record actual hours. Instructor must be competent in the declared seam examination method and selected equipment. A responsible UT Level III or equivalently authorized technical authority validates procedures, physical references/specimens, truth and scoring before delivery. Authoring this package does not constitute that physical validation.

Diagnostic: identify normal depth versus path, read a curved section, reproduce a basic approved UT/PAUT setup, calculate length/percentage and explain an unresolved boundary. Teach missing fundamentals before advanced assessment. Demonstrate a complete known seam example with reasoning, then let learners examine a changed known example. Do not disclose final specimen truth or coach during independent attempts.

Provide actual selected instrument/software, compatible probes/wedges/cables, representative calibration references with known locations/depths, suitable scanner/encoder if used, rulers/profile tools, compatible couplant/SDS, removable marking materials, stable coupon supports and training computer. Supplementary surface methods require their own qualified instructor, suitable procedure and safety controls; the default pathway uses safe visual/profile observations plus UT/PAUT. No radiography, live pressure work, excavation entry, hot work or unauthorized coating removal is part of the exercise. Preserve production configurations and data; use separate training files rather than factory resets.

## S66 original practical design

Teaching set T must include known examples of planar joining-region features, hook/laminar geometry, volumetric response, offset/trim, surface mark and metal loss. Where a safe artificial target is used, label its representational limits; do not claim it reproduces every response of a real service defect. Include supplementary original section drawings and known feature evidence for concepts that the actual bench cannot demonstrate. Written drawings do not replace required physical performance.

Protected sets A and B each contain at least five supported curved seam coupons across two geometries/thickness groups within the validated method range. Provide ten independently established reportable features and twenty designated sound regions; at least four of the ten are critical planar features, including a resolvable near-seam changing-orientation feature. Include an internal and an external connected feature, embedded feature, geometric anomaly and local loss. Five designated features have resolvable required height/ligament targets; all ten have defined endpoints. The technical authority validates that each declared primary method can interrogate them. Sets A/B must be distinct from T and each other.

Document specimen IDs, material/process evidence, local dimensions, feature truth and uncertainty, reference method, safe handling and validated detection/sizing technique. Obtain adequate independent qualified characterization or certified truth documentation. Do not derive protected truth from the same unvalidated scan being assessed. If the claimed feature type cannot be established, revise the training target before use or select another specimen; never invent truth to fill the matrix.

Technique sheet: scope/material/geometry; actual method and supplementary methods; probe/wedge/configuration; reference range/position/sensitivity checks; curved-geometry treatment; scan directions/offsets/speed/coverage validation; endpoint/height/ligament techniques; explicit reporting/interaction rules; verification events/intervals; recovery/reporting; authority/revision. Actual equipment button sequence comes from approved instructions. Every learner demonstrates setup rather than merely loading a file.

Original S66 requirements, announced before testing: reference position/depth guarded error ≤0.30 mm, where guarded error is absolute error plus declared reference uncertainty. Sensitivity reference within ±10 percentage points of its assigned 80% level where that level is appropriate to the validated method. Verify before/after each specimen and after relevant changes. All failed checks must be resolved with required recovery.

All assigned seam volume must be examined with limitations explicitly recorded. Detect at least nine of ten features and all four critical planar features. Correctly characterize at least nine of ten truth features, including applicable surface connection; a missed feature counts as incorrect, not removed from denominator. No more than one false feature call in the twenty sound regions. Endpoint guarded errors ≤3 mm and length guarded error ≤4 mm on at least nine of ten features, including all critical ones. Each of the five designated height and ligament targets must have guarded error ≤0.60 mm; no missing dimension is removed from denominator. Establish truth uncertainty ≤0.10 mm for height/ligament and ≤0.50 mm for endpoints/length. These are original training criteria, not API grading or production limits.

S66 reporting: record all validated features meeting the explicitly assigned response basis and length ≥5 mm; record required geometric anomalies and all five designated sizing targets. For the original training grouping exercise only, group axial gaps strictly less than 20 mm while retaining individual start/stop records. Equality does not group. This rule does not claim external or production applicability. No examiner may authorize operation, grinding or repair from a training score.

# VUC066 source review and coverage

Reviewed 2026-11-18. Original VNDRLY teaching and assessments based on available primary public sources. No external specimen truth or examination questions reproduced. No full proprietary-standard equivalence or external qualification claimed. S66 limits, examples, specimen matrix and scoring are independently stated training choices.

L01 — [API current LSP page](https://www.api.org/products-and-services/individual-certification-programs/certifications/apilsp). Supports the program's seam detection/characterization/sizing scope, practical emphasis and method-specific credential boundary. Public descriptions emphasize ERW and mention other seam processes. This VNDRLY package records actual demonstrated methods/geometries instead of claiming automatic qualification for every process.

L02 — [Currently linked characterization guide](https://apidam.widen.net/content/m7r8yqa6cb/pdf/=c&quality=80&x.app=portals). Supports considering location, connection, geometry and feature evidence separately. Terminology includes both hook crack and hook flaw; the course avoids claiming active cracking or a specific cause from shape alone. The guide's generalized detection/propagation language is not adopted as a universal safety or acceptance guarantee. Full API 5T1 text was not reviewed; no complete clause-by-clause equivalence is claimed.

L03 — [Currently linked candidate orientation](https://apidam.widen.net/content/cfbwwp9ism/pdf/=c&quality=80&x.app=portals), internally effective 2020. Supports independently demonstrated primary-method setup and separate performance categories. The document remains linked but is not assumed to settle every current administrative detail. Its external thresholds, data-reset rules and security procedures are not instructions to alter this workspace or production instruments. VNDRLY supplies separate explicit assessment rules and preserves training/production data.

L04 — [PHMSA-hosted historical seam evaluation](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/sites/phmsa.dot.gov/files/docs/technical-resources/pipeline/gas-transmission-integrity-management/65266/tto05lowfrequencyerwfinalreportrev3april2004.pdf), April 2004. Historical context for legacy seam features and the need to match examination capability to threats. It is not presented as current regulation, a modern acceptance standard or proof of a specific pipe's condition.

L05 — [Sonatest long-seam application note](https://sonatest.com/resources/application-notes/long-seam-weld-inspection-encoded-semi-automatic). Supports the importance of curved geometry and actual system configuration. Product examples do not establish performance of every instrument or qualify a learner. The course requires representative reference verification rather than assuming software correction is sufficient.

L06 — [Evident long-seam scanning application](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/applications/inspecting-long-seam-welds-efficiently-and-easily-using-the-axseam-scanner). Supports acoustic/mechanical challenges of axial seam scanning. It does not provide universal scan spacing, acceptance limits or guaranteed detection. S66 requires a validated actual technique.
