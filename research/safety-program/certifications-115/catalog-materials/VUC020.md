# VUC020 delivery and assessment guide

Audience: personnel who will evaluate and correct fall-protection hazards in defined general-industry/construction work. Prerequisites: experience with the relevant work and systems, ability to use the actual instructions and measurement methods, and employer support for prompt corrective action. Learners must first demonstrate ordinary user inspection/fit competence in station E. A course cannot make a person competent in systems they have never used or grant employer authority. Engineered lifeline/anchor design and technical rescue remain separate qualifications.

Fourteen objectives F01–F14 are the coursebook's fourteen chapters: role/scope, survey, prevention choice, collective systems, anchors/lifelines, compatibility, clearance, swing/transitions, inspection/fit, restricted alternatives/nets, falling objects/change, rescue, training/correction and integrated plan. Each has two formative and three final items. Every objective appears in physical or scenario assessment below.

Planned course:16 hours excluding breaks. Day one: roles/rules/survey90 minutes; hierarchy/collective systems90; anchors/compatibility90; clearance/swing90; equipment inspection/fit120. Day two: alternatives/nets/objects90; rescue90; teaching/corrective records60; coached planning/practical120; written examination60; independent practical/debrief60. Add assessment time and instructor capacity until every learner is independently observed. These are VNDRLY allocations, not a statutory duration.

Instructor must be competent and qualified in the systems and teaching duties actually presented, with documented relevant experience. Qualified design input is used for engineered systems, not improvised by a course facilitator. Use at most four learners per observed equipment station and individual assessment. All exercises are at ground level on inert mock-ups with no real unprotected edge or person fall. Do not load-test real rails, suspend learners, activate an absorber, damage service gear or perform a net drop during this class. Use labelled retired samples or photos for defects and controlled mannequin demonstrations for rescue planning.

Written final:42 original constructed responses,84 points; pass68/84 and4/6 in each domain, with no critical unsafe endorsement. Score2 correct decision plus essential reason;1 safe incomplete;0 wrong/absent/unsafe. Examples of critical failures: approving unknown anchorage, accepting insufficient clearance, exposing people without protection, bypassing rescue readiness or returning impacted gear without required disposition. Sixty minutes plus reasonable accommodations. Protected key remains separate.

Practical:ten stations, five criteria each scored0–2 (unsafe/absent, safe prompted/incomplete, independent correct). Require8/10 per station and every starred criterion2. Stop an unsafe attempt and preserve failure. Demonstrate and coach before assessment, not during it. Remediation targets the observed gap; reassess changed equipment evidence, measurements or site conditions. Protected alternate written cards cover failed domains plus F14; two reassessments maximum before further training and later assessment. A written score never substitutes for an individual practical demonstration.

Credential: after passing all material knowledge and observed skills, record demonstrated systems and tasks. Employer designation/authority and actual site-system suitability remain separate. Simulator-only evidence cannot authorize unassessed specialist systems. VNDRLY recommends annual capability review, with immediate retraining/reassessment for changed conditions or deficits; do not claim a universal federal certificate-expiry period. No OSHA, ANSI, manufacturer or other external approval is implied.

## A — Survey and rule selection

Equipment: ground-level layout representing a five-foot shop platform, construction edge, skylight, beam below and access path; tape, task cards and source excerpts. Learner marks hazards, chooses applicable provisions, identifies structural information and ranks controls. Fault: another worker says everything below six feet is safe.

Criteria: correct scope/role; *rejects wrong-threshold assumption; identifies route/holes/objects/structure; measures first obstruction; selects prevention where feasible. Correct solution distinguishes the shop's general-industry threshold and special dangerous-equipment/holes conditions rather than copying construction. Reassessment changes task and industry.

## B — Guardrail and cover review

Equipment: floor-level rail/cover props, installation/specification cards and tape. Include a36-inch toprail, loose marked cover and missing intermediate protection. Learner measures, compares evidence and rejects defects. Do not lean on the prop as a strength test. Present a synthetic cover load300 pounds; general intended-load criterion requires at least600-pound capacity, separately verified for actual configuration.

Criteria: finds dimension/intermediate faults; *rejects unknown structural adequacy; *rejects unsecured cover; controls exposure during repair; records corrected installation verification. A painted warning does not cure sliding. Change the cover to a roadway case on reassessment; learner must identify the separate axle-load basis.

## C — Anchor and lifeline acceptance

Equipment: anchor photos, substrate/fastener drawings, one-user approved design card, three-user task card, lifeline chart. Learner checks actual configuration and identifies matters needing qualified design. Fault: a pipe looks strong but has no evidence; second fault adds users to the approved system.

Criteria: *rejects unsupported anchor claim; checks installation/load direction/substrate; *does not exceed user/design limits; recognizes qualified horizontal-lifeline role and deflection; gives a safe hold/correction decision. No classroom force test establishes actual anchor capacity.

## D — Clearance, restraint and swing

Equipment: tape, floor plan, manufacturer chart or clearly synthetic worksheet, line/anchor props. Synthetic worksheet6+3.5+5+2=16.5 feet; available12 feet from same datum means reject with4.5-foot shortfall. Add lateral column and sharp flange. Learner then proposes a genuinely suitable alternative using its own supplied evidence, not merely shorter-looking gear.

Criteria: correct datum/inputs; correct arithmetic without deleted margin; *rejects inadequate clearance; identifies swing/edge path; *verifies restraint cannot reach exposure or refers redesign. Reassessment uses5+3+5+2=15 with14 available, and a different chart datum to test understanding.

## E — Inspection and fit

Equipment: actual harness/manual, connecting devices, marked retired/defect samples, history cards and clean ground-level trainer. Instructor demonstrates the model's inspection, donning, adjustment, attachment and function checks. Learner performs independently and identifies chemical damage, missing label, distorted connector and a visually sound impact-loaded item.

Criteria: complete inspection including history/labels; *quarantines suspect/impact-loaded equipment; correct actual-model fit/attachment; checks closure/compatibility; records decision and disposition without improvised repair. Use safe unloaded function checks only. No person is dropped or suspended.

## F — Connections and transitions

Equipment: compatible/incompatible connector props, dual-leg device manual, ground-level simulated anchors. Learner identifies gate loading and inappropriate attachment, then demonstrates the approved transition/unused-leg handling without a gap in the simulated required protection.

Criteria: *rejects gate/shape incompatibility; selects designated attachment; follows model-specific unused-leg instructions; *preserves continuous protection; identifies line-edge and swing issues across the route. Correcting a clip once after coaching is practice; assessment uses a changed layout independently.

## G — Alternatives and nets

Equipment: rule excerpts, roof/task/distance cards, construction-net drawing and installation/test record. Scenario1: general-industry low-slope infrequent temporary task8feet from edge. Scenario2: construction loading opening with a proposed warning line. Scenario3: construction net12feet below work with10feet outward extension. Learner selects applicable provisions, rejects invalid substitution and identifies net extension must be13feet plus all other criteria.

Criteria: distinguishes industry/activity; *rejects unauthorized alternative; explains actual designated-area/monitor conditions; checks net extension/clearance/test evidence; identifies qualified-plan/competent-supervision boundaries. No one performs a drop test. Reassessment changes distances and work frequency.

## H — Falling objects and changed conditions

Equipment: ground plan, tool/material props, traffic cards and weather/change injects. Learner designs effective controls for workers and traffic below, checks tether/attachment suitability and identifies immediate stop triggers. Fault: material stacked above a toe board and a vehicle entering an unblocked access point.

Criteria: identifies complete drop/exclusion area; selects effective containment/screening or access control; *does not rely only on hard hats/cones; *stops for changed ineffective protection; verifies correction and communicates reopening. Do not attach real tool loads to unapproved harness points.

## I — Rescue and teaching demonstration

Equipment: mannequin at floor level, access sketch, lift reach/availability card, radio and equipment-status cards. Learner activates a simulated fall response, demonstrates actual access feasibility, keeps rescuers protected and gives EMS handover. Then teach another learner correct inspection/connection using the actual equipment and observe their return demonstration. Fault: the planned lift is unavailable.

Criteria: *rejects unavailable rescue plan before exposure; clear response/communication; *prevents untrained release or unsafe rescue; teaches actual limits intelligibly; detects and corrects an observed misuse through reassessment. This assesses rescue planning/interface, not technical-rescue qualification.

## J — Competent-person capstone

Equipment: complete roof/platform scenario with fragile skylight, offset anchor, intervening beam, loading opening, added-worker card and absent rescue lift. Learner independently writes a plan and acceptance decisions, identifies qualified input, communicates a stop-work decision, assigns corrections and verifies evidence before simulated release.

Criteria: complete scope/hazard/prevention analysis; *rejects unresolved structure/anchor/clearance; correct compatible equipment and full travel path; *effective rescue/objects/setup protection; traceable correction/training/change record and employer-authority boundary. A plan with unresolved critical evidence stays on hold even if every field is filled. Change geometry for reassessment and retain the original failed plan.

# VNDRLY/U Fall Protection Competent Person

## F01 — Competence includes authority, not just a certificate

A competent person identifies existing and predictable hazards and has employer authorization to take prompt corrective action. Someone who recognizes a cut lanyard but cannot remove it from service or stop the exposed work lacks the necessary authority for that assignment. The employer must define the person's area, equipment and duties, and support their decisions. A course supplies knowledge and assessment; it cannot grant authority on behalf of every employer.

A qualified person has the relevant knowledge, training, experience or credentials to solve the specified technical problems. The titles are task-specific, not interchangeable. A competent equipment inspector is not automatically qualified to design a horizontal lifeline, certify an unknown anchor or approve an engineered alternate plan. Know when to stop and obtain qualified design input. Record the basis for decisions rather than writing “looks strong.”

Identify the governing industry and activity. General-industry walking-working surfaces and personal systems use 1910.28–.30 and .140. Construction Subpart M uses 1926.500–.503, with separate rules for scaffolds, steel erection, cranes and other named activities. Portable ladders and aerial lifts have their own applicable provisions. Do not select the most convenient height threshold from another activity.

Worked case: a foreman asks an inspector to approve an improvised lifeline across two pipes. The competent person stops reliance on the system and requests the appropriate qualified evaluation; the foreman's schedule and the inspector's certificate do not establish design capacity. Learners must state both the corrective action and who can resolve the technical question.

## F02 — Survey the complete exposure, including the route

Walk the proposed route from safe access to work position and return. Identify unprotected edges, holes, skylights, fragile surfaces, openings, changing elevations, slippery material, obstructions, moving equipment, overhead hazards and areas below. Determine whether the surface can support people, equipment and intended work. A solid-looking roof sheet or cover is not structural verification. Obtain drawings, ratings or competent/qualified evidence appropriate to the structure.

Measure the vertical distance to the lower level and identify intervening beams, piping, machinery and other objects. A fall may strike an obstruction before reaching the ground. Evaluate wind, rain, ice, heat, visibility, fatigue and tasks requiring force or overreach. Tools and materials can change balance, clearance and exposure to people below. Document what will change during the job, such as removing a guardrail for loading or creating a new leading edge.

Ordinary unprotected-edge thresholds include four feet in general industry and six feet in construction Subpart M. They are not a universal safe zone below those heights: dangerous equipment and particular holes/openings can require protection under their own conditions. Do not apply the six-foot number to a general-industry platform or use either number to waive a specific rule.

Worked survey: a five-foot-high shop platform has an open edge above a conveyor. Treat the applicable general-industry and dangerous-equipment hazards, not a memorized construction threshold. A low-level mock-up lets learners mark access, work and rescue positions without exposing anyone to a fall.

## F03 — Choose prevention before relying on arrest

First consider performing the task from the ground or relocating the work. Next consider a safe platform, guardrail, properly secured cover or other collective protection. A personal travel-restraint system keeps the worker from reaching the fall exposure; its length, anchor position, worker reach and every travel direction must actually prevent access. A label reading “restraint” does not change the geometry.

Fall arrest permits a fall to begin and then stops it. It requires compatible equipment, sufficient clearance, suitable anchorage and prompt rescue. Work positioning supports a worker for a task but is not automatically a complete fall-arrest arrangement; the applicable system requirements and additional protection must be evaluated. A body belt is not permitted as a personal fall-arrest body support. A full-body harness alone is not a system.

Choose the method for the actual task, not the stock available in a truck. A worker who must lean over an edge may defeat a restraint arrangement. A short available distance below the surface may make a lanyard arrest system unusable. A safe alternative may require different access equipment or a redesigned method, rather than deleting a clearance allowance.

Worked case: a restraint line is extended to reach a second workstation and now permits the worker's fall-exposed position at the edge. Stop and reevaluate; it has ceased to provide the intended prevention. Demonstrate the full permitted movement area on the ground, including corner approaches and foreseeable reach, before authorizing the arrangement.

## F04 — Inspect guardrails, covers and access openings

Verify installation and strength evidence as well as dimensions. Under the cited general-industry/construction provisions, toprails are ordinarily 42 inches plus or minus 3 inches above the surface, with appropriate intermediate protection. Toprails must meet the applicable 200-pound load criterion and intermediate members their applicable 150-pound criterion. Do not test an occupied live edge by leaning or jumping on a rail. Use installation specifications and a safe inspection/test arrangement under qualified direction.

Check loose posts, corrosion, fasteners, missing midrails, sharp surfaces, excessive openings and protrusions. The applicable provisions address intermediate spacing, toprail deflection and safe openings; a single height measurement is not acceptance. Access openings must be protected as required, such as the appropriate self-closing gate/offset arrangement in general industry, and must not leave a worker unprotected during entry or loading. Protect people before removing a rail and restore it before returning the area to normal use.

Covers must support the prescribed load, be secured against displacement and be marked where required. Construction covers generally support at least twice the intended employee/equipment/material load; roadway covers have a separate twice-maximum-axle-load requirement. General-industry covers support at least twice the maximum intended load and are secured. A loose plywood sheet over a hole fails even if it bears a painted warning. A skylight is not presumed a compliant cover.

Worked case: a marked cover is strong enough but slides when a cart turns. Secure it using the approved installation, protect the opening during correction and inspect the traffic interaction. The competent person's decision addresses both strength and displacement, not only the word HOLE.

## F05 — Evaluate anchors and lifelines as a system

Identify the approved anchor, structural substrate, fasteners, installation, load direction, number of users and intended system. Construction personal fall-arrest anchorage is independent of platform-support anchorage and must meet the 5,000-pound-per-attached-employee criterion or the qualified-person complete-system alternative maintaining a safety factor of at least two. The cited general-industry rule has its corresponding requirements. These are alternative compliance pathways, not permission for an inspector to invent a rating.

A certified roof anchor may be unsuitable when installed into the wrong substrate, with missing fasteners or in an unapproved loading direction. A pipe, handrail or lifting eye is not accepted by appearance. Read actual specifications and installation/design evidence. Do not conduct an improvised proof load on an occupied structure. Missing evidence means the system is not accepted until resolved.

Horizontal lifelines can generate significant forces and deflection. Their design, installation and use require qualified-person supervision under the cited construction rule. Span, sag, pretension, intermediate supports, users, fall location and energy absorbers affect forces and clearance. Do not stretch an arbitrary cable between anchors and apply a simple worker-weight calculation. Vertical lifelines also require correct user arrangements, compatibility and protection from abrasion/cuts.

Worked case: a one-user engineered system is proposed for three people during a repair. The existing approval does not cover the change. Stop and obtain a verified suitable design/configuration, or change the work plan. The competent person checks the use against the approved design rather than silently becoming its designer.

## F06 — Select compatible harnesses, connectors and connecting devices

Read labels and instructions for each actual component: harness, connector, lanyard, absorber, self-retracting device, rope grab and lifeline. Verify intended use, user weight including clothing/tools, environmental limits, connection location, fall distance and inspection status. Components from the same manufacturer are not automatically compatible, and mixing components is not automatically prohibited if the actual approved compatibility is established. The complete system governs.

Use the designated fall-arrest attachment point according to the actual harness/system instructions. Side positioning rings, tool loops and carrying handles are not substitutes. Connectors must be compatible in shape and size, close and lock properly and avoid gate loading, roll-out and cross-loading. Do not connect hooks to each other or to an already occupied ring unless the specific connection is designed and permitted for that use under the applicable criteria. Never knot a lanyard or tie it back around a structure unless it is expressly designed and approved for that configuration.

A self-retracting device may be limited to overhead anchorage and may not be approved over an edge. Check edge material/radius, setback, anchorage elevation, permitted fall distance, offset and required clearance. An edge-rated label does not mean every edge or loading direction is acceptable. Hot work, chemicals, sharp steel and electrical hazards may require different equipment or additional controls.

Worked case: a connector closes but its gate presses against a wide anchor eye. Reject the geometry rather than accepting the audible click. On a ground-level trainer learners compare compatible and incompatible props and explain the actual disengagement/load hazard without loading a person.

## F07 — Calculate clearance from the correct datum

Clearance is the unobstructed space needed for the specified system to arrest a fall without contact. Use the manufacturer's method and the actual anchor location, worker size, connection length, free fall, deceleration/deployment, harness/connection effects, lifeline deflection and required margin. Different charts measure from the work surface, anchor or dorsal ring; mixing datums can make a correct sum dangerously wrong. Record the chart revision and measurement point.

The cited construction harness arrest criteria include a maximum arresting force of 1,800 pounds, maximum deceleration distance of 3.5 feet, no more than 6 feet of free fall and no contact with a lower level. These are separate conditions, not terms to casually substitute into every clearance chart. General-industry 1910.140 expressly allows a greater-than-six-foot free fall only under its manufacturer design/testing demonstration conditions while retaining the force limit; do not transfer that exception into construction or infer it from a product nickname.

For a synthetic teaching worksheet only, suppose the method specifies 6 feet of free fall, 3.5 feet of deceleration, 5 feet of worker/connection geometry and a 2-foot margin from its defined datum. Total required distance is 16.5 feet. If only 12 feet is available from that same datum to the first obstruction, reject the arrangement. The shortfall is 4.5 feet. These illustrative terms are not a universal lanyard formula or a manufacturer's approval.

Self-retracting devices and horizontal lifelines often require different calculations or charts. Add the actual system's deflection and any chart-prescribed factors; do not double-count a factor already included. If a diagram cannot be reconciled with the site geometry, stop and obtain competent manufacturer/qualified design clarification. Do not round down a requirement or subtract the margin to make equipment fit.

Worked exercise: a learner measures to the ground while a beam lies four feet above it. The beam is the limiting obstruction. Recalculate using that level and also evaluate lateral/swing paths. Passing the arithmetic alone is insufficient; the assessor requires correct datum, inputs and accept/reject decision.

## F08 — Control swing falls, edges and transitions

An off-center anchor can produce a pendulum path into a wall, column or equipment even when vertical clearance seems adequate. Map the worker's whole travel and fall path, including lateral movement and the line's contact with edges. Moving the anchor closer overhead or changing the access/work method may reduce exposure, but any new arrangement must remain approved for the actual structure and equipment.

Edges can cut a tensioned line. Use equipment approved for the actual edge application, with manufacturer-specified setback and limits; do not rely on a loose rag or improvised padding as the whole solution. Protect lifelines against cutting and abrasion with a suitable secured arrangement. Consider whether an edge protector can shift during a fall or whether a cable can create another hazard.

Transitions between anchors or platforms need continuous required protection. A dual-leg device has specific connection and unused-leg instructions; parking an unused leg at the wrong point can defeat an absorber or create loading problems. Practice the actual system's sequence on the ground and keep within its connection restrictions. Never disconnect the only effective protection while exposed in order to reach a better anchor.

Worked case: a worker must walk around a pipe rack to reach the next anchor. The proposed line crosses a sharp flange and permits a swing into a column. Stop and redesign the route/protection before work. A drawing with arrows of motion and collision points is more useful than writing “100% tie-off” without showing how it is achieved.

## F09 — Inspect, fit, store and remove equipment from service

Inspect before use under the applicable rule and manufacturer instructions. Examine webbing on both sides and at bends for cuts, abrasion, burns, chemical damage, glazing, pulled stitches and distortion. Inspect hardware for cracks, corrosion, deformation, sharp edges and proper closure/locking. Check labels, identification, indicators, service history and recall status. A faded label can make suitability unverifiable. Do not repair life-safety webbing with tape or home sewing.

For a harness, hold and untangle it, identify straps/rings, don in the model's sequence, fasten and adjust the prescribed connections, position the designated attachment correctly and secure loose ends. Conduct a buddy check and demonstrate movement without loose/twisted straps or compromised fit. Follow the actual fit instructions rather than a universal finger-width rule. Work clothing and carried tools can change fit and total user weight.

Inspect retracting equipment and rope grabs using their instructed function checks in a safe unloaded setup. Do not open a sealed mechanism or create a test fall with a person. Quarantine impact-loaded or suspect equipment immediately and follow the applicable competent-person/manufacturer disposition; a manufacturer may require retirement or factory service even when nothing looks damaged. Keep rejected gear physically separated and clearly identified.

Store clean, dry and protected from sunlight, chemicals, heat and mechanical damage as instructed. Maintain traceable inspection/service records and control access to quarantined items. Worked case: a harness has no visible tear but was used in an arrested fall. It is not issued to the next shift pending proper disposition. The assessor tests this decision with a history card, not only obvious damage samples.

## F10 — Understand restricted-use alternatives and safety nets

Warning lines, designated areas, controlled access zones and safety monitoring are not universal substitutes for guardrails or personal protection. Determine the exact activity and rule before considering one. General-industry low-slope roof provisions distinguish work less than 6 feet from the edge, 6 to less than 15 feet and at least 15 feet, with specific infrequent/temporary conditions and work-rule obligations. Do not transplant those distances to construction roofing.

Construction low-slope roofing has its own listed combinations and conditions. A safety monitor must be competent, able to recognize hazards, on the same working surface within the required visual/verbal relationship and free of duties that interfere with monitoring. A person operating machinery while occasionally glancing at workers is not effective monitoring. Special fall-protection plans for eligible leading-edge, precast or residential situations require the employer's demonstration, qualified-person preparation/changes and competent-person supervision; a generic waiver is not a plan.

Safety nets require proper installation close below the work, adequate outward extension based on vertical distance, unobstructed clearance beneath and verified performance. Under construction rules, they must be no more than 30 feet below the working level, meet the applicable extension table and undergo the required jobsite drop testing or the stated competent-person certification alternative when testing is unreasonable. Testing is required after initial installation before use, relocation, major repair and six-month intervals when left in place. Inspect at least weekly and after events affecting integrity; remove debris promptly under the rule. Never perform a class drop test without a separately engineered safe setup.

For the construction net extension table, a net up to 5 feet below the working level extends at least 8 feet outward; more than 5 through 10 feet below requires 10 feet outward; more than 10 feet below requires 13 feet outward. Clearance beneath must accommodate the prescribed impact without contact. The net's mesh, border rope and panel connections also require verification; an extension measurement alone does not approve it. A qualified controlled test arrangement—not learners or an improvised weight drop—is needed for any actual installation test.

Worked case: a warning line is proposed around a general construction loading edge because it was used on another roof. Reject the analogy and select protection allowed for the actual task. The learner must find the governing provision and explain the conditions, not merely name an alternative system.

## F11 — Protect people below and manage changing work

Tools and materials can fall even when workers are restrained. Evaluate the drop zone, rebound/deflection, wind, openings and public/vehicle access. Use appropriate toe boards, screens, secure storage, canopies or exclusion as the rule/task requires, together with head protection where applicable. A hard hat does not replace prevention or safe exclusion. Stockpiles near an edge and debris thrown down create predictable hazards.

Toe boards under the cited provisions generally have a minimum 3.5-inch height, no more than a quarter-inch clearance above the surface and meet the prescribed strength/opening requirements. Higher stacked material may require screening or panels. Verify the actual application rather than adding a decorative strip to a rail. Tool tethers require appropriate ratings/attachments and must not overload a harness point or impede movement.

Set stop/change triggers: wind or weather beyond the equipment/work plan, missing rail/cover, changed anchor, added worker, moved rescue equipment, damaged component, changed task or unauthorized access below. Stop the affected exposure, protect the area and correct the condition before restarting. Document who verifies restoration. An inspection at dawn does not remain valid after the platform is modified at noon.

Worked case: a forklift route passes under a repair while tools are carried overhead. A cone at one end is insufficient if vehicles enter from another direction. Establish a workable controlled area or appropriate overhead protection, coordinate traffic and verify it remains effective while the work proceeds.

## F12 — Arrange prompt rescue before exposure

An arrested fall is an emergency, not completion of the safety system. The worker may be injured, suspended, unable to self-rescue or unreachable from the planned platform. Identify who initiates rescue, who can reach the actual position, what equipment is required, how rescuers remain protected and how EMS receives the person. Verify availability during the entire task. A phone number alone does not establish prompt capability.

Choose a feasible planned method with appropriately trained rescuers and approved equipment. A lift may be suitable only if it can reach, is available, has trained operators and can be safely positioned. Rope rescue requires separately demonstrated competence; this course does not turn a competent fall-protection inspector into a rescue technician. Protect the area and prevent an untrained coworker from disconnecting or cutting a loaded line.

If a fall occurs, activate the plan promptly, maintain communication if possible and do not encourage unsafe self-release. Provide care within current first-aid/medical training after rescue; avoid invented fixed suspension-safe time limits or outdated universal post-rescue positioning instructions. Preserve equipment and scene information, remove affected systems from service and investigate before reuse.

Worked case: the rescue lift leaves for another job while workers remain exposed. Suspend the work until a suitable rescue capability is restored. On a ground-level mannequin exercise, the learner activates the plan, demonstrates communication and access feasibility, controls the scene and provides an accurate handover without performing untrained technical rescue.

## F13 — Teach, inspect and document corrective action

Worker instruction must cover actual hazards and the systems used, including installation/use/inspection, anchoring, connections, care, limits and rescue actions. General industry requires the specified training by a qualified person; construction requires a competent person qualified in the listed training areas. A title alone does not satisfy those capabilities. Use understandable language and demonstration followed by the worker's own performance, not signatures alone.

Recognize retraining triggers: changed equipment or workplace, inadequate understanding or skill, unsafe use and other reasons showing the earlier training is no longer sufficient. Correct the cause. If a worker attaches to a tool loop, show the actual attachment and have them independently repeat the task; another attendance sheet does not prove correction. Keep the applicable training certification record, dates, identities and scope.

An inspection record should identify system, location, date, actual condition, evidence, decision, defect, immediate protection, corrective owner and verification of closure. Differentiate “not inspected,” “rejected,” “restricted use” and “accepted for specified use.” Never write “all good” for an anchor whose design evidence is missing. Maintain the ability to stop work while a technical question is resolved.

Worked exercise: a crew has current cards but cannot demonstrate the new retracting device's limits. The competent person identifies the training gap, restricts the use and arranges qualified instruction and observed reassessment before exposure. Existing cards do not automatically cover the new configuration.

## F14 — Approve a bounded work plan and reassess changes

Bring the survey, rule selection, protection hierarchy, structural/system evidence, compatibility, clearance, movement path, rescue and falling-object controls together. A complete plan identifies equipment models, allowable use, users, inspection status, installation references, training and stop triggers. Verify protection exists before workers enter the exposure, including setup and dismantling stages. Do not approve only the final working position while ignoring unprotected installation.

Separate facts from assumptions. Measured distance is a fact; unknown anchor strength is unresolved. A manufacturer's clearance chart applies only to its stated conditions. A competent person can reject an unsafe proposal and coordinate a suitable alternative, but must not fabricate engineering evidence. Refer design questions to the appropriate qualified person and keep the work protected pending resolution.

Run the plan against changed conditions: another worker joins, an anchor moves, the line length changes, a beam is added below, wind increases, a rail is removed or rescue availability changes. Each can invalidate an earlier acceptance. Record the new decision and communicate it to everyone affected. A signed permit is not immunity from reevaluation.

Capstone case: a roof repair has a fragile skylight, an off-center anchor, an obstructing beam, a loading opening and a rescue lift scheduled elsewhere. A harness alone solves none of those issues. Produce a safe method using verified access/collective protection where feasible, appropriate personal system if still needed, actual clearance/route evidence, protected openings and available rescue. The credential requires independent demonstration of these decisions and employer authority; authorship of this course does not establish either for a real person.

## F01-L1

Can a certificate alone make someone the employer’s competent person?

**Answer:** No. They need actual hazard-recognition/correction capability and employer authority for the assigned scope.

**Reason/remediation:** Identify who can stop and correct the work; a title alone is insufficient.

## F01-L2

May an equipment inspector automatically design an improvised horizontal lifeline?

**Answer:** No. That requires appropriate qualified-person design/supervision.

**Reason/remediation:** Competent and qualified duties are task-specific; refer unresolved design.

## F02-L1

Which ordinary edge threshold applies to a general-industry shop platform: four or six feet?

**Answer:** Four feet, with particular hazards governed by their own provisions.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not import the construction six-foot threshold.

## F02-L2

Why measure to an intervening beam rather than only the ground?

**Answer:** It may be the first collision surface and governs usable clearance.

**Reason/remediation:** Survey the entire fall path, not just the lowest surface.

## F03-L1

What makes a restraint system effective?

**Answer:** Its actual configuration prevents reaching the fall exposure throughout intended movement.

**Reason/remediation:** The label cannot overcome excess line length or reach.

## F03-L2

Does a harness alone prevent or arrest a fall?

**Answer:** No. It needs the complete appropriate system and work/rescue arrangements.

**Reason/remediation:** Evaluate prevention first, then compatible arrest if needed.

## F04-L1

A strong marked cover slides. Is it acceptable?

**Answer:** No. It must also be secured against displacement under the applicable rule.

**Reason/remediation:** Strength and marking do not cure movement.

## F04-L2

Is measuring toprail height enough to accept a guardrail?

**Answer:** No. Check strength/installation, intermediate protection, openings, condition and the actual application.

**Reason/remediation:** A correct height can conceal a weak or incomplete rail.

## F05-L1

A pipe looks substantial. Does that establish an anchor rating?

**Answer:** No. Obtain appropriate verified design/installation/use evidence or reject it.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance is not a load-capacity determination.

## F05-L2

Can three users attach to a one-user approved lifeline?

**Answer:** Not without a verified suitable changed design/configuration.

**Reason/remediation:** User count changes forces and deflection; do not exceed the approval.

## F06-L1

A hook closes but its gate bears on the anchor eye. What is the decision?

**Answer:** Reject the incompatible loading/geometry and use an appropriate connection.

**Reason/remediation:** Closure alone does not prevent gate loading or roll-out.

## F06-L2

Does an edge-rated device work over any sharp edge?

**Answer:** No. Check the actual edge, position, setback and manufacturer limitations.

**Reason/remediation:** A broad product label is not universal suitability.

## F07-L1

Calculate the synthetic worksheet6+3.5+5+2 and compare with12 feet available.

**Answer:** 16.5 feet required; reject, with4.5 feet shortfall from the same datum.

**Reason/remediation:** Keep every required term and margin; never round away the shortfall.

## F07-L2

Why record a clearance chart’s measurement datum?

**Answer:** To compare required and available distance from the same reference point.

**Reason/remediation:** Mixing anchor, platform and ring datums can invalidate correct arithmetic.

## F08-L1

Adequate vertical clearance eliminates swing impact. True?

**Answer:** No. Offset anchorage can create a pendulum collision with nearby objects.

**Reason/remediation:** Map lateral travel and fall paths.

## F08-L2

Can the only connection be removed while exposed to reach another anchor?

**Answer:** No. Plan and demonstrate continuous required protection using the approved system.

**Reason/remediation:** A slogan about tie-off is not a transition method.

## F09-L1

An impact-loaded harness looks normal. May it be reissued immediately?

**Answer:** No. Quarantine and follow the applicable competent/manufacturer disposition.

**Reason/remediation:** Hidden damage and mandatory retirement/service conditions matter.

## F09-L2

Does a universal finger-width rule replace the harness instructions?

**Answer:** No. Fit and attachments follow the actual model and wearer configuration.

**Reason/remediation:** Demonstrate proper fit and movement using its instructions.

## F10-L1

Can a roof warning-line method be copied to every construction edge?

**Answer:** No. Only use alternatives allowed under the actual activity and conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Find the governing provision before selecting the method.

## F10-L2

A construction net12 feet below the work extends10 feet outward. What minimum extension applies?

**Answer:** 13 feet outward, plus all other installation/clearance/performance requirements.

**Reason/remediation:** The more-than-ten-foot vertical band requires13; extension alone is not approval.

## F11-L1

Do hard hats alone control tools falling onto workers below?

**Answer:** No. Prevent falling objects or provide appropriate protection/exclusion as required.

**Reason/remediation:** PPE does not replace containment and effective access control.

## F11-L2

A rail is removed after the morning inspection. What follows?

**Answer:** Stop affected exposure, provide protection, correct and verify before resuming.

**Reason/remediation:** Inspection evidence is invalidated by relevant change.

## F12-L1

The designated rescue lift leaves. Can exposed work continue because nobody has fallen?

**Answer:** No. Restore a suitable prompt rescue arrangement before continuing exposure.

**Reason/remediation:** Readiness must remain available throughout the task.

## F12-L2

Does this course qualify a learner to improvise rope rescue?

**Answer:** No. Technical rescue requires separate demonstrated competence and approved systems.

**Reason/remediation:** Plan feasible trained response; prevent a second casualty.

## F13-L1

A worker has a card but misuses new equipment. What does the competent person do?

**Answer:** Restrict unsafe use, arrange suitable instruction and observe corrected performance before exposure.

**Reason/remediation:** Cards do not prove new-system proficiency.

## F13-L2

What should an inspection record say when anchor evidence is missing?

**Answer:** Not accepted/unresolved, with protective action and responsible qualified review—not all good.

**Reason/remediation:** Separate verified facts from assumptions.

## F14-L1

Does a work plan cover only the final working position?

**Answer:** No. Include access, setup, transitions, work, dismantling and rescue.

**Reason/remediation:** Exposure can occur before the finished system exists.

## F14-L2

A second user and a lower obstruction are added. Is the old acceptance still sufficient?

**Answer:** No. Reassess system capacity, clearance, travel and other affected conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Acceptance is bounded to the evaluated configuration.
