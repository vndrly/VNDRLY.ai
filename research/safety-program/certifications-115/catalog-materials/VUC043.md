# VNDRLY/U Welding Quality — Inspector Preparation

VUC043 · Original course material · Available-source learning package2026-11-18

The original VNDRLY welding/metallurgy foundation is reused from the verified VUC039 package. This program adds required instrument demonstrations, symbol interpretation, measurement uncertainty and supplied-specification navigation. External AWS CWI requirements are separate.

## Purpose and limits

This program teaches inspection before, during and after welding, with enough metallurgy to understand why a compliant-looking bead can still be unsuitable. Learners practice tracing materials, interpreting procedure and personnel evidence, monitoring specified variables, recognizing imperfections, evaluating examination coverage and assembling a defensible completion record. Intended learners are inspectors, quality technicians, maintenance planners and engineers with basic industrial safety, drawing and measurement skills.

This is VNDRLY learning material, not API577 certification, an AWS/CSWIP credential, a welder performance qualification, NDE certification or an engineering license. The user's available-source instruction governs: use accessible references and original teaching records without inventing unavailable code tables. Supplied exercise limits are synthetic project requirements, not ASME/API/AWS qualification ranges. Actual work requires the applicable controlled code, edition, contract and competent authorized roles.

## Q01 — Establish the inspection basis

A weld cannot be accepted solely because it is attractive or because the welder is experienced. Establish what is being made or repaired, its service, the governing code and edition, the design drawing, the approved welding procedure, inspection/test plan and acceptance requirements. Construction rules, in-service repair rules and the owner's specification can impose different obligations. Identify how conflicts are resolved before production, rather than choosing the least demanding document afterward.

The inspector checks evidence within assigned authority. The welding engineer approves technical changes; the owner or designated authority controls final disposition; NDE personnel work within their qualifications and procedures. A signature records a decision, not a transfer of every responsibility to the signer. Hold points must be honored before work obscures evidence.

**Worked case.** A nozzle repair drawing cites one revision while the work package uses an older preparation detail. Pause the affected fit-up acceptance, resolve the drawing/procedure basis and record the approved revision. Independent traceability checks can continue safely. **Changed case:** the new drawing arrives but the procedure still does not cover its joint. The drawing conflict is resolved; procedure applicability is not.

## Q02 — Read joints, dimensions and symbols

Butt, corner, tee, lap and edge joints describe arrangements; groove and fillet welds describe weld forms. Identify root opening, root face, groove angle, bevel, weld size, length, pitch, contour and any backing or back-gouging requirement. A drawing symbol depends on its stated convention. Do not assume arrow-side rules or dimensions without knowing the symbol system used by the drawing.

A fillet's leg and effective throat are different dimensions. For an ideal equal-leg right-triangle fillet, theoretical throat is approximately0.707 times the leg. This geometry is a teaching calculation, not a design approval or a substitute for effective-throat rules governing a real joint. Convexity, concavity and incomplete fusion can make visual size misleading.

**Worked case.** A synthetic drawing calls for a6 mm equal-leg fillet. The theoretical geometric throat is6/√2=4.24 mm. An observed large convex face does not prove that throat or fusion. **Changed case:** legs are unequal; do not blindly use0.707 times the larger leg. Establish geometry and the actual design/measurement basis.

## Q03 — Material identity and traceability

Trace base material from certificate to the installed piece through receipt, cutting, forming and assembly. Heat numbers, piece marks and maps are links in that chain. A certificate in the folder is not enough if it belongs to another item. Preserve required markings when cutting stock and record the controlled transfer process.

Chemistry, mechanical properties, heat treatment, dimensions and product form may all matter. Handheld alloy identification can answer selected composition questions but cannot establish every property, grade or treatment condition. A material grouping used by a qualification code is not permission to substitute materials in the design. Verify both the design identity and procedure applicability.

**Worked case.** Two plates share dimensions but only one has a traceable heat mark. Do not assign its certificate to both. Segregate the uncertainty and obtain the approved identification/disposition route. **Changed case:** chemistry matches the expected alloy family. This narrows identity but does not by itself establish toughness, treatment or every specification requirement.

## Q04 — Welding processes and characteristic controls

SMAW uses a consumable coated electrode; GTAW uses a nonconsumable tungsten electrode with shielding and, where needed, separate filler; GMAW uses continuously fed consumable wire and shielding gas; FCAW uses a flux-containing wire with process-specific shielding; SAW uses a consumable electrode beneath flux. Each has variables and characteristic risks. Their names are not interchangeable authorization.

Shielding, polarity, transfer mode, electrode/wire, flux, current, voltage, travel and technique can affect the weld. A process combination may use GTAW for the root and another process for fill. The records must identify which process deposited which region. Automated equipment still requires the appropriate procedure and personnel/operator controls.

**Worked case.** A crew proposes changing a GTAW root to a GMAW root because both use shielding gas. The process change needs qualification/applicability review; gas alone does not establish equivalence. **Changed case:** the process remains GMAW but transfer mode changes. Review the relevant procedure and qualification variables rather than assuming no change occurred.

## Q05 — WPS, qualification record and performance qualification

A welding procedure specification directs production. A procedure qualification record records how a qualification coupon was actually welded and tested. A welder or welding-operator qualification addresses the person's demonstrated performance within its applicable range. These records answer different questions. A welder's qualification does not repair an unsupported WPS, and a qualified WPS does not qualify every person.

Compare the production joint with the controlled qualification basis: process, material groups, deposited/base thickness, diameter where applicable, position, filler, backing, thermal requirements and other relevant variables. Essential, supplementary essential and nonessential classifications depend on the governing code and conditions. Do not memorize a range from one standard and apply it universally. Changes that do not require requalification can still require WPS revision and approval.

**Worked case.** A valid performance record is supplied, but the WPS cites a PQR with a different process. Hold procedure acceptance until support is established. **Changed case:** the PQR supports the WPS, but the individual has no demonstrated applicable qualification/continuity. The personnel gap remains separately open.

## Q06 — Consumable receipt, storage and issue

Filler classification, size, batch/lot, condition and storage history must match the approved requirements. Similar appearance is not identity. Moisture, contamination, mixed batches or uncontrolled reconditioning can undermine a consumable's suitability. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and approved procedure for handling, exposure time and any rebaking; do not invent universal oven temperatures.

Wire, flux, electrodes and shielding gases each require their own controls. A low-hydrogen designation does not prove that electrodes remained controlled after opening. Recovered flux can need defined screening and handling. Record issue and return where the project requires it, and segregate uncertain material until disposition.

**Worked case.** An open electrode container has no batch or exposure record. A dry-looking coating is not evidence of controlled condition. Use the approved quarantine/disposition route rather than guessing a bake cycle. **Changed case:** the batch certificate arrives but exposure history does not. Identity is resolved; condition remains unresolved.

## Q07 — Preparation, cleanliness and fit-up

Check bevel geometry, root opening, alignment, surface condition, access, backing and tack welds before the joint closes. Remove contamination using approved methods compatible with the material. Carbon-steel tools can contaminate corrosion-resistant materials; coatings or residues can introduce fumes and weld defects. Cleaning does not replace material identification.

Tacks that remain in the weld need the applicable controls for procedure, personnel, quality and incorporation. Temporary attachments and arc strikes can affect the base material and require controlled removal/examination. Forced alignment can introduce restraint and residual stress. Record measured deviations against the actual drawing/procedure criteria rather than a personal preference.

**Worked case.** A synthetic work instruction allows1.5–2.5 mm root opening; measured points are1.8,2.1 and2.9 mm. The last point is outside the supplied range. Correct under the approved fit-up process before welding. **Changed case:** gap is corrected but a tack crack remains. Geometry correction does not clear the crack.

## Q08 — Preheat and interpass control

Preheat and interpass requirements influence thermal history and potential cracking or property changes. Measure at the specified location, time and method with suitable verified equipment. A surface warmed beside the joint does not necessarily establish the required joint condition. Minimum preheat and maximum interpass can both matter; “hotter is always safer” is false.

Thermal controls depend on alloy, thickness, restraint, hydrogen, heat input and the qualified procedure. Interruptions, weather, heat sinks and repairs can change conditions. Do not improvise a temperature from a generic chart. Maintain and record the required controls for the applicable welding stages and pauses.

**Worked case.** Synthetic WPS limits are preheat≥100°C and interpass≤200°C. A reading of90°C before start fails the minimum; a reading of215°C before another pass exceeds the maximum. Both need the specified corrective response. **Changed case:** a correct temperature is measured far from the specified location; the record does not establish compliance at the required point.

## Q09 — Arc energy and heat input

For steady conditions, voltage×current gives electrical power. With travel speed in mm/min, arc energy in kJ/mm is V×I×60/(1000×speed). Some standards call this quantity heat input; others apply a process-efficiency factor to distinguish net heat input. State the convention and units before comparing values. Do not introduce an efficiency factor into a limit defined as arc energy.

Pulsed or waveform-controlled processes may require a measurement method appropriate to changing instantaneous values; simply multiplying unrelated displayed averages may not represent actual energy. Use the governing measurement procedure. Travel speed must reflect actual distance and time for the relevant bead, not a planned value copied from the WPS.

**Worked case.** At24 V,180 A and240 mm/min, arc energy is1.08 kJ/mm. If the exercise explicitly specifies efficiency0.8, net value is0.864 kJ/mm. Neither is compared to the other's limit without conversion and a defined basis. **Changed case:** speed halves at unchanged current/voltage; calculated arc energy doubles, but this is not permission to change the procedure.

## Q10 — Monitor production without creating false records

Verify the current WPS is available and understood, the correct process/consumables are used, thermal controls are met and any required cleaning or intermediate inspection occurs. Record actual observations with weld identity and time. A WPS lists permitted values; it is not evidence that those values were used. Sampling frequency must follow the inspection plan and be capable of supporting the required claim.

Weather and wind can affect shielding. Equipment changes, interruptions and operator changes may require checks. The inspector should not distract personnel into unsafe behavior to obtain a reading. When an excursion occurs, identify the affected weld segment and refer it for controlled disposition; do not erase or replace the original observation with a target value.

**Worked case.** A log contains exactly the same planned current and speed for every pass, but an actual observation differs. Retain the discrepancy, determine what was measured and assess affected work. **Changed case:** the operator corrects settings. Future compliance does not automatically validate the earlier segment.

## Q11 — Thermal cycle, HAZ and mechanical properties

Weld metal solidifies; adjacent base material experiences a heat-affected-zone thermal cycle without necessarily melting. Cooling rate, composition, peak temperature and prior structure affect the resulting properties. Grain growth, hardening, softening and precipitation can occur in different materials. The weld and HAZ may not share the parent material's original properties.

Strength, hardness, ductility, toughness and corrosion resistance are distinct. A hardness value can help characterize a condition but is not a universal substitute for toughness or corrosion testing. Dilution mixes base and filler contributions in the weld. An alloy calculation or chemistry reading does not alone establish the final structure and properties.

**Worked case.** A weld passes tensile strength but fails the project's supplied impact criterion. It is not accepted by averaging the two results; the required properties are independent. **Changed case:** hardness is normal. That does not negate the impact result or establish the missing acceptance basis.

## Q12 — Hydrogen cracking and delayed examination

Hydrogen cracking in susceptible steels involves hydrogen, a susceptible structure and tensile stress. Fabrication controls aim to manage those interacting factors. Moisture/contamination, cooling behavior, restraint and thermal history deserve attention. Cracking may appear after welding, so an immediate examination may not satisfy a required delayed examination.

Do not infer a universal delay period from this course. Use the applicable specification and procedure. If a delay is required, document the actual completion time and examination time. Repairing an indication needs cause review and the required post-repair examination, not simply grinding until it is invisible.

**Worked case.** A synthetic project requires examination at least24 hours after welding. Welding ends Tuesday14:00 and examination is recorded Wednesday09:00:19 hours, five short. The record does not meet the supplied timing requirement. **Changed case:** another five hours pass without re-examination. Elapsed time alone does not convert the earlier examination into the required later examination.

## Q13 — Solidification cracking, lamellar tearing and reheat concerns

Solidification cracking develops during the final stages of weld solidification under a susceptible combination of composition, bead geometry and strain. Lamellar tearing concerns through-thickness deformation in susceptible rolled plate, often in restrained joint arrangements. Reheat/stress-relaxation concerns have their own material and thermal-history basis. These are not interchangeable “bad weld” labels.

Use timing, location, morphology, material and process records to build candidates. A crack near a weld can be in the weld, HAZ or parent plate; location affects investigation and repair planning. Joint design and restraint can require engineering change. Repeating the same repair without addressing the cause may reproduce the problem.

**Worked case.** A tee attachment has parent-plate separations roughly parallel to the plate surface after a highly restrained repair. Consider lamellar tearing and seek appropriate characterization rather than assuming all indications are weld-metal porosity. **Changed case:** indications appear after heat treatment instead; the chronology changes the candidate review.

## Q14 — Porosity, inclusions, fusion and penetration

Porosity is trapped gas; slag or other inclusions are entrapped nonmetallic material; lack of fusion is failure to fuse at an intended interface; incomplete penetration means the specified penetration is not achieved. Some joint designs intentionally use partial penetration, so compare with the actual design before labeling a root condition unacceptable. A discontinuity becomes a rejectable defect when it fails the applicable criteria.

Preparation, cleanliness, shielding, technique, parameters and interpass cleaning can contribute differently. Appearance alone may not establish cause or full extent. Plan examination for expected morphology and orientation. A volumetric method useful for rounded indications may have limitations for tight planar indications in unfavorable orientations.

**Worked case.** Radiography finds rounded indications while a sidewall-fusion concern remains based on process observations. The rounded findings do not exclude planar problems. Review method capability and appropriate additional examination. **Changed case:** a smooth cap is produced after dressing; surface appearance does not establish internal fusion.

## Q15 — Weld profile, distortion and surface conditions

Undercut, overlap, excess reinforcement, concavity, misalignment and distortion describe different conditions. Measure the feature with suitable equipment and compare it with the supplied acceptance basis. A large bead does not automatically mean greater effective strength; profile can create stress concentration or conceal inadequate fusion. Arc strikes and temporary attachment scars can require examination even outside the finished weld.

Distortion arises from nonuniform heating and contraction, interacting with sequence and restraint. Straightening or heat correction requires an approved method appropriate to the material. Do not authorize hammering, flame heating or removal of material simply to make an assembly fit. Dimensional and metallurgical requirements both remain relevant.

**Worked case.** A joint meets weld-size requirements but is outside the drawing's alignment limit. It has a dimensional nonconformance despite adequate apparent size. **Changed case:** alignment is corrected by an undocumented heat treatment. The dimensional issue may improve while a new thermal-history issue is introduced.

## Q16 — Stainless, duplex and dissimilar welding

Stainless grades differ in structure and welding response. Austenitic, ferritic, martensitic and duplex materials do not share one universal heat-input or treatment rule. Corrosion resistance can be affected by composition, sensitization, phase balance, heat tint, contamination and fabrication history. Correct filler and shielding are part of a complete procedure, not independent guarantees.

Dissimilar joints require review of dilution, expansion, strength, corrosion and thermal treatment compatibility. A filler chosen solely because its nominal strength is high may be inappropriate. Follow the qualified procedure and preserve traceability through the transition. Purging and post-weld cleaning must use approved requirements and methods.

**Worked case.** A duplex weld has correct filler chemistry but exceeded its synthetic maximum interpass requirement. Chemistry does not erase the thermal excursion; assess the affected weld and properties under the approved process. **Changed case:** the cap color appears acceptable. Appearance is not proof of the required phase balance or all corrosion properties.

## Q17 — Other alloys, brazing and specialized joining

Aluminum, copper alloys, nickel alloys, titanium and cast irons have different conductivity, oxide, contamination and metallurgical behaviors. A successful steel procedure cannot be copied without qualification and compatibility review. Titanium needs particular contamination/shielding control; aluminum oxide and hydrogen-related porosity require suitable process control; cast-iron repair introduces its own cracking and structural concerns.

Brazing joins with a filler melting below the base material; joint clearance, cleanliness, wetting, filler and heating cycle matter. It is not fusion welding under another name. Specialized processes such as resistance, laser or electron-beam joining require their own equipment and qualification basis. This course teaches inspection questions, not operating instructions for unfamiliar processes.

**Worked case.** A brazed connection is proposed as a substitute for a specified welded pressure joint. The substitution requires design/code and procedure approval, not merely a leak test. **Changed case:** the leak test passes. It does not establish every strength, temperature or service requirement.

## Q18 — PWHT and other thermal treatments

Post-weld heat treatment may be required to achieve specified properties or stress relief, but it is not universally beneficial to every material. Verify the approved heating method, temperature range, hold duration, rates, uniformity, thermocouple positions, calibration and chart identity. A chart must be traceable to the actual weld and treatment load.

Local treatment introduces gradients and restraint that require a suitable engineered basis. Missing thermocouple coverage cannot be repaired by relabeling a nearby reading. Interrupted cycles and excursions need disposition. A final temperature alone does not prove the required history, and a treatment certificate does not replace the raw evidence needed by the inspection plan.

**Worked case.** Synthetic procedure requires at least60 minutes in a specified band. The chart records42 minutes before dropping out. It is18 minutes short; a technician cannot simply sign60 because that was planned. **Changed case:** a later cycle is performed. Its combined acceptability needs the approved treatment basis, not an assumed addition of unrelated periods.

## Q19 — Visual inspection and measurement practice

Visual inspection starts with access, surface condition, lighting and the required viewing method. Identify the weld, inspect the specified surfaces and measure relevant features using suitable verified gauges. A photograph without scale, angle or identity can support documentation but may not support precise acceptance measurements.

Calipers, fillet gauges, bridge-cam gauges, mirrors and borescopes each have intended uses and limitations. Select the tool for the feature and avoid forcing it into a misleading contact. Record actual values, location and uncertainty where it affects a boundary. Personal visual acuity and qualification requirements follow the applicable program, not an invented universal rule.

**Worked case.** A photo suggests a6 mm fillet, but the scale is behind the weld and perspective is oblique. Obtain a proper measurement rather than treating pixels as millimeters. **Changed case:** a calibrated gauge is used at one end only. It establishes that location, not uniform size along the entire required length.

## Q20 — Surface and volumetric NDE

PT addresses surface-breaking indications on suitable nonporous surfaces; MT requires suitable ferromagnetic material and magnetization for the expected orientation. RT and UT have different sensitivities, geometry and orientation limits. Advanced methods remain dependent on qualified procedures, calibration, coverage and interpretation. No method finds every possible flaw under every condition.

Select examination to meet the construction/repair requirement and the anticipated morphology. Check examiner qualification, procedure revision, equipment verification, coverage map, identification and acceptance basis. A report with no relevant indications is not a claim of defect-free material everywhere. Radiation work and other hazardous examinations require authorized controls; students review records, not operate sources.

**Worked case.** MT is specified by habit for an austenitic stainless joint. Verify suitability and select the applicable approved method instead of assuming all steel is magnetically examinable. **Changed case:** PT is used but an internal lack-of-fusion concern remains. A surface method does not automatically resolve it.

## Q21 — Destructive qualification tests and interpretation

Tensile, bend, impact, hardness, macrographic and other tests establish different aspects of a qualification or material condition. Verify specimen identity, location, orientation, dimensions, preparation, test method and actual results. A passed result for another coupon is not support for this PQR. Required tests depend on the governing qualification basis and service requirements.

In a tensile exercise, engineering stress equals load divided by original cross-sectional area. Unit consistency matters: N/mm² is MPa. Bend-test indications require the actual criteria and interpretation; not every surface feature is treated identically. Impact results require specified temperature, specimen size and individual/average rules where applicable. Do not substitute a generic pass number.

**Worked case.** A synthetic tensile specimen has area100 mm² and maximum load52,000 N:520 MPa. If the supplied minimum is500 MPa, that numerical criterion passes, but identity and all other required tests still matter. **Changed case:** specimen area is unverified; the calculated strength is not established.

## Q22 — Repairs, excavation and re-examination

A repair needs an approved scope, defect removal method, confirmation of excavation condition, suitable procedure/personnel, thermal controls and required re-examination. Repeated repairs can change geometry and material history. Identify limits and escalation requirements from the actual governing basis. Do not keep grinding or rewelding until a report appears clean without documenting the sequence.

Examination after excavation should address the removed indication and the remaining material; examination after welding should address the finished repair under the required timing and methods. A successful leak test is not a universal substitute for weld examination or property requirements. Temporary repairs and in-service welding require specialized engineering and safety controls outside this classroom's authorization.

**Worked case.** A crack is ground away visually, but no required excavation examination occurs before rewelding. The hold point was missed. Record the nonconformance and obtain disposition; do not backdate an inspection. **Changed case:** final RT is acceptable. It does not prove every missed intermediate requirement was satisfied.

## Q23 — Hot-work and examination safety

Welding introduces ignition, electrical, radiation, fume, gas and thermal hazards. Establish the applicable site authorization, isolation, atmosphere evaluation, ventilation, fire protection and access controls before work. An empty vessel can retain flammable or toxic residues and connected paths. Confined-space requirements can apply in addition to hot-work requirements. Oxygen must not be improvised as ventilation.

Consumables, coatings and base materials influence fumes. Use the relevant exposure controls and respiratory program where required; a generic face covering is not a universal welding-fume solution. Keep cylinders and electrical equipment under applicable controls. Inspectors must not enter an unsafe area simply to witness a hold point. Emergency response takes priority over finishing a measurement.

**Worked case.** Hot work is requested on a drained tank with no verified isolation or atmosphere record. Do not treat “empty” as safe. Use the approved authorization and hazard-control process. **Changed case:** an old permit exists from yesterday. Verify current conditions and validity; the old document is not automatic authorization today.

## Q24 — Final dossier and nonconformance closure

The completion record should connect weld identity to drawing revision, material traceability, WPS/PQR basis, personnel qualification, consumables, fit-up and production observations, thermal treatments, examinations, repairs and final disposition. Required signatures must reflect actual reviewed evidence. Missing records are identified as missing, not replaced with target values or memory.

A nonconformance record states the requirement, actual condition, affected scope, containment, authorized disposition, corrective action and verification. Rework, repair, engineering acceptance and rejection are different outcomes. The inspector cannot independently waive a contractual or code requirement because the schedule is tight. Preserve superseded and corrected records so the history remains auditable.

**Worked case.** A dossier has complete NDE but an unresolved consumable substitution and missing treatment chart. Do not label the weld fully accepted. Close only the demonstrated NDE requirement and retain the other decisions with owners. **Changed case:** an engineering disposition arrives for the substitution. That closes its actual scope; it does not create the missing thermal evidence.

# VUC043 process and metallurgy clinics

Eight required formative extensions ofQ04,Q09,Q11,Q13,Q16–Q21. Work them during the corresponding chapter/workshop allocations. They provide no field authorization or external-code acceptance values.

## C01 — Cooling rate and material history

The same chemistry can develop different microstructures after different thermal cycles. Heating affects phases and grain size; cooling affects transformation products. Rapid cooling can produce hard regions in susceptible steels and increase hydrogen-cracking concern when hydrogen and tensile stress are present. Slower cooling is not universally better: excessive exposure can impair other required properties. Follow the qualified thermal envelope rather than maximizing or minimizing heat without a basis.

Thickness, attachments, restraint and ambient conditions affect heat flow. A thick production joint can behave differently from a thin demonstration coupon. TWI's carbon/low-alloy guidance also distinguishes material-processing histories. [S17]

**Case.** Two joints use identical current/voltage but differ in thickness and restraint. Explain why that does not establish equivalent cracking risk. **Worked answer:** heat flow, stress, geometry and qualification applicability differ; verify the actual approved controls. **Changed case:** identical preheat values were measured at different locations. The measurement evidence may not establish equivalent joint temperatures either.

## C02 — Carbon equivalent is a bounded comparison

Carbon-equivalent approaches combine alloying contributions to help characterize steel weldability concerns. They are not universal weld/no-weld tests and do not establish preheat alone. Different formulas have different intended applications. TWI relates IIW carbon equivalent to composition's contribution to hydrogen-cracking susceptibility. [S18]

For this exercise the supplied formula is CE=C+Mn/6+(Cr+Mo+V)/5+(Ni+Cu)/15, using stated weight-percent numbers. SteelA: C0.12,Mn1.20,Cr0.30,Mo0.10,V0.05,Ni0.30,Cu0.15. CE=0.12+0.20+0.09+0.03=0.44. SteelB differs only inC0.18:CE0.50. The0.06 difference is a composition comparison within the method's applicability, not an acceptance limit.

**Case.** A supervisor saysA needs no preheat because its CE is lower. **Worked answer:** lower relative value does not settle thickness, hydrogen, restraint or qualification requirements. **Changed case:** someone proposes the same formula for a stainless alloy. Establish applicability first; a computed number is not automatically meaningful.

## C03 — Filler is not final weld metal

Fusion mixes filler and base-metal contributions; previous deposited layers may also contribute. This dilution changes final composition. Geometry, process and parameters influence the result. CWB distinguishes filler identity from weld-metal properties. [S19] A suitable filler certificate therefore does not alone establish a corrosion-resistant overlay's deposited composition and finished thickness.

**Synthetic exercise.** Assume a simple mass mixture without losses:70% filler containing20% elementX and30% base containing2%. Estimated weldX=0.70×20+0.30×2=14.6%. At50/50 it is11%. This model does not predict segregation, phases or actual mechanical properties; it demonstrates why20% filler composition cannot be assigned directly to the weld.

**Worked decision:** obtain the specified deposit evidence and procedure support. **Changed case:** outer-layer chemistry meets its criterion but finished overlay thickness is unmeasured. Chemistry does not close thickness or interface condition.

## C04 — Process-specific observation

SMAW inspection includes electrode handling, relevant arc technique and slag removal. GTAW requires attention to shielding, cleanliness, tungsten condition and filler identity. GMAW includes wire/gas, transfer mode and actual parameters. FCAW adds its flux-cored consumable/shielding conditions; SAW includes flux/wire controls and process-specific visibility limits. These are inspection prompts, not universal settings.

Record a precise observation linked to joint/pass/time. “Shielding interrupted during this root segment” is more useful than “poor technique.” Do not compensate for wind or contamination by improvising unapproved settings.

**Case.** A GTAW root loses shielding and is covered by fill without review. **Worked answer:** identify the affected region, retain the observation and obtain qualified examination/disposition. An attractive cap does not establish root quality. **Changed case:** shielding is restored before the next pass; future control does not retroactively clear the earlier deposit.

## C05 — Strength, ductility and toughness

Strength, ductility and toughness describe different aspects of behavior. Test results depend on specimen geometry, orientation, temperature and method. They cannot be detached from those conditions and treated as universal evidence.

**Original tensile exercise.** A50 mm gauge length becomes62 mm after fracture measurement under the supplied procedure. Elongation=(62−50)/50×100=24%. Final minimum area48 mm² from original80 gives reduction of area=(80−48)/80×100=40%. Neither establishes impact toughness, and an impact pass does not establish tensile strength.

**Case.** A passing impact value lacks test temperature and specimen identity. **Worked answer:** the value alone cannot establish compliance with the required test condition or coupon. **Changed case:** identity is corrected but temperature stays missing. Close only identity; do not infer temperature from a laboratory's usual practice.

## C06 — Hardness and macrosection evidence

A hardness survey needs method, scale/load, location, preparation and criteria. Different scales or locations are not automatically comparable. A traverse can locate hard or softened regions, but sparse points can miss a narrow band. A macrosection shows one section's profile and certain discontinuities, not every point along a production weld.

**Synthetic record.** A project limits specified HAZ readings to250 on the stated scale. Base180,HAZ265,weld220:HAZ exceeds by15. Their average221.67 does not satisfy a local maximum rule. Verify test validity and obtain the approved metallurgy/qualification disposition. Do not grind a location merely to improve its next reading.

**Changed case:** another location reads240. It does not erase265 without a valid reason to reject the first measurement and a suitable sampling decision. Retain both. A macrosection from another coupon cannot establish this weld's condition.

## C07 — Ferrite, sensitization and phase balance

A ferrite measurement, chemistry result and corrosion test answer different questions. Ferrite number and ferrite volume percentage should not be casually treated as identical. Use the specified method, unit, location and acceptance basis. A diagram prediction is not automatically a measurement of the finished joint.

Sensitization can affect intergranular-corrosion susceptibility in an appropriate environment. Duplex phase balance and harmful precipitates require their own review. Do not infer these conditions from cap color or one chemistry reading.

**Case.** A report says “8 ferrite” without method, unit or location while the project specifies a ferrite-number method. **Worked answer:** obtain traceable evidence before comparing the criterion. **Changed case:** method is confirmed but only the cap was measured while other regions are required. Retain the coverage gap.

## C08 — Qualification is not production acceptance

A successful procedure coupon demonstrates performance under its applicable qualification basis. A personnel test demonstrates that person's performance within its range. Neither proves every production weld was made correctly. Production still needs traceability, actual controls, inspection and deviation disposition. Conversely, a production examination pass does not reconstruct missing qualification.

**Case.** A vendor presents a successful qualification coupon and asks to waive examination on twenty production joints. **Worked answer:** follow the governing inspection plan; qualification evidence does not automatically waive production requirements. **Changed case:** all twenty have satisfactory surface results but one used an unsupported process. Surface findings do not establish qualification applicability or every internal/property requirement.

## Clinic assessment

Each clinic earns0–2 for five elements: correct distinction, use of evidence, calculation/interpretation, needed action and bounded authority. Pass8/10 with no invented acceptance, unsafe intervention or falsified record. Record original response, feedback and changed response. Clinics are required learning applications within mapped instructional time, not extra final-exam questions.

# Inspector-specific instruction

These eight chapters extend Q01–Q24 in the coursebook. They address outcomes distinct from simply reviewing metallurgy or accumulating more exam questions. External AWS CWI status requires its own eligibility, vision and examination process; VNDRLY completion does not establish that status.[A01]

## Q25 — Qualification, vision and professional integrity

Inspection depends on competence, suitable vision and the conditions in which an examination is made. A course certificate alone does not demonstrate experience, task-specific qualification or employer appointment. AWS's public CWI program specifies a vision test, documented education/experience and three examination parts.[A01] Do not advertise this learning program as fulfilling those separate requirements or an AWS endorsement.

Before an assigned inspection, establish the required role, scope and vision/qualification evidence under the actual program. A visual examination may be compromised by lighting, access, surface condition or unsuitable corrective aids even when the inspector has valid credentials. Report limitations and arrange appropriate controls rather than invent a result. Never falsify a vision form, examination record or witnessed inspection to help a colleague meet a deadline.

## Q26 — Read the entire drawing instruction

A weld instruction includes more than the small weld-type symbol. Identify the reference convention, arrow/joint, side, size, length, spacing, contour/finish and tail notes where present. In the AWS-style convention discussed by Miller, below the reference line denotes arrow-side information and above denotes other-side information.[A02] Do not silently transfer that rule to a drawing using a different standard/system. Ask which convention and edition govern.

On an intermittent weld, distinguish segment length from center-to-center pitch when that is the stated convention. Pitch is not the clear gap. A contour instruction is not necessarily a command to grind unless the actual finishing instruction says so. Requirements on both sides do not automatically prescribe sequence. Resolve illegible or contradictory callouts before fabrication.

**Original text drawing A.** One reference line points to sideA of a T joint. Its explicitly supplied legend says “below=line arrow side; above=other side.” Below: fillet6 mm, segment40 mm, pitch100 mm. No above instruction. ## Q27 — Gauge selection, setup and contact

Choose an instrument that measures the intended quantity: leg size, profile height, undercut depth, mismatch, gap or another specified feature. AWS's visual-testing description identifies a range of inspection tools; owning them is not evidence of competent use.[A03]

**Worked case.** A profile gauge bridges a raised spatter particle and reads0.4 mm higher than on the correct reference surface. The contact error is not actual reinforcement. ## Q28 — Measurement uncertainty and decision rules

Resolution is the smallest indicated increment; accuracy and uncertainty concern how the reading relates to the quantity. A digital display with extra decimals is not automatically more reliable. Calibration/verification status, contact, alignment, surface condition and operator technique can influence a measurement. Record units, reference and the decision rule required by the project.

When a tolerance boundary falls within the stated uncertainty, avoid an unjustified definite pass. The actual acceptance procedure may prescribe a decision rule or better measurement; obtain and apply it. Never round a nonconforming value upward simply because a nominal dimension has fewer decimals. Conversely, do not invent a universal guard band as if it were a code requirement.

**Worked case.** Synthetic local minimum6.0 mm; reported measurement6.1±0.2 mm spans5.9–6.3. Under the exercise rule “indeterminate when interval crosses the limit,” the result is indeterminate, not proven pass. **Changed case:** a qualified improved method gives6.12±0.05, interval6.07–6.17; it supports pass under that supplied rule, provided location and method validity are established.

## Q29 — Navigate requirements, exceptions and precedence

Code navigation means finding the applicable scope and definition, then the requirement and its referenced tables/notes/exceptions. A keyword search that lands on a convenient number is insufficient. Confirm edition, structure/service, material/joint/process and any project modifications. Keep the exact clause/table identifier in the inspection record; distinguish normative requirement from informative commentary.

An exception applies only when its conditions are established. A table note can change the meaning of a column or range. Precedence conflicts require the actual contract/authority route, not an inspector's invented hierarchy. Learn the navigation method using the complete synthetic specification supplied here; do not claim that passing it proves mastery of an unavailable external code.

**Worked case.** Specification S43 permits an alternate threshold only for classB joints with conditionX documented. The joint is B but X unknown; the alternate is not established. **Changed case:** X is documented but the project addendum disallows the alternate. Resolve under the supplied precedence rule; don't stop reading at the favorable exception.

## Q30 — Describe indications before calling them defects

An indication or observed discontinuity becomes a rejectable defect through an applicable acceptance basis, not merely because it has a technical name. Record morphology, location, dimensions, method/coverage and evidence quality. Surface observations may not reveal subsurface extent or root cause. Avoid labeling every linear feature a crack without appropriate examination, but treat credible crack concerns under the actual escalation requirements rather than dismissing them cosmetically.

Distinguish undercut, overlap, porosity, incomplete fusion and other features taught in the foundation. Some are visible only in certain locations or by suitable NDT. A visual inspector should accurately describe what is seen and route additional examination; they must not issue an NDT interpretation outside competence.

**Worked case.** A linear toe indication is visible but its nature is uncertain. Map and describe it, apply the project's referral/hold requirement and obtain appropriate examination. **Changed case:** qualified examination identifies a nonrelevant surface mark. Record that disposition and evidence rather than retain an unsupported crack label.

## Q31 — Perform a structured visual examination

Prepare by confirming object identity, drawing/procedure, acceptance basis, safe access, surface readiness, lighting/view and instruments. Examine systematically so locations are not skipped. Use an agreed map and record measured values, observed features and inaccessible areas. Separate examination completion from acceptance: all planned areas may be viewed while a finding still fails.

Reconcile readings and descriptions into a report that another inspector can follow. Recheck suspect measurements without erasing the original. A photograph can support a mapped observation but may distort scale or perspective; it does not automatically replace a required measurement. The physical mockup task in this course requires the learner to demonstrate this sequence; a written answer alone does not suffice.

**Worked case.** An inert specimen has three marked stations; the learner measures two and says100% covered. Correct the record to the actual coverage and examine the third if safely possible. **Changed case:** third station is deliberately obscured by the assessor; state incomplete coverage and the needed next action, not a guessed value.

## Q32 — Defend a final inspection disposition

Combine identity, qualification, production conditions, geometry, examination, tests and repair history without allowing one favorable result to erase another failure. State the applicable requirement, actual evidence, conclusion and authorized next action. Where evidence is insufficient, explain the precise gap and who must resolve it. An inspector can be decisive about an incomplete basis without inventing engineering authority.

Maintain independence when commercial pressure encourages a convenient interpretation. Preserve appeal/review routes and technically sound alternative explanations. A learning assessment should reward supported reasoning, not memorization of the assessor's wording. External CWI eligibility and exams remain separate from the VNDRLY award.

**Worked case.** A specimen meets geometry but the actual WPS did not cover the joint and final NDT is absent. Geometry is supported; overall acceptance is not. **Changed case:** valid NDT arrives but WPS issue remains. Close only the examination gap and retain procedure disposition.

# VUC043 delivery and qualification rules

## Program definition

Audience: prospective welding inspectors and quality personnel who need instruction in welding fundamentals, metallurgy, production evidence, visual measurement and specification navigation. Prerequisites: basic industrial safety, arithmetic and drawing literacy. Instructor diagnoses these with the supplied geometry/material records and provides necessary teaching. This program does not replace AWS's documented experience, vision or examinations and does not predict an external exam pass.

Outcome: accurately identify inspection basis, trace material/process records, recognize metallurgical/production issues, interpret supplied tests, use benign visual gauges, read the supplied drawing/specification and communicate a bounded decision. Instructor/assessor needs welding-inspection and measurement competence; use qualified specialists for issues outside competence. Employer authorization, live welding/NDT and actual code-based acceptance are separate.
