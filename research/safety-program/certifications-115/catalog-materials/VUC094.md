# VNDRLY/U Helicopter Administrator — Workplace Competence

VUC094. Original available-source package. This course supports assessment preparation and an evidence-based workplace decision; it does not issue OPITO accreditation, dispatch authority or dangerous-goods acceptance authority outside the assigned function.

## Q94-1 — Assessment scope, prerequisites and evidence

Workplace competence is demonstrated performance in a defined operating context, not an attendance record. Establish the employer, installation, aircraft-operator interface, personnel system and actual administrative duties to be assessed. Verify function-appropriate dangerous-goods preparation and system instruction. Check the candidate’s prior initial training and competence history against the applicable scheme. A training certificate is entry evidence; it does not prove today’s work is independent or correct.

Build an evidence plan covering ordinary movements, changes, identity discrepancies, freight queries, outages and handover. Assess real routine work where authorized and safe; use declared simulations for unusual or emergency conditions. Do not deliberately introduce wrong information into a live flight. Observe decisions before coaching. Identify work samples by date, revision and candidate contribution; authenticate witness accounts. A borrowed manifest with no evidence of who prepared it is not proof of competence.

Worked and changed applications: A portfolio contains ten manifests signed by the team but no individual attribution. Ask for authenticated candidate work and observe a current cycle; do not count ten signatures as ten competent performances. Changed case: prior training is current but system permissions differ at the new site. Require local system preparation and assess the actual function before endorsing it.

## Q94-2 — Planning actual sectors and amendments

Start with the current authorized schedule and distinguish flight, aircraft, sector, planned time, estimated time and actual event. Match people and consignments to each sector. A passenger continuing through an intermediate stop remains on the next sector; an offloaded passenger does not. The administration plan must remain coherent with the operator’s accepted routing and the HLO’s current restrictions.

Use a controlled change log when a flight is delayed, rerouted or replaced. Identify which records and recipients are affected. Check time zones and day changes explicitly. Do not manufacture a departure time to close an overdue screen. If a flight identifier is reused on another date, retain both date and sector identity so records do not merge. The assessor should challenge a changed route and require the candidate to trace every affected person and item.

Worked and changed applications: A Shore–Delta–Echo flight becomes Shore–Delta–Shore. The candidate updates the route but leaves Echo bags on the onward list. The task is incomplete until destination, bag routing, manifest and communications agree. Changed case: 23:50 UTC planned departure becomes 00:20 UTC next day; retain the correct date as well as time.

## Q94-3 — Identity, eligibility and actual boarding status

Verify identity through the approved records and check booking, destination and required travel eligibility. Similar names, duplicate bookings and a person using a colleague’s identifier are discrepancies to resolve, not opportunities to edit the record until it turns green. Refer eligibility decisions outside the role to the authorized verifier. Do not infer medical suitability from appearance or disclose unnecessary personal information.

Keep booked, checked-in, boarded and arrived states distinct. Reconcile removals and no-shows with bags and freight as required. The final headcount must agree with identities, not merely the number of lines. Observe the candidate responding to a plausible same-name mismatch. A correct decision includes holding dependent action, contacting the proper verifier and recording the resolution. A supervisor’s casual assurance does not replace the required evidence.

Worked and changed applications: Four names are on the manifest and four people are present, but one person is a substitute. Equal counts do not establish a correct manifest. Verify identity and eligibility, revise the record and obtain required acceptance before movement. Changed case: a booked person checks in and then cancels; remove associated baggage through the controlled process and do not mark arrival.

## Q94-4 — Mass inputs, measurement and independent checking

Apply the actual operator’s approved weighing basis, units and precision. Check scale status and the source of each input. Do not assume a historic value, clothing allowance or standard weight is permitted. The candidate must explain why the chosen input method is authorized and how an unreliable scale or missing measurement is handled. Accurate administration supplies data; it does not approve aircraft mass, balance or performance.

Calculate passenger, baggage and freight subtotals independently before checking the combined input total. Preserve counts and identities alongside masses. A replacement can leave total mass unchanged while changing every relevant person record. If pounds are converted to kilograms, record the original unit and conversion once. Never round down to pass a limit. Have a second authorized check where the actual procedure requires it, and retain correction history.

Worked and changed applications: People at 84, 79 and 92 kg total 255 kg; bags at 9, 6 and 11 kg total 26 kg; freight 35 kg gives 316 kg of recorded inputs. Removing the 79 kg person and 6 kg bag gives 231 kg. Changed case: add 86 kg plus an 8 kg bag, producing 325 kg, while revising identities, counts and distribution.

## Q94-5 — Manifest version control and acceptance

A manifest must describe the correct aircraft, date and sector with the required people, weights, baggage, freight and special information. Match the actual controlled items and people to it. The candidate should identify who prepared and checked the record, which version is current, and how the responsible ground and crew recipients received it. A saved record is not necessarily a transmitted or accepted one.

Late changes require deliberate revision and reconciliation. Supersede old prints and prevent an obsolete version remaining the boarding reference. If a recipient has an earlier version, hold the dependent administrative release until the required current-data process is complete. The assessor must inspect source inputs and receipt evidence rather than judging only formatting. Do not reward a neat document that contains unsupported data.

Worked and changed applications: Version 4 is correct in the system but the crew holds version 3. Provide and confirm the current checked version; do not say that the crew could have refreshed it. Changed case: an attachment is sent to the wrong recipient. Follow the actual privacy and operational correction process, confirm receipt by the proper party, and preserve the audit trail.

## Q94-6 — Baggage, freight and unusual items

Reconcile each item’s identity, count, mass, destination and authorized handling information. Maintain control after weighing so later additions do not invalidate the measurement. A bag tagged for a continuing passenger must not be left at an intermediate stop merely because most other bags are offloaded there. Unaccompanied or unusual freight requires the operator’s defined acceptance and tracking process.

Know the boundary between administrative inputs and loading approval. Aggregate mass does not prove floor loading, compartment suitability, restraint or center-of-gravity acceptability. Pass dimensions and handling details to authorized personnel when needed. If an item changes, remeasure or otherwise verify through the approved method and revise the affected record. Do not disguise freight as baggage to avoid review.

Worked and changed applications: A toolbox gains extra tools after its recorded weighing. Secure the change, reweigh through the approved method, revise the manifest and obtain required acceptance. Changed case: one of two identical-looking bags is destined for Echo rather than Delta. Use the item identity and route, not appearance or pile location, to reconcile it.

## Q94-7 — Dangerous-goods decisions within assigned function

Apply current function-specific preparation and operator instructions. Recognize possible undeclared dangerous goods in batteries, aerosols, fuel-containing tools or chemical packages without assuming that familiar appearance makes carriage acceptable. Ask the required questions and refer uncertainty to the trained authorized person. A workplace assessor must see the candidate’s actual boundaries, not merely a memorized list.

Record the query, safe hold, authorized decision and required communications. Do not open or repack a suspect leaking item as an administrative demonstration. Simulations use cards or harmless dummy objects. The candidate must not invent capacity or classification from size, and must not transfer an item from cargo to passenger baggage as a workaround. An unresolved hold remains unresolved even when the flight is late.

Worked and changed applications: A power bank has no readable capacity marking and the passenger is impatient. Follow the approved hold and specialist-verification process. Changed case: a damaged battery is discovered after initial acceptance. Use the operator’s safety or emergency route, notify responsible personnel and reconcile changed carriage information; a previous acceptance is not immunity from new evidence.

## Q94-8 — Briefing and the HLO–crew interface

Verify the required briefing and any special arrangements through the operator’s process. Record unresolved questions and ensure they reach the responsible person. Attendance is not proof of understanding; a person who misunderstands the destination or movement instructions needs correction before the next step. Administrative staff must not invent aircraft-specific emergency instructions.

Coordinate current manifest, actual identities and item reconciliation with the HLO and crew. Movement authorization belongs to the designated operational personnel. The administrator does not wave passengers onto a live deck because the computer shows ready. The assessor should observe clear, closed-loop communication and an appropriate hold when a restriction or discrepancy remains. A brief, accurate message is preferable to an ambiguous all-clear.

Worked and changed applications: The HLO reports a restriction after check-in is complete. Keep the hold visible and relay the changed status; do not equate check-in completion with permission to board. Changed case: the restriction is cleared but a passenger identity query remains. Resolve that separate hold rather than assuming one clearance covers everything.

## Q94-9 — Outage, approved fallback and restoration

Before an outage, know the approved manual or alternate process and the minimum information needed by crew, HLO and ground records. A fallback retains current sector, revision, identities, masses, counts, checks and acknowledgments. It does not justify using the only available stale print. If a required control cannot be achieved, hold dependent operations through the authorized route.

On restoration, reconcile manual events against the system before importing or re-entering them. Use unique movement identifiers, actual event sources and correction history. Prevent duplicate departures or conflicting site presence. Never infer arrival from a departure record. The assessor should trace one movement across both records and require an explanation for discrepancies. A successful synchronization message is not a data-quality verdict.

Worked and changed applications: A manual record confirms departure at 11:18 UTC while the system still shows booked. Verify the source and current revision, then reconcile through the authorized recovery process. Changed case: a retry produces two arrival events; establish which actual event occurred and correct the duplicate with an audit trail rather than silently deleting history.

## Q94-10 — Disruption, emergency information and handover

Maintain a clear distinction between confirmed events, estimates and unknowns during delays, diversions or emergencies. Supply authorized responders with current flight, sector and manifest information promptly through established channels. Do not invent a safe arrival or casualty report. The administrator supports information and accountability; air traffic, rescue and incident command remain outside this role.

Hand over current revisions, verified movement status, pending holds, manual records and next actions with acknowledgment. Preserve evidence after disruption. An incoming administrator must be able to identify the actual current version without guessing from file names. Restrict personal information to authorized recipients, including during emergencies. Assessor questioning should test how the candidate handles uncertainty, not pressure them to supply an unsupported answer.

Worked and changed applications: A flight returns to Shore instead of reaching Echo. Record the verified return and notify the proper parties; leave the planned Echo arrival unfulfilled. Changed case: relief staff receives an earlier manifest. Correct the version and obtain acknowledgment before another transaction relies on it.

## Q94-11 — Authenticity, consistency and competence judgment

A workplace decision must combine direct observation, authenticated work products and targeted questioning across the agreed scope. Distinguish an independent performance from one achieved after prompts. Record deficiencies and their consequences. A large portfolio of routine examples cannot compensate for a critical failure to hold an unsafe or unverified transaction. Sampling should cover meaningful variations rather than repeating the same easy task.

For this original package, require two independently observed routine cycles and declared changed-condition assessments for outage, identity mismatch, dangerous-goods uncertainty and diversion. Simulated evidence alone earns only a simulation result until required workplace observations are complete. Protect operational data using controlled references or redacted copies; never create public passenger dossiers as evidence. A second reviewer checks sufficiency and any appeal or conflict of interest.

Worked and changed applications: The candidate’s calculations are correct but a colleague supplied every revision decision. Record the prompting and assess a changed independent cycle after remediation. Changed case: a witness signs a generic statement with no date or task. Authenticate and supplement it; do not inflate the evidence count.

## Q94-12 — Maintaining scope, remediation and reassessment

Give feedback linked to the specific failed control: identity reconciliation, measurement basis, version distribution or actual-event tracking. Re-teach and practice that control before an independently assessed changed task. Retain the original failed evidence and the new result; do not overwrite the history to make the first attempt appear successful. The final decision must satisfy both knowledge and workplace performance requirements.

Record the role, operator/system context, observed tasks, limitations, assessor, reviewer and evidence references on the original VNDRLY outcome. It is not an OPITO credential or universal aviation authorization. Employer appointment remains separate. Changes to procedures, systems or assigned duties require review and additional evidence where material. Establish a review date under the employer’s competence scheme, not a promise that skills remain valid indefinitely.

Worked and changed applications: A candidate passes the written final but cannot manage an outage without coaching. Withhold workplace competence, provide targeted practice and assess a changed fallback and recovery sequence. Changed case: the candidate transfers to a different operator with different forms. Preserve prior evidence but require local preparation and assessment of changed controls before endorsing that context.

## I94 integrated competence decision

Observe two routine workplace cycles W-A and W-B, recording actual date, context, task, candidate actions, source records, required confirmations and observer. Then run both synthetic changed cycles independently. Controller supplies information only at the stated event; never coach the answer during the assessed attempt. The learner must leave uncertain actual events unresolved until evidence arrives. A competent reviewer maps every domain to authenticated evidence and records limitations.

Twelve dimensions follow P94-1 through P94-12. Score each 0 absent/unsafe, 1 major coaching, 2 safe but incomplete/prompted, 3 independently acceptable, 4 accurate independent performance with sound explanation. Each integrated assessment requires at least 39/48, every dimension at least 3, all required observations and stations passed, and no critical error. Critical errors include knowingly accepting a wrong/unmanifested person, fabricating verified weight or movement, unauthorized DG acceptance, knowingly using an obsolete manifest, directing movement outside authority or falsifying evidence. A high written score cannot offset these failures. Retain the result, remediate and reassess a changed case independently.

## Complete assessment trace

Each of twelve Q94 domains has two explained formative checks, a corresponding P94 observed station, a separate final case with three decisions and a changed retake. W94-1 authenticates evidence; W94-2 tests sector/identity; W94-3 tests mass inputs; W94-4 tests revision/fallback; W94-5 tests holds/operational interface; W94-6 tests recovery and award decisions. I94 requires two actual routine workplace cycles and both synthetic changed-condition cycles. F94 forms retain scope, actual evidence, revisions, movement status, handover and remediation. Protected exam forms and keys are outside the learner package.

## Maintenance and limitations

Before delivery, verify current operator procedures, aircraft interfaces, approved weighing and manifest methods, system permissions, DG prerequisites and employer competence policy. Record revisions and assess material changes. Do not manufacture live errors or emergencies for assessment. Actual workplace performance cannot be inferred from authored materials or simulation alone. No operational records have been changed by authoring this package.
