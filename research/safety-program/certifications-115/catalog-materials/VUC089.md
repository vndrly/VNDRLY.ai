# VNDRLY/U Blaster and Sprayer — Initial Training

Program VUC089. Original materials, available-source basis, 18 November 2026. This is not OPITO approval or an external award. Learners must be able to understand instructions and complete practical activity safely; provide accessible instruction without removing safety-critical performance. Twelve chapters teach supervised beginner work. Training examples C89/S89/D89 are fictional and must never control a live job.

## Q89-1 — Role, job scope and the coating system

Industrial coating work preserves a prepared substrate by applying a specified system. A visually attractive finish is not proof of adhesion, thickness or corrosion protection. A beginner works within supervision, the job specification and the equipment instructions; completing this course does not appoint an independent coating inspector or authorize confined-space entry. Identify the asset, substrate, existing coating, required preparation, each coating layer, inspection hold points and who may release them. Treat the specification, product technical data sheet (TDS), application guide, safety data sheet (SDS) and equipment manual as different documents serving different decisions. The TDS gives product application constraints; the SDS explains chemical hazards and controls; neither alone is a job permit.

Before work, compare product names, component identities, batch/shelf-life information and document revisions against the job pack. Resolve contradictions with the supervisor or coating specialist before preparing a batch. A changed product is not accepted because its color matches. Inspect access, simultaneous work, services, weather protection, ventilation and waste arrangements. Brief the crew on start, stop and emergency communication. Record outstanding hold points rather than allowing a schedule target to become authorization.

Worked example: J89 specifies system C89 on steel panel A. A storekeeper supplies component B for a different system. Although both cans say hardener, the learner quarantines the mismatch, identifies the missing compatible component and requests correction. The learner does not experiment with a small mix. Changed application: the correct product arrives but the surface-preparation release is unsigned; product availability does not release that hold. The job remains stopped at that boundary.

## Q89-2 — Hazard recognition and containment

Assess the old surface as well as the new materials. Abrasion can release hazardous material from existing coatings or the substrate, even when the chosen abrasive is described as low hazard. Unknown old paint requires characterization and an approved removal plan. Do not infer absence of lead, other metals or silica from appearance. Keep neighboring workers, vehicles and occupied areas outside the affected zone. Containment, extraction and suitable cleanup arrangements are planned before work, not after dust escapes.

Use the site risk assessment to address airborne contaminants, noise, moving hoses, flying particles, slips, manual handling, access and pressure. Select controls in order: eliminate or substitute where practicable, contain and ventilate the process, manage access, then use the specified personal protection. Confirm that the dust collector and ventilation are suitable for the material and ignition risks. A domestic fan is not an engineered control for a flammable spray atmosphere. Noise protection does not replace the assessment of exposure or equipment controls.

Worked example: a panel with unidentified legacy coating is placed in the training area. The correct response is to use the known clean training panel and hold the legacy panel for investigation. Simply wearing an extra disposable mask would not resolve the unknown hazard. Changed application: the assessment is complete but the extraction failure alarm operates. Stop using the process, make equipment safe by its procedure, leave the affected area as instructed and obtain restoration and release. Do not silence the alarm to finish the last patch.

## Q89-3 — Respiratory protection, chemicals and personal readiness

Respirator selection belongs to the employer's respiratory-protection program and exposure assessment. A face covering chosen by the trainee is not an adequate substitute. Where tight-fitting equipment is required, medical evaluation, suitable fit testing, training and a user seal check are distinct requirements. Facial hair or other interference at the seal is a problem to resolve before use. Supplied breathing air and its connections must be appropriate for breathing; ordinary plant compressed air is not presumed safe. Learn the actual equipment's checks, air-supply failure response and limitations before use.

Read the selected product's SDS for inhalation, skin, eye, sensitization, fire, incompatibility and first-aid information. Gloves must be suitable for the actual chemical and exposure duration, not merely described as chemical resistant. Arrange washing and contamination control so workers do not take residue into eating areas or vehicles. Keep labels with decanted materials under the applicable workplace system. Never use solvent to clean skin. Tell the instructor about a condition or discomfort affecting safe participation through the appropriate confidential route.

Worked example: a trainee's supplied-air hose has an unidentified connector that fits a nearby utility outlet. The trainee refuses connection until the designated breathing-air system and compatibility are verified. Physical fit does not establish safe air quality. Changed application: the approved respiratory equipment becomes damaged during practice. Stop and replace or repair through the authorized process; a reduced task duration does not compensate for failed protection.

## Q89-4 — Blast equipment and pressure control

Identify the actual compressor, receiver or pressure vessel, metering arrangement, hose, couplings, restraint devices, remote control, nozzle and extraction system using the manufacturer's diagram. Inspection is systematic: identity and pressure suitability, inspection status, hose condition, coupling security, restraints, controls and safe routing. Missing identity or damaged components are quarantined. A pressure rating on one part does not establish the entire assembly's suitability. Never connect, disconnect, open or repair pressurized equipment on the assumption that the supply switch being off proves zero stored energy.

The instructor demonstrates the exact manufacturer start, remote-stop and shutdown sequence on the approved training installation. The learner rehearses it unpressurized, then performs it under direct supervision after readiness checks. Verify the remote control operates as designed before production. Do not tie it open, bypass a control or use a makeshift nozzle restraint. Keep the nozzle directed into the designated safe work zone, with stable posture and controlled hose routing. Depressurization, isolation and verification precede maintenance or blockage investigation according to the actual manual.

Worked example: releasing the remote control does not stop flow during a controlled test. The instructor uses the installation's planned safe shutdown; the system is removed from service. The learner does not continue by relying on the compressor operator to guess when to stop. Changed application: a hose shows a bulge but no leak. Reject it before pressure is applied. The absence of a visible leak is not evidence that a damaged pressure component is safe.

## Q89-5 — Surface preparation and inspection hold points

Remove contaminants by the approved method before abrasive preparation; blasting is not a universal way to remove grease or soluble contamination. Protect surfaces and fittings not to be treated. Deal with sharp edges, weld irregularities and other specified preparation needs through the authorized procedure. Choose the approved abrasive and method for the substrate and coating system. Excessive preparation can damage thin sections or distort components, while insufficient preparation can leave weak material under the coating.

Separate three questions: cleanliness, profile and contamination. A clean-looking surface can have the wrong profile or residual salts. The job specifies the visual reference, measurement method, sampling and acceptance criteria; use the actual authorized standards or comparators where required. This package does not reproduce proprietary visual grades or supply a substitute visual acceptance standard. Practice recognizing these separate questions on labeled training samples, then perform the prescribed tests with verified instruments. Do not average away an unacceptable location unless the specified acceptance rule expressly allows that treatment.

Worked example: fictional S89 requires recorded profile values from 40 to 65 micrometres at every listed training location, with an instructor-issued cleanliness release and contamination release. Values 48, 56 and 68 fail the upper limit at the third location. The learner holds coating and asks for an approved correction; painting more thickly is not an authorized repair. Changed application: all profile values pass but the contamination release is missing. The hold remains. After preparation protect the surface and recheck if contamination, condensation or delay could invalidate its condition.

## Q89-6 — Climate, condensation and timing

Measure the conditions required by the actual coating documents at the work surface, not merely a distant weather station. Air temperature, relative humidity, surface temperature and dew point affect application and cure. A surface may be colder than the surrounding air. Record instrument identity, location, time and readings, then apply the product-specific limits. This course's synthetic limits are classroom rules only; they must never be copied into a real coating specification.

Card C89 requires surface temperature at least 3 degrees Celsius above dew point, surface temperature from 10 to 35 degrees Celsius inclusive, and relative humidity no greater than 80 percent. All three conditions must pass. At surface 18, dew point 14 and humidity 74, the margin is 4 and the card permits the climate condition. At surface 16 with the same dew point the margin is 2, so application is held. A good humidity reading does not offset a failed dew-point margin. Monitor changes during work and stop when conditions leave the permitted range.

Worked example: the initial reading passes, but the next recorded surface reading is 12 with dew point 10. The margin is 2, so the learner stops application and records the affected time and area for review. Waiting until the shift ends to report it would conceal a quality uncertainty. Changed application: surface 10, dew point 7 and humidity 80 meets all inclusive training boundaries; surface 9.9 fails the minimum even though the margin may remain adequate. Real sampling frequency and restart release follow the job plan.

## Q89-7 — Mixing, induction, pot life and batch traceability

Identify every component before opening it. Verify the mixing basis: a volume ratio is not automatically a mass ratio because component densities may differ. Follow the product instructions for pre-mixing, combining, mixing technique, induction and any permitted thinner. Do not guess a thinner or add solvent to extend a material's life. Use clean suitable measuring equipment, capture spills and label the mixed batch with its identity and timing. Keep traceability to the component batches and the area coated.

Fictional C89 uses base and curing component at 4: 1 by volume, no thinner, 10 minutes induction and 60 minutes pot life measured from initial combination at the stated training temperature. A 5 litre batch uses 4 litres base and 1 litre curing component. For 2.5 litres use 2 and 0.5. If combined at 09:00, the earliest application is 09:10 and the discard deadline is 10:00; induction consumes part of the pot life. This card is a calculation exercise, not a recipe for actual chemicals. Real products may define timing differently and their instructions govern.

Worked example: the crew expects to apply 1.8 litres but proposes a full 5 litre batch close to the end of the allowed window. The learner plans a permitted smaller batch only if the actual product allows accurate partial mixing; otherwise the supervisor resolves packaging and waste. Changed application: a batch remains liquid after the stated pot-life deadline. Appearance does not extend the deadline. Segregate and dispose through the approved waste route instead of blending expired material into a fresh batch.

## Q89-8 — Application technique and safe spraying

Set up the actual spray equipment, coating and tip using their compatible instructions. Confirm the work zone, ventilation, electrical and ignition controls, pressure suitability, guards, trigger lock and hose condition. Practice the movement first without energized discharge. During supervised application maintain the specified distance, orientation, overlap and speed appropriate to the actual system; excessive pressure is not the default cure for a poor pattern. Stop to diagnose material, tip, equipment and setup rather than compensating blindly.

Airless equipment can inject fluid through skin. Never point the gun at a person, place a hand over the tip or use a hand to find or stop a leak. Follow the manufacturer's pressure-relief and isolation procedure before clearing, servicing or changing relevant components. A small-looking injection wound needs immediate emergency medical attention; tell responders the material and mechanism and provide the SDS without delaying care. The actual manual determines the equipment sequence, not a generic sequence from another model.

Worked example: a spray pattern develops tails. The learner stops, secures and relieves the system as instructed, then asks the supervisor to review the approved setup and material condition. Reaching toward a pressurized tip is a critical failure. Changed application: a hose leak sprays onto a glove and the worker reports a pinprick. Activate the emergency response and urgent medical evaluation; do not dismiss it because bleeding is slight. Keep others clear and have the qualified person make equipment safe.

## Q89-9 — Film thickness, coverage and material planning

Wet film thickness is measured during application; dry film thickness is assessed after the appropriate drying or curing condition. A prediction is not a dry measurement. For the unthinned fictional coating, predicted dry thickness equals wet thickness multiplied by the volume-solids fraction. C89 has 60 percent volume solids. A target dry thickness of 150 micrometres therefore requires a nominal wet thickness of 150/0.60 = 250 micrometres. A wet reading of 200 predicts 120, not 150. Product-specific thinning, roughness, shrinkage and measurement procedures may complicate real acceptance; follow the actual specification.

For a simple flat-area estimate, theoretical coverage in square metres per litre equals 1000 times the solids fraction divided by dry thickness in micrometres. C89 at 150 gives 4 square metres per litre. Twelve square metres require 3 litres theoretically. At a stated planning transfer efficiency of 75 percent, required mixed material is 3/0.75 = 4 litres. This allowance is a planning estimate, not permission to overspray or a guarantee of yield. State whether a percentage means efficiency or additional allowance; they are different calculations.

Worked example: a planner adds 25 percent to 3 litres and obtains 3.75. That does not equal the 4 litres required at 75 percent efficiency. The learner explains the denominator and plans the correct quantity. Changed application: 20 square metres at 200 micrometres and 50 percent solids gives 2.5 square metres per litre and 8 litres theoretical; at 80 percent efficiency it requires 10 litres. Record assumptions and units so a reviewer can reproduce the calculation.

## Q89-10 — Cure, defects, repair and acceptance

Dry to touch, ready for recoating and ready for service are different conditions. Use the actual product and environmental history to establish the relevant timing. A surface that feels dry may not be ready for the next coat or service. Protect completed work from contamination and damage. If a recoat window is exceeded, obtain the specified preparation or manufacturer-approved disposition; another coat does not automatically restore compliance.

Recognize runs, sags, dry spray, missed edges, pinholes, contamination and nonuniform thickness as reasons to inspect and obtain a disposition, not as universal diagnoses with one repair. Record the location, extent, coating batch and conditions. Avoid hiding defects with an unapproved extra coat. Verify gauges and use the specified measurement method, substrate correction and sampling plan. The qualified inspector or authorized release person decides acceptance under the actual specification; the beginner supplies accurate evidence.

Worked example: the fictional D89 training rule requires each recorded corrected dry reading from 130 to 170 micrometres. Readings 145,152 and 125 fail at the last location, even though their average is about 140.67. This deliberately simple rule differs from some real statistical acceptance methods and must not replace them. Changed application: readings 145,152 and 168 pass the thickness card, but a visible contaminant is embedded. Hold for review; numerical thickness alone does not accept the surface. Repair is assessed against an approved instruction and reinspection, not an erased original record.

## Q89-11 — Shutdown, waste, fire and abnormal events

Plan shutdown before starting. Secure the work zone, stop and depressurize equipment by its actual procedure, isolate where required, then clean using compatible approved materials. Keep waste streams identified: spent abrasive, removed coating, liquid coating, contaminated wipes and solvents may need different handling. Do not assume spent abrasive is nonhazardous because the unused abrasive was acceptable; removed material changes the waste. Prevent releases to drains, soil or nearby operations, and use the site's waste determination and disposal arrangements.

Spray areas require control of ignition sources, suitable equipment and ventilation for the material. Loss of ventilation, unexpected vapor, fire, pressure-system damage or a person entering the exclusion zone calls for the planned stop and emergency response. Workers must know the alarm, escape route, muster and who contacts emergency services. This training does not authorize firefighting, spill entry, rescue in a contaminated space or maintenance on energized equipment. Only use an extinguisher if the site's policy, training and conditions permit it; escape takes priority.

Worked example: a container of cleaning waste has no label. The learner isolates it from use and informs the responsible person instead of pouring it into a familiar waste drum. Changed application: an ignition source appears in the controlled area while spraying. Stop safely, alert the supervisor and follow the emergency plan; do not try to complete the remaining panel while someone negotiates its removal. Record the event and inspect affected work before restart.

## Q89-12 — Integrated beginner performance and development

An initial-training award records demonstrated beginner tasks under defined supervision. It is not evidence of unrestricted trade competence. The learner must connect the full workflow: correct job and materials, hazard controls, equipment readiness, preparation, inspection hold points, climate, mixing, application, thickness evidence, cleanup and handover. A learner who can calculate thickness but bypasses a pressure control has not passed. Likewise, a neat panel with fabricated readings is unacceptable.

Maintain a personal development record after training. State the task performed, supervision, equipment, product, surface, date and evidence; distinguish observing from performing. The employer plans further supervised experience and any subsequent competence assessment. Do not fill a log with anticipated tasks or treat time served alone as proof. Report changed equipment, materials or conditions so training and authorization can be reviewed.

Worked example: learner A completes all written checks but has only watched the live application. Record the knowledge achievement and the missing practical task; do not issue the full initial skills award. Learner B completes the practical work but needs coaching to recognize an expired batch. Retain that result, remediate timing decisions and reassess on a changed batch independently. Changed application: a worker trained on the recorded enclosed installation is assigned a confined tank. That assignment requires separate planning, controls and authorizations; this certificate does not extend itself to the new environment.

## I89 integrated job and scoring

Learner completes a full planned coupon job from document/control review through preparation, release, mix, application, measurement, cleanup and handover. Include one changed paper condition before an appropriate boundary, such as expired batch or falling climate margin; do not create a live hazard. Actual cure time may require a later session and a second traceable coupon prepared by the learner for dry-film inspection. Do not substitute another person's work without identifying it as a teaching specimen. Final acceptance of the learner's own application remains required.

Twelve dimensions correspond to P89-1 through 12. Score 0=absent/unsafe, 1=major coaching, 2=safe but incomplete or prompted, 3=independent acceptable, 4=independent accurate with sound explanation. Require≥39/48, each≥3, all twelve stations passed, all actual job hold points fulfilled and no critical error. Critical: bypassed pressure/respiratory/ventilation controls, unsafe discharge direction, hand near pressurized leak/tip, unauthorized chemical substitution, continued known unsafe condition, fabricated evidence or inappropriate delay of injection emergency response. Stop unsafe attempts immediately. Retain failure, teach the gap and use a changed safe task; do not coach through and call it independent.

# VUC089 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q89-1-L1

A correct-color hardener has a different product identity. Use it?

**Answer:** No; segregate the mismatch and obtain the specified compatible component.

**Reason/remediation:** Color does not establish chemistry or approved system compatibility. Revisit Q89-1 and perform its changed application.

## Q89-1-L2

All materials match but surface release is absent. Start?

**Answer:** Hold at the preparation-release boundary.

**Reason/remediation:** Material readiness does not waive an inspection hold. Revisit Q89-1 and perform its changed application.

## Q89-2-L1

Can low-hazard abrasive make unknown old paint safe to remove?

**Answer:** No; characterize the existing coating and approve controls first.

**Reason/remediation:** Dust can originate from the substrate and coating. Revisit Q89-2 and perform its changed application.

## Q89-2-L2

Extraction fails during work. Finish a small area?

**Answer:** Stop safely and obtain restoration and release.

**Reason/remediation:** A small remaining area does not eliminate exposure. Revisit Q89-2 and perform its changed application.

## Q89-3-L1

Does a hose fitting a utility outlet prove safe breathing air?

**Answer:** No; verify the designated approved breathing-air system and compatibility.

**Reason/remediation:** Mechanical fit says nothing about air quality. Revisit Q89-3 and perform its changed application.

## Q89-3-L2

Can solvent be used to clean skin?

**Answer:** No; use the approved hygiene and first-aid procedure.
