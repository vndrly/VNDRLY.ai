# VNDRLY/U Inspection Methods — NDT Level II Preparation

VUC045 · Original available-source learning program · 2026-11-18

## Purpose and scope

Prepare learners to select, execute under approved instructions, interpret and document nondestructive examinations with honest coverage and method limits. Core instruction covers visual(VT), penetrant(PT), magnetic particle(MT), ultrasonic(UT) and radiographic(RT) methods, with complementary-method awareness. Practical learning records name only the methods actually demonstrated. This course does not award ASNT certification, employer Level II certification, radiation authorization or engineering acceptance authority.

ASNT's NDT Level II route has its own general/specific examination and eligibility requirements, with employer responsibilities for practical, vision and applicable job-specific requirements.[S01,S02] It is distinct from ASNT9712. The public exam page contains dated outline transitions; use the relevant current route, not a generic old outline. These original materials are not a claim of comprehensive proprietary-code or external-exam equivalence.

## Q01 — What a method qualification actually means

An NDT result is a statement about a particular examination under particular conditions, not a guarantee that a component has no flaws. Personnel competence, written instruction, equipment performance, material/geometry, access and interpretation all contribute. A person can be qualified in one method without being qualified in another. Training attendance, experience records, examinations and employer authorization are distinct evidence.

Before accepting work, compare the actual task with the person's supported method, technique and sector scope. Preserve the governing written practice and authorized responsibilities. A course certificate should say what was learned or demonstrated; it must not silently become an employer-issued Level II credential. Escalate tasks outside scope to the responsible qualified authority.[S02]

**Worked:** a PT learning record is presented for UT weld evaluation. It does not establish UT competence. **Changed:** the worker has UT training but lacks required employer practical/authorization evidence; the assignment still is not established.

## Q02 — Materials, manufacturing and expected discontinuities

Start with how the component was made and used. Casting, forging, rolling, machining and welding create different geometries and possible discontinuity patterns; service can add fatigue, corrosion or other damage. A rolled plate may contain planar features unlike a cast component's volumetric features. A weld's root, fusion boundaries and adjacent material demand a scan plan appropriate to geometry and expected orientation.

Do not identify a mechanism solely from one signal. Use material, fabrication/service history, location and corroborating evidence. Geometry can create responses resembling discontinuities. Record an observation separately from the interpreted cause. Method selection should follow the examination objective, not merely whichever instrument is available.

**Worked:** repeated UT responses follow a known geometric step. Investigate geometry and coverage before calling every response a crack. **Changed:** a response occurs away from the step and changes with probe orientation; further characterization is needed, not automatic dismissal.

## Q03 — Procedures, work instructions and acceptance

A procedure defines the examination method and controls; a work instruction translates an approved basis to a specific component. Acceptance criteria determine disposition. These are connected but not interchangeable. Identify document revision, component/material, preparation, equipment/standardization, coverage, examination variables, reporting thresholds, acceptance rules and authority before starting.

When drafting an instruction within authorized responsibility, preserve every required constraint and make coverage reproducible. State inaccessible regions and hold points. A missing acceptance clause does not permit the examiner to invent a favorable limit. A technical departure requires the responsible authority's disposition; notes added after the event must remain identifiable as corrections.

**Worked:** a report says “tested per procedure” but identifies no revision or region. It cannot demonstrate reproducible coverage. **Changed:** correct revision is supplied, but acceptance comes from a different product code; resolve applicability before deciding accept/reject.

## Q04 — Safety and examination readiness

NDT can involve electrical equipment, UV exposure, solvents, hot surfaces, moving parts, access hazards and radiation. “Nondestructive” describes the intended examination, not absence of hazard. Use the actual task risk controls, equipment instructions and chemical safety information. Isolate component hazards and obtain required access/work authorization. Do not enter a confined space or operate radiation equipment on the authority of this course.

Check conditions again when work changes. Stop if lighting, ventilation, shielding, access or equipment condition no longer meets requirements. Preserve the interrupted examination state so the resumed work does not omit regions. Separate production pressure from a valid restart decision.

**Worked:** PT is proposed beside an ignition source using an incompatible solvent. Hold until approved safe materials and conditions exist. **Changed:** chemicals are suitable but the component moves unexpectedly; isolation remains unresolved.

## Q05 — Vision, illumination and optical limits

VT depends on seeing the required feature, not simply looking at the part. Vision qualification, illumination, contrast, viewing geometry, surface condition and optical aid performance matter. Check actual viewing conditions at the examination surface. Glare may obscure a feature despite high illumination. Magnification can improve apparent size while reducing field of view or changing working distance; it does not restore information absent from the image.

Use the approved direct or remote technique and demonstrate required resolution where specified. Keep dimensional scales in the relevant plane; perspective can distort comparisons. Record any inaccessible surface. A remote camera cannot prove complete coverage if the probe never reached a required region.

**Worked:** a bright image contains a saturated reflection over the target. Reposition approved lighting/viewing rather than record “no indication.” **Changed:** glare removed but scale lies closer to camera than the feature; the apparent dimension remains unreliable.

## Q06 — Visual examination and dimensional records

Prepare the surface only by the authorized method so relevant evidence is not removed. Establish a coordinate/reference system, inspect systematically and record indication location, orientation, apparent dimensions and relevant surface condition. Distinguish a geometric feature, contamination and a potentially relevant discontinuity. Use the actual acceptance definition before calling an indication a rejectable defect.

Measure with suitable verified equipment and record individual readings, not just an average. Document viewing limitations, magnification and uncertainty where material to the decision. A dimension near a threshold may require a defined decision rule or better measurement; rounding must not manufacture a pass.

**Worked:** synthetic minimum6.0mm, measured5.9mm. Rounding to6 cannot establish conformity. **Changed:** measured6.1±0.2 with a rule requiring the whole interval above minimum; it remains indeterminate under that rule.

## Q07 — Penetrant principles and method choice

PT reveals suitable surface-breaking discontinuities when the process allows penetrant to enter and subsequently form a visible indication. It cannot establish the absence of wholly buried discontinuities. Surface porosity, contamination, coatings or preparation that closes an opening can interfere. Select the actual compatible penetrant/removal/developer system and approved process for the material and examination conditions.[S03]

Visible and fluorescent systems have different viewing requirements. Removal methods are not interchangeable shortcuts. Check consumable identity, condition, compatibility and required process controls. A cleaner-looking part after aggressive removal is not necessarily a more sensitive examination.

**Worked:** PT finds no indication on a coated region whose surface openings are inaccessible. Report that limitation, not “no cracks.” **Changed:** coating removed by an unapproved method that may smear the surface; resolve suitability before relying on results.

## Q08 — Penetrant process control

Execute the approved sequence: suitable preparation/drying, penetrant application and required dwell, controlled excess removal, appropriate development and timed examination, then required cleanup. Exact times, temperatures and removal limits come from the applicable procedure/products; do not borrow a universal number. Record actual start/end times and conditions, including interruptions.[S03]

Too much removal can reduce relevant indications; excessive background can also obscure interpretation. Resolve process-control failures through the approved repeat-examination route, not by changing the report. Check system performance with the appropriate reference/control before accepting production results. Handle reference panels so residue or damage does not undermine their purpose.

**Worked:** synthetic minimum dwell12min; actual8min. The required process was not performed. **Changed:** full dwell achieved, but the viewing period is missed; valid dwell alone does not complete the examination.

## Q09 — Penetrant indication evaluation

Record indications at the required time under valid viewing conditions. Determine whether an indication is relevant, nonrelevant due to geometry or false due to processing/contamination, using evidence. Shape and length categories must follow the actual specification. Indication dimensions need not equal the exact underlying discontinuity dimensions; avoid claiming unsupported depth from a surface bleed-out.

Map location and compare with the applicable reporting and acceptance criteria. A reportable indication may not be rejectable, and a test may be invalid before any acceptance decision can be made. When controls fail, report the invalid examination rather than an acceptable component.

**Worked:** a line appears at a known interface. Investigate before classifying it. **Changed:** repeated valid examination shows the line on a continuous surface away from that interface; record and evaluate the actual evidence.

## Q10 — Magnetic particle principles and suitability

MT uses magnetic fields and particles to reveal suitable surface/near-surface discontinuities in ferromagnetic material.[S04] It is not a universal method for every metal. Establish actual material suitability and the needed field direction. Discontinuity orientation relative to the field strongly affects detection; one convenient magnetization direction may leave an important orientation poorly examined.

The method must provide appropriate field conditions and particle application under the approved procedure. A magnet sticking to a part is not a complete examination-system performance test. Geometry and permeability changes may produce nonrelevant indications that still require sound evaluation. Keep material suitability, system function and interpretation as separate checks.

**Worked:** MT is requested for a nonferromagnetic component. Choose a suitable approved alternative rather than treating a blank MT result as clean. **Changed:** material is ferromagnetic but only one field direction was used despite two required directions; coverage remains incomplete.

## Q11 — Magnetization, verification and equipment care

Yokes, coils, central conductors and direct-contact arrangements create different field patterns and introduce different handling/electrical risks. Select and set up only the technique permitted by the approved instruction and competence scope. Field verification, equipment performance checks, current type, particle system, viewing conditions and bath/consumable condition must all fit the task. Do not improvise prod/direct-contact use on a component susceptible to damage.

Demonstrate the required direction/adequacy with the prescribed method. Different indicators do not necessarily measure the same property; a direction indicator is not automatically a quantitative field-strength measurement. Keep pre-use and periodic checks traceable. Failed post-checks require review of the affected examination interval, not simply fixing the instrument for the next job.

**Worked:** a yoke performance check fails after several parts. Identify the potentially affected parts since the last valid check and obtain disposition. **Changed:** replacement yoke passes, but the old parts were not re-examined; replacement alone does not validate them.

## Q12 — MT evaluation, cleanup and residual field

Under valid magnetization and viewing conditions, map indications and evaluate their relevance against geometry and applicable criteria. Repeat orientations or supplementary work when required; do not erase a relevant indication with cleaning before it is documented. Record actual locations, dimensions, field directions and coverage limitations.

After examination, follow required cleanup and demagnetization controls. A remaining magnetic field may matter to later work; use the specified verification rather than assuming power-off means demagnetized. Report residual-field acceptance separately from discontinuity acceptance. Protect consumables and equipment from contamination and maintain required inventory/condition records.

**Worked:** component indications satisfy criteria but required residual-field measurement is missing. Closeout is incomplete. **Changed:** residual field passes but a required second examination direction is absent; neither result substitutes for the other.

## Q13 — Ultrasonic wave and signal fundamentals

UT uses mechanical waves and received responses to infer conditions in a test object. Wave mode, velocity, frequency, wavelength, propagation path, coupling and interfaces affect results. Frequency is not velocity: for a given mode/material, wavelength equals velocity divided by frequency. For pulse-echo thickness, corrected round-trip transit time multiplied by velocity and divided by two gives thickness.[S05]

Identify the actual echo path and compensate approved fixed delays. A displayed number without valid coupling, material velocity and echo selection can be wrong. Higher frequency does not guarantee a better result in every material; attenuation and scattering can limit useful penetration. Treat lack of a useful signal as an examination limitation until resolved, not evidence of no flaw.

**Worked:** velocity5.9mm/µs, corrected round trip4µs gives11.8mm. **Changed:**2µs fixed delay was included in the4µs raw time; corrected thickness is5.9mm, not11.8.

## Q14 — UT standardization and thickness measurement

Select the approved probe, instrument, reference pieces, coupling and measurement technique for the actual material/geometry/temperature. Verify instrument condition and establish the required range, zero/delay and sensitivity using suitable references. Calibration traceability and task standardization are related but distinct; a calibration sticker does not prove today's setup suits the task.

Record reference checks before/after and as required during work. Scan systematically, retain meaningful echoes/measurement locations and investigate inconsistent readings. Coatings, surface roughness, curvature and small local pits can bias results. A few spot values may not represent the whole component. If a final reference check fails, determine the affected interval and required re-examination through the approved process.

**Worked:** nominal10mm reference reads10.6 under an exercise tolerance±0.2. The setup fails by0.4 beyond tolerance. **Changed:** setup corrected, but earlier component readings remain unrechecked; they are not retroactively validated.

## Q15 — Angle-beam geometry and coverage

Interpret geometry using the actual refracted angle and wave path defined by the technique. For a simple flat plate before reflection, depth equals sound path×cos(angle from the normal), and horizontal offset equals sound path×sin(angle). A first-leg calculation does not locate a second-leg reflector correctly. Check probe index, angle and relevant geometry through the approved reference method.

Build coverage around required volume and likely discontinuity orientation. Scan from required sides/directions where possible and document inaccessible regions. Probe travel distance alone is not examined volume. Weld caps, roots, backing and boundaries can create geometric responses requiring discrimination. Do not call an unexamined region accepted.

**Worked:**40mm path at60° from normal gives20mm depth and34.64mm offset in the simple first-leg model. **Changed:** plate thickness15mm means that model would cross the back wall; account for reflection before locating the indication.

## Q16 — UT characterization, amplitude and limitations

Signal amplitude depends on reflector size, orientation, distance, beam behavior, coupling and system settings; it is not a universal direct flaw-size meter. Apply the approved reference/evaluation method and retain relevant settings. When expressing amplitude ratios, use20log10 of the amplitude ratio; do not use a power-ratio formula indiscriminately. A gain change can make the same response look larger without a physical change in the part.

Differentiate detection, characterization, sizing and acceptance. Planar and volumetric responses may need different techniques; bonded structures and anisotropic/coarse materials introduce additional challenges. Where the technique cannot answer the engineering question, state the limit and obtain a suitable qualified method rather than overstate confidence.

**Worked:**doubling amplitude is about6.02dB, not proof of twice the crack size. **Changed:**gain increases6dB between scans; raw screen-height comparison cannot establish growth.

## Q17 — Radiographic principles and image formation

RT uses ionizing radiation and a detector to form a projection affected by material thickness/composition and exposure geometry. Film and digital systems differ in acquisition/processing, but both require suitable image quality and traceability. A projection combines information along its beam path; orientation and superimposed features can limit detection. A narrow planar discontinuity may be difficult to reveal in an unfavorable orientation.

Separate image sensitivity, resolution, coverage and acceptance. Required image-quality indicators establish specified image performance, not proof that every possible flaw is detectable. Choose exposure technique only within properly authorized qualified work. This course uses pre-existing images/synthetic records; it does not authorize radiation exposure.

**Worked:** a readable identifier and an acceptable quality indicator do not prove an obscured region was covered adequately. **Changed:** another view removes superposition but lacks traceable component identity; both quality and identity are required.

## Q18 — RT geometry and processing controls

For a simplified geometric model, unsharpness equals effective source size multiplied by object-to-detector distance divided by source-to-object distance. Increasing separation from object to detector worsens geometric blur with the other values fixed. Magnification and exposure geometry must be recorded correctly; do not interchange source-to-object and source-to-detector distances.

Film processing needs controlled chemistry, time/temperature, handling and viewing; digital acquisition needs appropriate detector checks, corrections and preserved raw data. Processing artifacts can mimic indications. Image enhancement must not destroy the original or hide relevant information. Record what was acquired and how it was evaluated, including limits of the display/viewing setup.

**Worked:**2mm source,20mm object-detector and400mm source-object gives0.1mm geometric unsharpness. **Changed:** object-detector becomes40mm; unsharpness becomes0.2mm, not unchanged.

## Q19 — RT interpretation and radiation boundary

Interpret the component, image markers, quality evidence and known geometry before categorizing indications. Compare with the applicable acceptance rules only after image validity is established. Retain interpretation records and any disputed or unavailable region. Film artifacts and digital defects need investigation; they cannot be relabeled as acceptable metal merely to close a report.

Radiation safety requires its own legal authorization, training, equipment and operating/emergency controls.[S06] Never manipulate an exposure source or cross a controlled boundary on this course's authority. If an alarm or abnormal condition occurs, keep clear, prevent unauthorized entry as safely directed and notify the authorized radiation-safety personnel. Do not attempt source recovery.

**Worked:** an image review trainee is asked to move an exposure device. Decline the unauthorized operation and refer to the licensed/authorized team. **Changed:** the device appears switched off; that observation alone does not establish safe source status.

## Q20 — Complementary methods and justified selection

Some examination questions need a different method or combined techniques. Eddy-current methods use electromagnetic responses in conductive material; permeability, conductivity, geometry and probe lift-off can affect interpretation. Thermal, acoustic-emission, leak and other methods have their own capability boundaries and qualification requirements.[S07] Awareness is not qualification in them.

Write the question first: surface opening, internal planar feature, wall loss, bond condition or leak path. Then compare material suitability, expected orientation/depth, access, environment and required sensitivity. A complementary method should address a named unresolved question, not merely add another favorable-looking report. Preserve disagreements for qualified resolution.

**Worked:** PT cannot answer whether a buried lamination exists. Choose a suitable qualified volumetric approach based on the task. **Changed:** the part geometry prevents the proposed approach's coverage; escalate rather than claim the method's reputation proves coverage.

## Q21 — Coverage, detection confidence and uncertainty

Inspection has finite capability. “No reportable indication” is bounded by actual method, coverage, sensitivity, conditions and interpretation. A sample may not represent inaccessible or differently damaged regions. Detection capability is not automatically a universal probability that every defect is absent.[S08] State what was examined and what was not.

Keep dimensional uncertainty and decision rules explicit near thresholds. Avoid false precision from arithmetic or instrument displays. A result can be inconclusive without being a pass or proof of failure. Resolve uncertainty through appropriate additional evidence or responsible disposition, not by deleting inconvenient readings.

**Worked:**90 of100 required regions examined means90% coverage, not100% because all90 passed. **Changed:** five additional regions examined means95%; the remaining five are still unexamined.

## Q22 — Work instructions and trainee oversight

A competent authorized Level II may have responsibilities for practical instructions and oversight within a governing program. This course teaches that task without granting the role. An instruction must let another qualified person reproduce setup, coverage, checks, evaluation and records using the correct approved basis. Include stop conditions, limitations and escalation, not just button presses.

Teach a trainee by demonstrating, explaining observations, observing their performance and correcting misunderstandings. Preserve who actually performed and evaluated work. Do not sign a trainee's incomplete inspection as independently performed by someone else. Assessment tasks must remain independent where required; coaching is recorded as coaching.

**Worked:** a trainee scans one side while the supervisor signs both. Correct the coverage record and perform required missing work. **Changed:** both sides were actually scanned but no setup checks were recorded; obtain valid evidence or required re-examination rather than invent entries.

## Q23 — Reporting, evidence and technical disposition

A defensible report links component identity, examiner/scope, procedure/revision, equipment/consumables, standardization, actual conditions, coverage map, indications, comparisons and authorized disposition. Separate raw observation from interpretation and acceptance. Keep original records when corrections occur. Photographs need scale/reference and traceable identity; they do not automatically show all examined surfaces.

When a report is incomplete, identify precisely what remains: missing coverage, failed check, uncertain identity, invalid processing or absent acceptance basis. Assign correction and require actual evidence before closing. The report may support an engineering decision, but does not independently authorize service outside the examiner's role.

**Worked:** a valid indication report is attached to the wrong serial number. Hold disposition until reliable identity reconciliation. **Changed:** identity corrected but post-check failed; a second independent issue remains.

## Q24 — Qualification maintenance and honest course closeout

Maintain method-specific training, experience, vision, examinations and employer authorization under the applicable written practice/program. Requirements may differ across routes and change over time; do not invent a universal hours table or renewal interval. Evidence must describe actual method work, not only job title. A lapse or task outside scope requires the proper review before assignment.[S01,S02]

VNDRLY course completion requires its own demonstrated tasks, knowledge and records; it does not mean an external examination was passed. State methods actually demonstrated and simulation-only portions. RT image interpretation exercises never become radiation-operator authorization. Preserve failures, remediation and changed reassessment. A complete learning package is not an actual student's completed qualification.

**Worked:** learner passes written RT questions and requests a source-operation credential. Issue no such credential. **Changed:** learner has an external certificate but employer-specific authorization remains missing; do not silently supply it through course paperwork.

# VUC045 method demonstration clinics

These clinics teach the connection between physics, controls and defensible results. Follow actual approved equipment/procedure instructions for hands-on work. All diagram coordinates and example thresholds below are invented learning data; they do not qualify an industrial technique. Preserve raw observations and distinguish a demonstration with known features from a blind independent practical.

## C01 — Build a visual map another examiner can repeat

Name a reference cornerA and label faces1–6 before inspection. A coordinate is useful only when its origin, axes and units are unambiguous. On an example100×60mm face, featureF1 is atx20,y15;F2 atx80,y45. A report saying “upper right” may reverse when the part is rotated. A reference photograph must show identity/orientation, and any dimensional scale must lie in the relevant plane.

Demonstration: instructor rotates a benign mock part and asks whether the coordinates or only the observer's view changed. The part coordinates remain fixed. Learner repeats the map from the marked origin, checks illumination at the actual locations and records local dimensions with suitable equipment. Compare repeated measurements; investigate disagreement rather than select the favorable one. Record roughness, glare, inaccessible edges and the technique used to resolve them. If no valid resolution is possible, identify the limit.

**Worked response:**first examiner writesF1=(20,15);second uses the opposite corner and writes(80,45). These can describe the same physical location only if the coordinate transformation is declared. Do not count them as two flaws without reconciliation. **Changed:**the second examiner uses an unknown origin; location agreement is unproved. **Practice output:**six-face map, two local measurements, viewing check and limitation statement. Instructor checks reproducibility, not artistic quality.

## C02 — Understand a PT sequence failure before interpreting its result

The examination is a controlled sequence. A surface indication depends on suitable openings, compatible materials, correct dwell/removal/development and valid viewing. A defect in an early stage can invalidate a later attractive image. Before interpreting, reconstruct the actual process timeline from recorded times rather than the technician's intended durations.

Use synthetic recordD:preparation accepted;penetrant07:10;removal07:25;developer07:30;evaluation07:45;approved exercise windows dwell12–20 and evaluation10–30minutes. Both intervals15minutes meet the stated timing conditions. This proves timing only. Add a card saying removal was performed by an unapproved method:timing remains correct but process validity is not established. Learner must resist converting one successful control into overall acceptance.

In supervised physical practice, compare an approved known reference indication with the specimen result, using actual compatible materials and method instructions. Do not deliberately overwash a production component; demonstration of failure can use supplied images/records. Record indication location, shape and size at the required evaluation time, and explain why bleed-out does not measure depth. **Changed:**valid process, no surface indications, question asks about internal laminations. The result still does not answer that question. **Practice output:**timeline, control checklist, indication map and correctly bounded conclusion.

## C03 — MT orientation is part of coverage

Draw a rectangular specimen with a long axisX and short axisY. Mark one artificial training feature parallelX and another parallelY. A field direction suitable for one orientation may be unfavorable for the other. The instruction must define required magnetizations and overlap/coverage, supported by the actual approved system checks. Merely placing the yoke on every part is not proof that all required orientations were examined.[S04]

Demonstration uses a known suitable training part under the actual safe procedure. Instructor identifies field direction and explains what the selected verification device establishes. Learner records each placement/direction and compares the mapped indication response with known training features. Keep the required performance check distinct from material attraction and from residual-field measurement. If the field-verification device does not measure a quantitative property, do not invent a numerical result from it.

**Worked response:**record showsX-directed field only; required two directions not complete. A blank indication map cannot establish the missing second-field coverage merely because no line was seen. **Changed:**both directions complete, but post-check fails. Coverage sequence is recorded, yet validity of the affected interval needs review. **Practice output:**field-direction map, actual checks, indications and post-cleanup/residual status. No direct-contact/prod practice without separately suitable authorized controls.

## C04 — Read UT paths before reading the displayed thickness

A pulse-echo display represents a time/path relationship. In a simple synthetic trace, fixed delay is1µs; echoes at5µs and9µs have corrected times4 and8µs. With velocity6mm/µs, the first back-wall path corresponds to12mm thickness and its repeated round trip to24mm path-equivalent thickness. Selecting the second echo as the first can double the displayed thickness. A real instrument may support echo-to-echo methods with specific controls; do not mix models silently.

Demonstration:instructor shows the known reference block and actual signal, then changes only an approved display range to illustrate that the specimen does not change when the display does. Learner identifies the selected echo, verifies delay/velocity/reference checks and records the measurement location. Poor coupling or a competing near-surface response requires investigation. A stable number is not sufficient proof of correct echo identity.

Angle-beam practice must identify the actual refracted angle/index, plate boundaries and beam leg before locating a response. For the W06 reflected case, vertical travel20mm in15mm plate means the beam has gone down15 and back up5:physical depth10. This is not a universal curved-surface model. **Changed:**curvature or mode conversion invalidates the simple flat model; refer to a suitable technique rather than force the arithmetic. **Practice output:**annotated trace/path model, reference results, actual scan map and supported interpretation.

## C05 — RT image validity precedes defect classification

Use only pre-existing authorized training images; no radiation exposure. Instructor presents the original image with identity, view direction, quality indicator and coverage map. Learner checks which regions can actually be evaluated. A dark feature may reflect thickness, geometry, processing or a discontinuity; image intensity alone does not establish cause. Trace the projection through the known component before classification.

Synthetic image-record set:FrameA identifiesS1 and covers0–100mm of a required0–150mm weld;FrameB covers90–150mm but identifiesS2;FrameC correctly identifiesS1 and covers90–150mm but required quality evidence is unreadable. A andB cannot simply be stitched intoS1 coverage. A andC give attempted coverage but not valid whole-weld examination. Overlap10mm is useful only when identity and quality are valid.

Film artifact review distinguishes physical image damage from a supported component feature; digital review retains original acquisition and transformation history. Instructor demonstrates how aggressive contrast adjustment can hide information, without deleting the original. **Changed:**a new validFrameD forS1 covers90–150mm with required quality evidence; combined valid coverage may be150mm, subject to all task criteria. **Practice output:**frame-by-frame usability table, valid coverage map, interpretation and preserved-data references. No image skill implies source-handling authority.

## C06 — Write the conclusion the evidence supports

Use three columns:performed,validly evaluated,accepted under named criteria. They often differ. Example:20areas assigned;18attempted;two attempts invalid;14of16valid areas accepted andtwo pending evaluation. Attempted coverage90%;valid coverage80%;accepted-area proportion70% of assigned scope. None is “100% inspected,allacceptable.” Reporting the denominator is essential.

Instructor asks learner to explain each unresolved area and the next evidence required. Some need safe access, others repeat examination after a failed check, others qualified interpretation. A single “retest” note is too vague to drive closure. Assign owners within their authority and preserve the original report. When corrections arrive, update the proper column rather than treating every new document as acceptance.

**Changed:**two invalid areas are validly re-examined and accepted;two pending evaluations are accepted;two never-attempted areas remain inaccessible. Valid coverage18/20=90%,accepted18/20=90%,two still unresolved. **Practice output:**corrected report, precise limitations and authorized disposition request. A report should enable the responsible decision-maker to understand uncertainty, not conceal it.

# VUC045 applied examination workbook

All acceptance numbers in this workbook are **invented teaching requirements**, not ASNT, ASME, ASTM or employer criteria. No exercise authorizes production acceptance. For each task submit method, evidence, calculation where applicable, disposition and limitation before reading the model. Score each of those five elements0–2; require8/10 per exercise with corrections demonstrated. Missing examination, failed controls or fabricated evidence cannot be averaged into a pass.

## W01 — Method and authority selection

Four requests: A: surface-breaking discontinuities in a clean nonferromagnetic metal plate; B: buried planar features in rolled steel; C: direct visual dimensions on an accessible machined part; D: live radiographic exposure at a field site. Learner holds only a VNDRLY knowledge record and has no employer method authorization. Select plausible examination approaches and distinguish selection from permission to perform them.

**Model:** A may suit an approved compatible PT technique; B may suit a qualified UT technique with appropriate orientation/coverage; C may suit validated VT/dimensional examination. D requires a separately authorized radiation operation. None is authorized merely by a course record. Material, geometry, expected flaw, required sensitivity and actual procedure must establish the final method. **Changed:** A is porous or coated, B has severe attenuation and C is inaccessible. Reassess suitability; do not repeat the first choice without resolving those limitations. D remains outside course operation authority.

## W02 — VT validity and measurement

Synthetic instruction V45: inspect all six named faces; surface illumination at least600lux; required local dimension at least6.0mm; dimension passes only if the entire stated uncertainty interval meets the minimum. Record: five faces accessible; light readings620/580/640lux; local result6.1±0.2mm; photograph scale lies closer to camera than feature. Determine coverage, validity and dimensional disposition.

**Model:** coverage5/6=83.333%; sixth face unexamined. One location20lux below minimum; averaging613.333lux cannot clear it. Dimension interval5.9–6.3mm crosses minimum and is indeterminate under the exercise rule. Perspective prevents treating photograph scale as exact. Correct conditions and obtain valid additional evidence. **Changed:** all six faces examined under650lux, dimension6.15±0.05mm with an appropriate direct instrument. Interval6.10–6.20 meets this dimensional rule, but overall disposition still needs all other criteria.

## W03 — PT timing, controls and indications

Synthetic P45: dwell12–20minutes; development evaluation10–30minutes after developer application; record each relevant indication longer than2mm; reject a relevant linear indication longer than4mm. PartA penetrant applied09:00, removal09:08, developer09:12, evaluation09:25, line3mm. PartB applied09:30, removal09:45, developer09:50, evaluation10:02, relevant line5mm. All other exercise controls valid. Decide process validity and indication disposition separately.

**Model:** A dwell8min fails minimum by4; development13min is valid but cannot rescue invalid dwell. Do not accept A based on its3mm line; repeat under authorized process. B dwell15 and development12 are valid;5mm is reportable and exceeds reject threshold by1mm. **Changed:** B's line is exactly4mm. The exercise says “longer than4,” so it is reportable but not rejected on that criterion alone. Do not change the inequality. Real specifications may differ.

## W04 — MT direction and post-check failure

Synthetic M45: two orthogonal field directions required on each of ten parts; valid pre/post equipment checks; residual field no more than3exercise units. Record: parts1–8 examined both directions;9–10 one direction only. Pre-check passed08:00; post-check failed12:00; no intervening valid check. Part4 residual4units; others measured2. Manager proposes accepting1–8 because their indications were acceptable.

**Model:** nominal directional coverage18/20=90%, but this is not proof of valid examination because the failed post-check affects the interval since08:00. All ten require the responsible authority's assessment/re-examination disposition. Part4 also exceeds residual limit by1; residual and discontinuity acceptance are separate. **Changed:** a valid10:00 check covers parts1–5 and post-check failure follows6–10. ## W05 — UT time, velocity and reference drift
