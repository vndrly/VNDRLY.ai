# VNDRLY/U Basic H₂S Detection and Escape

VUC051 · Original available-source materials · 2026-11-18

## Purpose and award boundary

This program develops basic H₂S hazard understanding and an integrated response using actual assigned detection and escape training equipment. The eight full foundation chapters are included. Two additional chapters and a mandatory integrated exercise distinguish this package from the earlier knowledge-and-response program. All six stations A–F, including actual equipment demonstrations D/E, are required for this award; they are not optional endorsements. This rule governs the retained foundation station text.

OPITO's public9014 page identifies basic hazard knowledge, detection/respiratory equipment and practical emergency response as its scope. Its published course and eligibility arrangements remain OPITO's, not VNDRLY's. This original program does not award an OPITO certificate, approval or complete proprietary specification equivalence.[B1] The user authorized best effort with public sources; no inaccessible specification is represented as reviewed.

No occupational experience is required to begin learning. Before physical exercises, provide an accurate description of activity and a confidential fitness-screening route; unresolved fitness concerns require qualified medical advice. For actual respiratory use, verify the applicable employer-program prerequisites and device suitability. Record only prerequisite verification, not private medical details, in the course record. A classroom answer does not replace actual donning or detector use.

## H09 — Prepare a usable detection-and-escape arrangement

Know what each component does before an alarm. A personal detector warns according to its configuration; it does not provide breathing air. An escape device provides only the protection and duration for which that device is approved and used correctly; it does not establish that a route is safe or turn its wearer into a rescuer. The site plan supplies routes, alarm meanings, muster arrangements and response authority. These three parts must work together.

At the start of the assignment, identify detector channels, readiness indications, required functional-check status, alarm modes and placement. Confirm that the instrument and clothing arrangement will not obstruct sensing or mask warnings. A phone display or radio link can be useful but must not be assumed to replace the actual local warning function. Follow the actual manufacturer's procedure; generic button sequences are not an equipment qualification.[B2]

Next identify the assigned escape device, storage/access point, condition, operating sequence and limitations. Practice with its approved training version under a competent instructor. Tight-fitting equipment requires its applicable fit prerequisites; a user seal check does not create a fit-test result. Do not assume a hood and a tight-fitting facepiece have the same prerequisites or donning sequence. Use the assigned device's actual instructions and the responsible program.[B3]

Connect equipment to movement. Can the learner reach the device, begin protection as instructed, move along the approved route and report at the designated safe point? A device left elsewhere may not satisfy the actual arrangement. A nominal duration is not a guaranteed time allowance in every use condition. Do not plan to stay until a cylinder or cartridge is exhausted.

**Worked case:** detector passes its prescribed readiness checks, but the escape training unit has a damaged seal and the operational device is locked in a cabinet the learner cannot open. The detector does not compensate for inaccessible/unsuitable escape equipment. Hold the assignment and obtain the approved complete arrangement. **Changed case:** equipment is ready but the route briefing is obsolete. Resolve the route gap before exposure-prone work; equipment alone is not the plan.

## H10 — Execute the sequence and preserve accountability

Respond to the warning according to the actual site plan without waiting for smell, symptoms or a second worker's opinion. Stop the task, use assigned escape equipment as trained, withdraw by the approved route that is safe for the declared conditions and reach the designated accountability point. Avoid improvising a universal wind-direction rule when structures, blocked paths and source location make it unsafe. The exercise controller supplies route safety; actual response uses the site's trained arrangements.

Report observed facts after reaching safety: identity, location, warning, persons unaccounted for and last-known locations. Do not claim a missing person is still where they were last seen. Do not re-enter or remove respiratory protection while still in the simulated affected area. An alarm that stops may reflect device failure; it is not an all-clear. A casualty response must avoid creating additional victims.[B4]

If the route changes, follow the trained alternate and response instructions rather than entering a known hazard to reach the familiar muster point. A buddy having difficulty does not authorize unprotected entry or a rescue beyond the learner's role. Activate the trained response from safety and communicate precise information. Potential exposure requires the appropriate medical response even if the person later says they feel better.

The final phase is controlled return. Keep the incident record, remain available for accountability, report equipment used or defective, and wait for the designated all-clear and work authorization. A successful training escape is evidence of the listed demonstration only; it is not proof of competence on every respirator, detector or facility.

**Worked case:** the learner reaches muster using the assigned training device, then notices a partner missing. They report the last-known work point and remain at accountability; they do not return with escape-only equipment to search. **Changed case:** a supervisor informally says “probably fine” while the response leader has not issued all-clear. Preserve the hold and request the designated instruction.

## H01 — Recognize the source before a release

**Objectives:** Identify credible release mechanisms; explain why job title and smell do not establish safety; recognize physical, combustion, and equipment hazards.

Hydrogen sulfide, abbreviated H2S, may occur with oil and gas, in wastewater and sewage systems, in manure-handling operations, and in other processes involving sulfur-containing materials. A sour-service designation warns that H2S is relevant to the process; it is not a measurement of today's breathing atmosphere. A worker opening a system may encounter a different exposure from a worker standing nearby when it was closed. Sampling, draining, agitation, maintenance, and an unintended equipment failure can change the conditions rapidly. Support personnel, drivers, and gate attendants can be affected without operating the process themselves.

The useful planning question is not merely whether H2S has ever been detected. Ask what material is present, what contains it, what the task could release, where it could travel, and who could be in that path. Review the hazard communication information, the current job assessment, the site's H2S classification, and the monitoring plan with the responsible supervisor. An unknown classification is a gap to resolve, not an assurance of absence. Do not collect a sample or open a hatch to answer that question unless specifically trained, equipped, and authorized for that task.

H2S is colorless. Its familiar odor can disappear as the sense of smell becomes impaired; absence of odor cannot demonstrate clean air. Its density permits accumulation in low areas under some conditions, but wind, turbulence, temperature, structures, and the release itself affect movement. Being outdoors, uphill, or above the floor is not a substitute for the approved monitoring and escape arrangements. A low place with poor ventilation deserves particular attention, but no simple height rule establishes a safe boundary.

H2S also creates fire and equipment risks. Ignition can produce sulfur dioxide, a separate toxic gas. Materials exposed to sour service can suffer damage such as sulfide stress cracking; an equipment failure can then become a release. Workers report leaks, corrosion concerns, unusual sounds, and damaged containment through the site's process. They do not tighten leaking components, select replacement metallurgy, or ignite a release as an improvised correction.

**Worked case:** A driver is asked to wait near a wastewater pit because the production crew is elsewhere. The pit will be agitated. The driver explains that the changing process may affect the waiting area, requests the designated safe staging location, and waits there after the site confirms arrangements. The driver does not decide that a non-production job is exempt from the hazard.

**Practice:** A colleague says the odor has faded, so a hatch can now be inspected. Explain the error and action. **Explained response:** Fading odor is not an atmospheric test. Stay outside the affected area, follow the site's warning and withdrawal arrangements, and report the concern from safety. Qualified personnel must evaluate the condition before any authorized inspection.

## H02 — Understand exposure without treating a number as permission

**Objectives:** Distinguish concentration from duration; interpret ceiling, TWA, STEL, and IDLH; respond to possible exposure without self-diagnosis.

Parts per million, or ppm, expresses the proportion of a gas in air. It is not the same as percent of the lower explosive limit, and the display must be read with its gas name and unit. A concentration reading alone does not describe an entire exposure. Where the worker was, for how long, whether conditions changed, and whether the instrument was suitable all matter. A reading taken at the doorway does not necessarily represent the air at the task.

A time-weighted average accounts for exposure over a stated period. A short-term exposure limit applies over its stated shorter period; a ceiling restricts the concentration according to that limit's definition. These are different constraints, not alternative numbers a worker can choose among. A low average does not excuse an excessive short exposure. Alarm settings implement a site's protective policy and may be below published limits. Workers must follow the alarm response immediately, not debate which limit is higher.

Federal OSHA's published H2S limits differ by industry. Its general-industry ceiling is 20 ppm, with a narrowly conditioned 50 ppm maximum peak for one period of up to ten minutes only if no other measurable exposure occurs in the shift. Construction and shipyard tables use a 10 ppm eight-hour TWA. NIOSH recommends a 10 ppm ten-minute ceiling and identifies 100 ppm as immediately dangerous to life or health. ACGIH publishes a 1 ppm TWA and 5 ppm STEL. These are reference distinctions, not a work plan: the employer must establish the applicable requirements, any state rules, and more protective company criteria. Do not plan to work into the exceptional peak provision or wait for 100 ppm to leave. [Exposure references](https://www.osha.gov/hydrogen-sulfide/standards), [ACGIH values](https://www.acgih.org/hydrogen-sulfide/).

Exposure can irritate eyes and airways and cause headache, dizziness, breathing difficulty, or more severe effects. At high concentrations a person may collapse before taking effective protective action. Symptoms vary and can have other causes; neither feeling well nor a coworker's tolerance proves safety. Someone affected may also suffer injury from a fall. Possible significant exposure requires prompt emergency and medical assessment through the response plan, even if the person initially improves after leaving. Do not send an exposed person back to work on the basis of a classroom symptom chart.

Health consequences do not always end when the person leaves the area. Serious exposure may have lasting neurological or respiratory consequences, and some people, including those with asthma, may be more sensitive. Repeated symptoms or recurring low-level exposure concerns must be reported for professional evaluation; the worker should not diagnose a chronic illness or assume that a tolerated exposure is harmless. Inhalation is the principal concern in ordinary gas exposure, while contact with liquefied material also presents severe cold-injury risks. Report the actual exposure circumstances to responders. [Health-effects reference](https://www.osha.gov/hydrogen-sulfide/hazards).

**Worked case:** A monitor alarms at the site's configured low alarm. A coworker points to the 20 ppm OSHA reference and says the reading is lower. The worker follows the alarm procedure and reports from the safe location. The alarm policy, changing exposure, and instrument limitations make waiting for a larger number unsafe.

**Practice:** Can an eight-hour average establish whether a brief high concentration was acceptable? **Explained response:** No. The relevant short-term and ceiling constraints must also be assessed. The worker preserves the record for the responsible exposure evaluator rather than calculating a justification for remaining in the area.

## H03 — Know who decides, and what the plan must resolve

**Objectives:** Distinguish worker, employer, and specialist responsibilities; use site instructions and stop-work authority; recognize jurisdiction-specific requirements.

The employer must assess the work and provide an effective protective system. Workers must understand and follow their assigned part, report failures, and stop when the controls are missing or conditions differ. A specialist may select equipment or evaluate exposure, but the presence of a specialist does not remove a worker's responsibility to report an alarm or defect. A completion card records training; it is not the same as site induction, respiratory clearance, or authorization for a particular job.

Before work, identify the relevant hazards and release points, monitoring arrangements, warning meanings, protective equipment, primary and alternate safe areas, communication method, emergency contact, and person controlling restart. Confirm who will account for contractors and visitors. Ask what to do if the normal route or communication system fails. These answers belong in the job briefing and contingency arrangements, not in assumptions carried over from another facility.

OSHA regulations, state requirements, offshore rules, and company standards have different applicability. NIOSH recommendations and ACGIH limits are not interchangeable with an OSHA regulation. ANSI/ASSP Z390.1 and API recommended practices are industry references; a public course syllabus is not the complete text of those standards. This VNDRLY program uses public authoritative material and does not claim an audit against inaccessible licensed clauses or another provider's approval.

For offshore operations within the scope of 30 CFR 250.490, the operator's approved H2S contingency plan is central. The rule includes annual personnel training and facility-specific instruction; an employee transferred from another facility still needs a supplemental equipment and procedure briefing. Site drills and attendance records are separate from completing this course. These requirements should not be described as universal rules for every onshore worker. The instructor identifies the learner's actual jurisdiction and site requirements instead of assigning an invented universal legal renewal period.

Warning colors, condition names, siren patterns, and API-based classifications must be taught from the actual site legend. Do not assume a color means the same thing at two sites. An unknown signal requires the conservative action in the emergency plan and clarification from a safe location. A worker never changes an alarm setpoint or bypasses a warning to align it with a memorized chart.

A confined space can have restricted entry or exit and need not be completely enclosed. A hazardous atmosphere is one reason it may require permit controls. Under the general-industry permit-space rule, crossing the opening with any body part counts as entry; leaning in for a quick look is not an exemption. An entrant, attendant, and entry supervisor have assigned duties under the applicable program. This H2S course does not qualify someone for those roles. Keep outside the entry boundary until the employer has established all required authorization, isolation, atmospheric evaluation, communications, and rescue arrangements. A buddy outside a tank is not automatically a trained attendant or rescue service. Construction and maritime have their own applicable rules. [General-industry reference](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.146).

**Worked case:** An experienced contractor arrives offshore with a current H2S card. Their previous platform used different signals. The contractor completes the new facility briefing and locates assigned equipment and briefing areas before duty. Experience does not fill the information gap.

**Practice:** Your permit describes a closed system, but the supervisor now asks for an open drain. **Explained response:** Stop and get the changed task reassessed. A signature on a different task does not cover the new release mechanism. Preserve the approved job boundaries while the responsible authority updates controls and briefings.

## H04 — Control the hazard, including what burning changes

**Objectives:** Apply the hierarchy of controls; recognize limits of ventilation and PPE; distinguish H2S from sulfur-dioxide exposure.

The most effective protective decision removes the exposure opportunity where feasible: perform the task without opening the hazardous system, use a closed method, or avoid the unnecessary activity. Engineering controls can then contain or reduce a release. Administrative arrangements determine access, sequencing, permits, monitoring, staffing, and response. PPE supports these controls when required; it does not make an uncontrolled release acceptable.

A worker's contribution is to verify the controls required by the job and report when reality differs. If the plan requires closed sampling but the connection cannot be used, do not improvise an open container. If a ventilation unit stops, stop the dependent task and follow withdrawal instructions. A running fan is not evidence that all spaces or breathing zones are safe. Qualified personnel must evaluate the ventilation arrangement and hazardous-location suitability. Moving a fan, blocking an exhaust, or opening a door can change the system and must not be done casually.

Hot work and ignition sources require their own controls. H2S may cause serious toxicity at concentrations far below its flammable range. Therefore, a combustible-gas reading does not substitute for an H2S measurement. Conversely, a low H2S reading does not exclude flammable hydrocarbons or oxygen deficiency. Control charging, radios, vehicles, tools, and other potential ignition sources according to the site assessment. Do not operate unapproved equipment in a classified area merely because one gas channel is quiet.

Burning or flaring may be part of an engineered operation, but combustion of H2S produces sulfur dioxide. The resulting plume still requires assessment and controls. Sulfur dioxide can injure the respiratory system; the H2S channel alone does not establish its concentration. NIOSH's sulfur-dioxide reference lists a 2 ppm TWA, 5 ppm short-term recommendation, and 100 ppm IDLH; federal OSHA's listed TWA is 5 ppm. These are SO2 values, not a replacement set of H2S alarms. Only the responsible program selects sensors, boundaries, and protective arrangements for the actual plume. [NIOSH sulfur-dioxide reference](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0575.html).

**Worked case:** A flare is operating, and a crew proposes downwind maintenance because the H2S detector reads zero. They stop and obtain the plume-specific assessment. The absence of one target gas cannot establish absence of combustion products or other hazards.

**Practice:** Arrange closed sampling, a warning sign, a respirator, and restricting access within the hierarchy. **Explained response:** Closed sampling is an engineering approach that reduces the release opportunity; warnings and access restrictions are administrative controls; a respirator is PPE. The actual job may require all of them, but PPE does not justify omitting feasible higher-level controls.

## H05 — Use monitoring as a system, not a magic clearance

**Objectives:** Distinguish fixed, portable, and personal monitoring; check an assigned instrument; explain bump tests, calibration, placement, and failed-check response.

Fixed instruments watch selected locations and may activate site warnings. Portable instruments can support specific testing or survey duties. A personal monitor travels with its assigned wearer. Some devices only alarm; others display concentrations and record exposure information. None measures every hazard everywhere. Selection depends on target gases, range, oxygen conditions, response characteristics, sampling method, environmental conditions, and hazardous-area approval. The employer's qualified program makes that selection.

Before relying on an assigned unit, check its identity, sensor configuration, physical condition, inlet condition, battery, status, and required test records using its current manual. Confirm that audible, visible, and vibration indications can be recognized with the actual clothing and noise conditions. Wear it in the prescribed breathing-zone position with the inlet unobstructed. A detector in a pocket, bag, or beneath impermeable clothing may not represent breathing exposure. Do not attach a sampling tube or pump unless the configuration is approved and you have the additional training for it.

A startup self-test checks selected electronics. A fresh-air setup establishes a reference under specified conditions. A bump test challenges sensor response and alarm operation with the correct known test gas; calibration checks or adjusts measurement response to a reference. These are not interchangeable. Use the exact instrument's specified procedure and frequency. For example, MSA recommends a bump test before each day's use for the ALTAIR 4XR and requires calibration after a failed bump test before use. That example is not a universal procedure for other models. Test gas, accessories, expiry, environmental conditions, and authorized operator competence matter. [Manufacturer reference](https://docs.msasafety.com/altair4xr/en-us/ALTAIR%204X%20R%20OPM%2010175896/Safety_and_Precautionary.htm).

A failed check, blocked inlet, unexplained fault, overdue required service, depleted battery, or unsuitable sensor means the unit cannot be relied on for the job. Withdraw from reliance, notify the responsible person, and obtain an approved checked replacement or correction. Do not clear a fault to make the display look normal. Resetting exposure history also destroys useful information if done before the record is retained under the procedure.

Remote sampling requires added competence: the sample must reach the sensor, the line may delay or affect response, and each location requires evaluation. Personal monitoring training is not gas-clearance authorization. A zero in one location does not authorize entering a tank, and a display beyond its range must not be interpreted as a useful maximum exposure value.

**Worked case:** The device powers on but fails its prescribed gas challenge. The worker removes it from service and obtains a verified replacement. A coworker's advice to use it because the battery is full confuses power with measurement capability.

**Practice:** A new connected monitor can transmit to the control room. Does this replace local response? **Explained response:** No. Communication may fail or be delayed. The wearer must recognize and act on the assigned local warnings and preserve the existing escape procedure. New technology requires training, not reduced vigilance.

## H06 — Respiratory protection and the boundary of this credential

**Objectives:** Distinguish air-purifying, supplied-air, SCBA, and escape-only equipment; identify prerequisites; reject unauthorized entry or rescue.

A filtering dust mask does not provide protection from H2S gas. Air-purifying equipment removes selected contaminants from ambient air using suitable elements; it does not supply oxygen. Supplied-air equipment and self-contained breathing apparatus provide breathing air from an independent source, but their suitability still depends on the configuration, conditions, supply, and program. An escape-only unit is designed for leaving, not for continuing a task, investigating a leak, or returning for another person.

The employer's respiratory program must select the protection and establish the applicable medical evaluation, fit testing, training, inspection, maintenance, storage, and use arrangements. A user seal check when donning a tight-fitting respirator does not replace a fit test. Facial hair or other interference at the seal cannot be solved by tightening straps harder. The worker reports a fit or condition problem before reliance. The instructor does not conduct a medical clearance or treat a quiz as proof of adequate fit. [OSHA respiratory protection](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.134).

Unknown exposure and IDLH conditions require the program's specialized protective and standby arrangements. A worker cannot select a convenient cartridge by looking only at a number on a handheld meter. A cartridge or canister has use limits; a cylinder's nominal service rating is not a promise of actual escape time. Work rate, leaks, conditions, and the equipment can change usable duration. The assigned device's alarms and limitations must be taught and demonstrated in safe conditions by a competent instructor.

For assigned users, the practical endorsement must use the actual make, model, and configuration. The evaluator checks identification, inspection, correct donning and adjustment, applicable seal check, recognition of warnings, communication, escape actions, and post-use handling against the manufacturer instructions and employer program. Training units must be safe, hygienic, and designated for practice. Do not consume or disable emergency stock without the program's approved training arrangement. Learners who lack required prerequisites do not perform respirator-use demonstrations and do not receive a use endorsement.

**Worked case:** A worker has completed classroom H2S instruction but has never practiced the site's escape hood. The worker tells the supervisor and completes device-specific training before an assignment relying on it. The card is valid evidence of classroom learning, but not of that missing skill.

**Practice:** Someone proposes using an escape pack to retrieve a tool from an alarmed area. **Explained response:** Reject the entry. Escape equipment is not a re-entry permit. The responsible emergency organization controls the area, and any later work requires its own assessment and authorization.

## H07 — Escape, accountability, and casualty response

**Objectives:** Follow the contingency plan; adapt through approved alternate routes; communicate useful facts; avoid secondary casualties; obtain medical response.

Preparation must precede the alarm. Identify the warning signals, available routes, safe briefing areas, wind information, communication method, and person managing accountability. A buddy arrangement helps recognize missing people or problems; it is not permission for one unprotected person to follow another into danger. Tell the responsible person where you will work and follow site movement controls.

On an alarm or credible release warning, stop the activity and follow the site's immediate protective and withdrawal instructions. Do not approach the source to verify the warning, finish the task, gather personal possessions, or silence the alarm before leaving. Use assigned escape equipment only as trained. The route must avoid the release and lead to the designated safe area according to the plan. Wind direction can change, so the familiar route or usual muster may not remain suitable. Use the approved alternate arrangements; do not walk through a suspected plume to reach a memorized assembly point.

Once safe, report who you are, your location, the warning or condition observed, the last known location of others, and any possible exposure or injury. Distinguish what you observed from what you assume. Report missing people through the response leader and remain available for accountability. Do not leave the site silently, because responders may otherwise search for someone who has already departed.

Seeing a collapsed coworker creates an urgent emotional impulse. Entering without training and appropriate protection can produce another casualty. Activate the emergency response from safety, keep others from entering when you can do so safely, and provide location information to the equipped response team. Rescue and retrieval methods belong to trained, authorized responders under the plan. This course does not teach improvised entry rescue.

Once a casualty is in an area declared safe by the response organization, trained first-aid personnel provide care within their qualifications and emergency-dispatch instructions. Someone who is untrained should obtain emergency assistance and follow the dispatcher's directions rather than improvise oxygen administration or treatment. Suspected exposure, breathing trouble, loss of consciousness, or collapse must be communicated to emergency medical personnel; apparent recovery does not establish fitness for return. Keep exposure information and instrument records available for responders without delaying urgent care.

**Worked case:** A worker sees someone down beyond an alarmed doorway. The worker does not cross the threshold. From safety they raise the alarm, give the precise location, and prevent an approaching coworker from entering. This is an active protective response, not abandonment.

**Practice:** At the safe area, you realize your partner is missing. **Explained response:** Tell the accountability leader the last confirmed location and time, avoid speculation, and remain at the instructed safe location. Do not undertake an individual search in the affected area.

## H08 — Restart, reporting, and maintaining readiness

**Objectives:** Preserve accurate records; distinguish an alarm reset from an all-clear; identify retraining and equipment-change triggers.

An alarm stopping does not establish that the hazard has been removed. A device can stop alarming because its battery failed, it was moved, the condition changed temporarily, or someone reset it. Re-entry requires the authority and verification specified in the plan. Workers follow the formal all-clear and task authorization, including any revised controls. A supervisor's production urgency is not a substitute for that process.

Report alarms, suspected exposures, failed equipment checks, route obstructions, and communication failures promptly. Record observations accurately: location, task, time, device identity, displayed gas and units if safely observed, warning type, action taken, and people involved. Do not reconstruct an exact concentration from memory or write that air was safe solely because nobody reported symptoms. Preserve instrument logs through the responsible equipment or safety program. Mark uncertain facts as uncertain.

After an event, the responsible organization determines causes and corrective actions. The worker helps by describing the actual sequence, including near misses and departures from the plan. Learning should change the relevant control, briefing, inspection, or equipment arrangement; it should not merely add a signature to a form. A failed demonstration requires practice and reassessment before the corresponding skill is endorsed.

Refresher needs arise when equipment, tasks, signals, routes, responsibilities, or rules change; when an incident reveals a gap; or when a worker cannot demonstrate the required response. VNDRLY uses a twelve-month refresher interval for this program as its credential policy, with earlier retraining on those triggers. Applicable laws and site requirements may impose their own timing or additional content. The course does not replace ongoing drills, employer respiratory training, or facility induction.

Connected monitors, electronic permits, and remote alerts can improve information, but each must have a failure response. If a dashboard loses connection, the worker must still know what local warning means and how to escape. Updates must not silently alter alarms or remove trained procedures. The responsible program evaluates the change, trains affected people, and verifies performance before reliance.

**Worked case:** Maintenance repairs a detector after a release, and its display returns to normal. The crew waits for the designated restart authority and revised briefing. Repairing a warning device is only one part of restoring the work conditions.

**Practice:** A record asks for the peak reading, but you did not see it. **Explained response:** Write that it was not observed and identify the device so the authorized person can retrieve its record. Inventing a value undermines both exposure assessment and prevention.

## Personal readiness discussion

Before an actual assignment, explain your site's warning, withdrawal route, alternate safe area, communication and accountability method, and the person who authorizes return. Identify the device assigned to you and any missing practical endorsement. If an answer is missing, record the gap for the supervisor and resolve it before work that depends on that control. This discussion supports site readiness; it is not a substitute for the practical packet or final exam.

## H08-L2

You did not observe a peak concentration. What belongs in the report?

**Answer:** Not observed, with device identity for authorized record retrieval

## Common evaluation method

Explain the task and let the learner respond without hints. Mark each observable criterion Pass, Needs practice, or Not assessed, with the observed action. Every criterion must pass for that station. A critical error stops the exercise immediately; explain the danger, reteach the relevant chapter, and use the changed retry. Not assessed is not a pass. The assessor signs only actions personally observed or explicitly identifies independently verified prerequisites.

## Station A — changed job and monitoring readiness

**Equipment:** the job and instrument cards below, blank reporting sheet, and learner coursebook for teaching only. During assessment remove answer guidance.

**Job card:** A closed sample connection at a fictional treatment unit is unavailable. The crew proposes opening the adjacent hatch. The existing assessment covers closed sampling only. The assigned instrument has H2S and oxygen sensors, an unobstructed inlet, a full battery, and a failed required functional challenge this morning. The crew says it can complete the task before the next shift.

**Task:** Explain whether you proceed, identify the gaps, and give a concise message to the supervisor.

**Observe:** learner stops the changed task; identifies open-system release potential; does not rely on the failed instrument; requests reassessment and an approved verified replacement; does not claim two sensors cover every possible hazard; identifies qualified selection/testing responsibility.

**Critical errors:** opening the hatch, accepting the failed instrument, or using smell as clearance.

**Worked response:** “The permit covers closed sampling. Opening the hatch changes the release potential, and this instrument failed its required check. I am holding the task for reassessment and an approved checked instrument.” The learner does not prescribe a replacement sensor set without the hazard assessment.

**Retry:** The sampler works, but ventilation required by the assessment has stopped and the monitor's inlet is blocked by a cover. The learner must recognize both independent failures instead of declaring the unchanged sampler sufficient.

## Station B — alarm, alternate route, and accountability

**Equipment:** draw this fictional plan on paper: work point W at center; suspected source S east of W; primary path W–North–A is blocked; approved alternate path W–West–B is clear; the drill controller declares B the safe briefing area for current conditions. A red card means simulated H2S alarm. A blue card means the approved alternate route is unavailable: hold the simulation and request controller instructions rather than inventing a path. No live movement into operating areas.

**Task:** At the red card, indicate your actions and route, then make a simulated radio report. Your partner was last seen at W before the alarm but is not at B.

**Observe:** stops work promptly; selects the declared safe alternate rather than the blocked primary; does not approach S; uses any assigned escape equipment only within demonstrated training; reports own identity/location and the missing partner's last known location; stays available for accountability; does not conduct an unauthorized search.

**Critical errors:** going toward S to verify the alarm, crossing the declared hazard to reach A, or re-entering for the partner.

**Worked response:** stop, follow the declared alternate to B, report “I am at B; my partner is unaccounted for, last seen at W before the alarm,” and follow the response leader. Do not assert where the missing person is now.

**Retry:** Give the blue card and have the controller provide a new safe-route instruction before the learner proceeds. Pass requires recognizing that the original plan no longer suffices and obtaining the approved alternate, not memorizing west as always safe.

## Station C — casualty, records, and restart

**Equipment:** casualty card, mock phone/radio, incident form, and separate all-clear card held by assessor.

**Casualty card:** A person is down inside an alarmed enclosure. You are outside the affected area without rescue qualification. The monitor display later goes blank. Another worker wants to enter because the sound stopped. Emergency contact for this exercise is the simulated site response leader, not a real telephone number.

**Task:** Demonstrate the notification, tell the approaching worker what to do, and complete the incident form. Explain when work can restart.

**Observe:** does not enter; activates simulated emergency response from safety; supplies precise location and observed facts; warns the approaching person without entering danger; identifies trained equipped responders as responsible for rescue; requests medical response for suspected exposure; records the blank display as an observation, not a zero reading; requires designated all-clear and work authorization before return.

**Critical errors:** unprotected rescue, treating a blank display as safe air, or self-authorizing re-entry.

**Worked response:** “Person down inside the alarmed enclosure. We remain outside; rescue and medical response are needed. The display went blank, so I cannot report a concentration.” The alarm's silence is a loss of information, not clearance.

**Retry:** The person reaches the safe area and says they feel better. The learner still communicates suspected exposure for medical assessment and does not clear the person for work.

## Station D — assigned personal detector endorsement

**Prerequisites and equipment:** competent instrument instructor; actual assigned device and current manual; safe training area; inspection/test records; approved training alarm mode or simulator; correct accessories. If bump testing is part of the learner's actual duty, the qualified program supplies the approved test arrangement and supervises it under its safety procedure. A paper exercise cannot endorse performance of a real gas challenge or calibration.

**Tasks and observable criteria:** identify make/model and target channels; find the relevant manual procedure; inspect housing, inlet, battery, status, and required records; demonstrate prescribed placement; recognize each assigned warning using safe simulation; state and demonstrate immediate withdrawal/notification within the training area; identify a failed check and remove the unit from reliance; preserve records and hand back through the proper route.

**Assessor:** record the exact manual revision and configuration. Compare actions directly against that manual and employer procedure. Do not substitute generic button presses. Introduce one safe simulated defect such as a labelled blocked-inlet picture; do not damage the device. Calibration/clearance testing is excluded unless separately trained and evaluated under its own program.

**Critical errors:** reliance after failure, obstructed placement, ignoring a warning, or changing alarm settings without authorization. Retry after specific coaching with a different defect. Endorsement applies only to the recorded equipment and user functions.

## Station E — assigned respiratory escape equipment endorsement

**Prerequisites and equipment:** qualified respiratory instructor; employer verification of applicable medical and fit prerequisites; actual approved training version of the assigned device; current manual; hygienic safe practice area; employer inspection criteria. No live toxic atmosphere, oxygen depletion, or rescue entry. Do not activate or consume operational emergency stock outside an approved training arrangement.

**Tasks and observable criteria:** identify the device and escape-only limitation; inspect the training unit according to the manual; locate and access it; demonstrate manufacturer-specified donning and adjustment; perform applicable seal check without claiming it replaces fit testing; recognize simulated warnings and limitations; communicate as trained; follow the safe simulated escape route; describe post-use removal/handling in the confirmed safe area and report defects.

**Assessor:** record each action, actual device/model, manual revision, and prerequisite verification. If a learner has an incompatible fit or missing prerequisite, mark Not assessed and refer to the responsible program. Never coach a learner to defeat a fit requirement. A video explanation or multiple-choice answer is not a donning demonstration.

**Critical errors:** attempted use with an unresolved fit/condition problem, treating escape equipment as entry protection, or removing it within the simulated affected area. Reteach and reassess safely before endorsement.

# VUC051 delivery, integration and award

## Learning plan

Plan eight hours:180minutes foundation instruction and explained checks,45minutes H09/H10 and equipment briefing,120minutes supervised equipment practice/stations,45minutes integration/remediation and90minutes controlled final. These total480minutes. Extend for actual learning and safe individual practice; this is VNDRLY's plan,not a representation of OPITO's published duration. Prior valid foundation outcomes may receive documented credit,but no knowledge credit substitutes for actual required equipment performance.

Instructor competence must include H₂S response and the actual equipment assessed. Obtain current detector/escape manuals,configuration,training devices,inspection records and a safe route area. Verify training fitness/prerequisites through the proper confidential process. Maintain supervision and a clear real-emergency stop signal. Never introduce H₂S,oxygen deficiency,live process releases,smoke that impairs safe breathing or a hazardous casualty location.

## W01 — Readiness decision

Fictional siteB51 assigns detectorD51 and escape deviceE51. D51 has current passing required functional records but an inlet covered by clothing. E51 training unit is appropriate and ready;the learner has not demonstrated its use. The proposed job could expose the worker to H₂S.

**Task:** state independent gaps and a usable corrective sequence.

**Model:** correct detector placement per manual and verify readiness;complete safe supervised E51 practice and independent demonstration;confirm current site plan and prerequisites. Neither a detector certificate nor an equipment video establishes the unperformed demonstration. Hold the affected assignment until its actual conditions are satisfied.

**Changed:** donning passes,but required fit prerequisite for the assigned tight-fitting device is unverified. Keep that issue pending through the responsible respiratory program;do not replace it with a user seal check.

## W02 — Route and accountability

Draw workpointW,sourceS east ofW,musterA north,musterB west. For this simulation the controller declares W-to-A blocked and W-to-B safe. Redcard means H₂S alarm. AtB,the roster has six assigned people but only five accounted for;person6 was last seen atW before the alarm.

**Task:** give actions,route,accountability message and limits. **Model:** stop/respond,use the assigned device as trained,follow the declared safe alternate toB,report five of six accounted for and the person's last-known location,remain available and do not re-enter. One unaccounted person is not a confirmed casualty or confirmed current location.

**Changed:** controller declares the west route unsafe before movement. Stop the simulation and obtain the controller's new safe route;do not invent one throughS. In actual operations follow the site's trained blocked-route response,not a classroom map rule.

## W03 — Equipment and facts after escape

Detector screen goes blank after a warning. Learner reachesB;another worker says “zero now” and requests return. A person reports headache but says it has passed.

**Model:** blank is unknown,not zero. Report warning and failure,maintain designated response arrangements,and request appropriate medical evaluation for possible exposure. No self-issued all-clear. Record the actual device state and who received the report.

**Changed:** replacement detector reads zero atB. That does not establish the original work area's atmosphere or authorize return. Appropriate assessment and designated all-clear remain necessary.

## W04 — Duration and timing are not clearance

An exercise card labels a training escape device “nominal15minutes under its specified test conditions.” The safe practice route takes4minutes;donning/practice communication takes2minutes. Explain the arithmetic and why it is not permission to remain another9minutes in a real hazard.

**Model:** training sequence6minutes;nominal-minus-sequence arithmetic9minutes. Actual protection depends on device,condition,use and its approval;nominal duration is not a discretionary work allowance. Start escape promptly and follow the manual/plan. No learner is timed to exhaust a device.

**Changed:** the route becomes8minutes in training. Arithmetic sequence10minutes and difference5minutes still do not establish real safe capacity. Reassess the actual arrangement through the responsible program rather than planning to use every nominal minute.

## Station F — Integrated safe alarm response

Use actual assigned detector and approved escape training equipment with current manuals and verified prerequisites. Controller uses the W02 safe map and a simulated alarm. Observe each learner independently after coached practice. Do not make the learner inhale test gas or manipulate production emergency stock.

Required observable criteria:

1. Identifies device/configuration and checks readiness under the actual procedures.
2. Places detector correctly and identifies its simulated warning without relying on smell.
3. Stops work and accesses/uses the escape training device in the correct manufacturer sequence.
4. Performs applicable adjustment/seal actions without confusing them with fit testing.
5. Follows the controller-declared safe route and keeps the device in use until the confirmed safe point as instructed.
6. Reports identity,location and unaccounted person's last-known information accurately.
7. Avoids unauthorized search/rescue and requests trained response/medical help as indicated.
8. Reports equipment state and remains out until designated all-clear/work authorization.

All criteria must pass;mark actual actions,not checkmarks inferred from attendance. Critical errors stop the attempt:unprotected entry,removal in affected area,ignored warning,use of known failed equipment or invented all-clear. Reteach and use a changed route/device-fault scenario. Retain original and retry observations. If prerequisite or equipment is absent,mark Not assessed and keep award pending.
