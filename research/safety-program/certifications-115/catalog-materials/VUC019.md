# VUC019 — Energy Control: Authorized Employee

## Definition, prerequisites and delivery

Audience: employees designated to isolate machinery for servicing, with affected-worker responsibilities included for coordination. Purpose: independently execute and maintain the employer's equipment-specific energy-control process within the employee's authorized scope. Prerequisites: understand the assigned equipment/task, communicate stop conditions, and receive employer instruction in its actual procedures and devices. This course confers no qualified-electrical-worker status, well-control authority, hot-tapping qualification or permission to improvise isolation hardware.

Objectives E01–E12: distinguish scope/roles; inventory all sources; prepare/notify/shut down; apply suitable devices; control stored/reaccumulating energy; verify the protected state; preserve it during work; coordinate groups/contractors; transfer shifts; conduct testing transitions; restore/handle exceptional removal; recognize narrow exceptions and maintain program proficiency. Each objective has written instruction, worked application, two formative and three final questions. Practical stations below test actual behaviors on a safe trainer and, for employer authorization, the actual equipment-specific procedure in a controlled evaluation.

Eight planned instructional hours excluding breaks: scope/inventory 60 minutes; shutdown/devices 60; stored energy/verification 60; group/contractor/transfer 60; testing/restoration/exceptions 60; supervised practice 90; independent written assessment 45; independent practical assessment/debrief 45. Extend practical time or rotate additional instructors to observe every learner; never award an unobserved skill to meet the clock. Hours are VNDRLY policy, not a statutory course-length requirement.

Instructor: employer-designated authorized employee proficient in the specific machinery, procedures and energy-control devices, with ability to teach and assess. Electrical demonstrations involving exposure require appropriately qualified personnel; ordinary learners use inert replicas. Maximum four learners per directly observed trainer station; assess each learner individually. Demonstrate each sequence, provide coached practice, then remove coaching for assessment. Use only zero-energy/inert simulators with represented hazards, not live voltage, compressed fluid or an unsupported real load. Never loosen actual fittings, defeat a live guard or remove a real worker's lock for class practice.

## Examination and credential policy

Final: 36 constructed responses, 72 points, pass 58/72 overall and 4/6 each E-domain. Each response:2 correct decision and essential reason;1 safe incomplete;0 wrong/absent/unsafe. Critical unsafe endorsements—working before verified isolation, bypassing another person's protection, unsupported stored-energy exposure or unqualified electrical testing—fail regardless of aggregate score. Forty-five minutes is planned, with reasonable language/access/time accommodation that preserves the safety objective. No keys or coaching during the controlled final.

Eight physical/tabletop stations have five scored criteria each:0 absent/unsafe,1 safe with prompting/incomplete,2 independently correct. Require8/10 at every station and every starred critical criterion at 2. Tabletop exceptions do not qualify actual exceptional-removal authority. A safety intervention ends that attempt. Retain the failed result, teach/practice the deficiency, then reassess a changed example with the same objective. Use protected alternate cards for failed written domains plus E12. Maximum two reassessments before a new targeted training plan and later assessment.

A VNDRLY materials package is not a learner credential. Issue the authorized-employee training/skills credential only after all required written and practical results and equipment-specific competence are recorded. Name the demonstrated equipment/procedures and exclusions; a simulator-only learner receives a conditional training record pending employer equipment demonstration. The employer designates authorization. Review annually alongside procedure inspection as VNDRLY policy, and retrain immediately for relevant changes or deficits. Do not describe annual inspection as a universal annual refresher mandate or renew on attendance alone.

## Safe trainer specification and worked procedure

Use a tabletop, nonenergized model labelled PRESS-P1. Physical props: manual disconnect D1; closable valve V1; dummy accumulator A1; a supported ram representation; an inert blocking device B1; labelled return-line valve V2 representing an alternate feed; a nonfunctional Start/Stop panel; pressure display cards; dedicated training locks/tags/hasps; lockbox and three distinct personal locks. There is no stored or connected hazardous energy. Instructor announces that any real-machine use requires its own approved procedure; these symbols are not instructions to operate a real press.

Training task: change a part inside the represented ram zone. Energy inventory identifies D1 electrical drive,V1 pneumatic feed,A1 stored hydraulic energy,gravity at the ram and V2 alternate pressure pathway. Device IDs and safe positions are on the trainer-specific drawing. Simulated stored-energy relief is represented by a control card, never a real valve discharge. A card shows whether the specified block is correctly engaged. A qualified-person verification card represents electrical absence-of-voltage testing; the learner must request it and must not pretend a start attempt performs that test.

Worked sequence: identify P1 and the task; compare drawing/props; notify operator and neighboring crew; follow trainer Stop sequence; isolate D1, V1, V2; attach personal devices to hold safe positions; represent approved pressure dissipation and B1 support; verify each source with the named method and appropriate qualified-person input; attempt Start with everyone clear, then restore the control to Stop; record results and reaccumulation monitoring; only then place the work token in the danger zone. If the instructor changes pressure from0 to20 on a card, stop the work token, preserve controls and investigate the pathway before resuming. The numbers are scenario signals, not safe pressure limits.

Restoration: remove work token/tools, verify represented guards/components and all people clear, reconcile group devices, have each owner remove their device through the procedure, notify affected people and perform controlled restart. This complete example is taught before the assessment variations below.

## A — Scope and energy walkdown

Props: P1 drawing and trainer, job cards for shop servicing and oilfield well servicing. Learner classifies role/task, identifies the applicable-rule boundary and walks every source. Fault:V2 exists on the trainer but not the first drawing. Correct response stops the planned exposure and resolves the discrepancy rather than relying on the printed procedure.

Criteria: accurately identifies scope/role; lists energy type/magnitude or needed information; *finds every represented source including V2; distinguishes isolation from controls; *does not proceed on an incomplete procedure. Reassessment moves the alternate feed and changes the task boundary.

## B — Notification, shutdown and device application

Props: control-room role card, P1, suitable and deliberately unsuitable training attachments. Learner notifies affected people, carries out orderly shutdown, physically identifies devices and applies assigned locks/tags. Fault: one valve attachment can slip off and allow movement. Correct response rejects it and obtains a suitable device; a tag is not a casual substitute.

Criteria: correct communication; correct shutdown order; *correctly identifies and isolates each required device; *uses suitable secured identifiable personal hardware; verifies attachment prevents intended operation. Assessor observes hands-on attachment rather than accepting a verbal description.

## C — Stored energy and verification

Props: accumulator/ram cards, blocking prop, pressure trend, qualified-electrical-test record card. Learner controls stored energy and identifies verification for each source. Instructor first withholds the qualified electrical result, then presents a valid result after the learner requests qualified help. Later inject pressure reaccumulation.

Criteria: *establishes specified support before exposure; controls all represented stored energy; *requests appropriate verification rather than treating Start as voltage proof; returns controls to safe state; *stops and resolves reaccumulation. No real load is lifted or electrically tested. Repeat with a gauge-discrepancy card instead of a pressure rise.

## D — Group and contractor protection

Props: three personal locks, controlled isolation keys/box, different contractor procedure cards and roster. Learner coordinates the two employers, establishes the group process, joins personally, adds a fourth worker correctly and removes one finishing worker without compromising the others. Worked resolution: everyone exposed retains equivalent personal protection; the lead lock alone is insufficient.

Criteria: reconciles procedures and boundaries; assigns coordination/exposure responsibility; *establishes individual protection for each exposed worker; *prevents key/restoration access while anyone remains protected; records arrivals/departures and verification status. Assessment is individual even when peers play other roles.

## E — Shift transfer

Props: active group box, outgoing/incoming personnel cards, delayed-arrival inject. Learner demonstrates the approved continuous-protection transfer and verifies incoming understanding. Fault: supervisor asks outgoing staff to remove all locks before incoming staff arrive. Learner keeps the safe state and invokes the documented continuity arrangement rather than a gap.

Criteria: reviews work/energy status; identifies all exposed people; *preserves continuous physical protection; establishes incoming personal protection/verification under procedure; documents transfer and restrictions. Reassessment introduces a contractor who remains working across the shift boundary.

## F — Test, position and re-isolate

Props: P1, tool/person tokens, test request. Learner transitions out of lockout for a represented positioning test, then reestablishes the complete controlled state before work. Instructor leaves a person token inside the zone to test whether clearance is real.

Criteria: *clears personnel/tools before release; correct device-owner/removal coordination; controlled defined test; *re-isolates every source and stored energy after test; *re-verifies before work token returns. Stopping the motor after the test is not enough. Repeat with a new source revealed by the test.

## G — Restoration and absent-owner case

Props: completion checklist, personnel board, lock-owner absence/contact records. First demonstrate ordinary owner removal and safe restart. Then present an absent-owner case with contradictory evidence that the person may still be present. Learner refuses casual removal and identifies required employer-directed safeguards and information before any exception. No real device is cut.

Criteria: inspects machine/tools/components; *verifies people clear; normal owner removal and affected notification; *rejects unresolved absence/exposure or informal permission; specifies contact and before-return notification under documented employer procedure. Tabletop success is not authority to approve actual exceptions.

## H — Procedure inspection and final complete sequence

Props: trainer, revised procedure with omitted accumulator, periodic inspection form, training-change case. Learner conducts an independent full sequence on a changed trainer task while the assessor observes, then reviews the flawed procedure. Identify annual inspection by an authorized person other than the users inspected, proper record fields and immediate correction/retraining needs. Do not merely initial every box.

Criteria: *independently completes the full safe sequence; detects procedure/equipment mismatch; identifies independent periodic inspector and required responsibility review; records specific corrective action and retraining; distinguishes actual equipment authorization from simulator attendance. Any critical failed step requires a changed complete reassessment, not just a corrected spoken answer.

# VNDRLY/U Energy Control — Authorized Employee coursebook

## E01 — Decide when energy control applies and who may perform it

Unexpected energy can move a machine, discharge pressure, drop a load, release material, burn a person or energize exposed conductors. Servicing includes cleaning, unjamming, lubrication, adjustment and setup where a person can be exposed—not only work bearing a maintenance work-order label. Normal operation with effective guarding differs from putting a hand into a danger zone or bypassing a guard. A short task can create the same injury mechanism as a long repair.

An authorized employee applies the employer's energy-control procedure for the covered servicing work. An affected employee operates the equipment or works in the area and must understand the controls and prohibition on restart. Other nearby employees also must not defeat controls. A production operator becomes subject to authorized-worker training when their duties include covered servicing. A course certificate alone does not assign equipment-specific authority.

Determine the governing work scope. Federal OSHA 1910.147 excludes construction, agriculture, specified maritime work, exclusive utility installations, electrical utilization exposure covered by Subpart S, and oil-and-gas well drilling and servicing. This matters in VNDRLY's industry: do not label a well-servicing activity compliant with 1910.147 simply because a lock is used. Identify the applicable sector/electrical rules and employer equipment procedure. The exclusion never means uncontrolled energy is acceptable. This course teaches the general-industry authorized-worker method and its boundaries; separate specialist authorization remains necessary.

Worked case: a shop technician clears a jam behind a conveyor guard while the operator holds Stop. The technician is exposed to unexpected movement and stored material. The task requires the applicable energy-control process; an operator's promise is not isolation. In contrast, a worker observing the intact guarded conveyor from outside remains an affected/other worker and must not interfere with another person's controls. Explain the actual exposure, not just the job title.

## E02 — Build an energy inventory before touching the equipment

Confirm the equipment identity, task boundary and current procedure. Walk the system with the approved drawing and competent personnel. Follow every energy source to its isolating device: normal and alternate electrical supplies, hydraulic and pneumatic feeds, accumulators, gravity, springs, flywheels, thermal energy, chemical reactions and material flow. Include connected machines and upstream/downstream processes that can move or refill the serviced equipment. A nameplate identifies a machine, not all its energy pathways.

For each source record type, magnitude or relevant range, hazard, isolation location, safe device position, stored-energy method, verification method and reaccumulation control. Labels must match physical devices. If a drawing and equipment differ, stop and correct the procedure through the employer's process before exposure. Do not guess which of two identical valves serves the machine.

Distinguish a control command from a physical isolating device. A touchscreen stop, PLC command, selector switch, remote disable or emergency stop can fail, reset or be bypassed and is not the required energy isolation. A suitable manually operated disconnect, valve, block or other physical device can prevent transmission when correctly selected and secured. An isolating valve must also be adequate for the actual hazardous substance and leakage potential; a closed handle alone does not establish safe pressure downstream.

Worked inventory: a synthetic shop press has motor supply D1, air supply V1, an accumulator A1 and an elevated ram. Locking D1 prevents motor power but does not release A1 or support the ram. The complete procedure must identify V1, controlled dissipation and specified rated blocking before a person enters the crush zone. Learners draw the four paths and identify what each control accomplishes and what it cannot accomplish.

## E03 — Prepare, notify and shut down in a safe sequence

Before shutdown, review the specific procedure, hazards, work scope and people involved. Obtain the correct dedicated locks, tags, hasps, valve/disconnect attachments and blocking equipment. Confirm their compatibility and condition; a device that can slip off does not protect the isolation. Plan the shutdown's effects on ventilation, cooling, pressure and neighboring operations. Stopping one system may create a different hazard if supporting systems are not managed in the required order.

Notify affected employees before applying energy control. Explain equipment identity, affected operations, purpose and expected restrictions. Coordinate with the control room and other crews, but do not treat a radio message or electronic work permit as a physical lock. If more than one employer or shift is involved, establish the group/transfer arrangement before anyone starts work.

Use the machine's established normal stopping sequence. Wait for required controlled coast-down or process stabilization while staying outside danger zones. Then physically locate and operate every identified isolating device. Verify the identity at the device, not from a memory of a neighboring machine. Keep yourself outside the exposure while isolating; operating electrical equipment may require separate qualified personnel and protective measures under the electrical program.

Worked case: a mixer needs a controlled stop to prevent a hot material surge. Cutting power immediately may defeat the intended safe shutdown. Follow the approved sequence, then isolate and secure all sources. The presence of an emergency does not authorize an untrained worker to open energized electrical equipment. Stop the work and obtain competent help when the procedure cannot be followed safely.

## E04 — Apply locks and understand what tags cannot do

Apply assigned personal energy-control devices to each required isolating point in the safe position. Confirm the attachment physically prevents operation as intended. Dedicated energy-control locks are identifiable and used for that purpose, not borrowed from a toolbox locker. The device and tag must survive the environment and remain legible. Facility standardization supports recognition; the employee's identity must be clear.

Use lockout when the isolating device is capable of being locked, unless the employer has established the applicable full-protection tagout alternative. Tagout is not a worker's shortcut because a lock is missing. A tag warns; it does not physically restrain the device. Where permitted tagout is used, the employer must establish equivalent protection and the required additional measures, training and inspections. Examples such as removing an isolating circuit element or blocking a switch must be appropriate to the system and performed by authorized/qualified personnel—not invented at the job.

Never bypass, ignore or casually remove a tag. A tag that cannot attach directly must be placed as close as safely possible so the prohibition is immediately apparent to someone attempting operation, under the applicable procedure. Secure it with the required durable attachment. A loose string or unreadable paper tag cannot be treated as equivalent to compliant hardware. In 1910.147, attachment requirements include nonreusable, self-locking, hand-attachable/nonreleasable means with at least 50-pound unlocking strength; the employer supplies suitable devices.

For covered equipment installed after January 2, 1990, or undergoing the specified replacement, major repair, renovation or modification, the standard requires energy-isolating devices designed to accept lockout. Report a design deficiency through the employer's program; do not use recurring tagout as an excuse to ignore that requirement.

Worked case: a valve attachment rotates freely and permits the handle to move. A padlock through that attachment does not establish isolation. Stop, obtain the correct approved device and verify its function. If a worker proposes a tag instead to save time, reject the substitution unless the employer's compliant tagout procedure genuinely applies. The assessment uses an inert trainer, never an improvised live-system lockout.

## E05 — Control stored energy and prevent its return

After isolation and device application, make stored or residual energy safe through the specified relief, disconnection, restraint or other control. Sources remain after a motor stops: a rotating flywheel, compressed fluid, elevated carriage, spring, capacitor, hot surface or reactive contents. Control the actual hazardous mechanism rather than looking only for a zero gauge or dark screen.

Use designed bleed/drain/discharge methods with safe routing and appropriate protection. Never loosen a fitting or cut a hose as a pressure test. Support elevated components with the specified rated means before exposing any body part beneath them; a hydraulic valve or jack alone may not be the required restraint. Do not release a spring or remove a support unless its force and movement are controlled by the approved procedure. Electrical discharge and testing require the appropriately qualified person.

Energy can reaccumulate through leaking valves, thermal expansion, connected feeds, gravity, recharge circuits or ongoing process conditions. Where hazardous reaccumulation is possible, verify continued control until servicing is complete or the possibility no longer exists. A single initial zero is not enough. Specify how the condition is monitored, what trigger stops work and who responds. If control is uncertain, withdraw from exposure and resolve it rather than accepting a guessed safe interval.

Worked example: a trapped hydraulic pocket reads zero after an approved bleed but rises again. Possible causes include a second supply or leakage. Stop exposure, communicate the change and correct the isolation/control plan. Repeatedly bleeding while hands remain in the crush zone is not proof of a safe system. A paper pressure trend during training lets learners identify the first unsafe change without releasing real pressure.

## E06 — Verify the protected state before work

Before servicing, the authorized employee verifies that isolation and deenergization are effective. Verification must cover every relevant source and the actual work boundary. Follow the equipment procedure: suitable tests, inspections and a controlled attempt to operate where appropriate, with everyone safely clear. Return controls to the safe position after the attempt so later restoration cannot cause an unexpected start.

A failed start is useful but not universal proof. A motor may not start because its control circuit is disabled while hazardous voltage remains. A pressure gauge may be blocked, failed or connected to the wrong pocket. A blocked ram may still conceal spring force. Match the verification method to the energy. Record actual readings and observations; do not copy a previous shift's result.

Electrical work on exposed deenergized parts has separate qualified-person testing requirements under 1910.333. The qualified person verifies the relevant parts and circuits are deenergized, including induced voltage and unrelated backfeed. The standard expressly requires tester-operation checking immediately before and after testing circuits over 600 volts nominal; employer/manufacturer procedures may require proving the tester at other voltages too. This course does not train an otherwise unqualified person to perform electrical testing or enter an electrical exposure boundary.

Worked case: the start button produces no movement, but a separate heater supply was omitted. The machine is not verified safe for the work. Stop, update isolation and conduct appropriate verification for that supply. The correct answer is not “try Start twice.” A knowledge check asks which hazard each verification addresses and what remains untested.

## E07 — Perform work without breaking the protected boundary

Begin servicing only after the planned energy controls and verification are complete and personal protection is established. Keep the work within the authorized scope. If a new task exposes another component, opens a line or requires removing a block, revisit the energy analysis first. A lock does not protect every activity on a connected installation.

Do not lend keys, let another person work under your personal protection without the approved group arrangement, remove your device while still exposed, or operate controlled equipment. Keep guards, components and tools managed so they do not create secondary hazards. An affected employee who needs equipment must contact the responsible authorized person, not attempt to restart it. Access control and communication support the physical isolation but do not replace it.

If a control is disturbed, a lock or tag is missing, an unexpected movement occurs or conditions change, stop and move to a safe position. Notify responsible personnel, preserve evidence and reestablish the approved safe state before resuming. Do not conceal the event by replacing a tag and continuing. When hazardous energy is involved, uncertainty is a reason to resolve the condition before exposure.

Worked case: a technician completes the planned belt change and decides to also free a downstream roller. The downstream equipment has a separate drive and gravity-fed material. The first procedure may not cover it. Expand the approved isolation boundary and verify the additional sources before the extra work; the small size of the added task does not remove the risk.

## E08 — Coordinate group work and contractors

Group energy control must give each exposed authorized worker protection equivalent to personal lockout/tagout. The employer assigns an authorized person primary responsibility for a defined group and provides a way to know each member's exposure. When several crews or crafts are involved, overall coordination is assigned so the protective arrangement stays coherent. A supervisor's single lock is not automatically everyone's personal protection.

Under a suitable lockbox method, the designated personnel identify, isolate, secure and verify the equipment; controlled keys are placed in the group box; each exposed worker applies and later removes their personal device under the procedure. Each worker understands the protected boundary and verification arrangements before work. Removal by one person must not allow restoration while another remains exposed. Equivalent protection must persist across additions, departures and changes in scope.

The on-site and outside employers exchange their energy-control procedures before covered contractor servicing. The on-site employer ensures its employees understand and obey the outside program's restrictions. Resolve conflicting colors, key arrangements, device ownership, verification and restoration responsibilities before work. A contractor's signature on a visitor sheet is not procedure coordination.

Worked drill: three workers attach personal locks to a correctly established training lockbox; one finishes and removes their lock. The box remains secured by the other two. Restoration cannot occur until everyone is safely clear and the entire approved release sequence is satisfied. A fourth worker arriving mid-job must join through the procedure, not assume the first three have protected them.

## E09 — Transfer protection across shifts and personnel changes

Shift changes require an orderly transfer that prevents any unprotected interval. Plan the overlap or other employer-approved continuity method. Incoming personnel review equipment identity, work status, controls, verification and changed conditions, then establish their required personal protection before outgoing protection is removed as the procedure requires. A verbal “you're covered” is not physical continuity.

Document the transfer and reconcile every person's exposure. Account for contractors, temporary workers and personnel who leave unexpectedly. If the incoming shift is delayed, maintain the safe controlled state and use the established procedure; do not remove all devices merely because the shift has ended. Equipment should remain unavailable rather than create an unprotected gap.

An outgoing employee's device is not a convenient permanent substitute for incoming personal protection. The incoming worker must understand the actual boundary and verify protection under the procedure. A work-order status, group board or key count can support the transfer but cannot replace the required protective arrangement.

Worked case: the day crew leaves at 18:00 and the night crew arrives18:15. A plan saying “remove day locks at 18:00; add night locks at 18:15” has a gap. Use an approved continuity arrangement and do not expose anyone until their protection is established. Learners demonstrate the transfer on a trainer and show precisely why no key or restoration path becomes available while a worker is exposed.

## E10 — Temporarily energize only through the testing sequence

Some servicing requires testing or positioning before work can continue. This is a planned transition out of, then back into, the controlled state—not permission to bypass a lock while a person remains in a danger zone. Determine the test boundary, personnel roles, guarding/protection and communication in advance. Energized electrical diagnostics require their own qualified-person work practices; general lockout training does not authorize them.

Under 1910.147's sequence, clear tools and materials, remove employees from the machine/equipment area, remove controls using the authorized removal procedure, energize for the test or positioning, then deenergize and reapply the complete energy-control process before further servicing. Include stored energy and verification again. The fact that the first isolation was correct does not establish the second one after energy has been reintroduced.

Keep the test to its authorized purpose. Communicate clearly that the equipment state is changing, and control access so someone does not assume maintenance protection still exists. If the test reveals a new fault or source, revise the plan before resuming work. Do not leave a temporary jumper or bypass that defeats the restored safety arrangement.

Worked case: a conveyor is positioned for belt alignment, then a worker wants to immediately reach in because the motor has stopped. The system has been energized and must be isolated, secured, stored-energy controlled and verified again. The assessment rejects a partial “stop and reach” sequence even if the learner completed the first lockout perfectly.

## E11 — Restore safely and handle absent-owner removal correctly

Before release, inspect the area and equipment: remove nonessential items, verify required components are intact and ensure employees are safely positioned or removed. Reconcile the work scope and everyone involved. Each energy-control device is normally removed by the employee who applied it. Notify affected employees after removal and before startup as required by the release procedure. Restore using the controlled equipment sequence, watching for abnormal conditions from a safe position.

When the applying employee is unavailable, removal is an employer-directed exception under specific documented procedures and training that provide equivalent safety—not a supervisor's casual permission. Under 1910.147, the minimum safeguards include verifying the employee is not at the facility, making all reasonable efforts to contact them to inform them of removal, and ensuring they know before resuming work at that facility. Those safeguards do not replace the rest of the safe restoration procedure. Do not cut a lock simply because a phone call is unanswered.

Record the authorizing person, basis, absence check, contact efforts, equipment/personnel checks, removal and return-to-work notification. If the person is still in the facility or their exposure cannot be resolved, the required exception conditions are not established. Maintain control and escalate through the employer's process.

Worked case: a worker went home sick and their lock remains. The production deadline is not the exception. Follow the documented process, verify all conditions and record how the returning worker will learn of removal before work resumes. A practical tabletop assessment includes an initially incorrect claim that the person left; learners must resolve the contrary sign-in evidence rather than accept an assumption.

## E12 — Recognize narrow exceptions and keep the program effective

Exceptions have conditions, not convenient labels. Cord-and-plug work requires unplugging to control the relevant unexpected-start hazards and exclusive control of the plug by the servicing employee. If another person can reconnect it or other energy remains, that reasoning is insufficient. Minor servicing during production must be routine, repetitive and integral and use alternative measures providing effective protection; simply being quick is not enough. The hot-tap exception has its own essential-continuity, impractical-shutdown, documented-procedure and proven-protection conditions and does not qualify this learner to perform hot tapping.

The narrow exception from documenting a particular energy-control procedure requires all stated 1910.147 conditions: no dangerous stored/residual energy or reaccumulation; a single readily identified/isolated source; complete deenergization/deactivation by its isolation; isolation and lockout during servicing; one lock achieves protection; exclusive control by the authorized servicing employee; no hazards created for others; and no employer accidents involving unexpected activation/reenergization during such servicing. It is not an exemption from energy control, training or other program duties. Do not use it for a multi-source machine.

The employer inspects energy-control procedures at least annually using an authorized employee other than those using the procedure being inspected. The inspection identifies and corrects deviations and reviews responsibilities with each authorized employee; tagout also requires the specified affected-worker review and tag limitations. Record equipment, date, employees and inspector. An annual inspection is not the same as an annual training mandate. Retraining is triggered by changed assignments, equipment/process hazards or procedures and by observed deviations or inadequate knowledge/use; certify employee training with names and dates.

Worked review: the procedure correctly describes last year's press, but an accumulator was added. Update the procedure and retrain affected/authorized personnel as required before continued exposure, and verify proficiency. A perfect quiz on the old diagram cannot demonstrate correct control of the new machine. The VNDRLY credential therefore records actual assessed equipment scope and does not replace employer authorization.

# Formative checks

Answer before reading, correct every error using its named chapter and explain a changed example. Physical verification is assessed separately.

## E01-L1

Does a thirty-second jam-clearing task automatically avoid energy control?

**Answer:** No. Determine the exposure and applicable requirements; duration alone is not an exception.

**Reason/remediation:** Review whether a body part enters a danger zone or a guard is bypassed.

## E01-L2

Does1910.147 directly cover oil-and-gas well drilling and servicing?

**Answer:** No. It expressly excludes that scope; identify applicable requirements and the equipment safe-control procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** Exclusion is not permission for uncontrolled energy. Distinguish scope from protection.

## E02-L1

Name four energy mechanisms on the example press.

**Answer:** Electrical drive, fluid pressure/accumulator, gravity at the ram and alternate feed; identify all actual sources.

**Reason/remediation:** The most obvious disconnect is not a complete inventory. Trace every path.

## E02-L2

Why is the touchscreen Stop not an energy-isolating device?

**Answer:** It is a control command, not a physical means preventing energy transmission or release.

**Reason/remediation:** Commands may reset or fail. Locate the actual isolating device.

## E03-L1

What should be communicated before applying controls?

**Answer:** Equipment/task identity, affected operations and restrictions to affected people, with contractor/group coordination as needed.

**Reason/remediation:** A correct lockout can still disrupt others if coordination is absent; notification does not replace isolation.

## E03-L2

Can an abrupt power cut always replace the normal shutdown sequence?

**Answer:** No. Use the established orderly shutdown to avoid creating additional hazards.

**Reason/remediation:** Review how supporting systems and process conditions change during shutdown.

## E04-L1

A locked attachment still lets the valve operate. Is it adequate?

**Answer:** No. Replace it with the suitable approved device and verify it holds the intended safe position.

**Reason/remediation:** The presence of a padlock is not proof of restraint.

## E04-L2

Why may a worker not substitute a tag simply because a lock is unavailable?

**Answer:** Tagout requires the applicable employer-established protective system and conditions; a warning does not physically restrain operation.

**Reason/remediation:** Review equivalent protection and tag limitations, not just tag appearance.

## E05-L1

The pump is isolated but the ram remains raised. What remains?

**Answer:** Gravity and potentially stored fluid energy; apply the specified rated support and stored-energy controls before exposure.

**Reason/remediation:** A stopped pump does not support a load. Trace what can still move.

## E05-L2

Pressure returns after an initial zero. What is the response?

**Answer:** Stop exposure and resolve reaccumulation through the approved control/verification process.

**Reason/remediation:** An initial zero is temporary evidence; continued control is required when energy can rebuild.

## E06-L1

Can a failed start attempt establish absence of electrical voltage?

**Answer:** No. Appropriate electrical verification is performed by a qualified person using suitable testing.

**Reason/remediation:** Control response and voltage absence are different findings.

## E06-L2

Where should operating controls be left after a verification attempt?

**Answer:** In the prescribed safe position.

**Reason/remediation:** An active Start command can create a hazard during restoration.

## E07-L1

A small extra task reaches another driven component. Is it covered automatically?

**Answer:** No. Reevaluate the scope and additional sources before exposure.

**Reason/remediation:** A lock protects the defined boundary, not every nearby machine.

## E07-L2

A tag disappears during servicing. May it simply be replaced and work continue?

**Answer:** Stop exposure, report and resolve the disturbed protective state before resuming.

**Reason/remediation:** The missing tag may indicate another control changed; do not conceal the event.

## E08-L1

Does the group coordinator’s lock alone automatically protect every worker?

**Answer:** No. The group procedure must provide each exposed worker equivalent personal protection.

**Reason/remediation:** Demonstrate how each person is protected and restoration prevented.

## E08-L2

What must host and contractor exchange before covered servicing?

**Answer:** Their respective energy-control procedures and restrictions, with resolved coordination.

**Reason/remediation:** A sign-in sheet is not a procedure exchange.

## E09-L1

Outgoing locks are removed fifteen minutes before incoming locks arrive. What is wrong?

**Answer:** The transfer can create an unprotected gap; use the approved continuity arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** Shift timing cannot waive continuous protection.

## E09-L2

What must incoming workers know besides the machine number?

**Answer:** Work status, isolation boundary, sources/controls, verification, changes and their personal protection arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** A verbal handoff without actual protective understanding is insufficient.

## E10-L1

After a positioning test, can a worker resume as soon as the motor stops?

**Answer:** No. Reapply all isolation, securing, stored-energy control and verification before servicing.

**Reason/remediation:** The test reintroduced energy and invalidated the prior protected state.

## E10-L2

What must be cleared before release for a temporary test?

**Answer:** Tools/materials and employees from the relevant machine/equipment area, with proper removal and test controls.

**Reason/remediation:** Testing is a controlled transition, not bypassing locks around exposed workers.

## E11-L1

Who normally removes a personal energy-control device?

**Answer:** The employee who applied it.

**Reason/remediation:** Exceptional removal is employer-directed under specific documented safeguards, not routine convenience.

## E11-L2

Is an unanswered phone call sufficient to cut an absent worker’s lock?

**Answer:** No. Follow the full documented employer procedure, verify absence, make reasonable contact efforts and ensure knowledge before return, plus safe release checks.

**Reason/remediation:** One failed call does not establish all safeguards.

## E12-L1

Does the annual procedure inspection mean all workers must repeat this course annually by federal rule?

**Answer:** No. Annual inspection and change/deficiency-triggered retraining are distinct; employer/VNDRLY review policies may add requirements.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not confuse the inspection duty with a generic refresher interval.

## E12-L2

Can a multi-source machine use the narrow exception from documented procedures?

**Answer:** No. All stated conditions must apply, including a single readily identifiable isolated source and no dangerous stored/reaccumulating energy.

**Reason/remediation:** The exception is narrow and does not waive energy control itself.
