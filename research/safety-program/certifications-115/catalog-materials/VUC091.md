# VNDRLY/U Onshore Fire/Emergency Response Team Leader — Further Training

VUC091. Original materials, 18November 2026. For previously trained and appointed leaders; not initial firefighter training. Includes required professionally controlled live-fire assessment resources. No live activity performed during authoring. Full external specification unavailable; no OPITO approval or exact equivalence claimed. Synthetic numbers never replace actual operational limits.

## Q91-1 — Further training entry and command responsibility

Further training refreshes an existing team leader's demonstrated capability; it is not a shortcut for an untrained person to command a fire team. Authenticate prior relevant training, employer appointment, recent practice, medical/respiratory readiness and the scope of duties before practical assessment. A long gap or changed equipment calls for development before demanding performance. Record the gap rather than inventing a renewal or backdating a certificate.

The team leader translates incident objectives into safe tasks for a particular crew and reports to the incident command structure. The leader does not silently assume overall command or exceed the brigade's organizational remit. Know whether the team is authorized for exterior response, interior structural work, particular process hazards or other defined tasks. A request outside that scope must be clarified and escalated. The leader owns the decision to withhold or stop the crew when readiness or conditions are inadequate.

Worked case: an experienced exterior-response leader is asked to command interior structural entry without the required current capability and support. The leader reports the limitation, maintains a safe defensive posture and requests appropriate resources. Changed case: a valid certificate exists but the team has never used the newly issued breathing apparatus. The certificate does not replace equipment-specific training and checks.

## Q91-2 — Preincident information and initial size-up

Before response, learn the site layout, access, hazardous inventories, isolation interfaces, fire-water arrangements, fixed systems, occupancy and emergency communications. During initial size-up distinguish known facts from assumptions: incident location, material, people at risk, wind or smoke movement, exposures, access and escape, available resources and special hazards. Observe from a safe position. Do not enter an uncertain hazard simply to gather a better report.

Transmit a short situation report and proposed task boundary to command. Update it when conditions change. A familiar building can contain unfamiliar contractors, stored chemicals or impaired systems. Use the current preplan and technical contacts, but do not treat an old drawing as proof of current isolation or structural condition. The leader should explicitly identify the critical unknown that prevents action and what evidence or specialist resource is needed.

Worked case: a workshop alarm is accompanied by smoke and an unidentified chemical-container label. Report the unknown material and hold incompatible attack choices while command obtains identification. Changed case: the container is identified but a neighboring cylinder is exposed. Reassess the exposure and exclusion arrangement with command; knowing the first material does not resolve the second hazard.

## Q91-3 — Objectives, risk decisions and task briefing

A task order must connect objective, method, limits and withdrawal. State what the crew is to achieve, where, with which resources, under whose supervision, how to communicate and what conditions require stopping. Confirm understanding by asking the crew to repeat the critical boundaries. Avoid broad commands such as deal with it that conceal assumptions about rescue, isolation or entry.

Use the actual incident risk process. Benefit, uncertainty and available controls change together; a task that was reasonable at the start can become unacceptable. The leader should request a change or withdraw when conditions exceed the plan, rather than continue because equipment is already deployed. Offensive and defensive strategies must be coordinated through command so one crew's action does not expose another. Do not start ventilation, open boundaries or alter a process without the authorized coordinated plan.

Worked case: task is exterior exposure protection from a marked safe position, not entry. A crew member starts toward the building to improve the view. The leader recalls the member, restates the boundary and reports any loss of control. Changed case: the safe position is affected by changing conditions. Stop, withdraw/account and request a revised task; do not simply move closer to preserve the original objective.

## Q91-4 — Personnel, equipment and readiness

Match people and equipment to the task before deployment. Check qualifications, fitness/readiness, protective ensemble, breathing equipment, communications and inspected firefighting equipment through the actual program. A spare cylinder or radio may be present but unusable or incompatible. Verify rather than count objects. Equipment defects are reported and removed from use, not hidden because the drill is about leadership.

Assign roles explicitly: team members, entry-control/accountability interface, supply support, backup/rescue resources and safety oversight as required by the actual plan and jurisdiction. A person cannot be counted simultaneously for incompatible critical duties. Interior structural and other immediately dangerous atmosphere work have specific staffing, respiratory and rescue requirements; apply the actual applicable rule and brigade procedure, not a generic arithmetic shortcut. The leader must confirm those arrangements with command before committing the crew.

Worked case: four names appear on the board, but one person is operating a critical pump and another is not qualified for the proposed duty. The leader does not equate four names with a compliant entry arrangement. Changed case: staffing is corrected but the emergency radio fails its check. Resolve communications and backup before deployment; adequate headcount alone is insufficient.

## Q91-5 — Breathing apparatus and endurance control

The leader checks that the crew follows the actual breathing-apparatus procedure and keeps track of the limiting member. Starting pressure, condition, work rate, return distance, alarms and reserves all matter. A nominal cylinder duration is not a promise of usable work time. Never plan to work until a low-air alarm as a routine target. Maintain crew integrity and initiate return according to the actual procedure before the limiting condition is reached.

Classroom card A91 is only arithmetic: starting indication 280 units, turnaround threshold 180 units and observed consumption 10 units/minute yields(280−180)/10=10 minutes to that threshold at a constant assumed rate. It is not a real cylinder rule or a safe entry duration. If rate increases to 20, the same allowance is 5 minutes. Real consumption changes with effort and conditions; actual procedures, alarms and safety margins govern. No classroom calculation authorizes entry.

Worked case: one member reports an unexpected pressure decrease while others appear normal. Stop the task and follow the team withdrawal/emergency procedure; do not average the cylinders. Changed case: radio contact becomes unreliable during a breathing-apparatus task. The leader applies the established lost-communication/withdrawal procedure and accounts for the crew, rather than using remaining air as justification to continue.

## Q91-6 — Communications, accountability and crew integrity

Track each person's identity, assignment, location, status and changes. A crew report must reflect the actual crew, not the number originally dispatched. Use closed-loop communication for critical orders: identify recipient, task/location/limits, receive acknowledgment and verify execution. Keep emergency traffic recognizable and concise. A message sent without acknowledgment may not have reached its target.

Maintain crew contact as required by the actual operation. No freelancing is permitted. Report entry, withdrawal, reassignment, rehabilitation and release to the accountability system. When a member is unaccounted for, activate the emergency process immediately with last known information; do not silently replace the name with another person to preserve a team count. The leader manages the assigned team while command coordinates broader resources.

Worked case: T1–T4 were assigned; return board showsT1, T2, T2, T4. Four entries represent three unique people andT3 is unresolved. The leader reportsT3 immediately, with last known location and task, and initiates the plan. Changed case: T3 is confirmed in medical care through the authorized channel. Update status/time explicitly; medical location is not active crew availability.

## Q91-7 — Fire behavior, media and process interfaces

The leader must understand why an apparently simple fire can change rapidly. Fuel, heat, airflow, confinement and process energy affect behavior. Unexpected smoke/heat change, structural concerns, loss of control or hazardous-material uncertainty require reassessment and potentially immediate withdrawal. Do not use this refresher as a source of improvised tactics for an unfamiliar process.

Select an extinguishing or exposure-control medium only within the approved preplan, material compatibility and equipment capability. Water is not automatically suitable for every energized, reactive or liquid-fuel situation. Foam choice, concentration and application are system/material specific and may have environmental constraints. Isolation belongs to authorized process personnel and must be confirmed; a request to close a valve is not proof it is closed. Fixed-system operation, ventilation and manual attack must be coordinated so one action does not increase another crew's risk.

Worked case: a proposed medium is not confirmed compatible with an unidentified material. Hold that task and obtain technical advice while protecting people and maintaining exclusion. Changed case: a process isolation is reported requested rather than verified. Keep the distinction in the briefing and do not plan as if energy release has stopped.

## Q91-8 — Water, foam and resource sustainability

A leader must know whether the task can be sustained and how supply failure will be communicated. Confirm actual available flow, pressure, water source, hoses, appliances and backup with qualified operators. A tank volume alone does not establish delivery capability. Hose routing must protect access and crew escape without creating uncontrolled trip or vehicle hazards. Changes in supply require prompt coordination and a safe response, not a hidden reduction in protection.

Synthetic W91 uses 1800 litres/minute available and two planned demands 700 and 600 litres/minute, leaving 500 litres/minute arithmetic margin. Adding a 700 litres/minute demand exceeds available flow by 200. This is a planning exercise only; actual hydraulic performance and safety reserve require the competent system plan. Foam card F91 assumes 3% concentrate by final solution volume.1000 litres solution requires 30 litres concentrate and 970 litres water.200 litres concentrate theoretically supports 6666.67 litres solution before losses/reserves; it does not prove usable incident duration.

Worked case: a task needs 1200 litres solution at the fictional 3%, so 36 litres concentrate. Only 30 litres are verified available. Do not label the task supplied based on container count; request additional suitable resource or revise through command. Changed case: enough concentrate exists but its compatibility/condition is unverified. Quantity does not resolve suitability.

## Q91-9 — Leading controlled deployment and withdrawal

Before movement, the leader gives the task brief, confirms readiness, communications, accountability, supply, escape and stop conditions. During deployment maintain observation of crew performance and changing conditions. Position to lead the assigned team without losing the command interface. Give clear corrections and stop unsafe action promptly. An exercise is not a reason to tolerate a real unsafe act until the assessor has finished taking notes.

Withdrawal is an organized task. Communicate it, confirm receipt, maintain crew integrity, move by the approved safe route and report all personnel/status at the designated point. If a person or route is lost, use the emergency procedure rather than assuming the crew will regroup later. A successful extinguishment does not compensate for an unaccounted member. After withdrawal do not re-enter because the original objective remains incomplete; obtain reassessment and authorization.

Worked case: the exercise controller gives a simulated supply-loss card. The leader stops the affected action, withdraws under the approved drill procedure and confirms every identity. The controller never actually removes a critical live-fire safety supply. Changed case: the team returns safely but one radio message was not acknowledged. Establish whether the other assigned group received the withdrawal order through command; do not infer scene-wide completion from your own crew's return.

## Q91-10 — Casualty and firefighter emergency coordination

A leader must rapidly recognize an emergency involving a team member and communicate identity, location, last task, condition if known and resources needed. Activate the site's emergency traffic/rescue process. Keep other crew members accounted for and avoid uncontrolled spontaneous rescue entry. The designated rescue resources and command structure manage the response within their competence and protection.

For casualty movement, first aid or medical treatment, use personnel trained and authorized for the actual task. The leader coordinates access, information and handover rather than inventing a lifting or treatment technique. A mannequin may be used in the safe exercise; no learner is trapped, exposed to smoke or deliberately deprived of air. A simulated casualty must not distract the team from a real safety issue. The real-emergency override stops training immediately.

Worked case: T3 does not respond and was last reported near a marked training prop. The leader declares the simulated emergency, provides last known information, keepsT1/T2/T4 controlled and requests the planned rescue resource. Changed case: a real learner reports chest discomfort during the exercise. Stop the exercise and activate actual medical response; do not continue treating it as a scenario inject.

## Q91-11 — Hazardous materials, contamination and environmental control

Fire response can expose crews to smoke, debris, chemical products and contaminated runoff. Identify the hazard and the limits of the team's training. A fire-team-leader certificate does not automatically confer hazardous-material technician or incident-commander qualification. Establish or respect control zones, access and specialist advice under the actual incident plan. Do not cross a boundary to read a label when safer information routes exist.

Plan gross contamination reduction, equipment segregation and transfer to appropriate decontamination/cleaning arrangements. Protect clean areas and vehicles from contaminated equipment. Runoff containment and environmental notifications are coordinated with the responsible specialists; they must not compromise immediate life safety or create unauthorized exposure. Do not declare a substance harmless because smoke has disappeared or because a generic detector reads zero for a different hazard.

Worked case: crew gear is visibly contaminated after a simulated chemical-exposure card. The leader routes it through the approved segregation/decontamination process and records exposure information rather than loading it into the clean cab. Changed case: an unknown residue appears on equipment. Keep it segregated, obtain identification/advice and do not prescribe a cleaning chemical by guesswork.

## Q91-12 — Rehabilitation, rotation and human performance

Sustained response demands attention to fatigue, heat burden, hydration, medical needs and cognitive performance. The leader uses the actual rehabilitation and medical-monitoring arrangements and reports crew availability accurately. A person in rehabilitation is not simultaneously ready for immediate redeployment. Rest duration alone does not prove medical readiness; qualified personnel and the applicable procedure determine return to duty.

Encourage early reporting of symptoms or equipment discomfort without ridicule. Watch for confusion, unusual behavior or reduced ability to follow instructions. Stop and obtain appropriate medical assessment when indicated. Rotate tasks through command so essential functions and accountability remain covered. After a distressing incident, provide access to support and follow-up without forcing public disclosure of personal reactions.

Worked case: a crew member insists on another task but has been referred for medical assessment. The leader marks that member unavailable and requests replacement through command. Changed case: the leader is fatigued and making communication errors. Request relief and conduct an explicit handover rather than conceal the condition to finish the exercise.

## Q91-13 — Transfer, recovery and honest records

Transfer of leadership requires a briefing and acknowledgment. State current objective, strategy, crew identities/locations/status, hazards, resources, communications, outstanding requests, limits and next decisions. Make clear when responsibility changes. A new leader arriving on scene does not automatically know what the previous leader assumed. Keep command informed of the transfer and preserve continuity in accountability.

After control or exercise completion, account for people, secure equipment through qualified operators, record defects/exposures and restore readiness only after checks. Operational restart belongs to the authorized site process, not simply to the fire team's declaration that the visible fire is out. Debrief against observations and identify corrective actions, owners and closure evidence. Preserve near misses and failed attempts; do not improve the record by rewriting them as successes.

Worked case: replacement leader receives equipment status but not thatT3 is in medical care. The handover is incomplete because crew availability is wrong. Correct it before transfer. Changed case: incident command releases the team, but a damaged hose remains mixed with serviceable equipment. Tag/segregate and record it before readiness is declared.

## Q91-14 — Integrated further-training standard and renewal decision

The learner must lead complete controlled practical scenarios as well as explain decisions. This original package requires two changed live-fire training scenarios at a professionally managed facility plus a non-live emergency/coordination exercise. Live-fire scope, prop, fuels, operating limits, safety staffing, medical cover and shutdown arrangements are approved by the facility; this course does not supply an improvised ignition recipe. Prior competence and current readiness remain prerequisites.

Assess leadership independently: briefing, resource decisions, crew control, communication, dynamic reassessment, withdrawal, accountability, casualty interface, rehabilitation and handover. Instructor intervention for safety prevents that attempt being counted independent. A high exam score cannot compensate for unsafe crew commitment or false accountability. Reassess after targeted development on a changed task; do not simply replay memorized radio lines.

Worked case: candidate controls the flame task but ignores a simulated withdrawal trigger. The attempt fails even if no one is harmed. Changed case: candidate recognizes the trigger, withdraws safely and leaves the task unfinished. That is the correct leadership decision. Issue only the scope actually demonstrated and recorded; do not claim external revalidation or employer authority from this original VNDRLY package.

After legitimate readiness release and live task, announce planned withdrawal cue at safe point. Require named accountability and explicit changed-status handover. No actual safety impairment is introduced.

Candidate sends emergency traffic with last known information, activates planned rescue interface, controls remaining crew, updates verified medical location and hands over to relief.
