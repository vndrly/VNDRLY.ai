# VUC078 — Well Operations Crew Resource Management

## Purpose and scope

This original VNDRLY/U programme develops nontechnical behaviors that support safe drilling, completion, intervention and workover. It covers awareness, decisions, communication, teamwork, leadership and performance factors through instruction, role-play, changed scenarios and individual assessment. It complements technical competence; it does not authorize well-control actions, replace a technical qualification or issue an IWCF/IOGP credential. An assertive person choosing an unsafe technical action has not demonstrated safe performance.

Entrants need role orientation and enough well-operations vocabulary to understand the fictional exercises. Explain unfamiliar terms before practice. The supplied response card determines technical boundaries; no real valve, alarm, safety device or well is manipulated. Assess observable behavior and reasoning, not personality, accent, seniority or confidence. This course does not diagnose fatigue disorders or provide medical fitness clearance.

## Q01 — Human performance in a work system

People work with in procedures, equipment, schedules, incentives and relationships. A mistake may be the visible end of a system that made the wrong action likely and the right action difficult. Distinguish an unintended slip or memory lapse, a mistaken understanding and a deliberate departure from a rule; do not infer intention with out evidence. Establish what the person knew, what the system showed and what competing demands existed at the time.

Crew resource management makes available people, information, procedures and support usable together. It is not a substitute for engineering barriers. Start by checking role competence, actual configuration, reliable information and the means to challenge or stop. If a procedure contradicts installed equipment, report the mismatch and obtain a safe resolution rather than inventing a local workaround.

**Worked case:** a screen uses the same color for commanded-closed and verified-closed. A trainee reports secure after a command. Correct the distinction, protect the operation and address the display/procedure weakness. **Changed case:** a supervisor knowingly pressures the crew to skip verification. Immediate protection and accountable escalation are required; a systems approach does not excuse deliberate unsafe conduct. Preserve facts and avoid unsupported blame labels.

## Q02 — Situation awareness: observation, interpretation and projection

Collect relevant cues, interpret their meaning and anticipate what could happen next. These are separate tasks. A gain is an observation; a tank-transfer explanation is a hypothesis until verified. Check operating phase, barrier state, pressures, volumes, alarms, people and environment. Seek independent evidence where appropriate, but do not delay a required response while seeking perfect certainty.

Original W78 begins with tank volume 120 bbl. An authorized addition of 5 makes expected volume 125. The display shows 129, leaving unexplained 4 bbl. Under the fictional response card, an unexplained gain requires the trained operator to invoke the approved secure response and the team to report/verify state. A likely benign explanation does not cancel that trigger. State the discrepancy with units and distinguish known transfer from unexplained volume.

**Worked case:** an operator focuses on a maintenance call and misses the trend. Another member reports the cue and the supervisor reallocates attention. **Changed case:** a later record proves another 4 bbl transfer, but an independent flow indication remains abnormal. Resolve each cue; explaining one does not clear all concerns. State what is known, what is uncertain and what evidence would change the interpretation.

## Q03 — Shared awareness, fixation and handover

A team can contain the right information with out sharing the same picture. One person sees a trend, another a stable gauge, and the supervisor hears that the last shift saw similar behavior. State current facts, identify disagreements and assign someone to reconcile them. Ask what would disprove the favored explanation. Watch for fixation, confirmation bias and normalizing repeated deviations because nothing bad happened before.

Handover transfers responsibility, not just a log. Prepare actual state, changes, overrides, permits, maintenance, trends and unresolved actions. Incoming personnel question and cross-check critical facts before accepting responsibility. Name the next decision owner and escalation trigger. Acknowledgment must mean understanding, not merely that a transmission was heard.

**Worked case:** outgoing personnel say all good, but an alarm is inhibited. Incoming staff hold the affected task until the impairment and approved controls are understood. **Changed case:** the alarm is restored but a maintenance isolation remains. Update and verify the whole relevant state rather than carrying forward reassurance. Do not erase unresolved issues through successive handovers. A short handover can be complete; a long one can still omit the essential barrier condition.

## Q04 — Decisions under time pressure

Match the decision process to urgency. For an immediate recognized hazard, use the trained approved response instead of a lengthy discussion. Once controlled, define the problem, identify constraints, compare feasible options, choose with in authority, assign execution and review results. Reassess when evidence changes. A command is not a completed decision until achieved state is verified.

W78 offers continue/check later, SIM-SECURE/assess, or invent an unfamiliar method. The supplied response card supports the second. The first delays protection; the third exceeds technical authority. After containment is verified, the team compares approved next steps against actual constraints with qualified support.

**Worked case:** a senior person recognizes another well's pattern and proposes its solution. Ask which assumptions match and which do not. Experience supports judgment but does not waive the current envelope. **Changed case:** a required path becomes unavailable. Hold and replan through the authorized process. Time already invested is not a reason to continue an invalid option. Record why the plan changed so the next shift does not restore the rejected approach.

## Q05 — Communication that closes the loop

Critical messages identify recipient, equipment/task, intended action, value and units where relevant, and limits/conditions. The receiver repeats essential meaning; the sender confirms or corrects; the receiver reports completion and actual result. Saying copy alone may not establish understanding. Avoid ambiguous pronouns, unshared jargon and numbers with out reference. Agree a working language, reliable channel and fallback for failure.

Original message: Operator, tank 129 bbl against 125 expected; unexplained gain 4 bbl; apply the approved secure response. Receiver repeats the discrepancy and action, sender confirms, and operator reports the achieved condition. If a message could mean 15 or 50, request repetition rather than guess.

**Worked case:** noisy instruction says open that one. The receiver requests exact identity and authority; no action follows ambiguity. **Changed case:** the instruction is clear but conflicts with the plan. Readback is not obedience to an unsafe command. State the conflict and use challenge/stop arrangements. Effective communication includes listening and correction rather than forcing the sender's first wording through the system.

## Q06 — Assertiveness, challenge and escalation

A useful challenge describes the observation, explains the concern and proposes a safe next step. Speak early enough to affect the decision. Respectful language helps, but protection must not wait for a perfectly polite argument. Use actual stop-work and escalation arrangements. A memorized fixed number of challenges must never delay an immediate required protective action.

Example: The gain remains unexplained; our response card requires securing. Continuing risks losing the detection opportunity. We need the approved response now. The lead acknowledges the evidence, protects state and names the next owner. If dismissed, escalate through the defined route while maintaining required safety. Do not invent authority to manipulate unfamiliar equipment.

**Worked case:** a new contractor notices a mismatch but assumes the experienced supervisor knows. Practice stating the fact with out apologizing for noticing it. **Changed case:** independent evidence shows part of the challenge mistaken. Thank the reporter, correct the shared picture and continue only when all criteria are met. Penalizing honest challenge suppresses future detection. Deliberate fabrication or harassment is different and must be judged on evidence.

## Q07 — Teamwork, roles and cross-monitoring

A crew needs a shared objective, clear responsibilities and awareness of who has relevant information. Assign operational lead, trained operator, monitor/volume role, recorder and technical liaison as appropriate. Clarify command, verification and support roles. Cross-monitoring catches discrepancies and provides backup; it does not mean conflicting commands or silent takeover of controls.

W78 assigns operator to response, observer to trends, supervisor to coordination and recorder to facts/actions. A maintenance specialist supplies evidence through the lead. When monitoring workload grows, the lead explicitly reallocates secondary work and confirms monitoring remains covered. A task list with out named acceptance can leave everyone assuming another person is responsible.

**Worked case:** two contractors each believe the other verified restoration. The lead requires actual evidence and a named competent verifier before release. **Changed case:** the verifier is unavailable. Reassign through the approved process and communicate the change; do not accept an empty checkbox. Assess safe achieved outcomes and individual contributions rather than allowing the strongest member to conceal everyone else's gaps.

## Q08 — Leadership, followership and standards

Leadership creates conditions for sound decisions: clear priorities, realistic workload, competent roles, maintained standards and a climate that hears concerns. Urgent action needs concise direction; planning and review need relevant expertise and challenge. Explain reasons when time permits. Delegation assigns a defined task; it does not prove the receiver has every required fact or authority.

Followership is active support with checking, reporting and challenge. Team members do not hide uncertainty to appear capable. Before starting, the leader states goal, roles, barrier state, limits, stop triggers and contingency communication. Ask critical roles to explain their part rather than asking whether everyone is happy.

**Worked case:** schedule pressure prompts we cannot afford another delay. A member identifies unresolved verification; the leader maintains the hold and obtains help. **Changed case:** a dominant voice overwhelms a quiet specialist's contradictory evidence. Invite it directly, reconcile and update the decision. Evaluation rewards effective listening and action, not a theatrical command style or loudness.

## Q09 — Workload, distraction and task recovery

Attention and working memory are limited. High demand hides cues; low stimulation can reduce vigilance. Identify critical monitoring that must remain covered when calls, paperwork or maintenance compete. Defer nonessential work, obtain help and use records/checklists instead of memory alone. After interruption, return to a known verified point rather than guessing the last completed step.

In W78 the operator receives a logistics call during an abnormal trend. The supervisor assigns the call elsewhere and protects control/monitoring. If safe staffing is unavailable, hold the affected task through the appropriate process. Be more careful does not create capacity or repair an unreadable display.

**Worked case:** interruption follows a command but precedes verification. On return, identify the last confirmed state and reconcile or repeat the affected step under the procedure. Do not tick completion from memory. **Changed case:** a helper arrives with out critical-task competence. Assign suitable support and retain competent coverage. More people do not automatically provide useful resources when roles and knowledge are unclear.

## Q10 — Fatigue, stress and readiness

Fatigue and stress can affect attention, memory, reaction and judgment. Work schedules, rest opportunity, travel, workload and environment are organizational as well as individual concerns. Encourage early reporting before an error proves the problem. Supervisors assess work risk, arrange competent relief/task changes and use employer fitness/support processes. This course specifies no universal safe shift length or medical diagnosis.

Original duty log: 12 scheduled+2 overtime+1 mandatory work handover=15 duty hours. Two commuting hours make 17 combined demand, with commute separately recorded rather than mislabeled paid duty. These numbers prompt policy/risk review; they do not calculate medical fitness. Caffeine or confidence cannot establish readiness for a safety-critical task.

**Worked case:** a worker reports lapses after disrupted rest. Protect the task, provide relief and support a safe journey/rest arrangement through policy. **Changed case:** a rested person becomes overloaded during an emergency. Reduce competing demands, provide clear commands/support and reassess readiness. Keep private health information out of broad operational logs; record the necessary work restriction and response.

## Q11 — Error trapping, debrief and learning

Reconstruct events from time-stamped observations, commands and achieved states. Ask what helped, what made work difficult and where a barrier or communication failed. Separate facts from presumed motives. Correct the conditions for success through displays, procedures, staffing, handover or checks; retraining alone may not fix the system.

Original log records cue at minute 2, acknowledgment 3 and response initiation 4. The 1- and 2-minute intervals describe the exercise, not universal acceptable response times. Compare actual behavior with the scenario trigger and technical procedure. If avoidable discussion delayed protection, identify how a future crew can act while analysis continues.

**Worked case:** the outcome is safe despite an ignored warning. Identify the unsafe process rather than praising only the result. **Changed case:** equipment failure produces a poor outcome despite reasonable actions. Investigate limits and evidence with out assuming bad intent. Give each improvement an owner, due point and effectiveness check. Attendance at a briefing is not proof of changed behavior.

## Q12 — Integrated performance and transfer

Skills must work together. Fluent communication can coexist with fixation; recognition can coexist with failure to assign action. In the integrated case, identify cues, apply the supplied response, share state, allocate roles, challenge unsupported assumptions, manage workload and hand over verified conditions. Observe each learner in more than one role and change facts to distinguish reasoning from memorized dialogue.

Workplace transfer needs usable procedures, clear stop authority, leaders who accept challenge and opportunities to practice. A certificate cannot promise an error-free crew. Create a specific personal action and a system improvement tied to a demonstrated obstacle. Review whether behavior occurred and helped, not merely how many people attended.

**Worked case:** the crew controls the event but hands over only finished. Incoming staff need actual barriers, unresolved faults, restrictions and next actions; the scenario remains incomplete until responsibility transfers reliably. **Changed case:** incoming staff find a contradictory log. Retain safe state, reconcile evidence and document the responsible decision. Course completion demonstrates the assessed nontechnical skills; it does not authorize an unfamiliar well or override technical procedures.

# VUC078 — Facilitator, role-play and evaluation resources

## Delivery and preparation

32 planned hours: 8 instruction, 6 guided applications, 8 team practice, 6 independent observed evaluation and 4 written assessment/debrief. Use a facilitator competent in well operations and nontechnical-skills instruction, plus an observer able to record behavior without directing the candidate. Twelve stations are actual observed role-play, not actual well operation. A classroom, remote breakout or simulator room may be used if every participant can communicate and see the required evidence. A simulator is optional; the supplied state cards are sufficient for this behavioral scope.

Provide the coursebook, role cards, scenario state board, clock, radio or equivalent communication channel, action log and rubric. Agree an exercise stop word, respectful conduct and accessibility arrangements. Do not introduce sleep deprivation, frightening surprise, live alarms or physical hazards. Explain technical vocabulary and the fictional response card before practice. During assessment, no assessor coaching; technical clarification of an ambiguous exercise fact is recorded and supplied equally.

## W78 fictional technical response card

The operation begins in a stable monitored state. The planned tank addition is 5 bbl. An unexplained gain, abnormal independent flow indication, uncertain barrier status or an instruction conflicting with the approved plan requires the team to hold the affected task and the designated trained operator to invoke SIM-SECURE. SIM-SECURE is an educational action card, not a real valve sequence. The facilitator reveals whether achieved containment is verified. If not verified, maintain the fictional hold, report actual uncertainty and call the technical lead. No participant may invent a real operating procedure.

After containment is verified, remain in SIM-HOLD while the team reconciles data and technical authority. SIM-RESUME is permitted only when every listed abnormal cue is explained, required monitoring/barriers are verified, the named supervisor has the scenario's technical release card, and the team reads back roles/limits. A tank explanation alone does not resolve an independent abnormal flow cue. Any team member can call EXERCISE-STOP for a safety or exercise problem. These rules define assessment behavior without teaching a field shut-in recipe.

## Participant role cards

**Operator:** attend to state display, apply only the response card, read back critical instructions, report command versus achieved state and challenge conflicts. Do not handle the logistics call while critical monitoring is uncovered.

**Supervisor/lead:** establish roles, receive and acknowledge observations, protect the required response, assign analysis/support and decide within the release card. Invite challenge; do not dismiss data because of seniority. Keep a named person monitoring.

**Observer/volume role:** compare observed with expected values, distinguish facts from possible explanations, communicate discrepancy and escalate if ignored. Continue monitoring other cues after one discrepancy is explained.

**Recorder/incoming shift:** retain time, data, decisions, owners and achieved state; request missing handover information. Do not rewrite the record to match the final interpretation.

**Technical liaison/contractor:** supply the assigned evidence only when asked or at the timed inject; identify what the evidence does and does not establish. Do not issue competing operational commands.

Small groups may combine recorder/liaison roles, but operator and operational lead remain distinct. Rotate so each candidate is observed as a responsible decision maker and as a reporting/challenging team member. Individual assessment cannot be awarded because another person carried the scenario.

## Guided laboratories

### W01 — Cue map and competing explanations

Initial tank 120; authorized addition 5; observed 129. Calculate expected 125 and unexplained 4. Make three columns: observed fact, possible explanation, evidence needed. Add abnormal flow and an unrelated maintenance call. Expected result: invoke the supplied response without waiting for complete diagnosis, allocate monitoring and distinguish transfer hypothesis from proof. Changed practice uses initial 90/addition 3/observed 98: expected 93, unexplained 5. A later transfer explanation does not erase the separate flow cue.

### W02 — Closed-loop communication and challenge

Pairs exchange a critical message with recipient, exact task, discrepancy/units and action under the response card. Receiver reads back; sender confirms/corrects; receiver reports actual outcome. Inject a broken message that could mean 15 or 50 and an ambiguous 'open that one.' Expected response: request clarification and do not act on guesses. Then practice a specific observation-concern-action challenge against a senior-role dismissal. Immediate required protection is not delayed to complete a fixed number of polite challenges.

### W03 — Decision and role allocation

Compare continue-and-check-later, SIM-SECURE-and-assess, and invent-new-method options. Identify which is allowed and why. Allocate operator, lead, observer and recorder; each accepts the role. Inject an unavailable monitoring person and a new helper without technical competence. Expected response: explicitly reassign competent critical coverage, use the helper only within competence and hold the task if coverage cannot be maintained.

### W04 — Handover and interruption recovery

Outgoing record says 'normal,' but a monitoring alarm is inhibited and a maintenance isolation remains. Prepare a two-way handover containing actual state, limitations, permits/maintenance, trends, owners and next decision. Incoming candidate cross-checks and challenges the contradiction. Interrupt a checklist between command and verification; expected recovery starts from the last confirmed state and reconciles the incomplete step under the response card. Do not infer completion from memory.

### W05 — Workload, stress and fatigue response

Compute 12 scheduled+2 overtime+1 work handover=15 duty hours, plus 2 commute=17 combined demand. Separate categories; no numeric total is a fitness clearance. A worker reports lapses and inadequate rest. Expected supervisor response: protect the task, arrange competent relief/support and use the employer process, without publicizing private health details. Changed case: 10 scheduled+3 overtime+0.5 work handover=13.5 duty, plus 1.5 commute=15 combined; do not declare safe merely because lower than the first case. Add an urgent competing call and require role prioritization.

### W06 — Debrief and improvement

Use a log with observation at minute 2, acknowledgment 3 and response initiation 4. Calculate 1-minute observation-to-acknowledgment and 2-minute observation-to-action; do not treat these as acceptable field standards. Compare with the scenario requirement for prompt trained response. Write a fact-based debrief identifying helpful behavior, missed opportunity, system condition, corrective action, owner and effectiveness check. Changed case has a safe outcome despite an ignored warning; explain why outcome alone cannot validate behavior.

## I78 integrated individual evaluation

Run A then changed B as required for reassessment; a learner must demonstrate leadership/decision responsibility and reporting/challenge responsibility in separate rotations. The team follows the full state board. Every candidate's rubric includes all six skill families, handover and recovery from a contradiction. Team success does not automatically pass each member. Retain role assignment, inject version, time/action log, observed behavior, scores and critical errors.

Score six families 0–4: awareness, decision, communication, teamwork, leadership, performance-factor management.4=consistent independent effective, 3=effective with minor noncritical omission, 2=safe but substantial gaps, 1=weak, 0=absent/unsafe. Require≥20/24, every family≥3 and no critical failure. Separately require complete verified handover and evidence-based debrief. A fluent script with unsupported restart fails. The scenario's technical state rules take precedence over speed or theatrical realism.

## Written final and credential

Private Form A/B each has 12 original cases, 36 decisions, 72 points; 90 minutes. Pass 58/72 and≥4/6 in each domain with no unresolved critical error. Each decision 2=correct supported, 1=safe incomplete, 0=wrong/missing/unsafe. Coursebook allowed; keys, collaboration and outside assistance prohibited. These are VNDRLY criteria, not the IWCF online course's assessment. Written knowledge does not replace observed behavior.

Completion requires 24 corrected learning checks, six labs, twelve passed observed stations, I78 in both assigned role types and the written final. Issue VNDRLY/U Well Operations Crew Resource Management—nontechnical skills training and assessment. Record technical-authority exclusions and material version. Recommend employer-supported practice and an annual learning review, plus review after role/procedure changes or significant events; this reminder does not renew an external certificate.

Remediation identifies a specific observed gap, uses the linked chapter and a different practice case, then repeats the independent changed assessment. Retain initial outcomes. Do not pass a learner solely because the team rescued their role or because they attended all hours.

# VUC078 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Does a systems explanation excuse deliberate unsafe conduct?

**Answer:** No; protect the operation and examine evidence/accountability.

**Reason/remediation:** Understanding conditions and holding people accountable are compatible. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

Can CRM replace technical well-control competence?

**Answer:** No; it supports use of qualified people, procedures and information.

**Reason/remediation:** Good communication cannot make an unauthorized technical action safe. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

What is the difference between an observation and a hypothesis?

**Answer:** A measured gain is an observation; a transfer explanation needs evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not let a preferred story cancel a response trigger. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

Does resolving one cue resolve every cue?

**Answer:** No; independent abnormal indications remain to be evaluated.

**Reason/remediation:** Awareness requires the complete current picture. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

What does incoming staff do beyond listening?

**Answer:** Question and cross-check task-relevant state before accepting responsibility.

**Reason/remediation:** Handover is two-way transfer of reliable understanding. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

How do you counter fixation?

**Answer:** Seek contradictory evidence and share differing observations.

**Reason/remediation:** One familiar pattern may not fit the present state. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

Should urgent required protection wait for a full meeting?

**Answer:** No; use the trained approved response first, then deliberate review when controlled.

**Reason/remediation:** Analysis must not displace a known immediate protective action. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L2

Does effort already invested justify continuing?

**Answer:** No; changed evidence can invalidate the plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Sunk effort is not proof of safety. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L1

Does team success automatically pass every learner?

**Answer:** No; individual behavior and role evidence are required.

**Reason/remediation:** One competent person can conceal another participant gap. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L2

Does this credential authorize unfamiliar well operations?

**Answer:** No; technical competence/authorization remains separate.

**Reason/remediation:** CRM supports but does not replace technical qualification. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.
