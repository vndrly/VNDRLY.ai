# VNDRLY/U Asset Integrity — Pressure Vessel Inspection

VUC035. Original VNDRLY instructional material package, version 1.0. Materials completed and author-reviewed on 2026-11-17. The course develops inspection decisions, calculations and repair review; it does not grant API certification, jurisdictional authority, engineering licensure or NDE personnel qualification.

## Program purpose and learning contract

The learner prepares a defensible inspection record for an in-service pressure vessel: establish the correct equipment basis, recognize credible damage, select useful examination evidence, evaluate supplied dimensional data, review repair documentation and distinguish an established conclusion from an unresolved condition. The intended audience is inspection personnel and technically prepared maintenance or integrity personnel developing these responsibilities. Entry requires technical-drawing literacy, unit conversion and basic algebra. The instructor guide includes the diagnostic and remediation route; attendance or employment title does not replace these prerequisites.

On successful assessment, the learner can:

1. Reconcile equipment identity, chamber boundaries, construction/service records and responsibilities; identify unresolved conflicts before using them in calculations (V01–V02).
2. Distinguish the listed corrosion, cracking and hydrogen-related damage hypotheses and propose evidence and coverage that address the actual suspected mechanism (V03–V06; M01–M15).
3. Review thickness measurement validity and calculate corrosion rates, remaining life and inspection scheduling from a stated governing basis (V07–V09).
4. Trace joint-efficiency selection, calculate the taught shell/head pressure or thickness quantities, apply allowance and elevation consistently, and state limitations of the result (V10–V13).
5. Review toughness evidence, welding process controls, qualification records and examination validity separately from the applicable acceptance decision (V14–V18).
6. Evaluate material verification and the installed relief system without transferring a limited test result into an unsupported whole-system acceptance (V19–V20).
7. Review the listed repair approaches, special welding controls, additional design concerns and the evidence required for an authorized return-to-service decision (V21–V24; R01–R14).
8. Produce a traceable integrated report that preserves findings, uncertainty, corrective actions and authority boundaries rather than hiding an unresolved condition behind a passing calculation (V24 and application lab H).

Each objective is developed through original explanation, worked cases and assessed decisions. The program uses inert specimens, instruments where available, synthetic records and simulation. It does not conduct live pressure tests, radiography, hot tapping, welding or confined-space entry. A simulation result is recorded as simulation; it cannot be converted into a practical operating qualification.

Authorized technical references are required for the designated code-application activities. The instructor must supply the applicable editions and assess the learner's actual selection of clause, conditions and result. Synthetic exercise criteria are labeled throughout and are never substituted for real equipment criteria. The program record must identify the course edition and the references actually used.

Completion requires all teaching and formative work, the separately scored practical/application activities and the independent final examination under the instructor guide's criteria. The resulting VNDRLY record describes assessed learning, its date and its scope. It does not replace API's examination, experience eligibility, external credential or an employer's authorization.

The completion record remains a historical record of that version. Before relying on it for a changed role or code edition, the provider/employer reviews the gap and requires the affected instruction and reassessment. Significant changes to the learner's responsibilities, referenced provisions or demonstrated performance trigger targeted refreshers; a long period without relevant practice triggers a diagnostic before reliance. This approach avoids treating an old course date as proof of current capability. It is a VNDRLY learning-maintenance rule, not a statement of API renewal requirements.

## V01 — Establish the inspection basis

An inspection begins with identifying the pressure boundary and the rules under which it was built and is operated. A vessel number painted on insulation is not sufficient identification. Match the nameplate, manufacturer's data report, equipment register, drawing, materials record and physical connections. Record conflicting information rather than choosing the most convenient value. A vessel may have several chambers with different design pressures, temperatures and corrosion allowances. An exchanger shell and tube side must not be treated as one interchangeable pressure system.

Distinguish design pressure from operating pressure and maximum allowable working pressure. Operating pressure describes use. Design conditions are the specified construction basis. MAWP is a calculated pressure limit for the vessel under stated conditions, with the governing component and reference elevation identified. A relief-valve setting is not proof of vessel strength. A pressure test is not a substitute for a missing design calculation or evidence that a crack will remain stable throughout future service.

Create the inspection basis in this order: identify equipment and jurisdiction; recover construction code and edition; establish current service, temperature and pressure; identify materials and weld records; retrieve previous inspection and repair history; identify applicable in-service code and owner procedures; document gaps requiring resolution. The applicable construction edition need not be the latest examination edition. Never silently apply a new construction rule retroactively to resolve an inconvenient result.

The owner/user maintains the integrity program and operational decisions. The authorized inspector performs and documents inspection within the applicable program. Engineering evaluates design and fitness questions beyond the inspector's authority. Qualified NDE personnel perform examinations under approved procedures. A repair organization carries out the approved repair. These roles can cooperate without transferring responsibility through an unsigned email. For a covered process, OSHA mechanical-integrity requirements provide a separate regulatory layer; applicability must be established rather than presumed for every oilfield vessel.

**Worked case.** Register V-410 lists 250 psi at 400°F, but the visible plate reads 225 psi at 400°F. A spreadsheet uses 250 psi to set an inspection threshold. Place the calculation on hold, preserve both records and trace the discrepancy to the data report and approved rerating history. Do not assume that a previous spreadsheet is an authorized rerating. The resolution record must identify the accepted basis, approving authority and affected calculations or operating limits.

**Practice.** Build a one-page basis sheet with equipment ID, chamber, code/edition, material, dimensions, design conditions, current MAWP/reference elevation, minimum temperature, corrosion allowance, relief protection and unresolved records. Explain which missing items prevent a pressure calculation and which prevent safe inspection access.

## V02 — Plan safe access and meaningful coverage

Inspection planning has two different questions: can the inspector reach the surface safely, and will the chosen examination find the expected damage? Answering one does not answer the other. A safe external walkdown can miss internal cracking. Excellent ultrasonic coverage does not authorize entering an inadequately isolated vessel.

Prepare a marked boundary drawing, inspection locations, anticipated mechanisms, examination method, surface preparation, required coverage, acceptance authority and reporting requirements. Include support skirts, saddles, anchor bolts, nozzles, insulation penetrations, drains, low points, injection connections and attachments. Prioritize discontinuities and places where water, deposits, stress or changing flow concentrate damage. Document inaccessible areas and the compensating examination or deferral decision; do not label them inspected.

Before internal entry, verify the site's permit, positive isolation arrangements, energy control, draining, cleaning, atmospheric testing, ventilation, attendant, communication and rescue provisions. Residues can regenerate hazardous atmospheres. Nitrogen is not breathable simply because it is inert. Pyrophoric deposits can react when exposed to air. The inspector does not improvise cleaning or neutralization. Operations and safety personnel establish the authorized method and verify conditions throughout the work.

At external inspections, consider hot surfaces, insulation containing hazardous material, work at height, pressurized leaks, electrical equipment, radiation boundaries from radiography and adjacent operations. Never approach a suspected leaking pressure boundary merely to obtain a better photograph. Report from a safe location and follow the site's operating response. Removing insulation can disturb fragile metal or expose a previously contained leak; scope and precautions must reflect that possibility.

**Worked case.** A planned internal inspection is canceled because isolation cannot be established. The scheduler proposes external UT at six convenient points and calls it an equivalent internal inspection. Reject the equivalence assumption. Review expected mechanisms, geometry, coverage, code provisions and engineering approval for a legitimate alternative. If an alternative cannot demonstrate adequate coverage, record the inspection as outstanding and escalate operating disposition.

**Practice.** On a vessel sketch, distinguish examined, inaccessible and not-in-scope surfaces using different markings. Write the access hold point and examination hold point separately. State who can release each.

## V03 — Recognize aqueous metal loss

Corrosion is not one uniform process. Identify the corrosive species, presence of water, temperature, metallurgy, flow, deposits and operating excursions before selecting monitoring locations. A wall-thickness trend at a dry upper shell does not characterize a wet bottom head. Likewise, a clean visual surface does not exclude thinning beneath an inaccessible deposit.

Atmospheric corrosion involves external exposure, especially retained moisture and damaged coatings. Examine water traps, support interfaces, crevices and poorly drained attachments. Corrosion under insulation can remain hidden beneath apparently intact jacketing. Inspect penetrations, seams, low points and areas repeatedly wetted or thermally cycled. A small inspection window establishes the condition of that window, not the entire insulated surface. Use a documented sampling or screening plan, then expand examination where evidence requires it.

Boiler-water and steam-condensate corrosion depend on chemistry, dissolved gases and operating conditions. Examine locations affected by oxygen ingress, poor treatment, deposits and condensate accumulation. Ask for water-treatment trends and shutdown history, not just the current sample. Hydrochloric-acid attack depends strongly on water and local condensation; changes in process chemistry can move the vulnerable region. Caustic corrosion is metal loss and must be distinguished from caustic stress-corrosion cracking, which can require a crack-sensitive method even where general thickness appears adequate.

Erosion and erosion-corrosion often concentrate at impingement points, changes in direction, restrictions or high-velocity regions. Smooth directional wear and localized grooving suggest a different coverage plan from random external rust. Thickness readings must capture the thinnest affected zone, not average a groove into surrounding sound metal. Operating changes that increase velocity or solids loading can invalidate a formerly stable corrosion-rate assumption.

**Worked case.** A lower nozzle reads 8.0 mm while adjacent shell readings are 11.2–11.5 mm. The inspection history contains only shell points. Do not calculate a nozzle rate by subtracting its reading from an unrelated shell's historical value. Verify nozzle geometry and original thickness, map the affected area, investigate flow and deposits, and calculate its required thickness separately. A new baseline may be necessary, with a conservative follow-up interval approved through the integrity process.

**Practice.** For atmospheric corrosion, CUI, condensate corrosion, HCl attack, caustic metal loss and erosion, identify one likely location, one operating record needed and one examination limitation. Explain why routine spot UT alone can miss each localized condition.

## V04 — Recognize cracking and hydrogen-related damage

Cracking can threaten a component that still has substantial wall thickness. A remaining-life calculation based only on uniform metal loss cannot establish the acceptability of a crack. Describe orientation, location, apparent extent and examination method, then refer the indication for qualified characterization and engineering disposition. Do not grind away evidence without an approved examination and repair plan.

Amine stress-corrosion cracking is associated with susceptible material, amine service and tensile stress, often near welds. Caustic cracking similarly requires attention to service chemistry and stressed regions. Chloride stress-corrosion cracking is a concern for susceptible stainless steels when chloride exposure, temperature and tensile stress combine. Insulated stainless equipment may suffer externally introduced chlorides even if the process fluid is clean. Distinguish these mechanisms from simple wall loss and select crack-sensitive examinations with appropriate surface preparation and qualified interpretation.

Wet H2S damage includes several different forms. Blistering involves hydrogen accumulation within metal. Hydrogen-induced cracking can link internal defects without requiring the same externally applied stress conditions as sulfide stress cracking. Stress-oriented hydrogen-induced cracking links damage in a stress-oriented pattern. Sulfide stress cracking is particularly relevant to susceptible strength/hardness and tensile stress. Surface hardness, weld treatment, materials selection and service chemistry matter; a normal bulk thickness reading is not a clearance certificate.

Brittle fracture is a rapid failure with limited plastic deformation. Consider material toughness, thickness, flaws, restraint and temperature, especially during startup, shutdown and pressure testing. Low process pressure at normal temperature does not demonstrate safety at a colder test condition. Recover the minimum-temperature basis and governing code provisions. An inspector must not infer impact-test exemption from a material trade name alone.

**Worked case.** A carbon-steel amine vessel has acceptable UT readings but linear indications near a weld. The production supervisor asks to keep the previously calculated twelve-year remaining life. Explain that the calculation addressed thinning, whereas the new evidence raises a different failure mechanism. Preserve indication mapping and operating history, obtain appropriate characterization and place the continued-service decision with the authorized engineering/inspection process.

**Practice.** Compare wet-H2S blistering, HIC, SOHIC and SSC by damage form and information needed. For each, identify why an ordinary thickness reading can be insufficient. Write an escalation note without prematurely diagnosing an unconfirmed mechanism.

## V05 — Evaluate high-temperature mechanisms

Sulfidation, high-temperature hydrogen/hydrogen-sulfide corrosion, naphthenic-acid corrosion and high-temperature hydrogen attack are distinct. A process containing sulfur and hydrogen does not establish which mechanism controls. Review material composition, actual metal temperature, hydrogen partial pressure, fluid chemistry, flow and excursions. Use qualified materials expertise and current applicable technical references for susceptibility boundaries.

Sulfidation can produce significant metal loss, with susceptibility varying between materials and components. Material verification and representative inspection are important because a nominal piping or vessel class does not guarantee every installed component has identical resistance. Naphthenic-acid corrosion can be strongly affected by temperature, velocity and local flow. Examine vulnerable regions and use process history; a single current chemistry value cannot reconstruct years of exposure.

High-temperature H2/H2S corrosion concerns metal loss in an elevated-temperature environment. High-temperature hydrogen attack instead damages the steel internally through metallurgical reactions and fissuring. It is not simply another uniform corrosion rate. A surface appearance or ordinary thickness survey cannot alone establish absence of HTHA. Inspection capability, detection limits and material/service suitability require specialist assessment. Do not present an empirical operating boundary as a guarantee against damage.

The CSB's Tesoro Anacortes investigation concerns a fatal heat-exchanger failure during a process-switching operation. Use the investigation to examine how operating history, equipment age, mechanism assumptions and inspection capability can interact. A recommendation in an incident report is not automatically an adopted code requirement. Separate the investigation's findings, recommendations and the site's governing requirements when writing an inspection basis.

**Worked case.** An exchanger's current temperature is below its historic maximum, and management proposes using only the current condition to dismiss damage concerns. Recover historical excursions, material records, prior examination techniques and service duration. Lowering today's temperature does not remove damage already accumulated. Recommend a qualified mechanism-specific assessment and document the uncertainty rather than asserting fitness from one present reading.

**Practice.** Prepare a mechanism table separating metal loss from internal fissuring. For each mechanism, list the operating variables that must be verified and the evidence that would cause the inspection plan to change.

## V06 — Turn visual observations into usable evidence

A visual inspection should produce reproducible locations and meaningful descriptions. Identify the vessel orientation and datum before recording findings. Use a drawing or grid, distance from a known seam or nozzle, clock position and photograph scale. A photograph labeled only 'corrosion' cannot reliably support future comparison.

Externally inspect deformation, coating condition, insulation damage, leakage evidence, supports, foundations, anchors, settlement, attachment welds, nozzles and connected loads. Internally inspect shell and heads, seams, weld attachments, trays, baffles, liners, cladding and regions obscured by deposits. Distinguish process deposits from corrosion products and avoid destructive cleaning without authorization. Record bulges, dents, distortion and alignment changes for engineering evaluation; a thickness pass does not dispose of structural deformation.

For lined or clad equipment, determine whether the observed layer is pressure-retaining, corrosion-resistant or both. A sound liner can hide a degraded base material; a damaged liner can expose otherwise unsusceptible base metal to aggressive service. Identify the lining construction, attachment and approved examination method. Do not include a nonstructural coating in measured pressure-retaining thickness.

Inspect exchanger components with their actual functions in mind: tubes, tube sheets, channel covers, partitions, shell, expansion provisions and gasket faces. Tube plugging or replacement affects more than leak tightness; thermal duty, pressure differences and approved repair limits require review. Report inaccessible tube-side areas and method limitations rather than treating an external shell walkdown as a complete exchanger inspection.

**Worked case.** A vessel has no visible leaks, but its saddle coating is cracked and a nozzle appears displaced relative to the connected piping. Record the position, photograph with scale, check historical alignment and escalate possible support movement or imposed load. Do not simply repaint the crack and close the finding as coating maintenance.

**Practice.** Rewrite 'bad rust near bottom' as a finding another inspector can locate and compare. Include location, morphology, extent, method, photographs, immediate operating concern, recommended follow-up and the person responsible for disposition.

### Tower internals: inspect function as well as condition

A tower's internals direct contact between rising vapor and descending liquid. The shell pressure boundary and the process internals therefore need different, connected inspection questions. A sound shell does not prove that a damaged tray can carry its intended load or distribute liquid correctly. Conversely, restoring tray performance does not resolve pressure-boundary corrosion.

Start with the as-built internals drawing and operating symptoms. Identify tray or packing arrangement, supports, liquid distribution and collection, access openings and attachment points. For a trayed unit, document missing or distorted components, loose fasteners, deposits, damaged support connections and obstructions to the intended flow path. Compare configuration with the approved drawing rather than assuming every similar-looking tray is interchangeable. On a packed unit, investigate distribution and support condition along with damaged or displaced packing. Capture inaccessible areas and the access method used; a photograph through one manway cannot establish the condition of all elevations.

Separate observation, interpretation and disposition. “Tray panel at elevation 12 m visibly displaced” is an observation. “Possible flow or mechanical upset” is an interpretation needing operating-history review. “Repair under approved plan and reinspect before closure” is a disposition request. Do not claim a pressure surge occurred merely because distortion exists. Ask operations about startup, shutdown, differential pressure and temperature changes; obtain a competent engineering assessment of possible causes and implications.

**Worked case.** Fictional tower T-35 has a clean shell thickness report but two bent tray panels and a missing clamp. The maintenance request says to install a spare clamp and close the vessel. First locate and record the damage, preserve evidence and compare the spare with the required configuration. Have the responsible specialist evaluate the affected panel and support, potential loose parts elsewhere and whether the initiating condition remains. Verify the approved repair and internal closure inspection. The thickness report does not settle these questions.

**Changed practice.** A replacement panel fits the opening but has a different pattern of holes. Is dimensional fit sufficient? No: fit alone does not establish the intended hydraulic behavior, strength or approved design. Hold substitution for documented technical review. A learner must identify the drawing difference, explain its functional significance and write the hold without inventing a permitted tolerance.

### Compare access and coverage before selecting an inspection plan

A fixed-tubesheet exchanger, a U-tube bundle and a floating-head exchanger do not offer identical access. Sketch the separate shell-side and tube-side boundaries first. Identify the surfaces reachable by visual examination, the passages available to probes and the regions hidden by geometry. In a U-bend, a technique demonstrated on straight tube does not automatically establish bend coverage. At a tubesheet, a strong response or inaccessible end region can restrict the examined length. Document such exclusions in the tube map and decide whether a complementary method is needed.

For a vessel with a lining, distinguish the lining's barrier function from the pressure boundary behind it. A sound-looking lining does not establish backing-metal thickness. A local holiday or lifted edge can help direct examination, but do not damage the lining to obtain access without an approved plan. With refractory, examine anchorage and evidence of localized overheating as well as apparent surface condition. A repair that restores appearance but leaves unsupported refractory may not restore function.

**Planning case.** Exchanger E62 has 160 tubes, 120 accessible straight sections and 40 partially obstructed passages. A proposal labels the job “100% exchanger inspection” because all inlet ends were photographed. Revise the scope to state what was actually observed. Record each inaccessible or incompletely examined passage, the intended damage detection, chosen complementary examination and unresolved exclusions. The right denominator is tied to the examination objective; counting photographs is not measuring wall coverage.

**Changed exercise.** All tubes are accessible, but the suspected mechanism affects the shell beneath a support. The tube examination does not answer that question. The learner must relocate the inspection objective to the susceptible boundary and design access accordingly. Score boundary identification, method-to-mechanism match and honest coverage reporting separately.

## V07 — Make thickness measurements defensible

An ultrasonic thickness value is a measurement with conditions and uncertainty. Identify the instrument, probe, calibration reference, material sound velocity, surface temperature, couplant, coating treatment, surface condition and measurement location. Verify setup before use and at the procedure's specified checks. A calibration certificate establishes instrument service history; it does not establish that today's velocity, zero offset and echo selection are correct for the component.

Compare reference blocks of known thickness that appropriately bracket the measurement range. Investigate unexpected readings through repeat measurement, surface preparation permitted by the procedure, alternative probe placement and qualified waveform review. Roughness, curvature, laminations, coatings and poor coupling can mislead a direct-reading gauge. A plausible display is not proof that the back-wall echo was measured. Echo-to-echo or coating-compensation modes have limitations and require an appropriate validated procedure.

Retain the minimum valid reading and location rather than averaging away local thinning. A point reading cannot characterize the depth and extent of a narrow pit. Map the affected area using a suitable resolution and examination technique. Record whether the reading includes a liner or cladding and whether that material may be credited structurally. Where temperature correction is necessary, use the validated procedure, not an improvised percentage.

**Worked case.** A gauge reads 10.0 mm on a 10.0 mm reference before inspection, but 10.6 mm afterward. The procedure's permitted drift is 0.1 mm. Mark the intervening data suspect, identify its time/location extent, investigate and repeat the affected measurements with valid setup. Subtracting 0.6 mm from every reading assumes a constant error that has not been established. Never quietly retain only readings that pass a pressure calculation.

**Practice.** Complete a thickness record with before/after checks, raw readings, accepted minimum, rejected readings with reasons, map and technician identification. State the uncertainty that remains when a surface cannot be prepared adequately.

### Separate velocity error from zero error

Thickness gauges infer a distance from sound transit time. Velocity calibration addresses propagation in the material; zero calibration compensates for delays outside the intended sound path. A two-point setup uses precisely known samples spanning the intended thickness range. The material and actual measurement conditions must suit the application. Follow the instrument's approved procedure; do not assume a factory setup is valid for every alloy, temperature or probe. Manufacturer explanation: [Evident velocity and zero calibration](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/thickness-gauge/zero-calibration).

**Original calibration-analysis case.** Known samples are 5.00 and 15.00 mm. A training gauge displays 5.40 and 15.40 mm. Both errors are +0.40 mm; the displayed span is correctly 10.00 mm. This pattern suggests an offset error. A second setup reads 5.25 and 15.75 mm: both readings are 5% high and the span is 10.50 mm, suggesting a scale/velocity error. These patterns guide diagnosis; they do not authorize adjusting a real instrument without following its procedure and verifying the resulting setup.

The inspector does not correct field records by subtracting a guessed offset after the fact. Determine which readings were made with the suspect setup, preserve them, identify the interval since the last valid check, and follow the approved remeasurement/disposition process. A corrected setup establishes future measurement capability, not automatic validity of earlier data.

**Changed exercise.** The thin reference reads correctly but the thick reference is high. A colleague proposes keeping the setup because the vessel is “mostly thin.” Reject the unsupported range claim. Confirm the range of actual measurements and achieve the required verification across it. Then change the probe and repeat the required setup verification. Record probe, block, material, temperature, settings and checks so another examiner can reproduce the measurement basis.

### Establish method applicability before accepting a thickness number

ASTM E797/E797M-21's public scope describes contact pulse-echo measurements through 93°C, with resolvable back-wall echoes and consistent propagation velocity. Flat parallel surfaces are its primary application; other geometries have limitations. This is the ASTM source's scope, not an assertion that every provision of ASME's adopted SE-797 text was compared. Source: [ASTM published scope](https://store.astm.org/e0797_e0797m-21.html).

**Original procedure-selection case.** A report claims this method for a 120°C wall using an ambient-temperature calibration and an unidentified echo. Three independent questions remain: temperature applicability, valid calibration for the actual conditions, and identification of the reflecting interface. A stable digital number answers none of them by itself. Obtain the applicable qualified hot-service procedure, equipment limits and verification evidence; do not apply an improvised temperature correction. For a second, cool specimen, an inclined back surface produces inconsistent echoes. Cooling resolved temperature but did not resolve geometry. Map unmeasurable regions and arrange an appropriate method rather than filling missing cells with nearby readings.

**Practice output.** Complete a measurement-basis sheet containing component and location, material, temperature, surface/coating, geometry, probe and instrument identity, reference block and range, calibration checks, echo selection, scan coverage, readings and unresolved areas. Mark the result unusable for a retirement calculation when the measurement basis is unresolved. This is an evidence decision, not an assertion that the component has failed its minimum thickness.

### Distinguish probe configurations when standardizing

The reviewed ASTM practice distinguishes direct-contact, delay-line, dual-element and delayed-sweep thick-section configurations. Delay and velocity corrections are different; dual-element behavior can become nonlinear in thin sections. An A-scan can expose an echo-selection problem hidden by a numerical display. Source: [ASTM E797/E797M-21, selected sections 7–9](https://studylib.net/doc/28138878/astm-e797-e797m-%E2%88%92-21), publisher-authored text on a third-party host.

**Original two-reference exercise.** Known blocks are 4 mm and 14 mm. A delay-line setup displays 6 and 16 mm. The displayed difference equals the actual 10 mm separation, while both readings contain a 2 mm offset. Identify a delay/zero issue for controlled correction and verification, not evidence to change sound velocity arbitrarily. If the readings are instead 4.4 and 15.4 mm, their 11 mm separation differs from the known 10 mm range; a simple constant subtraction cannot correct both. Perform the prescribed setup and recheck the full range before collecting component data.

**Thin-region exercise.** A dual-element setup agrees on 8 and 12 mm references but the inspection includes approximately 2 mm areas. Those checks do not demonstrate accuracy at the thin end. Supply appropriate reference evidence and any validated instrument-specific correction before reporting that region's measurements. Do not derive a correction from a single unknown corroded reading.

**Delayed-display exercise.** A fictional training display intentionally omits the first 40 mm and shows a 6 mm increment. Total thickness is 46 mm, not 6 mm. Record the display convention and verify against a known specimen near the total thickness. In the changed case the operator cannot recover the delay setting; the digit alone is insufficient to reconstruct a valid result. Preserve it as unresolved evidence rather than entering it into a corrosion-rate calculation.

## V08 — Calculate corrosion rate and remaining life

Use comparable readings at the same location or a justified equivalent location. Long-term rate is historical thickness loss divided by elapsed years. Short-term rate uses the most recent comparable interval. Review both against process changes and measurement quality; selecting a low long-term rate can hide accelerating damage. A negative apparent rate usually signals measurement variation or a changed reference, not metal regeneration.

For a uniform thinning exercise, remaining allowance equals current valid thickness minus required thickness. Remaining life equals that allowance divided by the justified corrosion rate. These calculations do not establish the acceptability of cracking, severe localized damage, deformation or uncharacterized mechanisms. Required thickness itself must come from the applicable component calculation, including relevant loads and code provisions.

**Worked case.** At the same shell location, readings are 12.0 mm in 2016, 11.2 mm in 2022 and 10.8 mm in 2026. Long-term loss is 1.2 mm over ten years: 0.12 mm/year. Short-term loss is 0.4 mm over four years: 0.10 mm/year. With required thickness 8.4 mm and justified rate 0.12 mm/year, remaining allowance is 2.4 mm and calculated thinning life is twenty years. This is not permission to wait twenty years for inspection.

Now change the current reading to 10.0 mm. The recent rate becomes 1.2/4 = 0.30 mm/year, while the long-term rate becomes 2.0/10 = 0.20 mm/year. Using 0.30 gives 1.6/0.30 = 5.33 years. Investigate the acceleration. Neither averaging the two rates nor selecting the older lower one is justified merely because it improves the result.

If current thickness is at or below required thickness, do not produce a reassuring negative or zero 'life' and leave operation unchanged. Escalate for authorized disposition, which may involve repair, operating restriction, replacement or a qualified fitness-for-service assessment. If the rate is zero or below measurement resolution, do not report infinite life. Document the basis for a conservative rate or an approved alternative inspection strategy.

**Practice.** Calculate rates for 0.500 in in 2020, 0.470 in in 2023 and 0.440 in in 2026. At required thickness 0.380 in, both rates are 0.010 in/year and calculated life is six years. Explain why a newly observed crack invalidates use of that result as the sole continued-service decision.

## V09 — Set intervals through the governing rule

A calculated life and an inspection interval are different outputs. Establish the governing interval rule, maximum interval, previous inspection date, mechanism-specific requirements, jurisdictional requirements and any approved risk-based approach. Then document the earliest controlling due date. Do not treat a company spreadsheet default as the code requirement.

**Training rule, not a quotation of API 510:** For this exercise only, the internal inspection interval is the lesser of half the calculated remaining life and ten years; an external inspection is due within five years or before the internal due date, whichever is earlier. With 5.33 years calculated life on November 17, 2026, the internal interval is 2.665 years, not ten. Convert that interval conservatively through the site's scheduling convention and document the actual due date. The external inspection cannot be scheduled after that internal due date under this supplied exercise rule.

Actual API 510 provisions include conditions and alternatives that must be checked in the applicable controlled edition. A participant must retrieve the governing clauses and document applicability before making a real interval recommendation. This manuscript does not substitute its simplified teaching rule for the complete current code.

Risk-based inspection evaluates probability and consequence using an approved methodology, credible mechanisms, operating history and uncertainty. A lower financial consequence does not erase a personnel exposure or legal inspection requirement. An RBI result is only as sound as its input data and examination effectiveness. Changes in service, damage findings or operating envelopes require review. A deferred inspection needs authorized justification and follow-up; renaming a missed inspection 'RBI' is not an assessment.

**Worked case.** An internal inspection is due in six months, but a new chloride exposure is discovered beneath insulation. Waiting six months simply because the schedule is current ignores new evidence. Review the damage threat and examination capability promptly; the existing due date does not prohibit earlier inspection or operating action.

**Practice.** Prepare an interval worksheet showing the thinning result, supplied rule, maximum, external due date, overriding findings, approvals and actions. Add an uncertainty statement if historical measurements are not comparable.

## V10 — Determine joint efficiency from evidence

Weld category describes location in the vessel; joint type describes construction. Radiographic extent is a further variable. These are not interchangeable labels. Locate the seam on the drawing, identify its category and joint configuration, retrieve the examination evidence and apply the relevant joint-efficiency provisions. A seamless head does not automatically make every connected vessel component eligible for efficiency 1.0.

Nameplate RT markings require interpretation with the applicable code and construction records. 'Spot radiographed' does not mean that every seam was fully examined. Confirm which joint controls the selected formula and whether adjacent weld requirements affect a seamless component. Welded pipe or tubing has its own applicable provisions. Do not infer an efficiency from the quality of a visible weld bead.

**Worked case.** A synthetic calculation sheet supplies two approved alternatives for the same cylinder: E = 0.85 with verified records, or E = 0.70 pending their recovery. With S = 20,000 psi, t = 0.400 in and inside radius R = 24 in, the simplified cylinder equation gives 280.53 psi at E = 0.85 and 231.02 psi at E = 0.70. These efficiencies are exercise inputs, not a replacement for the code table. The discrepancy demonstrates why unsupported credit for examination can inflate the pressure result.

**Practice.** Create a joint register with seam ID, category, joint type, examination extent, record number, applicable clause and selected E. Leave unresolved entries visibly unresolved; identify which calculations depend on them.

## V11 — Calculate cylindrical shell thickness and pressure

For the internal-pressure circumferential-stress teaching model, t = PR/(SE − 0.6P), and rearranging gives P = SEt/(R + 0.6t). P is local internal pressure, R is inside radius in the condition being evaluated, S is allowable stress at the governing temperature, E is applicable efficiency and t is the available structural thickness. Use coherent units. These equations have code applicability limits and do not cover every load, opening or geometry.

**Worked US-unit case.** P = 200 psi, R = 24 in, S = 20,000 psi, E = 0.85. Required pressure thickness is 4,800/(17,000 − 120) = 0.28436 in. If the exercise requires 0.060 in future allowance, required current available thickness is 0.34436 in before any other applicable requirement. A measured 0.400 in provides 0.340 in after reserving that allowance. The corresponding pressure is 17,000 × 0.340/(24 + 0.204) = 238.80 psi. This is component pressure under the stated model, not automatically vessel MAWP.

**Worked SI case.** P = 1.4 MPa, R = 600 mm, S = 138 MPa and E = 0.85. Required pressure thickness is 840/(117.3 − 0.84) = 7.213 mm. With 1.5 mm future allowance, current thickness must be at least 8.713 mm for this pressure-only calculation. Do not mix a psi stress value with MPa pressure or millimetres with inches.

Corrosion changes geometry as well as available thickness. Establish whether supplied dimensions are nominal, measured or future-corroded. Use the appropriate corroded radius when required. Subtract future allowance once from available thickness, not twice. Do not credit paint, scale or a nonstructural liner. Additional checks may govern: longitudinal stress, openings, supports, wind, seismic loads, thermal stresses or other applicable design conditions.

**Practice.** Recalculate the US example with E = 0.70, then explain the effect without rounding required thickness downward. Identify every supplied value that must be traced to a controlled record in real work.

## V12 — Evaluate heads and spherical shells

Shape determines the calculation. A hemispherical head is not a 2:1 ellipsoidal head, and a shallow formed head cannot be assumed to satisfy either formula. Confirm actual geometry and manufacturing records. Forming can reduce thickness locally; use measurements that characterize the relevant thin region.

For this teaching model, a spherical shell or hemispherical head uses t = PR/(2SE − 0.2P) and P = 2SEt/(R + 0.2t). A standard 2:1 ellipsoidal head uses t = PD/(2SE − 0.2P) and P = 2SEt/(D + 0.2t), with inside diameter D. The different dimensional term matters. Verify applicable code limits and any additional requirements before real use.

**Worked case.** At P = 200 psi, S = 20,000 psi and E = 0.85, a hemispherical head of R = 24 in requires 4,800/33,960 = 0.14134 in for pressure. A 2:1 ellipsoidal head of D = 48 in requires 9,600/33,960 = 0.28269 in. Using radius in the ellipsoidal formula would incorrectly halve the calculated requirement. Future allowance and other governing requirements remain additional considerations.

At net structural thickness 0.300 in, the ellipsoidal head's calculated component pressure is 34,000 × 0.300/(48 + 0.060) = 212.23 psi. The shell might withstand a higher pressure, but the head can control the vessel. Account for each component's elevation before selecting a common vessel reference-pressure limit.

**Practice.** Given an undocumented head profile, explain why a thickness number alone cannot establish its pressure rating. Specify the drawing, geometric measurements and engineering review needed rather than selecting whichever equation gives the larger rating.

## V13 — Apply static head and plan pressure testing

Liquid pressure increases with depth. For water in US units, use 0.433 psi per vertical foot for these exercises. In SI, water head is approximately 9.80665 kPa per vertical metre. Use vertical elevation difference, not shell length along a sloping vessel. Actual liquid density must be considered for real conditions; the published API examination scope specifies water specific gravity 1.0 for its static-head problems.

**Worked case.** A lower component has allowable local pressure 220 psi and lies thirty feet below the vessel pressure reference. Water head is 12.99 psi. Its contribution to the permissible reference pressure is 207.01 psi. If an upper component permits only 205 psi at the reference, 205 psi governs. Adding the head to 220 instead would overstate permissible top pressure.

A pressure-test plan identifies the governing rule, test medium, pressure, stress-ratio adjustment where applicable, temperature, pressure measurement elevations, component limits, supports, isolation, vents, drains, overpressure protection, exclusion area, inspection method and depressurization sequence. Obtain the authorized plan; do not select a multiplier from memory. Hydrostatic testing stores less compressible-fluid energy than pneumatic testing but still creates serious hazards. Trapped gas increases stored energy. Water weight can overload supports, and incompatible test water can harm materials or contaminate service.

Pneumatic testing requires a specifically justified and approved approach with appropriate safeguards. Never substitute compressed gas because filling with water is inconvenient. Confirm adequate toughness at test temperature, instrument ranges and calibration, pressure boundaries and remotely observable indications. Do not tighten or repair a pressurized leaking joint. After a test, confirm depressurization, drainage, drying where required, restoration of protective devices and documentation before return to service.

**Practice.** A synthetic approved plan requires 300 psi at the top reference of a forty-foot water-filled vessel. Calculate bottom pressure: 317.32 psi. Explain why the bottom component and connected equipment must be checked against that local pressure, and why this exercise does not itself authorize the test.

### Review a test as a sequence of controlled states

Pressure testing is not merely reaching a gauge number. Review the proposed test in chronological order, because a correct final pressure does not correct an unsafe filling, isolation or restoration step. The following is an inspection-planning exercise, not an executable field procedure.

**Before filling or pressurization:** establish the test boundary on a marked drawing; reconcile that boundary with the equipment in the field; identify all components exposed to pressure; verify their applicable ratings, test temperature and elevation. Review temporary closures, fasteners, hoses, gauges and protection. Confirm the completed repair/examination records and the authorized test method. Establish communications, responsibilities, exclusion controls and the stop criteria. Do not assume an isolation blind suitable for entry is also suitable for a pressure test.

**During filling and temperature stabilization:** verify the approved medium, venting arrangements and support loads. Confirm where trapped gas could remain. Establish that the equipment and medium satisfy the approved temperature conditions before pressure is applied. A liquid-level indication is not proof that every high point has been vented. Treat an unexpected leak, unsupported component or unexplained temperature reading as a hold rather than something to work around under pressure.

**During controlled pressurization and examination:** follow the approved pressure increments, hold points and examination pressure. Record time, pressure, gauge identity, elevation and temperature. Compare readings on a common reference basis; two gauges at different water elevations should not be expected to show identical pressure. Stop and safely depressurize under the procedure when a specified stop condition occurs. Personnel must not tighten a leaking connection or approach it simply to improve the test result. Gas testing demands its own approved safeguards and sequence; it is not the hydrostatic procedure with the word water replaced.

**After the test:** confirm controlled depressurization and zero-energy verification, drainage and required drying, removal of temporary test equipment, reinstatement of service connections and protection, inspection of any observed change and closure of outstanding findings. Preserve actual readings and deviations. A test record should distinguish the pressure reached, the examination performed and the final authorized disposition. None of these is interchangeable with a checked PASS box.

**Original sequencing exercise.** A fictional approved hydrostatic plan defines 260 psi at its upper reference. The lower gauge is 20 ft below that reference and reads 268.66 psi; the upper gauge reads 260 psi. The readings are consistent with the stated water head. Nevertheless, a hose shows damage before the next planned hold. The correct response is to implement the approved stop and depressurization process, then resolve the equipment issue and document the interruption. Reaching the planned reference pressure is not permission to continue with defective test equipment.

**Changed exercise.** Following a satisfactory test, a relief-device isolation remains in its temporary test position. The learner must identify incomplete restoration, prevent unauthorized return to service and require documented reinstatement verification. Award credit only when the learner connects the test record with the actual restored configuration.

### Tabletop pneumatic sequence: test pressure is not inspection pressure

The selected UG-100 sequence increases pressure gradually to no more than half the test pressure, then in increments approximately one-tenth of test pressure. Examination occurs after reduction to test pressure divided by 1.1. The complete approved plan must also establish temperature, stability, safeguarding and examination requirements. This is a planning exercise, not permission to operate test equipment.

**Original timeline.** An authorized fictional plan specifies a 330 psi test. Its initial stage is at or below 165 psi; nominal subsequent steps of 33 psi lead through 198, 231, 264 and 297 to 330 psi. The calculated examination pressure is 330/1.1 = 300 psi. The inspector checks the recorded sequence against the approved plan and the applicable code, including any needed holds or adjusted increments. Do not round the first stage above its permitted bound or mistake reaching 300 psi for completion of a 330 psi test.

**Changed case.** The log shows an immediate jump from zero to 300 psi, followed by examination, with no record of reaching 330 psi. It fails to demonstrate both the planned progression and the specified test pressure. A successful-looking leak inspection cannot reconstruct those missing steps. Preserve the actual log and require authorized disposition; never insert invented stages afterward.

**Boundary question.** Would this sequence alone make a pneumatic test safe? No. The method justification, actual boundary and component ratings, metal-temperature/toughness basis, instruments, stored-energy controls, personnel protection and restoration remain required. The calculation is one reviewed part of the test package. Selected technical basis: [2025 VIII-1 UG-100](https://studylib.net/doc/29030014/asme-sec-viii-div1-2025).

## V14 — Review toughness and minimum temperature

Impact-test decisions depend on material specification and condition, governing thickness, temperature, weld details and applicable exemptions. Recover the material test report and construction records. A statement that a vessel is 'carbon steel' is not enough to select a toughness curve or exemption. Distinguish the vessel's documented minimum design metal temperature from the ambient temperature on the day of inspection.

Trace the applicable construction-code path: determine material and thickness basis, identify the relevant toughness provisions, check exemption conditions and any permitted adjustments, and verify required impact tests for base material and weld qualification. Record each condition used. Do not combine favorable parts from different exemption routes while ignoring their restrictions. Pressure reduction may affect an assessment, but the applicable rule and engineering approval must support it.

**Worked case.** A repair is scheduled during a cold outage. The old vessel file contains a minimum-temperature limit but no evidence supporting a proposed colder test. The welder's procedure qualification includes room-temperature tensile and bend results only. Those results do not establish low-temperature toughness. Hold the proposed cold test and obtain the required material, weld and engineering evidence.

**Practice.** Given a synthetic exemption checklist with four required conditions and only three supported, mark the exemption unestablished. Identify the missing evidence, the affected activity and who must resolve it. Never invent a Charpy result or treat an unperformed test as passed.

### Read an impact-test report as evidence

A Charpy machine measures energy absorbed during rapid fracture; it does not directly measure the vessel's allowable operating pressure. Machine verification, specimen identity and test temperature are separate controls. NIST explains why certified reference specimens are used to verify the machine and why temperature control matters. A current machine verification cannot repair a specimen taken from the wrong heat or tested at the wrong temperature.

Follow the evidence chain from component to heat, specimen location and orientation, specimen dimensions, notch, conditioning and actual test temperature, machine identification, individual results and applicable acceptance rule. Identify whether the report represents base material, weld metal or heat-affected zone. Keep individual results visible: an average alone can conceal an unacceptable individual result. Do not scale a subsize specimen result to a full-size result using an invented multiplier. The applicable construction provisions determine required specimens, temperature adjustments, acceptance and any retest route.

**Original report-review case.** The fictional purchase specification for heat H71 requires three results at −20°C, a mean of at least 27 J and no individual below 20 J. The report lists 31, 29 and 19 J. Its mean is 26.33 J; both the mean and individual requirements fail. A supervisor proposes removing the 19 J result and averaging the other two. Reject that alteration: retain the complete original record and follow the authorized disposition or retest procedure. These numbers are classroom purchase requirements, not universal ASME thresholds.

**Changed case and explanation.** Results are now 32, 30 and 28 J, but the report says +20°C. The arithmetic passes the fictional energy criteria, yet the required temperature is unsupported. Place the item on hold rather than converting a warm-temperature result into cold-service evidence. A verified machine and attractive numbers do not establish that the specified test occurred.

**Record exercise.** Prepare a review sheet with specimen IDs, provenance, dimensions/orientation, actual temperatures, individual energies, mean, acceptance-source revision, each unmet condition and disposition authority. The instructor awards credit only when the learner identifies both numerical and traceability failures. Reassessment changes the failing condition to an unidentified weld specimen so the learner must apply the evidence chain rather than repeat the temperature answer.

Source for measurement and verification principles: [NIST Charpy Machine Verification Program](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/charpy-machine-verification-program). Acceptance examples above are original synthetic requirements.

## V15 — Understand welding processes and metallurgy

SMAW uses a consumable coated electrode; GTAW uses a nonconsumable tungsten electrode with shielding gas and, where specified, added filler; GMAW feeds consumable wire under gas shielding; SAW uses consumable wire beneath granular flux. These differences affect variables, shielding, deposition, access and likely discontinuities. They do not establish that one process is automatically suitable for every repair.

Heat input, preheat, interpass temperature, cooling rate, hydrogen control and postweld heat treatment affect weld and heat-affected-zone properties. Excessive hardness or hydrogen can increase cracking risk in susceptible materials. Overheating can also harm properties. Consumable storage and handling are therefore part of the repair quality plan, not merely housekeeping. Inspectors verify compliance with the approved procedure; they do not invent a preheat temperature from material appearance.

Recognize incomplete fusion, incomplete penetration, porosity, slag inclusion, undercut, overlap and cracking as different conditions. Visual examination can find some surface problems but cannot establish internal soundness. A discontinuity is not automatically rejectable; apply the governing acceptance criteria after qualified characterization. Conversely, do not accept a crack because the weld looks smooth after grinding.

**Worked case.** A repair crew replaces specified low-hydrogen electrodes with a convenient alternative and maintains the same amperage. Equal amperage does not make consumables equivalent or preserve qualification. Stop the affected work, identify deposited weld metal, compare the change against the WPS and qualification requirements, and obtain authorized disposition.

**Practice.** Review a synthetic weld log for process, filler, storage, preheat, interpass, heat treatment, welder identity and examination hold points. Explain how a missing temperature record differs from a measured temperature outside the allowed range.

### Carbon equivalent: use the corrected composition basis

The September 2025 errata to API RP 577, Section 10.8.2, explicitly identifies the composition terms as percentages. In the displayed relationship:

`CE = %C + %Mn/6 + (%Cr + %Mo + %V)/5 + (%Ni + %Cu)/15`

Use numerical weight-percent values from a traceable material analysis. Thus 0.18% carbon enters as 0.18, not 18 and not 0.0018. The correction is available in the [official API errata download](https://ecommiddlewareprod.blob.core.windows.net/webstore/addenda-errata/RP_577_E3_EN_E1.pdf), retrieved and visually checked on 2026-11-17.

**Worked calculation.** A fictional material record gives C 0.18%, Mn 1.20%, Cr 0.10%, Mo 0.05%, V 0.02%, Ni 0.20% and Cu 0.15%. CE = 0.18 + 1.20/6 + (0.10 + 0.05 + 0.02)/5 + (0.20 + 0.15)/15 = 0.4373. Keep the intermediate terms visible so an omitted element or misplaced divisor can be detected.

Carbon equivalent is a weldability-related indicator, not a standalone welding procedure or a universal accept/reject rule. Evaluate the applicable material, thickness, restraint, hydrogen control, thermal cycle, service and qualified procedure. A calculated CE does not tell an inspector to choose a preheat temperature from memory, waive qualification or approve a repair. Where an instrument cannot measure the required carbon content, an alloy-library label cannot supply the missing value.

**Changed practice.** Change only Mn to 0.90%. The result becomes 0.3873, a reduction of 0.05. Explain why that arithmetic difference does not by itself authorize lowering the WPS preheat. The approved procedure and its qualification remain controlling; the calculation is information for competent technical review, not a field revision to the WPS.

### Relate the process to the inspection question

SMAW's covered electrode supplies filler and protection but also creates slag that must be controlled between passes. GTAW separates the tungsten electrode from filler addition, making electrode contamination and shielding important review topics. GMAW's wire-feed operation can use different transfer modes; the process abbreviation alone does not establish the mode or fusion capability. SAW uses a wire/flux combination, so reviewing the wire designation without the flux misses part of the consumable system. These process distinctions are explained by [Lincoln Electric's arc-welding fundamentals](https://qacd10.lincolnelectric.com/en/Welding-and-Cutting-Resource-Center/Process-and-Theory/Arc-Welding-Fundamentals) and the [National Board's process comparison](https://www.nationalboard.org/Index.aspx?ID=528&pageID=164).

**Original process-review case.** A fictional production instruction specifies a particular GMAW transfer mode and qualified range. The log says only “MIG,” while the equipment setup record differs from the qualified instruction. Do not infer mode compliance from the colloquial process name or accept a visually attractive bead as proof of fusion. Recover the actual setup and compare each applicable variable with the authorized WPS. The disposition concerns the deposited work as well as correcting the next setup.

**Changed case.** The process and wire now agree, but a SAW job's flux designation and conditioning history are missing. The wire certificate alone does not reconstruct the wire/flux system. Identify the missing consumable and handling evidence before accepting the weld package. A replacement container arriving after the job is not retrospective proof.

Hydrogen cracking risk combines hydrogen, susceptible microstructure and stress; controlling only one recorded number does not establish the entire risk picture. Preheat and cooling behavior affect this interaction, but the appropriate controls depend on the actual joint and procedure. [TWI's prevention guidance](https://www.twi-global.com/technical-knowledge/job-knowledge/defects-hydrogen-cracks-in-steels-prevention-and-best-practice-046) explains the dependence on composition, thickness, joint, consumable and heat input.

**Thermal-history exercise.** A fictional WPS requires recording voltage, current and travel speed as well as temperature. Two passes share 200 A and 24 V; one travels at 240 mm/min, the other at 120 mm/min. Using the exercise's arc-energy expression `60VI/(1000 × travel speed)`, the results are 1.2 and 2.4 kJ/mm. Halving travel speed doubles calculated arc energy per unit length. These values exclude any process-efficiency factor and are not a recommendation. Check the actual procedure's calculation convention and limits before evaluating compliance. Equal amperage did not produce equal thermal input.

The instructor changes current to 180 A and travel speed to 180 mm/min while holding 24 V. The learner should calculate 1.44 kJ/mm, state the convention and explain why a calculated value cannot replace missing actual temperature records or prove required heat treatment. Record both calculation and limits of inference.

## V16 — Review WPS, PQR and welder qualification

The welding procedure specification gives instructions and permitted ranges for production welding. The procedure qualification record documents the actual variables and test results supporting qualification. A welder performance qualification establishes an individual's demonstrated range, not the procedure's metallurgical suitability. One document cannot substitute for the other two.

Begin with document identity, revision, organization and certification. Confirm the WPS references its supporting PQR. Compare process, base-material grouping, filler grouping, thickness range, deposited thickness where relevant, position, backing, electrical variables, shielding, heat treatment and other applicable variables. Essential-variable changes require the applicable requalification response; supplementary essential variables become relevant when toughness is required. Nonessential changes still require an accurate WPS even where requalification is not required.

**Synthetic review exercise rules—not ASME qualification tables:** PQR A records GTAW on 12 mm plate and supports a WPS thickness range of 3–24 mm for this case. It supports filler F-A, no PWHT and backing condition B. WPS A proposes 3–30 mm and filler F-B. The review fails at least two supplied constraints: maximum thickness and filler. A welder card valid only for SMAW does not authorize that person for the GTAW weld, even after the WPS is corrected. Real ranges must be determined from the applicable Section IX tables and variables, not these fictional exercise values.

Verify the actual test results, not just a 'pass' typed in a spreadsheet. Check specimen type, number, acceptance basis, reported results and certification. Confirm welder continuity and any renewal or requalification conditions through the employer's controlled records. A familiar welder's reputation is not a substitute for evidence.

**Practice.** Build a three-column comparison: production requirement, supporting qualification evidence, disposition. Include one unresolved item requiring document recovery and one demonstrated mismatch requiring correction. Distinguish those dispositions.

### Translate the drawing before reviewing execution

First identify the drawing's symbol convention and edition. Under the AWS convention illustrated in the [AWS welding-symbol chart](https://app.aws.org/mwf/attachments/64/225364/AWSWeldSymbolchart.pdf), placement below the reference line identifies arrow-side information and placement above identifies the other side. Size and length/pitch occupy different positions. Pitch is center-to-center spacing, not the unwelded gap. A weld-all-around indication and a field-weld indication describe different attributes. Do not apply one convention's side interpretation to a drawing using another system.

**Original reading exercise.** A fictional drawing explicitly uses that AWS convention and defines millimetres. Its arrow-side fillet callout specifies size 6, length 40 and pitch 100. Translate it into an inspection instruction: verify the identified side, 6 mm fillet size, 40 mm increments and 100 mm center spacing, plus the drawing's remaining detail and procedure requirements. The nominal intervening gap for equally spaced 40 mm increments is 60 mm; it is not 100 mm. This exercise teaches symbol interpretation and does not authorize intermittent welding of a pressure boundary.

**Changed case.** The drawing requires both sides, but the inspection sheet records only the accessible arrow side. Credit what was actually measured; the other side remains unverified. If access prevents the required examination, record that limitation and obtain the specified disposition. Do not mirror a measurement into an inaccessible location.

For groove details, distinguish root opening, groove angle, preparation depth and specified weld size. A fit-up record should preserve the measured dimension under its correct name. When a symbol or drawing note is ambiguous, obtain the design clarification before welding. The inspector does not resolve ambiguity by choosing the easiest joint to produce.

**Assessor prompt:** change the drawing to another declared symbol system or remove its convention note. The learner must identify the need to establish that basis rather than repeat the AWS interpretation as universal. Record the drawing revision, convention, translated requirement and actual measured evidence in the qualification/fit-up worksheet.

### Weld surface condition and independent acceptance checks

UW-35 distinguishes fusion/penetration, surface interpretability, remaining base thickness and reinforcement. Its undercut check requires both adequate remaining design thickness and a reduction no greater than the lesser of 1/32 in. or 10% of nominal thickness. Reinforcement limits depend on the applicable table row and joint category. Source: [ASME VIII-1 2025, UW-35](https://studylib.net/doc/29030014/asme-sec-viii-div1-2025).

**Original worked exercise.** Nominal material is 0.25 in.; the design minimum is 0.23 in. An undercut reduces thickness by 0.02 in. The reduction limit is min(0.03125, 0.025) = 0.025 in.; remaining thickness is 0.23 in. Both numerical conditions pass. Change only the design minimum to 0.24 in.: the reduction still passes, but the remaining thickness fails. Do not use percentage loss as a substitute for the required remaining section. If a ridge prevents reliable examination, numerical thickness compliance does not dispose of that separate issue. Obtain the permitted corrective process and re-examination; indiscriminate grinding can create a thickness failure.

**Joint restriction exercise.** A proposed repair drawing specifies a joint detail different from the construction record. Before selecting efficiency, identify its category, physical type, service restrictions and required examination. The joint's location category does not establish its construction type. A supplier's statement that “this joint has always worked” is not a demonstrated permitted detail. Record the actual applicable detail and restrictions, then check the efficiency basis and repair authorization. Submit those decisions separately; a high efficiency cannot legalize a prohibited configuration.

## V17 — Review radiographic examination evidence

Radiography records transmitted radiation affected by material thickness and internal features. Its ability to reveal a discontinuity depends on geometry, orientation, technique and image quality. Planar flaws may be difficult to detect in an unfavorable orientation. An image-quality indicator demonstrates aspects of technique sensitivity; it does not guarantee every flaw was detected.

Review equipment and technique identification, material thickness, source/object/detector geometry, coverage, identification markers, IQI selection and placement, required sensitivity, image quality/density provisions and backscatter evaluation. Use the applicable procedure and current examination requirements. Acceptance of weld indications comes from the governing construction or in-service criteria, not simply from the fact that the radiograph meets a technique standard.

**Worked case.** A report states 'RT acceptable' but the images cannot be tied to the vessel seam map. The technician's certification is valid. Personnel qualification does not repair missing traceability. Hold acceptance until identity and coverage are established; repeat examination if reliable recovery is impossible. Do not relabel an image from a similar seam.

Radiation work is controlled by qualified personnel and the applicable radiation-safety program. The inspector reviews records and coordinates access without entering an exclusion area or operating a source without authorization. Production urgency never justifies crossing a radiography boundary.

**Practice.** Given a seam map requiring full length coverage and image records covering only half, calculate the unexamined length and write the hold notice. Identify the additional records needed to establish technique quality and indication acceptance separately.

### Film technique review before interpreting a weld

In ASME V (2025) Article 2, ordinary single-film density minima differ by source: 1.8 for X-ray and 2.0 for gamma, with maximum 4.0. Composite viewing has separate conditions. A light B image on a darker background identifies inadequate backscatter protection; absence of its image alone is not rejection if the placement requirements were met. These selected checks do not replace IQI selection, sensitivity, geometry, coverage and complete procedure review. [Publisher text, Article 2](https://studylib.net/doc/29028772/asme-v-2025).

**Original case.** Film R-17 has a clearly identified gamma source and a reported area-of-interest density of 1.70. The reviewer sees no obvious weld discontinuity and wants to accept the joint. The technique result does not meet the stated minimum, so the absence of a visible flaw is not a valid acceptance basis. Identify the deficient record, obtain qualified technical correction and repeat the affected examination as required. Do not edit the reported reading to the expected value.

**Changed case.** Density is satisfactory, but a bright B is visible against a darker background. Resolve the backscatter problem; the favorable density result cannot cancel it. Conversely, do not automatically reject an otherwise valid exposure simply because no B image is visible—verify the required setup evidence. The learner submits separate technique-validity and weld-disposition fields, leaving the latter unresolved when the examination is invalid.

### Image quality must represent the region being accepted

An IQI image demonstrates sensitivity at its represented location; it does not establish every region's image quality. Thickness transitions, scatter, geometry and detector behavior can still compromise the region of interest. ASNT's technical discussion explicitly warns against treating IQI visibility as a universal image-quality pass: [IQI interpretation](https://www.asnt.org/me/26/11/why-is-your-image-quality-indicator-lying-to-you).

**Original image-review case.** Three training exposures cover adjacent weld segments. Exposure A has clear identity and overlap markers, B has a visible IQI but no traceable weld location, and C has a clear location but a saturated region across the weld. Do not assemble three “passing” images by borrowing A's identity for B and B's IQI for C. Each image must support its own applicability and the required coverage. Identify the unresolved identity and image-quality issues separately, then obtain the qualified examination disposition.

**Changed exercise.** B's location is recovered, but the exposure map shows a 20 mm gap between two examined segments. The reports individually remain valid within their coverage. The proposed statement that the entire required length was examined is false. Mark the gap on the map and require examination or an explicitly authorized scope disposition. Do not stretch a marker's stated location to hide the gap.

The qualified radiographic procedure establishes IQI material, selection basis, placement and number for the actual configuration. The inspector links these choices to the applicable source rather than choosing whichever wire or hole happens to be visible. For the exercise, record the procedure clause and actual setup separately from the discontinuity acceptance result. A good-quality image may show an unacceptable weld; an invalid image cannot establish an acceptable weld.

### IQI selection: establish the input before reading the image

For the selected 2025 Article 2 rules, the selection basis for a reinforced weld includes nominal single-wall thickness and representative reinforcement on both faces, excluding backing strips. Material attenuation and source-side versus permitted film-side placement also affect selection. Source-side placement is the normal arrangement; a film-side choice needs its applicable justification. Source: [ASME V, Article 2, T-276 and T-277](https://previewnorm.com/asme/ASME%20BPVC.V%20%28ASME%20BPVC%20Section%205%29%202025.pdf).

**Original calculation exercise.** A training shell has nominal wall 0.60 in., estimated reinforcement 0.06 in. outside and 0.04 in. inside, and a 0.10 in. backing strip. The selection basis is 0.70 in., not 0.80 in. Under the source-side wire column of Table T-276, this lies in the over-0.50-through-0.75 range, requiring essential wire 8. This does not establish image acceptance: verify material group, actual placement, image density, essential-wire visibility and coverage. If reinforcement changes to 0.10 in. on each face, the basis becomes 0.80 in. and enters the next row, essential wire 9. Do not keep the former row because the shell plate is unchanged. The exercise does not authorize a radiographic exposure.

**Assessment evidence.** Submit the thickness arithmetic, selected row and column, setup sketch and separately stated unresolved image-validity checks. A correct wire number with an invented placement justification is not a passing response.

## V18 — Select surface and ultrasonic examinations

Liquid penetrant reveals discontinuities open to the examined surface on suitable nonporous material. Cleaning, drying, penetrant application, dwell, excess removal, developer, lighting and interpretation must follow the qualified procedure. Aggressive cleaning or smeared metal can close a surface opening. Too much removal can reduce sensitivity; inadequate removal can create excessive background. An indication requires interpretation, not automatic classification as a crack.

Magnetic-particle examination applies to ferromagnetic materials. Field direction matters: a discontinuity poorly oriented to the field may be missed. The procedure establishes magnetization technique, direction changes, equipment checks, surface condition, lighting and acceptance. Yoke and prod techniques have different controls; do not improvise prod use on a pressure boundary where arc damage is unacceptable. Stainless material is not automatically suitable for MT.

Ultrasonic thickness measurement and ultrasonic flaw examination are different tasks. A thickness gauge reading is not a volumetric weld examination. Geometry, beam direction, calibration and qualified interpretation control flaw detection. Select methods based on the expected damage and demonstrate coverage. Combining methods may be necessary when one cannot resolve the question.

**Worked case.** A stainless weld has suspected surface-breaking cracking. MT is proposed because the instrument is available. Verify material response; choose a suitable authorized method rather than forcing an unsuitable one. If PT is used and an indication appears, characterize and apply the governing criteria. A subsequent normal thickness reading does not cancel the indication.

**Practice.** For surface cracking, internal laminations, general thinning and inaccessible weld roots, compare the information each proposed method can and cannot provide. Record the qualified procedure and acceptance authority for the selected combination.

### Yoke capability and actual field setup

ASME V (2025) T-762 requires daily pre-use verification and reverification after damage or repair. The lifting criteria are 10 lb for an AC yoke and 40 lb for DC/permanent yokes, at maximum pole spacing with representative contact. These are equipment checks, not proof of complete examination coverage. [Publisher text, Article 7](https://studylib.net/doc/29028772/asme-v-2025).

**Original case.** The examiner presents yesterday's satisfactory AC-yoke check. Today's job uses wider pole spacing on a curved surface, and the yoke was dropped after the recorded check. Reject the record as sufficient evidence for today's setup. Verify the equipment and representative contact as required before relying on the examination. A bright power lamp is not a lifting-power verification.

For the examination itself, establish suitable material, surface condition, particle system, illumination, coverage and field directions under the qualified procedure. Indication visibility depends on orientation; one field direction can miss a discontinuity aligned unfavorably. Prod methods additionally require control of contact and the potential for local surface damage. Do not substitute prods for a specified yoke method without the required procedural review. Record the actual technique, not a generic “MT performed” checkbox.

**Changed case.** Equipment verification passes, but the inspection sketch shows only one direction across an area requiring examination in different directions. Equipment capability and coverage are separate: complete the required coverage and preserve the limitation in the initial record. Score the learner's equipment, technique and coverage conclusions independently.

### Review the examination procedure before accepting its conclusion

For penetrant examination, trace the sequence: suitable surface preparation, drying, application and dwell, controlled excess removal, developer application, interpretation under the required viewing conditions, recording and post-cleaning. Excess penetrant left on the surface can obscure indications; excessive removal can reduce useful indication evidence. A surface-breaking flaw must be accessible to the penetrant. Surface smearing or a coating can defeat that premise even when the examiner completes every timed step.

**Original procedure case.** A fictional approved PT procedure requires a dry, uncoated examination surface and its identified cleaner/penetrant/developer system. The technician examines through a transparent coating and reports no indications. The coating's transparency does not establish penetrant access. Hold the examination result and arrange a valid repeat under the approved procedure. In the changed case the surface is properly prepared, but the consumable batch certificate belongs to a different batch. Resolve the actual material's required contaminant evidence; do not transfer a certificate because the product name matches.

For magnetic examination, establish that the material is suitable and the applied field can reveal the discontinuity orientation of interest. A discontinuity poorly oriented to one field can be less visible than one favorably oriented. Complete the qualified directional coverage and record actual technique conditions. Prod contact introduces a different electrical-contact hazard from a yoke; the approved process must control contact and current application. A yoke lifting check alone does not establish an entire prod setup.

**Nonstandard-temperature review.** Article 6 Mandatory Appendix III uses comparison evidence to qualify examination outside its standard temperature range. The proposed procedure's demonstrated temperature range matters, not merely a product's advertising. Mandatory Appendix II addresses material-specific contaminant controls and batch evidence. Source: [2025 Article 6 and mandatory appendices](https://studylib.net/doc/28638988/asme-section-v-2025-liquid-penetrant-examination-guidelines).

**Original qualification exercise.** The fictional approved technique record demonstrates 60–85°C for its specified system. The proposed part is 92°C. A cleaner label states “high temperature,” but there is no revised qualification. The part lies 7°C above the supplied upper bound; the label does not extend that bound. Require the applicable qualified technique or approved change in examination conditions. In the changed case, the part is 75°C but the comparison record used a different material system. Identify the system mismatch rather than approving on temperature alone. Retain procedure identity, qualification evidence and actual field conditions together.

**Original coverage case.** The fictional MT plan requires two specified field directions. The report records only one because the first showed no indications. That absence cannot justify omitting the direction intended to reveal differently oriented flaws. Complete the missing coverage with the approved method. In the changed case, both directions were performed but the examination area omitted a 25 mm strip hidden by a clamp. Record the exclusion and obtain access or an approved alternative; do not certify full coverage.

For thickness UT, distinguish a valid back-wall measurement from an unresolved echo. A repeatable digital number can still represent the wrong reflector. For flaw-detection UT, distinguish indication characterization from a thickness result and from a code acceptance conclusion. Retain the method, coverage and evaluation basis for each. The examiner's certification must match the task; an inspector reviewing a report does not acquire the examiner's qualification by reading it.

**Assessment record.** For each method, write the examined boundary, material, procedure revision, operator qualification, setup checks, consumable/probe identity where applicable, actual conditions, coverage exclusions, indication record, acceptance source and disposition. The instructor changes one condition after the learner's first answer and requires a revised decision. Credit requires identifying the changed condition's effect rather than repeating “refer to procedure.”

### Prod-technique tabletop calculation

The reviewed T-752 rules specify direct or rectified current and different current-per-spacing bands above and below 3/4 in. section thickness. Contact must be established before energizing; spacing, surface condition and directional coverage remain independent checks. Source: [ASME V 2025, T-752](https://studylib.net/doc/29028772/asme-v-2025).

**Original exercise.** For a 1.0 in. training section and 5 in. prod spacing, the 100–125 A/in. band yields 500–625 A. A recorded 450 A is below that range. For a 0.50 in. section with the same spacing, the 90–110 A/in. band yields 450–550 A. The same amperage can therefore receive a different arithmetic disposition when thickness changes. Neither calculation proves adequate field direction or permits a technique prohibited by the governing procedure. Never demonstrate these cases by applying current to operating equipment. Use photographs, a disconnected training fixture and setup records.

**Changed exercise.** A thick-section record shows 600 A at 6 in. spacing, but the operator lifted a contact while energized. The numerical lower bound is met; the contact practice still requires disposition. A numerical pass cannot cancel possible arc damage. Record both findings and obtain authorized examination and repair decisions before release.

## V19 — Verify materials without overstating PMI

A material verification program links specified material, procurement records, installed component identity and examination results. Positive material identification is an important tool but has instrument-specific limits. An alloy match does not by itself establish heat treatment, mechanical properties, toughness, product form or full material specification compliance.

Plan which components and weld deposits require verification, how they will be identified and marked, and how nonconforming results will be segregated and resolved. Ensure surface preparation and instrument calibration suit the method. Some portable techniques cannot reliably determine certain light elements or carbon; select the method for the actual distinction needed. Do not call two materials equivalent because the instrument cannot distinguish them.

**Worked case.** A replacement nozzle's report matches chromium and molybdenum, but the heat number is missing and the job requires documented heat treatment. Keep the material on hold. Recover traceability and required properties or obtain an authorized alternative disposition. Do not stamp a guessed heat number on the component.

**Practice.** Complete a material chain from purchase specification through receipt, cutting, fabrication and installation. Show how identity is maintained when one plate becomes several repair pieces and how a rejected result is prevented from reentering the job.

### Build a material-verification workflow that survives maintenance

Define the decision before choosing an instrument. For example, “confirm the specified chromium-molybdenum alloy family” and “distinguish a low-carbon grade” require different analytical capabilities. Establish the specification, required elements and limits, method suitability, surface preparation, calibration/verification checks, acceptance authority and record format. If the method cannot measure a distinguishing element, record that limitation and use an appropriate additional method; never fill a missing value with the instrument library's nominal composition.

Assign responsibilities at each transfer. Purchasing identifies the required specification and documentation. Receiving links the physical item with its records and controls its status. Fabrication preserves identity through cutting and assembly and controls filler materials. Examination personnel produce traceable results using an appropriate procedure. The authorized technical function resolves discrepancies. The installer verifies that the accepted item is the one actually installed. A completed receiving check cannot prevent a later mix-up unless those later transfers remain controlled.

A practical record connects asset tag, component location, material specification, item/heat identification where available, instrument and method, required elements, measured results, verification checks, surface condition, operator, date, acceptance basis and disposition. Record actual values and limits, not only “PMI OK.” Marking must be compatible with the material and service. A color mark helps identify status but is not itself analytical evidence. Keep rejected and unidentified items under a physical or electronic hold that prevents installation.

For an existing plant, prioritize uncertainty using the consequences of an incorrect material, the reason that material was specified, credible damage mechanisms and the reliability of construction/maintenance records. A replacement installed during an emergency repair deserves explicit traceability review rather than automatic credit from the original construction file. Do not invent a universal sampling percentage. State the lot boundary, selection logic, escalation response and who can approve it.

**Worked mixed-lot case.** A fictional repair order requires twelve individually identified alloy studs. The receiving sample includes three studs, one of which does not match the specified chemistry. The coordinator proposes replacing that stud and treating the remaining nine as accepted. That conclusion is unsupported: the finding calls the lot's control into question. Hold the affected lot, preserve measured identities, investigate supplier and handling records, and implement the approved expanded verification/disposition. Test results for three items do not transfer to nine untested identities.

**Changed case.** All twelve initially pass, but four are mixed with an unverified bin after marking is removed. Preserve the eight whose identity remains intact. Hold the four whose verification cannot be linked, recover reliable identity or reverify through the approved process. Do not copy the first twelve results onto whichever twelve studs happen to be available.

**Practice record.** Fill: required distinction____ method and limitation____ component identity____ result and acceptance basis____ discrepancy____ hold owner____ verification scope____ final installation link____. Explain which evidence proves composition and which required properties remain outside that evidence. The instructor scores identity, method suitability and discrepancy control separately; all must be corrected before credit.

### Close the verification loop

RP 578 sections 6–9 distinguish sorting from specification conformance, require method capability to match the objective, and address acceptance, identification and records. An out-of-range field result can require more accurate analysis; a rejected sample makes its lot suspect. Results must remain traceable to installed components. Color identification does not replace required manufacturer markings. These principles were checked against fourth-edition publisher text on a third-party host, not an issuer-controlled copy: [source](https://studylib.net/doc/27679361/pmi-rp-578-e4-en).

**Original decision exercise.** The fictional project procedure requires carbon no greater than 0.030% and a quantitative method with demonstrated uncertainty at that boundary. A field instrument reports a grade match but no carbon result. Do not accept the component against that carbon criterion. Send the identified component through the approved capable method; preserve custody between field identification and laboratory specimen. A result of 0.029% with an uncertainty statement spanning the limit requires the project's documented decision rule, not optimistic rounding. The specification limit in this exercise is supplied project data, not a universal acceptance limit.

The second item has the correct chemistry, but its installation tag was detached and two visually identical pieces were mixed. Chemistry establishes information about the tested object, not which unmarked object was installed. Resolve identification, retain the discrepancy and document re-verification. Do not attach a convenient passing report to both pieces.

**Build the record.** For each fictional component record its installed location, persistent identifier, required material, procedure revision, method and instrument, operator, date, calibration/performance evidence, measured result, acceptance basis and discrepancy disposition. Keep the original result when a second method resolves the discrepancy. The resolution explains why the conclusion changed; it does not erase the first observation.

**Changed case.** Three independently identified items pass. A fourth sampled item fails. The learner must identify the affected inspection lot and propose the approved expanded examination, not reject every unrelated delivery or release the remaining lot without review. The instructor changes the heat number on one item; the learner must recognize that a common delivery is not automatically a single heat-based lot. Completion requires an auditable disposition, not merely a list of instrument readings.

## V20 — Inspect pressure-relieving devices as a system

Pressure protection depends on the protected equipment, credible overpressure scenarios, device sizing and setting, installation and maintenance. A valve that opens on a bench is not proof that the installed system has adequate capacity. Inlet restrictions, outlet backpressure, blocked discharge, isolation valves, corrosion, fouling and incorrect installation can compromise protection.

Match device identification and set pressure to the protected equipment and approved design. Inspect inlet and outlet arrangements, supports, drainage, isolation status, seals and evidence of leakage or tampering. Review inspection/test history, as-found results and service conditions when setting maintenance intervals through the governing program. Preserve as-found findings before cleaning or adjustment; otherwise evidence of sticking or set-pressure drift can be lost.

Distinguish safety/relief valves, pilot-operated devices and rupture disks. A rupture disk's orientation, specified rating conditions and installation arrangement matter. Combining a disk and valve can introduce additional monitoring and capacity considerations. Do not remove a fouled device from service without confirming the authorized protective arrangement for the vessel.

**Worked case.** A valve passed its shop test but its discharge line is obstructed after maintenance. The shop certificate remains genuine, yet installed protection is deficient. Notify operations and apply the approved operating response; clear and verify the system through authorized work. Never increase set pressure to stop nuisance lifting without a complete approved review.

**Practice.** Make a protection record linking vessel, device, set pressure, capacity/design reference, inlet/outlet inspection, as-found results, restoration and next due date. State which findings require immediate operational escalation.

### Investigate abnormal behavior without changing the protection basis

Chatter is rapid valve cycling that includes seat contact; it is not a normal demonstration that the device is protecting correctly. Inlet and outlet system conditions can contribute. LESER's [chatter explanation](https://www.leser.com/en/leser-update/10-2022/function-chatter) shows why discharge backpressure can affect the balance of forces. The inspector records the condition and obtains the authorized system/device assessment rather than increasing the set point or restricting a discharge to quiet the valve.

**Original operating-history case.** PSV-62 began cycling after a discharge-header modification. Its earlier bench certificate is valid for the earlier test, but the record contains no assessment of the modified installed system. Preserve the event history and modification drawings, identify the potentially changed inlet/outlet conditions, and obtain the responsible relief-system review. Do not assume causation solely from timing; the changed system is an evidence lead, not a complete diagnosis.

**Changed case.** The header is unchanged, but the valve was replaced with a different model of the same nominal connection size. The reviewer must verify the model's suitability, capacity basis, setting and installation requirements. Equal flange size is not functional equivalence. A replacement's clean appearance also does not establish the correct device was selected.

**Shop-to-installation record exercise.** Build a chronological chain: device received with identity and as-found condition; inspection/test limitations; authorized repair and parts; as-left test evidence; preservation and transport; installation identity and orientation; inlet/outlet and sensing paths; restoration of the approved protective arrangement. Emerson's [Type 84 instructions](https://www.emerson.com/is/content/emerson/pt/final-control/pressure-management/documents/manuals-type-84-spring-operated-pressure-relief-valves-anderson-greenwood.pdf) illustrate model-specific handling and as-received evidence; they are not instructions for every valve.

The instructor removes one record from the fictional chain. The learner must explain the exact conclusion no longer supported and how to resolve it. Missing as-found evidence limits understanding of service deterioration; missing installation identity prevents linking the shop work to the installed device. Neither is repaired by copying the as-left test pressure into another field.

## V21 — Select repair methods with explicit limits

Inspection identifies a condition; engineering and the governing repair process determine an acceptable solution. Separate repair, alteration and rerating according to the applicable code. A change described as 'maintenance' may still change design conditions or pressure-boundary configuration. Identify jurisdictional and authorized-inspector involvement before work starts.

A butt-welded insert replaces an affected section and requires controlled removal geometry, material, fit-up, welding, examination and testing. External weld buildup for internal thinning requires an approved analysis of remaining metal and welding effects. Overlay or clad restoration must address both structural and corrosion-resistant functions. A fillet-welded patch introduces load-transfer and crevice considerations and must not be assumed equivalent to an insert.

Flaw excavation and weld repair require confirmation of flaw removal and acceptable remaining geometry before welding. Flange repair must preserve required dimensions, sealing function and material condition. Shell-and-tube exchanger repairs require review of pressure boundaries, differential pressure and thermal function. Each selected method needs its applicable PCC-2 article and governing in-service requirements; a method title alone is not a repair procedure.

**Worked case.** A contractor proposes covering an uncharacterized crack with a patch. This may conceal an active flaw while adding restraint and stress. Hold the repair, characterize the flaw and obtain an approved design and examination plan. A successful leak test alone would not establish that the concealed crack is acceptable.

**Practice.** Compare insert, buildup and patch options for a synthetic thinning area. State the information needed to select among them, required hold points and the evidence that would reject each option.

### Turn a repair drawing into measurable hold points

Separate dimensions established before removal, during fit-up and after finishing. Once a damaged section is removed, some baseline evidence cannot be recovered. Record the original shape, mapped defect extent, nearby welds and attachments, and reference coordinates before cutting. During fit-up, compare the actual part with the approved detail, not a remembered nominal dimension. After finishing, measure what remains rather than reporting only the amount deposited or machined.

**Original dimensional-control case.** A fictional approved repair drawing requires finished thickness at least 12.0 mm at every marked grid point and a smooth transition within the drawing's specified contour envelope. Deposited thickness is 13.5 mm, but final grinding leaves one point at 11.8 mm. The weld initially contained enough metal; the finished condition nevertheless fails the supplied minimum by 0.2 mm. Do not average the thin point with 12.6 mm readings. Mark its location and obtain an approved correction or technical disposition. These are exercise drawing values, not PCC-2 default limits.

**Inspection-plan exercise.** Build a six-column table: hold point, required record, measured result, acceptance-source revision, disposition and release signature. Add rows for original damage map, removal boundary, replacement identity, fit-up, welding record, final geometry, NDE and required test. A row must not say “pass” when its record is absent. Different responsible parties may sign different stages; a final signature does not retroactively establish a missing observation.

**Changed case.** Finished thickness passes, but the insert interferes with an internal tray support. The mechanical-integrity calculation and internal function are separate requirements. Resolve the actual geometry and attachment effects before closeout. A pressure-boundary repair cannot be accepted as a complete equipment restoration while a necessary internal component remains unusable.

## V22 — Control special welding and repair verification

In-service welding and hot tapping introduce burn-through, cracking, ignition and process hazards. They require specialized engineering, procedures, qualified personnel and operating controls. This course does not authorize a learner to perform either operation. Inspection review must verify that remaining thickness, material, process conditions, heat removal, weld procedure and emergency arrangements were evaluated by the authorized team.

Cr-Mo repairs require attention to material identity, service degradation, weld consumables, heat treatment and properties. Alternatives to PWHT are not a convenience waiver: applicability, procedure qualification, examination and approval must be established under the relevant provisions. A repair crew cannot substitute a heating blanket schedule from a previous unrelated job.

Develop an inspection and test plan with hold points: original condition documented; material accepted; excavation examined; fit-up accepted; qualifications verified; welding variables recorded; heat treatment reviewed; final NDE accepted; required pressure/tightness test completed or approved alternative justified; protective systems restored; final report approved. NDE in lieu of pressure testing is a controlled code decision, not an automatic trade of 'more UT' for an inconvenient test.

**Worked case.** Final NDE passes, but the heat-treatment chart cannot be linked to the repaired vessel. The repair is not ready for release. Recover traceability and evaluate actual compliance; do not create a retrospective chart. Record the missing evidence and authorized disposition.

**Practice.** Write a repair release checklist that separates completed work, accepted evidence and outstanding conditions. Include a place for the inspector's limitations and the responsible operating-release authority.

## V23 — Recognize design checks beyond simple thickness

External pressure can cause instability even when internal-pressure thickness is adequate. Vacuum can arise during draining, cooling or condensation. Shell geometry, unsupported length, stiffening and material properties matter. Do not use the internal-pressure equation with a negative pressure to assess buckling. Obtain the applicable external-pressure evaluation.

Openings remove load-carrying material and create local stress. Nozzle reinforcement evaluation considers geometry, available reinforcement and applicable limits. Weld dimensions and attachment details matter. For an equal-leg fillet weld, the theoretical throat is approximately 0.707 times leg size; this geometric relationship does not establish that the weld size meets the governing design requirement.

**Worked case.** A drawing specifies a 6 mm equal-leg fillet. Its theoretical throat is about 4.24 mm. An inspector measures a 4 mm leg and records '4 mm throat acceptable.' The record confuses two dimensions. A 4 mm leg has theoretical throat about 2.83 mm. Compare the actual geometry against the approved detail and disposition the discrepancy.

Nozzle loads, supports, thermal expansion and differential pressure can control fitness. A vessel with a calculated internal-pressure margin may still be unfit because of settlement, external loads or fatigue. Report those conditions for the correct analysis rather than forcing every finding into a corrosion spreadsheet.

**Practice.** Identify the additional engineering evaluations needed after adding a larger nozzle, changing to vacuum service or increasing thermal cycling. Explain why a thickness survey alone cannot approve these changes.

## V24 — Integrate findings and control return to service

An inspection report must let a qualified reviewer reconstruct what was examined, what was found and why the disposition follows. Include equipment and chamber identity, basis documents, operating history, methods and coverage, personnel/procedures, measurements, indication maps, calculations, assumptions, limitations, repairs, protection review, due dates and approvals. Attach original records rather than replacing them with a summary that hides exceptions.

Rerating requires the applicable engineering calculations, condition assessment, protection changes, documentation and authorization. It does not consist of editing a spreadsheet or changing a relief setting. Consider the weakest component, temperature limits, other loads, service mechanisms and jurisdictional requirements. Preserve the original rating history and approved change basis.

**Integrated case.** Vessel V-410 has a shell calculation supporting 238.80 psi after future allowance, a lower head permitting 220 psi locally thirty feet below the pressure reference, and an upper component permitting 205 psi. The pressure-only reference limit is 205 psi. However, a crack-like weld indication remains unresolved and the installed relief discharge is obstructed. The correct report does not release the vessel at 205 psi. It records that the numerical pressure calculation is only one part of fitness and requires disposition of both unresolved conditions before authorized operation.

Closeout includes repair acceptance, removal of temporary equipment, restored isolation/protection arrangements, required operating instructions, approved limits and inspection schedule. Track each recommendation to an owner and due date. A signed report is not a substitute for completing the actions it requires.

**Practice.** Produce a final report for the integrated case with separate headings for established facts, calculations, unresolved findings, required actions and release decision. State the credential boundary: successfully completing this VNDRLY course demonstrates assessed learning within its documented scope, not API certification or authority to release real equipment independently.

Source: [2024 API-authored text, sections 6.4.2 and 6.4.4](https://studylib.net/doc/28051709/api-rp-576--inspection-of-pressure-relieving-devices-2024). Those checks support the reasoning here; the exercise does not replace a device-specific overhaul procedure.

**Changed practice.** The pilot path is correctly restored, but upstream deposits obstruct the inlet. The certificate and pilot check both pass. Identify the remaining failure and what evidence closes it.

**Explained response.** The restricted installed flow path remains unresolved. Required closure includes authorized assessment/restoration of that path and verification of the complete protective arrangement, not another copy of the same certificate. Preserve deposit/as-found observations for mechanism and interval review.

Score system trace, distinction between device types/evidence and operating disposition 0–2 each. Pass 6/6 after correction. Removing protection from operating equipment without an authorized protective arrangement is a critical error.

## T05 — A readable penetrant result can still be invalid

**Learning outcome:** evaluate the examination conditions before relying on the indication result.

The 2025 Section V Article 6 standard-temperature range is 5–52°C. Color-contrast examination requires at least 1,076 lux at the examination surface. Final interpretation normally occurs 10–60 minutes after the applicable developer timing condition, unless otherwise qualified. Source: [ASME-authored Article 6 text, T-652 and T-676](https://studylib.net/doc/28638988/asme-section-v-2025-liquid-penetrant-examination-guidelines). These selected requirements are not the entire procedure.

**Training record PT-17:** color-contrast examination; approved standard-temperature technique; workpiece temperature 61°C; surface light 860 lux; developer dry at 10:00; final interpretation 10:07; no special qualification supplied. Report: “No relevant indications; acceptable.”

**Worked review.** Three independent technique deficiencies prevent accepting the report on that basis: temperature exceeds the stated range, illumination is below the required minimum, and interpretation occurs after only seven minutes. The inspector records all three rather than choosing the easiest one to fix. Cooling the part later does not validate the earlier examination. Request qualified disposition and a valid repeat examination under the approved procedure, including its cleaning, dwell, removal, development and timing requirements. Preserve the original report and the repeat result separately.

**Changed practice.** Part is 30°C, light is 1,200 lux and interpretation is at 10:20. The procedure requires a particular penetrant/developer family, but the report identifies an unapproved mixed family. Explain whether the three favorable numerical checks establish acceptance.

**Explained response.** They do not. The material-system discrepancy remains. Verify the procedure, product identities and compatibility/qualification requirements. Do not mark the examination acceptable merely because temperature, lighting and time pass. Once technique validity is established, indication classification and the referencing code's acceptance criteria remain separate decisions.

**Assessment:** identify each initial deficiency, explain repeat/disposition without falsifying history, and identify the changed case's remaining failure. Score each 0–2; pass 6/6 after correction. Calling an invalid examination a passed examination is a critical error.

## T06 — An alloy label is not a carbon measurement

**Learning outcome:** select evidence capable of answering the material question and preserve component identity through a verification program.

Handheld XRF does not measure carbon. A displayed alloy-library match cannot supply a carbon result that the instrument did not measure. Manufacturer sources: [Evident handheld PMI](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/applications/handheld-pmi) and [Thermo Fisher technology comparison](https://www.thermofisher.com/blog/metals/pmi-technologies-whats-the-difference-between-xrf-libs-and-oes/). The selected instrument, calibration, preparation and operator competence must suit the elements and concentrations being distinguished.

**Case.** Repair spool M-61 is required to meet an identified low-carbon stainless specification. The XRF record shows chromium, nickel and molybdenum consistent with the intended alloy family, a library label including “L,” and no measured carbon result. Its certificate is missing. The receiving clerk proposes accepting it as fully verified.

**Worked decision.** Record the actual elements measured and the scope of the result. Do not infer low-carbon compliance from the label. Recover a traceable certificate and obtain the verification required by the material program, using a capable authorized method where direct carbon measurement is needed. A spark or laser method also needs appropriate setup and qualification; merely naming OES or LIBS is not sufficient evidence. Chemical verification does not by itself establish heat treatment, mechanical properties or toughness.

**Program task.** Construct a chain for plate M-62 cut into repair pieces A, B and C. Before cutting, reconcile specification, heat identity, certificate and required verification. Transfer identity through a controlled marking/map procedure, retain the cut plan and link the final installed piece to its weld map. Mark B as on hold if its identity is lost. Keep it physically or administratively segregated so a later shift cannot install it as an accepted piece. Document any authorized recovery rather than stamping an assumed heat number.

**Changed practice.** All three pieces have valid base-metal verification, but filler-wire issue records show a different alloy batch on one repair weld. Explain why base-metal acceptance does not resolve deposited-weld suitability.

**Explained response.** Evaluate the affected weld deposit, consumable identity, procedure and applicable material-verification plan. Identify its extent and hold acceptance pending authorized resolution. Repeating only the adjacent plate's XRF result answers the wrong question.

**Assessment:** distinguish measured properties from library inference, maintain piece identity, and disposition the changed weld case. Score each 0–2; pass 6/6 after correction. Inventing material identity or accepting an unmeasured critical property is a critical error.

## T07 — Evaluate indication size, shape and grouping separately

**Learning outcome:** apply the selected PT acceptance provisions without substituting a different examination method or ignoring grouping.

For this exercise use the 2025 VIII-1 Appendix 8 rules: a major dimension above 1.5 mm makes an indication relevant; length greater than three times width is linear; relevant linear indications, rounded indications above 5 mm, and a line of four or more relevant rounded indications with edge spacing at most 1.5 mm are unacceptable. More restrictive applicable provisions still govern. Source: [ASME-authored 2025 Appendix 8, public reproduction](https://www.studocu.com/cl/document/instituto-de-ciencias-tecnologicas-ciisa/sistema-de-gestion/asme-sec-viii-div-1-2025-mandatory-appendix-8-liquid-penetrant-exam-methods/155422905).

Assume technique, personnel, traceability and measurement validity are established for this case. The measurements below are indication dimensions, not a claim about the exact underlying flaw size. Do not convert between inch and millimetre limits mid-evaluation to obtain a preferred result.

| Observation | Recorded dimensions/arrangement | Worked evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| A | 4 mm long, 1 mm wide | Relevant and linear because 4 is greater than 3×1; unacceptable under the stated rule |
| B | Isolated ellipse, 4 mm long, 2 mm wide | Relevant and rounded; does not fail the stated individual-size criterion, but still review all other applicable criteria |
| C | Isolated circle, 6 mm diameter | Relevant rounded indication exceeding 5 mm; unacceptable |
| D | Four 2 mm rounded indications in one line, each edge gap 1 mm | Individual sizes do not exceed 5 mm, but the grouping criterion fails |
| E | Blurred indication, width cannot be established | Classification is doubtful; obtain the required re-examination rather than assuming the favorable rounded classification |

**Changed practice.** Three 2 mm rounded indications are in a line, each separated by 1 mm. A fourth is then identified in that same line at a 1 mm edge gap. Explain the decision before and after finding the fourth, without declaring the entire component acceptable from this one criterion.

**Explained response.** The three-indication arrangement does not meet the stated four-or-more grouping rejection condition. Adding the fourth does. Other criteria and the complete examination still apply in both cases. Preserve the final map with the additional observation; do not retain an earlier passing summary after the evidence changes.

**Assessment:** classify A/B/E correctly, apply individual and grouping criteria to C/D, and explain the changed case with appropriate limits. Score 0–2 each; pass 6/6 after correction. Concealing an indication or accepting doubtful classification without resolution is a critical error. These numeric criteria are for the named PT exercise; do not silently use them as RT or UT acceptance criteria.

## T08 — Radiographic disposition with a defined examination basis

**Purpose.** Separate identification of an indication from the acceptance decision. Record the governing examination provision before measuring a film: full and spot examination do not have interchangeable acceptance rules. A sharp-looking image is not evidence that its identification, coverage and image-quality requirements were met.

**Reference rule for this exercise.** ASME VIII-1 (2025), UW-51 rejects cracks and incomplete fusion or penetration. For other elongated indications, the length limits are 0.25 in for weld thickness up to 0.75 in, one-third of thickness above 0.75 through 2.25 in, and 0.75 in above 2.25 in. These are selected criteria, not the entire examination standard. Grouping and rounded indications need their own evaluation.

**Worked case.** The fictional record identifies full radiography under UW-51, thickness 1.20 in excluding reinforcement, satisfactory image-quality review and an isolated elongated slag indication 0.43 in long. Compute 1.20/3 = 0.40 in. The indication exceeds that limit by 0.03 in; reject it under this criterion. Adding 0.10 in of reinforcement to obtain a larger limit is not valid. Record the location and result, refer the repair disposition to the responsible authority, and require the applicable reexamination. Do not turn the decision into permission to grind or weld an operating vessel.

**Changed practice.** A second record has a 0.60 in weld and a 0.20 in elongated indication. A reviewer calls it acceptable. The interpreter then identifies incomplete fusion at that location. Does the measured length save it?

**Explained response.** No. Classification changes the applicable criterion: incomplete fusion is unacceptable regardless of this small measured length. The original length comparison cannot override the identified defect type. Preserve both the initial observation and corrected interpretation rather than erasing the record.

**Evidence and scoring.** Submit thickness basis, applicable criterion and written disposition. Score each 0–2; require 6/6 after correction. An automatic release based only on one indication passing is a critical error. This is a record-based exercise, not radiographic interpreter qualification. Allow 45 minutes.

## T09 — Inspection coverage for a two-sided exchanger

**Purpose.** Build an inspection plan from the equipment's physical arrangement and service history. One equipment tag can contain two fluid circuits with different pressure, chemistry and damage histories. Inspecting the convenient side does not establish the condition of the other side.

**Teaching case.** Fictional exchanger E-35 has hot process fluid in the shell and cooling water in the tubes. Its fixed tubesheets prevent simple removal of the bundle. Operations reports increased pressure drop, reduced heat transfer and intermittent contamination. These observations support investigation; none uniquely identifies a leaking tube or a particular corrosion mechanism. Establish which stream can contaminate the other at normal operation, startup and shutdown. The pressure difference can reverse.

Map both pressure boundaries and isolation points. Obtain materials, operating excursions, prior tube maps and repair records. Plan access and decontamination for each circuit. Select tube examination for the actual material and expected damage, with a documented calibration standard, coverage and blind zones. Record inaccessible areas explicitly. Compare the indications with tube position, inlet region and support locations. Do not describe a sample of tubes as complete bundle coverage.

API RP 572's exchanger annex distinguishes fixed, floating-head and U-tube arrangements and discusses restricted access. Those distinctions explain why a generic instruction to pull every bundle is unsuitable. Use the equipment drawing to decide what is physically possible, and engineering review to address remaining uncertainty.

**Worked disposition.** The record contains 200 tubes, 40 examined and four suspect results. Forty divided by 200 is 20% coverage; four divided by 40 is 10% of the examined sample. Neither fraction establishes that exactly 20 tubes in the entire bundle are damaged. Explain how the sample was selected, extend examination based on the damage pattern, and obtain an approved disposition. Plugging a tube changes the operating system and requires evaluation of plugging method, remaining capability and future inspection—not just a count.

**Changed practice.** All 200 tubes are examined, but the procedure records an unexamined end region on every tube. Can the report state “100% of every tube examined”?

**Explained response.** No. State that all tube identities were included and separately identify the axial coverage limitation. Select an appropriate supplementary method or document the unresolved limitation for the responsible engineering disposition. Complete identity coverage and complete surface/volume coverage are different claims.

**Evidence and scoring.** Submit a two-circuit boundary sketch, coverage statement and disposition request. Score each 0–2 and require 6/6 after correction. Hiding a blind zone or authorizing operation from sample arithmetic is a critical error. Allow 45 minutes.

## T10 — UT investigation, rejection and recording are different decisions

**Learning outcome.** Apply three different thresholds without treating a recording threshold as permission to accept an unidentified indication.

**Reference basis.** ASME VIII-1 (2025), Mandatory Appendix 12 directs investigation above 20% of reference response. Cracks, lack of fusion and incomplete penetration are unacceptable regardless of length. Other imperfections require comparison of both amplitude and thickness-based length limits. Uncorrected responses above 50% of reference also have recording requirements. Read the complete applicable provision and written procedure; these selected facts do not qualify someone to conduct UT.

**Worked record.** Fictional weld U-10 is 1.50 in thick excluding reinforcement. A calibrated examination record reports an indication at 70% of reference, length 0.60 in. The length comparison is 1.50/3 = 0.50 in. The indication exceeds the length value but not reference amplitude. It is therefore not rejected merely by the combined amplitude-and-length criterion for other imperfections. That does not establish acceptance: it must first be characterized. If investigation identifies lack of fusion, reject it regardless of its 70% response. Its response also exceeds the recording threshold; retain location, depth, dimensions, classification and response information.

**Decision sequence.** Check record identity and procedure applicability, calibration and coverage; investigate; classify; apply the relevant acceptance provision; document; obtain the authorized disposition. A percentage displayed on a screen is not automatically a percentage of reference amplitude. Confirm the reference convention and gain corrections before comparing numbers.

**Changed practice.** Three supplied records concern the same thickness: A is a characterized noncrack imperfection, 110% reference and 0.55 in long; B is a confirmed crack, 35% reference and 0.08 in long; C is an unclassified response at 25%. State the immediate disposition for each.

**Explained response.** A exceeds both reference amplitude and the 0.50 in length limit and is unacceptable under the stated criterion. B is unacceptable because of its classification. C requires investigation; it cannot be accepted solely because its response is below the recording threshold. Do not authorize operation from any of these isolated decisions without completing the governing examination and release process.

**Scoring.** Score reference interpretation, classification/acceptance reasoning and traceable disposition 0–2 each. Require 6/6 after correction. Calling an unidentified response acceptable without investigation is a critical error. Allow 45 minutes.

# VUC035 repair decision instruction and casebook

This is original instruction for reviewing a repair proposal, not a set of welding instructions. All cases are fictional. Work is evaluated on the actual equipment condition, approved design, applicable construction/in-service requirements and documented execution. The selected PCC-2 article identifies a technical method; it does not replace the governing authorization or the assessment that established whether repair is needed.

For every case, identify: the defect and evidence; why the proposed method does or does not answer it; necessary design inputs; inspection hold points; and the evidence required for closure. This structure turns a vague “repair per code” note into a reviewable work package.

## R01 — Establish the repair decision before choosing a method

The inspection map shows a local depression, but the work order calls it “corrosion.” No baseline, surface profile or examination has distinguished metal loss from deformation. A contractor has priced a patch.

The first engineering question is what condition actually exists. A method chosen for lost thickness may not address deformation or a crack associated with it. Preserve the measured profile and location, obtain the necessary characterization, identify load/service history and evaluate the condition using the applicable assessment route. The commercial quote is not a technical diagnosis. Review the assessment and repair authorization before accepting a method-specific plan.

**Practice:** a later report proves uniform thinning with no detected cracking using a suitable examination. What changed? **Answer:** the condition is better characterized, enabling a method comparison. It does not establish the design, execution or acceptance of the proposed repair. The learner must identify these as separate decisions.

## R02 — Butt-welded insert: define what is removed and what remains

V-510 has a mapped damaged area extending 80 mm beyond an old marked outline. The proposed insert drawing still follows the old outline. The replacement plate has the specified thickness and a traceable certificate.

Correct material does not make the removal boundary correct. Reconcile the current damage map with the insert geometry and engineering design. Identify whether all affected material is removed or whether retained material requires an explicit acceptable assessment. Plan temporary support and stability during removal, dimensional/fit-up checks, material traceability, welding qualification, examination and required testing. Confirm access to both sides where the approved detail requires it. A completed weld hiding the questionable edge makes later verification harder.

**Practice:** the drawing is corrected, but the installed plate's identity was lost during cutting. **Answer:** the geometric correction closes only the boundary issue. Material traceability remains a separate hold. Record both findings and their individual closure evidence.

## R03 — External buildup: measure the wall under the proposed weld

An external buildup proposal assumes a minimum remaining wall of 5 mm. A new mapped examination finds 2 mm locally in the proposed welding region. The crew proposes depositing extra metal to make up the difference.

The initial thickness is an input to the repair process as well as the final structural condition. Additional deposited thickness does not prove the thin region can safely tolerate the operation. Reconcile the design with the actual minimum and distribution, material, process contents and thermal conditions. Obtain an authorized revised evaluation before work. Do not interpolate away the thin point or average it with nearby readings merely to preserve the original assumption.

**Practice:** a crack-like indication is also found. **Answer:** remove the proposal from a thinning-only disposition and obtain characterization and an applicable repair decision. A thickness restoration calculation does not resolve a crack. PCC-2 Article 202 expressly distinguishes this limitation; the selected original text was checked in [its limitations section](https://studylib.net/doc/28281618/asme-pcc-2-2022).

## R04 — PWHT alternatives: identify the function being replaced

The original repair plan includes PWHT. A revised plan deletes it because the outage is short, while keeping only a note that a special welding technique will be used.

Ask what PWHT was intended to achieve for this material and service: the relevant stress, hardness, hydrogen or metallurgical requirements must remain addressed. A technique name is not the required qualification evidence. Identify the applicable permitted alternative, procedure qualification, service-specific requirements, specialist approval and examination/verification plan. If the owner requires a property that the alternative does not demonstrate, the proposal remains incomplete even if welding can physically be performed.

**Practice:** the alternative has demonstrated hardness results, but the service review requires a separate residual-stress assessment. **Answer:** credit the hardness evidence within its scope; retain the other unresolved requirement. Do not equate one measured property with every purpose of heat treatment.

## R05 — In-service welding: operating conditions are design inputs

A proposed attachment weld is supported by an engineering calculation at an identified process temperature, flow and wall thickness. Operations changes the flow during the work window. The welding supervisor says the work is small.

Determine whether the change remains within the approved engineering/procedure envelope. Process conditions affect heat transfer and hazards; the size of the attachment does not make that input irrelevant. Establish explicit operating limits, monitoring, communication and stop conditions before the authorized work begins. The inspector verifies the approved controls and records; this training does not qualify the learner to design or perform in-service welding.

**Practice:** every listed operating value is within range, but material identity is uncertain. **Answer:** the thermal envelope does not establish metallurgical suitability. Resolve the material basis separately before accepting the plan.

**Technical distinction:** PCC-2 Article 210 addresses carbon-steel in-service welding, not stainless, Cr-Mo or nonferrous work. Its central thermal problem is balancing burn-through against hydrogen-cracking susceptibility. Increasing cooling potential beyond the qualified condition requires requalification under its specified provisions. Read the article's actual applicability and essential variables; a general construction WPS is not automatically evidence of the required in-service qualification. Source: [Article 210, scope and qualification provisions](https://studylib.net/doc/28281618/asme-pcc-2-2022).

**Original qualification-envelope case.** The training engineering report identifies a qualified cooling envelope and says the proposed changed operating condition exceeds it. The contractor proposes increasing current without revising the qualification basis. Reject the unsupported adjustment: it changes the thermal balance without establishing either concern is controlled. The necessary work is a qualified revised evaluation and procedure disposition, not an inspector-selected current. In the changed case, the material is identified as stainless steel; recognize the cited article's scope mismatch before considering the operating envelope.

## R06 — Overlay and clad restoration: two functions need two checks

A vessel has structural backing metal and an internal corrosion-resistant layer. A repair record reports adequate combined thickness but does not identify how much of each remains.

The reviewer must distinguish the pressure-resisting design basis from the corrosion-protection function. Determine what material and thickness the design credits, how dilution and bonding are controlled, which defects are relevant, and how the restored surface is verified. Adding unlike thicknesses and treating the sum as an approved structural material is unjustified unless the applicable design explicitly supports it.

**Practice:** structural thickness is verified, but the final exposed chemistry does not meet the approved repair specification. **Answer:** structural acceptance does not close corrosion-resistance acceptance. Identify the affected surface and obtain correction or an authorized engineering disposition.

**Original finished-layer exercise.** A fictional approved overlay procedure and drawing require a particular final chemistry at a finished depth of 3.5 mm. The shop deposits 4.2 mm, verifies the exposed chemistry, then machines the layer to 2.8 mm without another technical review. The earlier result does not establish the newly exposed surface's chemistry or compliance with the supplied minimum. Record the 0.7 mm dimensional shortfall and obtain the required procedure/chemistry disposition. In the changed case, the final dimension is correct but the deposited layer sequence differs from the qualified procedure: geometry alone does not close qualification.

## R07 — Fillet-welded patch: do not confuse sealing with load transfer

A patch stops leakage during a low-pressure check. Its design package does not identify the retained damaged area, load path or duration of intended use.

A leak-tight result alone does not establish a pressure-boundary repair's structural adequacy. Establish whether the patch is a permitted solution for the actual damage, the design conditions, attachment details, retained material, future degradation and any time limit. Ensure the work package identifies the inspection plan and the next action if it is temporary. An undefined “temporary” label can otherwise become indefinite service without review.

**Practice:** the design gives a six-month limit and required follow-up, but the equipment register lists the normal five-year inspection date only. **Answer:** correct the maintenance/inspection tracking before closeout. The approved repair's limit must remain actionable after the contractor leaves.

## R08 — Cr-Mo repair: identify condition as well as grade

A high-temperature component has a known nominal alloy grade and long service history. A repair proposal selects filler based only on the grade name.

Evaluate whether service degradation, prior repairs or heat-treatment history changes the repair basis. Obtain the specialist's material-condition assessment and applicable procedure. Verify consumable identity and handling, prescribed temperature controls, treatment records and required examinations. A newly purchased matching piece does not describe the condition of the aged surrounding metal. Do not invent hardness or heat-treatment targets from this example; the authorized procedure supplies them.

**Practice:** the completed heat-treatment chart has correct temperatures but the thermocouple map belongs to another component. **Answer:** identity/coverage is unestablished. Preserve the chart and obtain reliable reconstruction or required corrective work; do not relabel it based on a convenient match.

## R09 — Hot tap review: the new connection changes the system

A proposed hot tap includes a fitting and valve rated for the planned pressure, but no documented review of process contents, actual wall, cutting arrangement or emergency isolation.

Component ratings are necessary inputs, not a complete hot-tap plan. Verify the authorized engineering/procedure package addresses the host equipment, contents and operating envelope, attachment, examination/testing, tooling clearances, execution responsibilities and credible abnormal conditions. The inspector's role is defined in that plan. Do not improvise execution steps or begin work merely because the valve rating looks adequate.

**Practice:** a later drawing adds a nozzle load from attached piping. **Answer:** reconcile that load with the host-equipment and attachment design before accepting the change. The original pressure rating alone does not address the added load.

**Original setup-review exercise.** The training drawing identifies a 60 mm cutter and a required unobstructed path through the fitting and valve. The installed valve's documented clear opening is 55 mm. All pressure ratings agree. The setup is nevertheless inconsistent with its supplied geometry: the cutter cannot be assumed to traverse a smaller opening. Record the dimensional conflict and require an approved corrected arrangement before execution. Do not solve it by improvising a smaller cutter, because that would change the authorized connection and cutting plan. In the changed case, the opening is adequate but the coupon-retention arrangement is undocumented; the geometry check does not close that independent execution requirement.

## R10 — Excavation: record the end condition, not just grinding time

A surface indication is excavated until it is no longer visible to the grinder. The repair log records thirty minutes of grinding but no qualified examination or remaining-geometry measurement.

Elapsed work time is not evidence of complete flaw removal. Use the approved examination to verify the excavation and measure the resulting geometry for the applicable disposition. Determine whether the remaining section can be accepted, needs welding, or requires another design. Preserve the original indication map and the post-excavation record so a reviewer can connect them. Prevent contamination of materials through inappropriate tooling or consumables.

**Practice:** examination establishes removal, but the excavation exceeds the approved depth. **Answer:** credit flaw-removal evidence while holding the dimensional/design disposition. The original approval cannot silently be extended to a deeper excavation.

**Original remaining-section exercise.** A fictional authorized assessment requires 8.0 mm after reserving 0.8 mm future allowance. Excavation leaves 8.6 mm at the minimum. Available net thickness is 7.8 mm, not 8.6 mm, so the supplied criterion is unmet. Obtain a revised authorized repair or assessment rather than releasing the area because the flaw is gone. The changed case leaves 9.0 mm but has an abrupt unapproved contour: the arithmetic passes while geometry remains unresolved. The assessor requires both decisions and a marked section sketch.

**Removal-process selection exercise.** A fictional drawing requires a dimensionally controlled excavation with a defined final profile. The proposed work instruction names only “polish until clear.” Identify whether the method can remove the mapped defect while maintaining geometry, surface condition and the required remaining section. Specify intermediate measurement and examination hold points in the review, not a field tool-operating recipe. An attractive finish cannot prove that a subsurface crack is gone. In the changed case, a thermal removal method changes the surface condition; the plan must address its effects before final examination or depositing repair metal. Preserve before/after maps and the authorized method's acceptance evidence.

## R11 — Flange repair: a smooth face is not the only requirement

A machined sealing face looks clean. The machine shop reports material removal but no final dimensional sheet or surface-finish result.

Compare the proposed and actual removal with the applicable design, sealing geometry and tolerances. Identify whether thickness, facing configuration, alignment, bolt geometry or other relevant dimensions changed. Confirm that the specified gasket and joint arrangement remain supported. Surface appearance alone does not demonstrate the required finish or remaining strength. Any conversion requires its own approved basis rather than a cosmetic work description.

**Practice:** dimensions pass, but the replacement gasket differs from the approved assembly specification. **Answer:** resolve the gasket/joint compatibility and assembly requirements separately. Do not assume a same-size gasket has the same function.

**Original finish-record exercise.** The fictional gasket assembly specification supplies separate finish and flatness limits. The shop records a compliant finish reading but leaves flatness blank. Do not convert the single measurement into a complete face acceptance. Identify the missing measurement, the actual seating surface and the approved assembly criteria. In the changed case, both measurements pass but a deeper refacing cut reduces an evaluated structural dimension below its approved minimum. Keep sealing-surface acceptance and structural disposition separate.

## R12 — Exchanger repair: pressure integrity and thermal duty both matter

An exchanger has 200 tubes. Twelve have been plugged in previous repairs and another eight are proposed for plugging. The operator asks whether ten percent plugged means automatic acceptance.

The arithmetic is 20/200 = 10%, but that percentage is not a universal repair limit. Review tube locations, damage distribution, pressure separation, plugging design, flow/thermal effects and the approved performance assessment. Retain a tube map identifying every plug and the evidence for the current repair. Different chambers and load cases must remain distinguished. A leak between fluids can matter even when no external leak is visible.

**Practice:** a tube is replaced rather than plugged, and the individual tube is sound but its tubesheet joint leaks during the approved test. **Answer:** the boundary joint remains deficient; tube material acceptance does not close it. Retain the test finding and obtain the applicable joint repair/retest. PCC-2 Article 312's scope is shell-and-tube equipment, not every exchanger geometry; verify applicability before selecting it.

**Inspection-plan comparison.** A fictional exchanger plan requires 25% of its 240 tubes, including specified high-risk zones. The first report covers 60 tubes, all near the easiest access opening, and none in a required zone. The count is correct but the distribution is wrong. Retain the tube map and identify missing planned locations; do not exchange difficult locations for convenient ones without an approved technical revision. If a revised sample reveals additional damage, reconsider the extent and mechanism rather than extrapolating a clean inaccessible region. PCC-2 Appendix 312-I distinguishes inspection count and planning considerations; the fictional plan here is supplied teaching data, not a universal percentage.

## R13 — Pressure/tightness testing: define the question and limits

A signed test sheet states “250 psi passed,” without equipment identity, medium, reference elevation, temperature, instrumentation, duration or observations.

The number cannot be interpreted without its basis. A reviewer needs the approved test plan, actual setup and traceable results, including the specific boundary and acceptance criteria. A structural pressure test and a tightness check may answer different questions. Consider local pressure from liquid head, support loading, trapped pressure, protection of excluded components and safe depressurization through the authorized plan. Training calculations do not authorize pressurizing an actual vessel.

**Practice:** the missing fields are recovered, but the gauge calibration was invalid at the test date. **Answer:** evaluate the reliability of the result through authorized metrology and engineering disposition; do not automatically credit a later calibration certificate as proof of the earlier measurement.

**Stored-energy input review.** A fictional specialist-approved calculation uses 2 cubic metres of enclosed gas at an identified absolute pressure. The setup crew connects an additional 0.5 cubic metre reservoir. At unchanged gas, pressure and temperature assumptions, an expression linear in enclosed volume predicts a 25% increase in stored energy. The original exclusion-area review cannot simply be stamped unchanged. Reconcile the whole connected boundary and have the authorized specialist reassess protection, blast and fragment consequences. A passing pressure gauge calibration does not address this configuration change.

**Changed case.** The calculation requires absolute pressure but its input sheet contains gauge pressure without the atmospheric-pressure basis. Correct the input convention through the controlled calculation. Do not perform a physical demonstration to discover whether the original exclusion area was adequate. Submit boundary sketch, units, pressure convention, changed input, required re-evaluation and the hold decision. PCC-2 Article 501 and its appendices separate test preparation, stored energy and consequence considerations; these tabletop cases do not prescribe a safe field distance.

## R14 — NDE instead of pressure testing: justify the substitution

The repair team proposes substituting UT because filling the vessel would delay startup. The proposal does not specify technique, coverage, acceptance or approval.

Establish whether the applicable in-service and jurisdictional requirements permit the alternative for this repair. The selected examinations must address the actual potential defects and relevant boundaries. Define procedures, personnel, coverage, acceptance, records and any additional leak check or limitations. Obtain the required approval before treating the alternative as the accepted test route. Schedule pressure is not itself technical proof that the substitution is suitable.

**Practice:** the alternative is approved and the required weld examination passes, but a closure was assembled afterward without the required verification. **Answer:** the repaired weld evidence does not automatically cover the later closure. Complete the specified assembly/boundary verification before final disposition.

**Examination matrix exercise.** The fictional repair has a surface-breaking concern, a possible embedded planar flaw and a mechanically assembled closure. Prepare three rows: suspected condition, evidence capable of addressing it, and unresolved limits. A surface-only report cannot establish the absence of an embedded flaw; a volumetric weld examination does not automatically demonstrate closure leak tightness. Require qualified procedure coverage and acceptance for each relevant question under the approved alternative. In the changed case, every required method is listed but a geometric blind zone covers the repair edge. The list of method names is complete, yet the examination evidence is not. Record and resolve that coverage limitation before release. Assess the learner's reasoned matrix, not mere recall of a method acronym.

## Individual assessment and instructor use

Use each worked situation in teaching. Present its changed practice independently and score: identify the unresolved technical issue, distinguish what existing evidence proves, and specify the correct next disposition. Each criterion earns 0–2. Pass every case at 4/6 or better, with no unsafe release, fabricated evidence or unsupported qualification. Require correction and a second changed condition where a critical error occurs. Record both attempts; do not replace the original failure with a clean score.

**Case response form:** learner____ case____ original evidence____ established facts____ unresolved condition____ applicable reference/approved plan____ disposition____ owner/due condition____ scores____ critical error____ feedback____ reassessment____ assessor/date____.

Allow six instructional hours for these cases, including the individual changed conditions. These hours supplement the chapter instruction and practical stations. No live repair, pressure test, hot tap or welding operation is performed as part of this casebook.

# VUC035 damage-mechanism application casebook

These original cases extend V03–V05. They teach how to turn a mechanism hypothesis into an examination and disposition plan. All equipment, readings and operating histories are fictional. A hypothesis is not a diagnosis. No case authorizes continued operation, entry or live examination. Use the applicable mechanism references identified in sources-and-coverage.md and a qualified materials/examination team for real equipment.

For each case, submit four items: the mechanism hypothesis and competing explanation; evidence needed to distinguish them; a coverage/method plan; and the disposition boundary. Score each item 0–2. Pass every case at 6/8 with no fabricated evidence, unsupported operating release or unsafe access. On failure, teach the missed distinction, change the stated condition and reassess. Retain both records.

## M01 — Atmospheric corrosion: follow retained moisture

An outdoor vessel has peeling coating above a support saddle and rust staining below it. An accessible shell spot measures 10.8 mm against an earlier 11.0 mm reading. The crew proposes coating the whole area and recording “minor external corrosion.”

The accessible reading describes its own location. It does not measure the concealed saddle interface where moisture can persist. Map the coating damage, water path and accessible metal condition; establish an approved way to examine the affected interface. Distinguish loss in the pressure boundary from deterioration of the support and evaluate both functions. A small measured shell loss cannot establish support adequacy.

**Worked decision:** preserve the observation before surface preparation, obtain the relevant coverage and separate shell and support findings in the report. Coating repairs should follow condition assessment; hiding the evidence is not an inspection disposition.

**Changed exercise:** the shell is sound, but a support attachment is cracked. **Explained response:** the atmospheric-corrosion hypothesis no longer answers the entire finding. Characterize the attachment and load-path condition through the proper engineering route. Do not transfer the shell's passing thickness result to the attachment.

## M02 — CUI: a window is not a vessel

Insulated vessel V22 has a damaged weather seal near a nozzle. Three opened windows show 12.1, 11.9 and 7.4 mm. The first two were deliberately selected where the jacket looked best; the third lies below the damaged seal. The work order proposes averaging the readings.

Water entry and retention can make the damage distribution nonuniform. Keep each result tied to its location and thickness basis. The arithmetic mean would conceal the thin area and the biased sampling. Expand the investigation according to the suspected wetting path, insulation condition and approved coverage plan. Distinguish screened regions from directly examined metal and document limitations of each method.

**Worked decision:** retain the 7.4 mm minimum, characterize its extent, determine its applicable required thickness and obtain disposition. Do not infer a corrosion rate from another window's historical reading.

**Changed exercise:** all three windows are sound, but the nozzle penetration remains inaccessible. **Explained response:** those windows do not eliminate concern at the unexamined water-entry location. Record the exclusion and required follow-up rather than reporting full coverage or no CUI everywhere.

## M03 — Boiler water and condensate: reconstruct chemistry history

A condensate vessel has localized pits near its normal liquid interface. Today's chemistry report is within the site's operating limits. Records show repeated shutdowns and a period when treatment monitoring was unavailable. The proposed conclusion is that corrosion is impossible because the current water sample passes.

Current chemistry cannot reconstruct past exposure. Recover available treatment, makeup, oxygen-ingress and shutdown records. Map the pit distribution and establish whether the measurement method captured pit bottoms. Compare the pattern with likely wetting and deposit locations; do not turn a plausible explanation into a confirmed mechanism without evidence.

**Worked decision:** document the missing history, obtain mechanism-appropriate examination and evaluate the actual minimum section. Correcting treatment may control future exposure, but does not restore lost wall.

**Changed exercise:** the pits are shallow but a crack-like indication is detected near a weld. **Explained response:** the pitting evaluation and crack characterization are separate. An adequate pitting thickness calculation cannot clear the linear indication. Record the new condition and route it for qualified assessment before any continued-service conclusion.

## M04 — HCl attack: separate bulk conditions from local condensation

A process vessel's bulk stream is described as dry. The overhead section has a colder region and a history of intermittent water introduction. Local loss is found near that region, while a warm lower shell location remains stable. The team wants to use the lower-shell rate everywhere.

Determine whether local water and corrosive species could have coexisted during operation or excursions. Recover temperature profiles and water/chemistry history. Match examination coverage to the suspected condensation region, connections and affected flow paths. A stable reading in a different environment is not a defensible rate for the damaged location.

**Worked decision:** establish local geometry, baseline and loss trend where possible; document uncertainty where history is absent. Have the responsible technical team evaluate chemistry control and examination follow-up together. Do not claim that insulation or temperature changes alone prove the remaining wall acceptable.

**Changed exercise:** the operating change removes the suspected future condensation condition. **Explained response:** that may change future exposure, but accumulated loss still requires assessment. Record both the operating-control change and the independent pressure-boundary disposition.

## M05 — Caustic corrosion: concentration can be local

A vessel handles a caustic-containing stream. General shell readings are stable, but metal loss appears under a deposit near a heated surface. A bulk sample is offered as proof that the local environment was benign.

The examination plan must address the actual location, deposits and thermal history. Preserve useful deposit evidence through the approved sampling process before cleaning destroys it. Determine whether the local condition differs from the bulk sample. Characterize the loss profile and remaining wall rather than assuming uniform corrosion.

**Worked decision:** report a mechanism hypothesis supported by location and history, identify the remaining analytical questions and assess the measured condition with qualified review. Do not label the mechanism confirmed solely because caustic exists somewhere in the process.

**Changed exercise:** the deposit is removed and no significant thinning is found, but surface examination reveals branching indications at a stressed weld. **Explained response:** consider a cracking mechanism and obtain suitable characterization. A metal-loss inspection and a cracking inspection answer different questions; satisfactory thickness does not close the new finding.

## M06 — Erosion and erosion-corrosion: measure the affected geometry

An inlet region has a narrow groove aligned with flow. Historical readings were taken 50 mm away on a smooth shell surface. The operator recently increased solids loading and flow. The groove's minimum measures 6.8 mm; nearby wall is 10.2 mm.

Map the groove, impingement area and local geometry. Verify the minimum using an examination capable of resolving it. Recover changes in flow, solids and operation, and distinguish mechanical wear from combined corrosion/wear where evidence permits. The rate at the nearby historical location does not establish the groove's rate.

**Worked decision:** evaluate the localized condition against the appropriate assessment basis and establish a defensible local baseline/follow-up plan. A proposed operating reduction requires its own verification and does not replace the current condition assessment.

**Changed exercise:** a spot reading increases after probe repositioning. **Explained response:** investigate coverage and geometry rather than concluding the vessel gained wall. Record the minimum valid reading and its location; do not select the largest repeat merely because it supports continued operation.

## M07 — Amine cracking: thickness is the wrong sole metric

A carbon-steel amine vessel has a stable thickness trend but linear indications adjacent to a weld. The file includes an old repair with incomplete heat-treatment records. Management requests an ordinary thinning-life calculation to close the inspection.

Establish the indication's character and extent with qualified examination. Recover the relevant service, stress and fabrication/repair history. Stable thickness is useful evidence about loss at measured points; it does not resolve a crack-sensitive mechanism. Do not infer that missing heat-treatment records prove no treatment occurred, or that a statement of treatment proves its actual coverage.

**Worked decision:** preserve indications and records, identify the material/repair-history uncertainty and refer the condition for the applicable crack assessment and repair decision. Keep the corrosion-life calculation separate from the unresolved cracking disposition.

**Changed exercise:** a later record verifies heat treatment of a different weld on the same vessel. **Explained response:** its identity limits its relevance. Do not extend its acceptance to the indicated weld without reliable linkage and technical justification.

## M08 — Caustic SCC: identify the stressed region

A caustic-service vessel has acceptable general wall thickness but several linear surface indications at a repair attachment. The proposal is to remove the attachment and polish the surface until no line is visible.

Preserve the indication map and determine the affected material and stress/thermal history. Qualified characterization must distinguish a surface mark from cracking and establish extent. Uncontrolled polishing can remove evidence without proving complete flaw removal or acceptable remaining geometry. The examination and repair plan should specify verification after any authorized removal.

**Worked decision:** hold the unsupported cosmetic disposition, obtain characterization and evaluate the mechanism and condition together. Thickness results cannot substitute for crack-sensitive evidence.

**Changed exercise:** approved removal eliminates the indications, but exceeds the permitted excavation depth. **Explained response:** flaw removal is established within the examination's capability; structural acceptance remains unresolved. Require the appropriate revised disposition instead of treating a clean surface result as a complete repair acceptance.

## M09 — Chloride SCC: look outside the process boundary

A stainless-steel vessel carries a process reported free of chlorides. Cracks are suspected externally beneath insulation near a repeated leak from an adjacent utility. The initial report dismisses chloride involvement based only on the internal process analysis.

The external environment is a separate exposure path. Recover insulation, wetting and temperature history; establish alloy identity and examine affected regions using suitable crack-sensitive methods. Do not assume that every crack in stainless steel is chloride SCC, but do not eliminate the mechanism by sampling the wrong environment.

**Worked decision:** distinguish the internal process evidence from external exposure evidence and identify what further characterization would discriminate between hypotheses. Track affected locations, access exclusions and the required engineering disposition.

**Changed exercise:** the utility leak is repaired before inspection. **Explained response:** removing the source of wetting does not remove existing cracks. The operating repair and the vessel-condition assessment require separate closure records.

## M10 — Brittle fracture: temperature and pressure must be considered together

A vessel operated successfully at a warm temperature for years. During an outage, a pressure test is proposed at a substantially colder metal temperature. Its minimum-temperature basis is missing, and the crew cites previous warm operation as proof of safety.

Recover material condition, thickness, construction and repair records, and the applicable toughness/test basis. Consider flaws, restraint and service-related changes in material condition. Neither past warm operation nor a nominal material label establishes suitability for a colder pressure event. Measure actual metal temperature where required rather than substituting a weather forecast.

**Worked decision:** hold the unsupported test condition and obtain the qualified temperature/pressure assessment and approved test plan. Use V14 and T01 to distinguish a component exemption calculation from the complete assembly decision.

**Changed exercise:** the proposed pressure is reduced but the temperature remains unsupported. **Explained response:** a pressure reduction is an input to the applicable assessment, not an automatic waiver. Document the actual permitted combination and its conditions before proceeding.

## M11 — Wet H2S: distinguish damage forms

A vessel in wet sour service shows a bulge-like feature and subsurface indications. A surface hardness survey is within the project's specified range. The initial conclusion is that all hydrogen-related damage has been excluded.

Blistering, HIC, SOHIC and SSC are not interchangeable labels. Describe the morphology and location before selecting a mechanism. Hardness evidence may address one susceptibility consideration but cannot rule out every internal damage form. Use qualified examination to establish distribution and connectivity and recover material, weld, stress and service information.

**Worked decision:** report what the hardness survey establishes and what it does not. Preserve the indication map and obtain mechanism-specific evaluation; do not flatten every observation into a uniform-thickness calculation.

**Changed exercise:** surface examination finds no relevant indications, but the internal indications remain. **Explained response:** the surface result is not a substitute for volumetric characterization. Avoid both unsupported rejection of all equipment and unsupported acceptance of the indicated region; define the additional evidence and responsible disposition.

## M12 — Sulfidation: the material class is not every component

A hot sulfur-service circuit includes several nominally similar components. Most locations show slow loss, but one replacement component has significantly faster thinning. Its material record is incomplete. The team proposes averaging it with the rest of the circuit.

Investigate actual material identity, local temperature, flow and exposure. Components grouped under one service label need not have identical susceptibility or history. Establish the fast-loss component's own condition and evaluate whether other replacements share the same uncertainty. Retain the individual reading and location.

**Worked decision:** combine material verification with targeted condition examination; neither alone answers every question. A composition result does not measure remaining wall, and a thickness reading does not establish why the component behaves differently. Review the broader inspection scope through the integrity program.

**Changed exercise:** PMI confirms the intended nominal alloy but the high loss remains. **Explained response:** do not discard the measured deterioration. Continue evaluating local conditions and measurement validity, and assess the actual minimum regardless of whether the initial material-substitution hypothesis survives.

## M13 — Naphthenic-acid corrosion: operating history matters

A crude-service vessel has localized loss in a high-flow region. Today's feed analysis is less aggressive than earlier batches. A report uses the current feed alone to dismiss the inspection finding.

Recover the relevant feed and temperature/flow history and establish the actual damage distribution. Chemistry indicators support susceptibility assessment but do not directly measure the accumulated condition. Compare locations with similar exposures rather than transferring a quiet-region trend to an impingement region.

**Worked decision:** retain the local loss evidence, identify the historical exposure uncertainty and obtain a mechanism-informed inspection/assessment plan. Separate the operating strategy for future control from the current equipment disposition.

**Changed exercise:** an operating change reduces the suspected exposure, but no post-change trend exists. **Explained response:** do not invent a zero corrosion rate. Establish monitoring and approved follow-up appropriate to the uncertainty while retaining the current-condition assessment.

## M14 — High-temperature H2/H2S corrosion: loss is not the same as HTHA

A hot hydrogen/sulfur-service exchanger has measured wall loss. A meeting note labels every hydrogen-related condition “HTHA,” although the examination was a conventional thickness survey and no internal damage characterization was performed.

Separate the observed thinning from the unproved mechanism label. Review actual material, temperature, hydrogen partial pressure, sulfur environment and history with qualified materials expertise. Select examination methods that address the plausible damage forms. A thickness measurement may establish loss but cannot establish the absence or presence of every metallurgical mechanism.

**Worked decision:** revise the report to state the measured condition, mechanism hypotheses and necessary discriminating evidence. Avoid making a precise diagnosis from a broad service description.

**Changed exercise:** the thickness loss is assessed as acceptable, but the separate HTHA susceptibility review is unresolved. **Explained response:** retain that unresolved issue. Acceptance under a thinning calculation does not close a different potential failure mechanism.

## M15 — HTHA: detection capability belongs in the decision

An older hot hydrogen-service exchanger has no reportable findings from an earlier routine thickness survey. Historical excursions are incompletely documented. The current review asks whether that survey proves no HTHA.

A test result must be interpreted within the method's demonstrated capability, coverage and condition at the time. Recover the procedure, examiner qualifications, locations, limitations and operating history. Obtain a specialist evaluation of susceptibility and appropriate examination. Do not equate “no findings reported” with “all relevant internal damage excluded.” The CSB Tesoro investigation supplies a real incident context for reviewing such assumptions; it does not make this fictional case a diagnosis.

**Worked decision:** document the specific limits of the prior evidence and the technical work required to resolve them. Lower current operating temperature does not erase damage that may already have accumulated.

**Changed exercise:** a newer qualified examination covers most of the exchanger but excludes an inaccessible region. **Explained response:** credit the actual examined volume and retain the exclusion. The final decision must address the unresolved region through the authorized integrity process; do not silently convert partial coverage to whole-equipment clearance.
