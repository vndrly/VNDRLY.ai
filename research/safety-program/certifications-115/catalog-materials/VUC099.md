# VNDRLY/U Helicopter Refuelling — Initial Training

VUC099. Original available-source specialist initial instruction and supervised practical package. Full OPITO specification unavailable. Actual aircraft, fuel-system and quality procedures govern all practical delivery. No external accreditation or independent workplace authorization is conferred.

## Q99-1 — Role, entry and controlled procedures

Helicopter refuelling is a specialist function. Verify prior helideck preparation, fitness and assigned responsibilities before practical training. Identify the aircraft operator, HLO, fuel operator, quality-control authority and authorized maintenance provider. The refueller must know who can accept fuel, authorize transfer, stop the task and release a quarantined system. A general HLO certificate does not establish all these skills.

Use current aircraft-specific, system-specific and fuel-quality procedures with the relevant safety data sheet. This original initial course teaches safe decisions and supervised practice; workplace competence assessment and employer authorization follow. Do not improvise a valve sequence, pressure limit or repair method from generic notes. If the required approved procedure is absent or inconsistent, hold the task and resolve the gap.

Worked and changed applications: A crew requests a rapid top-up but the operator cannot confirm the applicable procedure revision. Do not proceed on memory. Changed case: a routine inspection reveals a defect requiring invasive fuel-system work. Isolate/report through the authorized process rather than treating initial training as maintenance authorization.

## Q99-2 — Fuel identity, properties and exposure

Verify the required aviation turbine fuel grade and specification through approved documentation and aircraft/operator confirmation. Similar appearance does not prove identity or suitability. Fuel can present fire, vapor, skin/eye and environmental hazards; use the current SDS and actual risk controls. Never taste fuel, deliberately inhale vapor or use an open flame to identify it.

Understand why contamination, mixed product or uncertain traceability can endanger aircraft operation even when the fuel looks clear. Water, particulate matter and other contamination require defined checks and disposition. Keep containers and tools clean and compatible. Personal protection, ventilation, ignition control and spill arrangements must match the actual activity. A course cannot assign one universal exposure limit or fuel acceptance criterion to every installation.

Worked and changed applications: A container label says one grade while the release document identifies another. Quarantine and resolve identity; clear appearance does not overrule the discrepancy. Changed case: product identity is correct but the sample is suspect. Identity and quality are separate gates.

## Q99-3 — Receipt, transport and traceability

Before accepting a delivery, reconcile tank or consignment identity, product, batch, release documentation, seals, condition and required inspection evidence. Check for damage, leakage, missing labels or signs of interference. Record source and date so the fuel can be traced through storage and dispensing. A handwritten batch substitution without authorized evidence is not a correction.

Transport and lifting of fuel containers require separate competent arrangements and approved equipment; this course does not authorize crane or dangerous-goods transport duties. During receipt, preserve segregation and prevent uncertain product entering accepted stock. Apply the specified settling, sampling and release process before use. Do not shorten required controls because the next aircraft is waiting.

Worked and changed applications: Delivery tank T8 arrives with certificate for T9. Hold receipt/release and obtain authenticated reconciliation. Changed case: tank identity matches but seal integrity is uncertain. Keep the separate concern open and follow the quality authority’s process.

## Q99-4 — Storage, segregation and stock control

Maintain approved storage condition, identification, access control, drainage/containment and environmental protection. Keep accepted, quarantined and waste/reclaim material clearly separated. A valve label or screen selection must correspond to the actual intended source. Do not return suspect samples or mixed residues to usable stock without an explicitly approved recovery process.

Track receipts, dispensed volume, sampling, waste and closing stock on a consistent basis. Investigate discrepancies rather than editing totals to make the ledger balance. Temperature, measurement method and calibration can affect comparisons; record the basis and seek competent review. Stock arithmetic is a diagnostic tool, not proof of product quality.

Worked and changed applications: Opening 5,000 L plus receipt 2,000 L minus delivery 1,200 L and sampling/waste 20 L gives expected 5,780 L. If measured closing is 5,730 L, investigate a 50 L shortfall. Changed case: a receipt is entered twice; authenticate and correct with audit history, not a hidden balancing entry.

## Q99-5 — System layout and protective functions

Trace the actual system from supply through pumps, filtration, meters, controls and delivery hose to the aircraft interface. Identify isolation points, emergency stop, deadman or equivalent control, pressure protection, bonding provisions and sample points as applicable. Explain each component’s function and what information indicates a fault. Do not assume every installation uses the same filter technology or control arrangement.

Use the approved schematic and manufacturer instructions. A protective trip is a reason to stop and investigate, not bypass. Pressure and flow limits must match the aircraft and system; never set them from a generic classroom value. Maintenance that could affect fuel quality or integrity belongs to the authorized competent provider. The learner should demonstrate safe recognition and reporting rather than unauthorized disassembly.

Worked and changed applications: Differential pressure rises beyond the actual approved criterion. Stop/hold through the procedure and seek authorized investigation; do not bypass the filter. Changed case: a zero differential reading appears unexpectedly at established flow. Investigate the abnormal indication rather than assuming zero is always ideal.

## Q99-6 — Sampling, inspection and quality decisions

Sample at the required points and times using clean suitable equipment and the approved safe method. Identify sample source, batch, date/time and operator. Avoid contaminating the sample through dirty containers or poor handling. Perform the specified visual and other checks using current methods and valid equipment or test media. Appearance alone does not establish every required quality characteristic.

Understand the difference between a passing observation, an invalid test and a failed sample. Expired or mishandled test media cannot support release. Suspect results require quarantine/hold and authorized investigation, with traceable records. Do not repeatedly sample until one result looks favorable while discarding the failures. Retain and dispose of samples under the actual procedure, not by returning them casually to storage.

Worked and changed applications: A sample contains visible water or particles. Hold affected fuel and notify the quality authority rather than filtering the evidence out of the jar. Changed case: sample looks clear but required test media is expired. The check is incomplete; obtain a valid method before release.

## Q99-7 — Pre-use equipment checks and maintenance boundaries

Inspect hoses, couplings, caps, bonding equipment, controls and required emergency resources using the actual checklist. Check status of inspection, calibration and maintenance records. A damaged hose, leaking connection or unreliable control is not made acceptable by a short delivery quantity. Keep equipment clean and protected when not in use.

Distinguish routine authorized checks from invasive maintenance. Do not open pressurized equipment, replace safety-critical components or use unapproved lubricant/parts outside authorization. Safely isolate and report defects through the approved system. A repair must be verified and released by the appropriate authority before service. The practical station uses a training rig and defect cards, never a deliberately damaged live fuel system.

Worked and changed applications: A hose has a suspect bulge and the deadman does not return correctly on the approved pre-use test. Hold equipment and report both defects. Changed case: hose is replaced by authorized staff but functional verification remains incomplete. Replacement alone is not release to use.

## Q99-8 — Fuel quantity, units and confirmation

Confirm the requested fuel quantity and unit with the authorized crew, including whether it means an amount to add or a final total. Distinguish liters, gallons and mass; do not silently assume US versus Imperial gallons. Use approved density and temperature basis when mass-volume conversion is required. Aircraft fuel planning and final load acceptance remain with crew.

For classroom arithmetic, mass equals volume times density when units and conditions are consistent. At an explicitly supplied 0.800 kg/L, 600 kg corresponds to 750 L. This is not a universal fuel density. Confirm calculations and meter units before transfer and record actual delivered quantity. If the request changes, repeat the required confirmation rather than relying on an earlier number.

Worked and changed applications: Crew requests 800 kg but meter is in liters; supplied approved exercise density is 0.800 kg/L, giving 1,000 L. Changed case: instruction says bring total to 800 kg, with 300 kg already aboard; the addition is 500 kg or 625 L on the same exercise basis, subject to crew confirmation.

## Q99-9 — Bonding, ignition control and safe setup

Apply the actual approved bonding and connection sequence before opening or transferring fuel, and maintain required protection through disconnection. Bonding and grounding are related controls but are not interchangeable terms; a ground connection alone does not prove the required aircraft-to-system bond. Verify equipment condition and any required continuity test using the approved method and limits.

Control ignition sources, access, hose routing and emergency escape. Position equipment according to aircraft and site procedures so it does not create rotor, exhaust, trip or collision hazards. Do not create sparks as a demonstration. A missing approved bonding point or failed check is a hold, not permission to clip onto a convenient painted surface. Instructor-led practice uses an inert interface or approved rig before any supervised fuel handling.

Worked and changed applications: A bond lead looks intact but its required test is failed. Hold setup until corrected and verified. Changed case: the ground connection is present but the required aircraft bond is absent. Do not treat the ground as a substitute.

## Q99-10 — Crew/HLO coordination and operating modes

Confirm aircraft identity, fuel request, product, required quality release, communication signals and permission to begin through the approved crew/HLO interface. Establish who operates controls, monitors the aircraft connection and can stop transfer. Maintain the required view or communication throughout. Do not begin because the hose is connected if authorization has not been given.

Engine/rotor-running or passengers-on-board operations require specific approved arrangements and competent personnel; they are not a routine shortcut authorized by this course. Passenger embarkation/disembarkation must not be improvised concurrently with refuelling. Follow the applicable aircraft manual and operator controls. If required communication is lost or a hazard develops, stop through the approved process and notify.

Worked and changed applications: The crew request is confirmed but HLO reports passenger movement is beginning. Do not combine tasks to save time; restore the approved safe arrangement. Changed case: a radio fails during transfer. Apply the stop/fallback procedure, not an assumption that silence means continue.

## Q99-11 — Transfer monitoring and abnormal stop

Start and control transfer only through the actual approved sequence and authorization. Monitor quantity, flow/pressure indications, connection, leaks and communication as required. Keep required controls attended; never wedge or bypass a deadman. Stay alert to a changed request or stop signal and confirm it clearly. A smooth flow does not prove every quality or safety gate was met.

On leakage, abnormal indication, loss of communication or other stop condition, use the approved stop/emergency process, protect people and notify responsible personnel. Do not disconnect pressurized equipment or improvise repair. Restart requires the specified cause resolution, verification and renewed authorization. Do not reset a trip repeatedly until it stays running.

Worked and changed applications: A leak card is introduced during the training transfer. Stop, communicate and apply the controlled response; do not tighten an energized connection. Changed case: the leak is reported fixed but the responsible release is missing. Keep the restart hold until required evidence and authority are established.

## Q99-12 — Post-transfer checks and records

Complete the approved shutdown, disconnection, bonding removal, cap security, equipment stowage and final area-clear checks in the actual required order. Confirm no hose, lead or tool remains connected or in a hazardous location. Carry out required post-transfer sampling and quality checks and obtain crew acknowledgment of actual delivery through the procedure.

Record aircraft, date/time, product/batch, meter start/end, delivered quantity and units, quality/sample references, operator and acceptance. Preserve corrections and abnormal events. A meter difference does not by itself prove what entered the aircraft if there was a leak or other discrepancy; investigate before reporting certainty. Restore system readiness and identify remaining defects for handover.

Worked and changed applications: Meter starts 12,450 L and ends 13,200 L: indicated delivery 750 L. Record units and reconcile with request and event history. Changed case: a spill occurred; do not assert all 750 L reached the aircraft without the required investigation and crew reconciliation.

## Q99-13 — Spill, fire, misfuelling and environmental response

Know the actual emergency stop, alarm, exclusion and spill-response plan before work. Protect people, stop transfer when safe through approved controls and notify HLO/crew and responsible responders. Do not wash fuel into drains, create ignition sources or attempt firefighting beyond training. First aid and medical referral follow actual exposure guidance and competent direction.

Suspected wrong fuel or contaminated delivery requires immediate notification and prevention of further use through the authorized aircraft/operator process. Do not conceal the issue, dilute it away or assume a small amount is harmless. Preserve batch, sample, meter and event evidence. Environmental containment, cleanup and waste handling require appropriate resources and authority; course exercises use paper injects and inert training materials.

Worked and changed applications: After transfer, paperwork reveals a product-identity mismatch. Notify immediately and preserve evidence; do not let embarrassment delay the aircraft safety response. Changed case: a spill is contained but waste disposal is unresolved. Keep it controlled under the approved process, not returned to usable fuel.

## Q99-14 — Integrated initial performance and workplace progression

The learner must complete receipt/quality reasoning, pre-use checks, quantity confirmation, safe setup, supervised rig operation, abnormal stop, post-checks and records. Each gate remains independent: correct arithmetic does not compensate for wrong product, failed quality or missing bonding. The instructor records individual performance and prompting rather than relying on group attendance.

This original initial-training outcome requires supervised practical demonstration on an approved training system, including valid quality-control practice where safely provided. A tabletop-only result is recorded as theory/simulation, not full practical completion. Workplace competence and aircraft-specific authorization follow through the employer’s process. Remediate critical gaps and use changed independent assessment; do not issue an external qualification or claim actual aircraft capability from course authorship.

Worked and changed applications: Learner calculates quantity correctly but bypasses a control. Stop and withhold practical completion pending remediation and changed performance. Changed case: learner correctly refuses transfer because quality release is missing. Credit the hold and assess the remaining skills; turnaround speed does not override release gates.

## Facility, personnel and procedure gates

Instructor must be competent in offshore helicopter fuelling, fuel quality and the specific training system. An independent safety supervisor controls practical activity. Verify relevant helideck foundation, fitness, assigned functions and applicable specialist permissions. Use a professionally controlled training facility with an approved risk assessment, emergency/spill arrangements, suitable PPE, ventilation, ignition controls, compatible equipment and authorized waste disposal. Do not run improvised fuel exercises on an office desk or live helideck.

These are controlled equipment-specific inputs, not missing universal numbers to invent. If a required field cannot be established, do not perform dependent practical work.

Use a dedicated approved rig or inert aircraft interface. Manufacturer-approved nonflammable training medium may be used only in equipment explicitly designed/authorized for it; never introduce water or other substitute into an operational aviation fuel system. Actual fuel sampling/quality practice requires the competent facility's approved handling controls and valid methods. If only paper or visual sample images are available, record theory/simulation rather than full practical completion. No live-aircraft or rotor-running transfer is required by these original exercises, and none is authorized by the course text.

No deliberate leak, wrong-fuel delivery, damaged pressurized hose, control bypass, fire or vapor exposure is created. Faults are controller cards or safe simulator states. Do not open energized equipment. Emergency practice uses safe inert props and isolated communications. A real unsafe condition stops the exercise. Learners practice before independent assessment, with individualized accommodations that preserve assessed competence.

## Assessment trace

Fourteen chapters each have two explained checks, one observed station, one protected three-decision final case and one changed retake. Seven labs cover receipt/scope, stock, system/quality, equipment/setup, quantity, transfer/stop and post-checks. I99 requires both changed supervised practical cycles. Nine stations involve safe physical training tasks and five calculation/review. F99 forms capture actual equipment/procedure/medium, quality evidence, release authority, delivery, stock, abnormal events and remediation. Controlled keys remain outside learner materials.

## Delivery inputs and limitations

The competent facility must verify actual manufacturer manual, aircraft procedure, fuel specification/SDS, quality methods/media, operating limits, bonding checks, sample handling, maintenance scope and emergency/environmental controls. Those inputs vary by system; synthetic density and quantities are not universal operating data. Approved substitute medium belongs only in dedicated compatible training equipment, never an operational fuel system. Paper-only work is not full practical completion. No actual learner, aircraft refuelling or hazardous event is claimed by authoring. Review material procedure/equipment changes before reuse and retain earlier evidence and limitations.
