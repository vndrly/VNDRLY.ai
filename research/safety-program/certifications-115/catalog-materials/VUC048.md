# VNDRLY/U Advanced Coating Inspection Preparation

VUC048 · Original available-source materials · 2026-11-18

## Scope and foundation

This program develops inspection planning, verification of junior inspectors' work, specialized substrate/system assessment and technically defensible reports. It includes the reviewed24-chapter basic foundation and16 advanced chapters below. The foundation's S47 cases remain fictional; advanced S48 cases add new requirements without rewriting the original examples. Award rules are in this program's delivery guide and supersede foundation award wording.

AMPP's current public Level2 guide adds specialized inspection and reduced-supervision responsibilities subject to project risk, with its own prerequisites and examinations.[A01,A02] VNDRLY preparation does not confer that credential or authorize a learner to claim experience not actually earned. No complete proprietary-code equivalence is asserted. Actual practical work is required for this preparation award; hazardous production operations remain outside course authorization.

## Q25 — Build an executable inspection plan

An advanced inspector translates the approved specification into steps that people can perform and verify. For each stage identify the asset/zone, requirement and revision, method, sampling, equipment, timing, responsible person, hold/witness status and record. A list of tests without sequence can allow an irreversible operation to hide required evidence.

Start with scope and service, identify differing substrates and systems, then map preparation, application, cure, inspection and repair. Review whether access makes the sampling executable. Allocate effort to actual consequences and uncertainty rather than only production area. A small field joint may deserve more attention than a large accessible shop-coated surface.

Separate normative requirements from guidance, contract additions and manufacturer constraints. A newer document is not automatically the contract edition; a contract edition does not authorize violating applicable law. Resolve conflicts with the responsible authority before work. Quantify the cost of inspection honestly, including rework avoidance, without manufacturing a numerical safety benefit.

**Worked:** a lining holiday test is scheduled after the vessel is closed with no access plan. Move the hold to an accessible stage under an approved revision. **Changed:** access exists but cure will not be complete; revise timing without waiving cure.

## Q26 — Verify personnel work and data quality

Review a junior inspector's report against raw readings, locations, instrument checks, actual scope and decision rules. Verification is not copying a signature onto an attractive summary. Select records across time, zones, operators and adverse conditions, and follow anomalies to their source. Identical repeated numbers can be legitimate, but require investigation rather than automatic fraud allegations or unquestioned acceptance.

Distinguish arithmetic errors, transcription errors, invalid measurements and missing work. Each has a different correction. A transcription error can be corrected from retained raw evidence; a missing measurement cannot. A failed post-check may affect an interval rather than only the last reading. Track these boundaries explicitly.

Give feedback on the task and evidence. Preserve original records, correction reason and responsible reviewer. If the junior inspector's task exceeds demonstrated competence, arrange supervision or reassignment; the advanced learner's preparation certificate alone does not establish employer supervisory appointment.

**Worked:** six reports have four missing checks and three wrong IDs, with two reports in both groups. Five unique reports are affected, not seven. **Changed:** a seventh record has a missing zone; revise the affected set using identities, not a guessed percentage.

## Q27 — Corrosion, cathodic protection and coatings

Coatings and cathodic protection can complement each other, but neither makes the other's condition irrelevant. Galvanic systems use the potential of a suitable sacrificial material; impressed-current systems use an external power source. Their design, adjustment and verification require appropriate competence. A coating inspector should record relevant coating/CP interfaces and refer electrical criteria to authorized specialists.[A03]

Disbonded coating may create a shielded or chemically altered region. Cathodic reactions can contribute to disbondment under some conditions. A laboratory disbondment result describes a defined test: substrate, preparation, film, defect, solution, temperature, electrical conditions and duration. Comparing results obtained under different conditions as a simple ranking can be misleading.

Do not convert a laboratory radius into a universal years-of-service forecast. Identify the actual specification and test basis, including the defined measurement origin. General, crevice and flow-related corrosion also require material and exposure evidence; a coating photograph cannot establish a unique mechanism.

**Worked:** one specimen has5mm disbondment after7days, another8mm after28days. The numbers alone do not establish which system is superior. **Changed:** conditions and duration match; comparison becomes more meaningful but still does not predict every field condition.

## Q28 — Environmental control and logger evidence

Enclosures, ventilation, filtration and dehumidification create a managed environment only when actual conditions are maintained. Refrigeration removes moisture by cooling and condensation; desiccant systems adsorb moisture and require regeneration. Heating can lower relative humidity without removing water, so it is not equivalent to dehumidification. Equipment selection and safe ventilation must suit the process.[A04]

Place sensors to represent critical surfaces and regions, not just the supply-air outlet. Validate time settings, interval, sensor identity and data retention. A logger's missing interval is not a passing interval. Alarms need a defined response and affected-work assessment. A power interruption may compromise both environment and records.

Evaluate substrate temperature, dew point and product constraints throughout the required period. Record enclosure opening, ventilation changes and sensor relocation. A graph can look smooth because software interpolated a gap; raw timestamps establish whether observations exist.

**Worked:**120 hourly records expected,108 present. Data availability is90%, not proof of90% compliant cure. **Changed:** all120 records exist but the probe was beside a heater; representativeness remains unresolved.

## Q29 — Waterjetting, centrifugal blasting and media control

Waterjetting can remove contamination and deteriorated coating and expose existing texture; the achieved condition and flash rust must be evaluated under the specified method. It does not automatically reproduce abrasive-blast profile. Inspect water quality, access, recontamination and time between preparation and coating. High-pressure equipment requires trained operators and safe controls; the course does not authorize its operation.[A05]

Centrifugal equipment throws abrasive mechanically. Media mix, contamination, wear, component geometry and travel affect coverage and profile. Recycled abrasive can change over time. Plan checks for the actual supply/process and do not accept a clean new bag as evidence for all recycled media. Blast-media standards for mineral/slag, recycled ferrous or metallic media have different scopes; identify the applicable specification rather than treating their names as interchangeable.

Remote equipment reduces some exposure but introduces visibility, control and exclusion-zone concerns. Inspect output at difficult geometry, not just the center of a flat panel. Do not reach into running machinery or defeat an interlock to observe it.

**Worked:** a waterjetted surface is visually clean but has insufficient existing profile for the specified system. Hold for an approved solution. **Changed:** profile is adequate but flash rust exceeds the specified condition; that separate issue still controls.

## Q30 — Concrete and substrate-specific preparation

Concrete coating work requires attention to surface strength, laitance, contamination, profile, moisture, joints, cracks and service exposure. Concrete age alone does not establish dryness or suitability. A surface moisture screen and a specified in-situ test may measure different things; do not convert one to the other's acceptance value. Use the actual product/system requirements.[A06]

Compare prepared texture with the specified concrete reference profile, while recognizing that texture does not prove soundness or absence of contamination. Record the method and locations. Address movement joints and cracks through the approved design; coating over them is not a universal repair. Acid cleaning, pickling or passivation on other substrates can leave residues and hazards requiring their own controlled process and verification.

Wood and polymers introduce moisture, porosity, flexibility and solvent-compatibility issues. Nonferrous alloys and galvanized surfaces require compatible preparation. The advanced inspector recognizes these differences and applies approved instructions, not a steel-only recipe to every surface.

**Worked:** a28-day slab has a failing specified moisture result. Calendar age does not override it. **Changed:** moisture passes but surface strength is poor; that separate preparation issue remains.

## Q31 — Plural-component and specialized application

Plural-component equipment meters and combines reactive materials. Correct ratio, temperature, pressure balance, mixing and usable residence time are all relevant. An indicated setpoint is not the same as verified delivered proportion. Obtain actual ratio-check records, alarms and product instructions; electronic systems can monitor defined variables but do not prove every downstream condition.[A07]

When an off-ratio or purge problem occurs, identify the material applied since the last established good condition. Do not assume a restart cures material already on the asset. Keep component identity and batch traceability through changes. Equipment maintenance and depressurization belong to trained authorized operators.

Electrostatic, thermal, flow and centrifugal application have distinct controls. Powder may require substrate or oven-temperature/time evidence; air temperature alone may not represent part temperature. Thermal spray uses a different deposition process from liquid paint. The inspector records the approved process evidence rather than inferring equivalence from film appearance.

**Worked:** delivered volumes3.8L and1L differ from a required4:1 ratio. Under a fictional ratio tolerance±2%, the5% low ratio fails. **Changed:** ratio is4:1 but mixer condition is unverified; correct proportion alone does not prove adequate mixing.

## Q32 — Pipeline systems and field joints

Pipeline coatings protect surfaces through handling, installation and service. Factory coating, field joints and repairs form one system but may use different processes. Fusion-bonded epoxy, multilayer polyolefin, tapes, shrink systems and liquid repairs have product-specific preparation, application and inspection requirements.[A08] An approved factory system does not automatically qualify any field-joint material.

Inspect cutback preparation, overlap, contamination, application temperature, thickness, cure, continuity and handling damage according to the actual procedure. A repair must be compatible with the existing coating and intended service. Concrete weight coating is not automatically the corrosion barrier; identify the layer functions and inspect before later layers obscure them.

Legacy coal-tar/enamel/asphaltic systems require material identification and appropriate hazard controls before removal or heating. Do not burn an unknown coating to identify it. Internal flow coatings and external corrosion systems have different purposes. Coating inspection does not certify pipeline pressure integrity or weld acceptance.

**Worked:** a holiday-free field joint has undocumented overlap and cure. Continuity alone cannot release it. **Changed:** all joint records pass, but lowering-in damages the coating; the later damage requires mapped repair/retest.

## Q33 — Specialized linings, metallic and fire-protection systems

Thick rubber linings, sheet membranes and reinforced linings introduce seams, bonds, terminations and cure/installation details beyond ordinary paint DFT. Examine the approved joining method, surface preparation, adhesive/cure, overlap and discontinuity checks. A seam can fail while nearby thickness meets its requirement.

Galvanizing and thermal-spray metallic layers require their own acceptance rules and compatible subsequent coating. Appearance alone does not determine a zinc layer's function. Fire-protection coatings depend on system design, section geometry, reinforcement, thickness and service conditions; a generic coating-inspection pass does not establish a fire rating. Antifouling and other specialized coatings also require service-specific and environmental controls.

Treat the product system as an approved assembly. Changing one layer, mesh, adhesive or repair material may alter its qualification. Refer such changes rather than improvising. Inspection reports should identify the precise system and exclude unverified design/performance claims.

**Worked:** intumescent DFT passes but required mesh is undocumented. The film measurement does not prove system conformity. **Changed:** mesh is verified but a different topcoat is proposed; compatibility remains a separate approval.

## Q34 — Ultrasonic and nonferrous coating measurement

Ultrasonic coating gauges interpret reflected signals from interfaces. Coupling, material sound speed, layer contrast, substrate roughness and selected gates influence the result. A displayed number is not proof that the intended interface was selected. Verify using a suitable known reference and inspect signal behavior where the instrument provides it.[A09]

Some systems can resolve multiple layers only when material contrast and thickness support it. A blended interface may not produce a distinct echo. Do not force the number of layers to match the desired report. For nonferrous conductive substrates, suitable eddy-current instruments require correct mode and geometry/range verification; they do not measure every nonmetallic substrate.

In a simplified travel-time exercise thickness=velocity×round-trip time/2. This teaches sensitivity to the assumed velocity, not a replacement for an actual coating gauge's calibration method. If velocity is changed, identify affected results rather than silently recalculating favorable values.

**Worked:** assumed velocity2mm/µs and echo time.6µs gives.6mm. **Changed:** actual appropriate velocity1.8 yields.54mm, a.06mm difference; the wrong model biases thickness.

## Q35 — Destructive thickness and adhesion testing

Destructive tests require permission, safe specimen handling, defined method and repair. A cross-sectional cut can reveal layers but depends on cut geometry, optics and interpretation. A ragged cut or uncalibrated scale cannot support precise thickness. Preserve the location and actual image/reading.

For pull-off work, identify dolly size, adhesive preparation/cure, scoring requirements, loading apparatus and rate, alignment and failure mode. Force divided by area gives nominal stress, but a valid test also requires correct procedure. Report mixed failure locations with their proportions rather than only the largest number.[A10]

Cut/tape and pull-off methods answer different questions and have different rating systems. Do not translate a tape grade into MPa. Actual practical evaluation uses prepared benign panels and competent supervision; production destruction is not authorized by completing a classroom exercise.

**Worked:** force2000N on a20mm diameter dolly gives approximately6.366MPa. **Changed:** the same force on10mm gives25.465MPa; using the wrong diameter can quadruple the reported stress.

## Q36 — Hardness, cure and method-specific evidence

Hardness measures resistance to a defined indentation or marking action under particular conditions. Shore scales, pencil tests and other methods are not interchangeable numbers. Substrate support, film thickness, dwell, temperature and instrument condition affect results. Follow the specified scale and procedure, and record them with the value.[A11]

A hardness result may support a product-specific cure decision but does not universally prove full chemical cure, adhesion or service readiness. Solvent-rub methods likewise need the actual reagent, procedure and interpretation; they can damage the coating and create exposure. Use safe laboratory arrangements and authorized repair. Classroom recognition does not authorize uncontrolled solvent use.

Compare readings only when method and conditions match. If a specification requires ShoreD, a ShoreA result cannot be accepted merely because its number is larger. If the sample is too thin or unsupported for the method, a stable display is still not necessarily valid.

**Worked:** required scaleD≥70; recordA75. The requirement has not been demonstrated. **Changed:** validD72 meets that criterion, while a missing service-cure interval remains unresolved.

## Q37 — Coating surveys and maintenance decisions

A condition survey describes the current system, deterioration distribution, exposure and access limits to support maintenance planning. Divide the asset into meaningful zones, define observation and sampling methods, record defect types and estimate extent with a stated denominator. Do not describe inaccessible surface as sound.

A survey may support spot repair, overcoating, removal or further investigation, but system compatibility and substrate condition must be evaluated. Overcoating an unknown weak layer can transfer rather than solve the failure. Include hazardous-material screening and disposal constraints where relevant. Prioritize using consequences and evidence, not appearance alone.

Keep area estimates, measured readings and engineering judgments distinct. A large count of small blisters is different from the same count of large damaged regions. State the uncertainty and need for further testing before making an expensive intervention recommendation.

**Worked:**12m² damaged in an80m² inspected region is15% of inspected area; if the asset is100m²,20m² remains uninspected. **Changed:** the unseen region is in a more aggressive exposure; do not extrapolate the inspected percentage without support.

## Q38 — Laboratory evidence and failure investigation

An investigation starts with questions and preserved evidence, not a preferred culprit. Collect location, chronology, product/batches, preparation, application, environment, cure, service and repair history. Use authorized sampling with sample identity, orientation, storage and chain of custody. Do not contaminate a chemical sample with an undocumented cleaner.

Laboratory analysis can examine composition, cure, contamination or disbondment, but its conclusion depends on sampling and method. A sample from one failed patch may not represent the whole asset. Compare sound and failed regions when justified. Preserve alternative explanations until the evidence discriminates between them.

State findings, limitations and further work separately. A laboratory test conducted at one temperature or exposure duration is not automatically the contract's required test. Do not substitute an attractive report title for checking the actual conditions and acceptance basis.

**Worked:** two laboratory results use different exposure temperatures. A direct ranking is not justified without technical interpretation. **Changed:** identical conditions but missing sample identity still prevent reliable asset attribution.

## Q39 — Repair verification and electronic records

Repair is a controlled sequence: define affected extent, approve method, prepare, apply, cure and reinspect. Boundaries may extend beyond the visible defect; use the authorized plan. Link every repair to the original finding and actual new results. Avoid a closure list that contains only “done.”

Electronic records must preserve units, timestamps, instrument configuration, raw data and revision history. A spreadsheet formula copied to the wrong column can misclassify a whole batch. Validate import mappings with known records, retain original exports and investigate missing or duplicated locations. Access controls and backups protect records but do not prove the measurements occurred.

When a report is corrected, notify affected recipients if the decision changes. Do not leave an obsolete release circulating while a private spreadsheet shows a hold. Separate administrative completion from physical compliance.

**Worked:** repaired zonesR1–R4 are listed, but onlyR1–R3 have retests. Closure is incomplete. **Changed:**R4 retest exists under a duplicateR3 label; reconcile actual identity from evidence before closing.

## Q40 — Integrated technical judgment and leadership

Advanced inspection combines measurement, scope, communication and authority. Explain why work is held, what evidence is missing, who can decide, and how the affected area can be evaluated. Avoid both unsupported release and vague indefinite rejection. Use bounded statements that allow the next responsible person to act.

Review junior work fairly, distinguish error from misconduct and maintain independent technical judgment under schedule pressure. A new owner instruction must be incorporated through its proper authority and change process; it does not rewrite history. Protect the learner's and inspector's right to stop an unsafe task.

The final portfolio integrates foundation inspection with concrete, specialized application, destructive tests, survey and records. It must contain actual observed instrument evidence and supplied-case decisions. The preparation award records those demonstrated outcomes; external certification, production appointment and specialist operation remain separate.

**Worked:** a complete-looking dossier lacks destructive-test authorization and raw data. Return the specific gaps rather than signing the summary. **Changed:** authorization and valid results arrive; update the affected disposition while retaining any unrelated open item.

# Reviewed coating foundation

## Q01 — Inspection responsibility and the protection system

A coating is part of a system: substrate, preparation, primer, subsequent layers, application conditions and service exposure. An attractive topcoat can conceal contaminated steel or an incompatible underlying layer. Inspection therefore records the work while evidence is accessible, rather than relying only on the finished appearance.

Identify who applies the coating, who performs contractor quality control, who witnesses or audits, and who can authorize departures. The basic inspector reports against the assigned plan; the owner or designated authority makes decisions outside that delegation. Quality control checks the work. Quality assurance examines whether the system of controls is suitable and followed. Neither permits an inspector to invent acceptance criteria.

Begin each shift by confirming component identity, work stage, governing revisions and hold points. End by recording what remains uninspected. Do not mark an entire tank complete because accessible shell measurements passed.

**Worked:** the contractor requests signature for internal lining based on external paint readings. Decline the unsupported scope and identify the required internal inspection. **Changed:** internal inspection was performed, but on a different tank; traceability still prevents release.

## Q02 — Corrosion and what coatings can control

Electrochemical corrosion involves anodic metal loss, a cathodic reaction, an electrical path and an electrolyte. Conditions that alter wetness, oxygen access, chemistry or temperature can change attack. General thinning and localized attack can have very different consequences even when total metal loss is similar. A small pit is not harmless because the surrounding surface looks sound.

Coatings can isolate the surface from the environment; some systems also inhibit reactions or provide sacrificial protection. A defect can concentrate attack. Galvanic interaction requires attention to the connected materials, electrolyte and exposed areas; coating only one member without design review can change the risk. Mill scale is not interchangeable with a specified prepared substrate and can detach or create problematic local conditions.

Inspection cannot restore lost structural capacity. Record corrosion exposed during preparation and refer significant or unexpected metal loss to the responsible authority before coating hides it. Coating selection must consider actual immersion, chemical, temperature and atmospheric exposure.

**Worked:** localized deep pits become visible after cleaning. Record location and hold for assessment rather than filling them invisibly with paint. **Changed:** only discoloration is observed; document the observation without inventing a depth measurement or mechanism.

## Q03 — Safety before measurement

Blasting and coating work can expose inspectors to dust, hazardous old coatings, noise, moving abrasive, high pressure, solvents and restricted access. Review the task risk assessment and SDS before entering the work area. Determine the actual controls and boundaries, not just the PPE worn by nearby workers. Inspection does not authorize confined-space entry, respirator use without its required program, energized work or access beneath suspended loads.[S03]

Keep clear of blasting and spraying exclusion areas. Do not inspect an operating nozzle or use a hand to investigate an airless injection injury. A suspected injection injury requires urgent medical response under the site emergency plan even when the external wound appears small. Check access, lighting, ventilation and emergency communications again when the work location changes.

For this course, practical work uses clean prepared panels, safe water-based surrogate liquids and approved low-energy instruments. No live blasting, production spraying, solvent exposure or high-voltage holiday operation is required. A learner who stops an unsafe station has made the correct decision; the instructor fixes the setup before resuming.

**Worked:** a requested salt test is inside an unassessed tank. Hold outside and obtain the required safe-entry arrangements. **Changed:** the tank is open but adjacent spraying creates an exposure; access is still not automatically safe.

## Q04 — Documents, pre-job meeting and hold points

Read the coating specification together with the inspection and test plan, drawings, current product data/application sheets and SDS. The specification defines project requirements; product instructions constrain safe, effective use. Resolve conflicts through the authorized route before work. A purchase order saying “epoxy” does not define a coating system, preparation grade, thickness or service-cure requirement.

At the pre-job meeting identify zones, access, sequence, inspection frequency, notification lead times, hold/witness points, sample locations, repair controls and reporting contacts. A hold point requires its defined release before the next stage. A witness point follows the contract's actual notification and attendance provisions; it is not automatically equivalent to a hold.

Use a revision register. When an approved change arrives, identify affected work and instruments, brief the team and preserve the prior records. Do not quietly replace an old limit on yesterday's signed report. The practical S47 specification is a training document only and must never be issued as a production coating instruction.

**Worked:** blasting is finished, but the required preparation hold has not been released. Painting waits even if the applicator is ready. **Changed:** a witness did not attend; consult the actual witness clause instead of assuming permission or prohibition.

## Q05 — Measurement integrity and units

Calibration establishes an instrument's relationship to reference values under defined conditions. Verification checks whether its performance remains suitable. Adjustment changes its response. These are different actions. A calibration certificate does not eliminate field checks after damage, a probe change or changed measurement conditions. Use the actual manufacturer's method and a reference appropriate to the range and substrate.[S04]

Record instrument/probe ID, reference ID/value, before-and-after results, units and relevant configuration. If a post-check fails, identify the readings since the last valid check and evaluate them; simply adjusting the device does not repair historical evidence. Avoid repeated readings until a favorable value appears.

One mil is 25.4 micrometres. A thickness of 4 mil is 101.6 µm, not 4 mm. Preserve enough resolution to make the decision; do not round an out-of-limit result into acceptance. When an uncertainty interval crosses a specified decision boundary, use the governing decision rule or seek disposition rather than claiming certainty.

**Worked:** reference 100 µm reads 107 with allowed training error ±3. The error is +7, outside the check. **Changed:** it reads 102; the check passes, but it proves neither every location's thickness nor coating adhesion.

## Q06 — Environmental conditions

Measure air and substrate temperatures, relative humidity and dew point with suitable instruments. The substrate can differ from the surrounding air, especially in shade, at dawn or beside a heat source. Condensation risk depends on the substrate relative to dew point. Record the actual spread, Ts−Td, as well as the individual readings.[S05]

Use the project and product limits, including application and curing conditions. A familiar dew-point margin is not a universal permission to paint. Check the coldest relevant locations, not only the warm convenient plate. Recheck at the planned frequency and when weather, enclosure, ventilation or work location changes. Instrument stabilization and sensor cleanliness matter.

S47 requires a spread of at least 3°C and RH no more than 85%, plus its temperature range. At Ts 18°C and Td 16°C the spread is 2°C and application is held. A dry-looking surface does not override the failed requirement. Conditions acceptable during application do not prove acceptable conditions through cure.

**Changed:** Ts 22°C, Td 18°C and RH 88% gives a passing spread but failing humidity. Evaluate every required variable independently.

## Q07 — Precleaning and substrate defects

Examine substrate identity and condition before preparation. Oil, grease, salts, weld spatter, sharp edges, laminations and fabrication defects require different responses. Blasting alone is not a reliable substitute for specified degreasing; contamination can be spread or embedded. Use the approved cleaning process and control dirty rags, recycled liquid and subsequent handling.

Separate fabrication correction from cleaning. An inspector may identify a sharp edge needing treatment under the specification, but cannot authorize grinding away structurally significant material. Record weld and edge conditions before coating. Compare the condition to actual project documents rather than an imagined universal edge radius.

After cleaning, prevent recontamination from hands, oily compressed air, nearby work or weather. A previously accepted surface may require reinspection after exposure. Masking must protect required surfaces without concealing areas needing inspection.

**Worked:** a clean-looking panel has visible oily fingerprints. Return it for approved cleaning and reinspection. **Changed:** the fingerprints are gone but the solvent-wipe record is missing; obtain real evidence, not a backdated signature.

## Q08 — Preparation methods and visual cleanliness

Hand tools, power tools, abrasive blasting and water cleaning remove material in different ways. Method choice affects remaining contamination, profile and production hazards. Visual cleanliness describes the surface condition achieved; anchor profile describes texture. Neither automatically proves the other. Use the specified written standard and suitable visual reference for the actual starting condition; a photograph alone may not resolve all acceptance requirements.

Abrasives differ in hardness, shape, size and contamination. Recycled media require process control. Nozzle wear can change air demand and performance. Straight-bore and venturi arrangements influence the stream; the inspector observes equipment condition and the approved setup rather than redesigning compressor capacity on site. Moisture or oil in compressed air can spoil an otherwise adequate preparation process.

Water cleaning can expose an existing profile without creating a new abrasive profile. Flash rust may appear afterwards; allowable condition is system-specific. Stop and obtain the specified assessment rather than painting over it because work is behind schedule.

**Worked:** the visual grade is acceptable but measured profile is below the required range. Preparation is not fully accepted. **Changed:** profile passes but residual contamination remains; the result is still incomplete.

## Q09 — Surface profile measurement

Select an appropriate profile method for the surface and required parameter. Comparators, depth gauges and replica tape do not necessarily report identical quantities. Follow the instrument and consumable instructions, including range, preparation, seating and number of readings. Do not combine unlike measurements as if they were interchangeable.

Replica tape captures an impression. Correct interpretation accounts for its backing and instrument mode; some readers apply corrections automatically. Subtracting a backing twice produces a false low result. Select the actual tape grade and current range rather than a remembered range from an older product.[S06] Preserve the impression and location when required.

Spread readings across the defined inspection area and include difficult geometry. A single favorable profile does not represent a whole component. If readings fall outside the tape's valid range, change the method/grade as instructed rather than extrapolating.

**Worked:** in a fictional manual-reading exercise, total impression thickness is 112 µm and the supplied backing is 50 µm; profile is 62 µm. **Changed:** the electronic display already says corrected profile 62 µm; report 62, not 12. These exercises do not override a real reader's processing instructions.

## Q10 — Salt, dust and other contamination

Visual examination cannot establish that soluble salts are absent. Extraction testing measures the material recovered from a defined area using a defined solution and method. Control the blank, extraction volume, area, cleanliness, recovery and instrument units. Conductivity is not a species-specific chloride result unless the method validly supports that claim.[S07]

Compare with the project limit for the stated method and units. A result in mg/m² cannot be compared directly to µg/cm² without conversion: 1 µg/cm² equals 10 mg/m². A leaking patch or incorrect volume can invalidate the measurement. Do not average an invalid test into a passing batch.

Dust assessment uses its specified method and rating basis, distinct from a salt extraction. Oil detection also requires the prescribed method; a water-break observation is not a complete chemical analysis. Following remedial cleaning, retest affected areas under the plan and document the new result alongside the original failure.

**Worked:** S47 limit 20 mg/m², valid result 26: hold and remediate. **Changed:** result 18 but patch leaked; the numerical value cannot establish acceptance.

## Q11 — Coating families and adhesion

Coatings include binder, pigments and other constituents that produce the intended film; liquid products may contain solvents or water. Organic systems include familiar polymer binders; inorganic systems use a different binder chemistry. Generic names such as epoxy, polyurethane, alkyd, acrylic or zinc silicate describe families, not interchangeable approved products.

Adhesion involves surface interactions and mechanical effects. A rough surface is not sufficient if contamination prevents effective contact. A coating may be a barrier, inhibitive system or sacrificial zinc-rich system; the mechanism depends on the actual formulation and installation. Primer, intermediate and finish layers have different roles and compatibility constraints.

Curing may rely predominantly on physical loss of volatile material or chemical reaction, including reactions between components or with the environment. Dry-to-touch is not proof of service cure. Follow the product's actual temperature, humidity, thickness and time requirements. A coating suitable outdoors is not automatically suitable for immersion or high-temperature duty.

**Worked:** two products are both called epoxy but only one is approved for the specified lining service. Substitution needs authorized technical approval. **Changed:** the same trade name has a different curing agent; identity and compatibility still require checking.

## Q12 — Storage, mixing and thinning

Verify product, component, batch, shelf-life and storage history before mixing. Check the approved ratio and whether it is by mass or volume. Ratios cannot be exchanged without valid density information. Inspect for damaged containers or abnormal material and obtain disposition rather than guessing that stirring will restore it.

Record component amounts, mixing start, method, induction requirement, pot-life clock and actual temperature. Induction is a required waiting period when specified; pot life limits usable time after mixing under stated conditions. Adding thinner does not reset a reaction clock. Use only permitted thinner and limits. Manufacturer application guidance illustrates why product-specific instructions are essential.[S08]

For fictional S47 ratio 4:1 by volume, a 5 L unthinned mix uses 4 L base and 1 L curing component. Adding 0.25 L permitted thinner to 5 L at 60% volume solids gives 3 L solids in 5.25 L, or 57.14% applied volume solids, assuming the supplied simple volume-addition model.

**Changed:** a batch mixed at 09:00 with two-hour pot life cannot be used at 11:15 merely because it still flows.

## Q13 — Application controls and edge coverage

Brush, roller and spray produce different access and film-build patterns. Inspect the approved application method, operator records, material condition and environmental controls. Airless spray uses pressurized material; conventional and plural-component arrangements have different equipment controls. Observing a pump ratio does not establish correct atomization, proportioning or delivered film.

Edges, welds, bolts and recesses can receive inadequate coverage while broad surfaces look uniform. Where specified, stripe coating addresses these difficult geometries; record its sequence and condition before it is hidden. Avoid treating a stripe coat as permission to exceed maximum local thickness or ignore intercoat cleanliness.

Check application while correction is possible. Runs, dry spray and missed regions suggest a need to review technique or conditions. The inspector reports the evidence and nonconformance; changes to material, pressure or thinning follow the authorized process. Do not reach into operating equipment to demonstrate a defect.

**Worked:** flat-panel WFT passes but the specified edge stripe is omitted. Hold the next stage until the requirement is resolved. **Changed:** stripe is present but contaminated before overcoating; presence alone is not sufficient.

## Q14 — Wet film and material quantity

Wet-film measurement provides timely application feedback. Use a clean suitable comb or wheel on the uncured film following its instructions. With a comb, report the interval bounded by the last wetted and next dry tooth; a coarse interval is not an exact value. Repair the disturbed spot as required. Curvature, roughness and technique can affect interpretation.[S09]

The simple estimate DFT=WFT×applied volume-solids fraction assumes the specified product model and no unusual losses or changes. To target 150 µm dry at 60% solids, estimated WFT is 250 µm. Thinning changes the applied fraction. Actual final DFT still needs measurement; the estimate is not acceptance evidence.

For planning, theoretical spreading rate in m²/L is 1000×solids fraction/DFT in µm. At 0.60 and 150 µm it is 4 m²/L. An 80 m² area needs 20 L theoretical mixed coating; at 80% utilization it needs 25 L. These calculations do not authorize extra thinning or replace the actual application allowance.
