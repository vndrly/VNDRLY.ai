# VUC113 — Remote Pilot and Part 107 Preparation

Original VNDRLY/U ground instruction and supervised mission practice. FAA certification, recency and operational approvals remain separate.

## Q113-01 — Remote pilot authority and certification

The remote pilot in command is responsible for the operation and must be able to direct the aircraft safely. A business request, landowner permission or automated mission does not transfer that responsibility. Part 107 generally applies to civil small unmanned aircraft below 55 pounds including everything carried. Determine the actual operation and legal basis; recreational rules are not a shortcut for work flights. A remote pilot certificate and current aeronautical knowledge are separate from aircraft registration and any airspace authorization.

Initial certification uses the FAA's applicable knowledge-test/application pathway, with a separate qualifying Part 61 pathway. Minimum age is 16 and other eligibility requirements apply. Maintain required knowledge recency within 24 calendar months through the applicable FAA process. This original VNDRLY course does not issue an FAA certificate or replace FAA recurrent training.

Before accepting a mission, verify pilot documents, recency, aircraft registration/marking, Remote ID compliance, airspace/operational permissions and employer/site requirements. A person manipulating controls under direct supervision must be supervised as the rule requires; a remote phone call is not assumed sufficient. Worked decision: the client says an inspection is urgent but the pilot's recency is not current. Delay and restore eligibility or use a qualified current pilot; urgency is not an emergency exception.

## Q113-02 — Operating limits, right of way and waivers

Plan visual line of sight sufficient to know position, altitude, attitude, direction and hazards, using corrective lenses as permitted. A camera feed, telemetry or binoculars does not replace the required visual capability. A visual observer supports the crew but does not authorize an otherwise prohibited operation. Yield to crewed aircraft and never interfere with airport traffic. Maintain separation and land or reposition safely when needed.

Normal Part 107 limits include 100 mph/87 knots groundspeed, 400 feetAGL with the specified within 400-foot-radius/400-feet-above-structure allowance, 3 statute miles visibility from the control station, and cloud separation 500 feet below/2000 feet horizontally. These are legal limits, not recommended operating targets. Airspace authorization may impose a lower altitude. Do not interpret structure allowance as overriding controlled-airspace limits or other hazards.

Waivers authorize only specified deviations under their conditions. An airspace authorization is not a waiver for BVLOS or operations over people. Do not operate multiple aircraft simultaneously or from moving platforms except where the applicable rule/authorization permits. Hazardous material carriage and careless/reckless operation are not ordinary inspection options. Worked case: a tower permits a higher geometric envelope but the authorization caps 120 feetAGL. The authorization cap remains controlling; do not use the structure provision to exceed it.

## Q113-03 — Airspace, charts and authorization

Identify the site using current aeronautical information, not only a consumer map. Class B, C, D and the relevant surface Class E airspace require prior authorization forPart 107 operations; ClassG does not require that specific authorization but other restrictions still apply. Read sectional symbols, airspace floors/ceilings, airport information, obstacles and terrain. Chart altitudes may be MSL while operational limits are AGL; convert using the actual local elevation.

A chart shelf labelled 40/20 represents 4000/2000 feetMSL under the supplied chart convention. At ground elevation 1500 feetMSL, a proposed 300 feetAGL flight is 1800 feetMSL, below that shelf floor at that point; this alone does not clear other airspace or restrictions. Verify boundaries, terrain changes and current chart legend. UAS facility-map grid values support authorization planning; they are not permission by themselves. Use LAANC or FAA's applicable process and comply with time, area, altitude and conditions.

Check TFRs, NOTAMs, special-use airspace, security restrictions and site-specific constraints before and during the operation as appropriate. A geofence unlock is notFAA authorization; an app's green display is not a complete briefing. Worked decision: yesterday's authorization does not cover today's time window. Obtain valid permission or change the mission lawfully; do not backdate the flight log.

## Q113-04 — Weather products and local judgment

Obtain current aviation weather and assess the actual site. METARs report observations; TAFs forecast conditions at specified locations/times. Decode wind direction/speed/gusts, visibility, weather, cloud layers, temperature/dew point and altimeter setting. Reports may not describe a sheltered industrial site or wind at flight altitude. Compare observation time and trend, not just an icon.

Synthetic METAR: KZZZ 181755Z 18012G20KT 3SM BKN008 18/17 A2992. It indicates 18 th day 1755UTC, wind 180 degrees 12 knots gust 20, visibility 3 statute miles, broken layer 800 feet above station, temperature 18°C/dewpoint 17°C and altimeter 29.92 inchesHg. KZZZ is fictional. At a same-elevation site, 500-foot-below-cloud separation would cap flight at 300 feetAGL under that cloud, subject to all other restrictions. Meeting 3-mile visibility exactly leaves little margin.

Thunderstorms, gust fronts, icing, fog, precipitation and turbulence can exceed aircraft/pilot capability. Buildings and terrain create mechanical turbulence. Higher density altitude can reduce lift/performance; heat and cold affect batteries. Use aircraft limits and conservative mission margins. Worked decision: surface wind 12 knots is below a 15-knot limit but gust 20 exceeds it. No-go under that supplied limit. Do not average gusts away.

## Q113-05 — Loading, performance and energy

Confirm total takeoff mass, center of gravity, payload security and aerodynamic effects. A camera or propeller guard can change endurance, handling and category eligibility. Manufacturer limits are necessary but not a complete risk assessment. Check propellers, motors, structure, battery health, firmware/configuration and environmental compatibility. Do not install an unapproved payload merely because it fits.

Plan energy for launch, mission, return, contingency and landing reserve. In an original example, usable mission budget 18 minutes minus 3 outbound, 4 return and 3 reserve leaves 8 minutes for inspection. Wind may make return slower than outbound; tailwind outbound can hide a difficult headwind return. Time estimates are not a guarantee of battery performance. Use actual state/temperature/load trends and early abort triggers.

For a 1000 m return at 5 m/s ground speed, time is 200 seconds or 3.33 minutes. At 2 m/s it becomes 500 seconds or 8.33 minutes. A battery plan based only on still-air speed can fail badly. Do not deliberately exhaust a battery to validate a model. Land before limits with a suitable contingency area. Record battery condition and anomalies after flight; swelling, damage or abnormal heating requires removal from use and approved handling.

## Q113-06 — Crew resource management and fitness

Define remote PIC, control manipulator, visual observer and site coordinator roles. Brief flight area, altitudes, people/vehicle controls, weather, airspace conditions, communications, emergencies and authority to call an abort. Use clear concise calls and closed-loop acknowledgment. Observers must know what to watch and how to communicate immediately; they are not spectators.

Assess illness, medication, stress, alcohol, fatigue and emotion before flying. Apply applicable alcohol/drug rules and do not fly impaired even if a minimum time interval has elapsed. Distraction, client pressure and fixation on the screen can undermine see-and-avoid. Share workload without dilutingPIC responsibility. A crew member must be able to challenge unsafe continuation.

Worked brief: the observer calls 'helicopter approaching from west, low.' PIC acknowledges, maintains awareness and executes the prebriefed safe descent/landing or separation action. Do not continue the camera task until the aircraft disappears behind equipment. Record the interruption and reassess before relaunch. If communications with the observer fail, follow the planned safe response rather than assuming silence means clear airspace.

## Q113-07 — Preflight, control links and automation

Inspect aircraft condition, secure attachments, battery, propellers, controls and navigation sensors. Verify registration/Remote ID state, home point, altitude reference, return-to-home settings and geofence behavior. Check command/control link, telemetry and expected loss-link actions. A software update can change settings; do not discover that during an urgent job. Understand limitations of GNSS, compass and obstacle sensors near steel structures, power lines and reflective surfaces.

Plan launch/recovery and emergency landing areas clear of people and hazards. Confirm airspace/weather/permissions immediately before flight. Test controls safely and establish visual orientation. Automatic return-to-home may climb into an authorization ceiling or obstruction if configured poorly. Set a lawful safe strategy based on the actual site; automation is not an independent pilot.

Worked example: authorization ceiling 150 feetAGL, return-to-home configured 250 feet. Correct the plan/settings before launch and verify obstacle clearance within authorized limits; do not simply reduce altitude if that would create a collision route. If no lawful safe return path exists, change the mission or do not fly. Record preflight discrepancies and their resolution.

## Q113-08 — Night, people, vehicles and Remote ID

Night operation underPart 107 requires the applicable updated knowledge/training and anti-collision lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles with sufficient flash rate, unless an applicable authorization permits otherwise. Lighting does not replace visual line of sight, weather or airspace permission. Dark adaptation, visual illusions, obstacle visibility and depth judgment require additional planning. Do not extinguish required lighting for a better photograph.

Operations over nonparticipants require the applicable rule pathway, category-compliant aircraft and operating conditions or a valid waiver. Category 1 requires 0.55 pounds or less including attachments and no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate; open-air assemblies have additional Remote ID conditions. Categories 2/3 use required eligibility/compliance and operating restrictions; Category 4 uses an airworthiness-certificated aircraft and approved limits. A low mass alone does not prove every condition met. Moving-vehicle operations have additional restrictions; do not assume a brief transit or road closure automatically authorizes it.

Remote ID compliance can involve a standard Remote ID aircraft, a broadcast module under its restrictions, or operation within an authorized FRIA under the applicable conditions. Verify actual eligibility and operating state; registration alone is not Remote ID. If requiredRemote ID fails, follow the applicable rule and safe termination requirements. Worked case: a 249 g aircraft gains a 20 g accessory and exposed blades remain. Do not claim Category 1 eligibility from its original marketed weight.

## Q113-09 — Emergency response and reporting

Prepare for loss link, GNSS/compass anomalies, flyaway, low battery, propulsion failure, unexpected people, crewed traffic and weather deterioration. Define actions appropriate to the aircraft and site, including safe landing areas and emergency contacts. Do not chase a malfunction into traffic or a hazardous industrial area. Protect people first and avoid creating a second emergency.

The remote PIC may deviate fromPart 107 to the extent necessary for an in-flight emergency requiring immediate action; this is not advance permission to plan an illegal flight. Preserve facts, logs and settings after an event. DetermineFAA and NTSB reporting duties separately and follow employer/site reporting. UnderPart 107, the FAA reporting rule includes serious injury/loss of consciousness or qualifying damage to property other than the small unmanned aircraft, and a 10-calendar-day deadline; apply the actual rule's damage valuation conditions. Do not invent a damage amount to avoid reporting.

Worked scenario: link loss begins while a vehicle enters the landing area. Execute the preplanned safe contingency and communicate with the site coordinator; do not rely blindly on an automatic landing zone now occupied. After landing, quarantine the aircraft if necessary, document the sequence and investigate before return to service. An apparently undamaged aircraft can still have a latent fault.

## Q113-10 — Field inspection mission and data ethics

Plan the inspection objective and required image quality without allowing the camera task to override flight safety. Identify hazardous equipment, vents, flares, cranes, helipads, power lines, traffic and protected information. Obtain lawful site access and coordinate operations; landowner permission does not replace airspace authorization. Avoid flight paths whose failure trajectory threatens workers or critical equipment.

Use a mission sheet with route, altitude, visual coverage, people controls, weather limits, energy, emergency areas and abort triggers. Record what was actually inspected and any gaps. A photograph does not by itself establish structural integrity; refer technical interpretation to qualified personnel. Protect personal/confidential data and retain only authorized records. Do not use zoom to claim visual-line-of-sight compliance when the aircraft cannot be adequately seen.

Worked closeout: wind increases before the final tower face is photographed. Abort safely and report that face as not inspected. Reusing an old image without its date creates false evidence. The original VNDRLY training award records learning and observed practice; FAA certification, recency, aircraft compliance and mission permissions remain separate. No course-completion claim authorizes a real flight without those requirements.

## Flight-knowledge application supplement (Q113-03/04/06/09)

Airport awareness begins with runway orientation, traffic pattern and approach/departure paths. Runway 18 is approximately magnetic south; 36 is the reciprocal north direction. These numbers are not assurances of active runway use. Check current airport information and traffic. Helicopters may use paths different from fixed-wing patterns. Stay clear; do not orbit an approach path because the airport is uncontrolled. A common traffic advisory frequency supports situational awareness but a radio call does not grant airspace permission. Learn standard phonetic words and unambiguous position reports; listen before transmitting, identify who you are addressing and your position/intention, and follow authorization-specific communications. Never impersonate ATC or transmit clearance instructions. In the exercise, an observer says 'traffic west' without distance or direction of travel: request clarification while taking the safe prebriefed response, rather than assuming a conflict-free route.

Read chart obstacle elevations carefully: top elevation above MSL and height above ground are different quantities. Terrain changes can alter AGL along a constant-MSL flight. A 2,400-foot MSL aircraft above 2,100-foot ground is 300 feet AGL; above 1,900-foot ground it is 500 feet AGL. A local compass/GNSS indication may be degraded near steel or electromagnetic interference. Do not fly by a single unverified sensor. A stable air mass can support haze and poor visibility; unstable air can produce turbulence and rapidly growing clouds. A small temperature/dewpoint spread suggests possible saturation if cooling occurs, not a guaranteed forecast. TAF change groups describe forecast time periods, not current observations; compare the valid period and use current observations/site conditions. Pressure altitude and temperature influence density altitude and performance; use the aircraft's performance information rather than applying a crewed-aircraft formula blindly to a multirotor.

The safety-event rule distinguishes damage to the aircraft itself from other property. For the property trigger, the repair cost includes labor and materials; the stated exception is cost not exceeding $500, or fair market value not exceeding $500 for total loss. Injury/loss-of-consciousness triggers are independent. Preserve estimates and use the current rule when evaluating an actual event. The rule's ten-calendar-day reporting period is not permission to delay emergency assistance.

# VUC113 workbook and instructor delivery

## Program definition
Audience: prospective commercial inspection remote pilots and field coordinators. Entry: English aviation vocabulary/numeracy support, ability to read charts, safe participation; legal pilot eligibility is separately verified. Outcomes: plan a lawful mission, interpret airspace/weather, calculate energy, configure contingencies, coordinate a crew and produce an honest closeout. Forty planned instructional hours: 10 teaching, 6 weather/chart workshops, 8 equipment/crew stations, 10 mission simulations, 4 assessment, 2 remediation. These are original course hours, not an FAA requirement. Use current FAA rules, current charts and aircraft instructions. Instructor must be competent in Part 107 and the selected equipment; any real operation requires a qualified current remote PIC and lawful operating conditions.

## Equipment and safe delivery
Supply current chart legend and a local chart for instructor demonstration, the fictional chart/weather cards below, calculator, aircraft manual, inert aircraft with batteries disconnected for physical inspection, simulator, sample registration/authorization/maintenance records and mission/log forms. Do not energize propellers in class. Simulate emergencies; do not induce flyaway, low battery or collision risk. Ground simulations do not count as observed flight skill. If actual supervised practice is included, log separately the aircraft, site, weather, authorization, PIC, learner actions and observed outcome. This course is not an FAA practical test or approved certificate issuer.

### W113-1 — Chart and altitude desk
Card A: fictional Class C shelf 40/20; ground 1,500 MSL, target 300 AGL, adjacent terrain 1,300 MSL; grid says 100 and no authorization exists. Compute 1,800 MSL at launch and 500 AGL over lower terrain if altitude remains constant. Mark the shelf relationship only; identify missing permissions, current TFR/NOTAM review and route hazards. Do not turn a synthetic chart into real navigation. Card B: 50/25 shelf, ground 2,100 MSL, target 300 AGL; result 2,400 MSL below floor at that point, still not general permission. Learner draws launch, route, boundary, obstacles and emergency landing on blank gridded paper, with north arrow, units and a legend.

### W113-2 — Weather briefing
Card A: KZZZ 181755Z 18012G20KT 3SM BKN008 18/17 A2992. Site elevation equals station; wind limit 15 knots, authorization 120 AGL. Decode each element, identify 300 AGL cloud limit and lower 120 authorization, and reject gusts. Card B: wind 8 gust 12, visibility 5 SM, BKN012, same site/limits. Identify improved margins but require current complete briefing; this is not automatic dispatch. Instructor injects a gust front and demands a reassessment. Retain the observation time and limitations.

### W113-3 — Energy and payload
Budget 18 minutes, outbound 3, return 4, reserve 3: 8 inspection minutes. A 1,000 m route at 5 m/s takes 3.33 minutes, at 2 m/s 8.33. Recompute rather than spending the reserve. Weigh an inert payload/aircraft assembly or use 249 g plus 20 g card: 269 g. Record mass, center-of-gravity/manual compatibility and category limitations. Do not infer safe lift from a scale alone.

### W113-4 — Preflight discrepancy board
Cards: cracked propeller; RTH 250 feet versus 150 authorization; home point at yesterday's site; required Remote ID not functioning; healthy aircraft but occupied emergency landing area. Learner identifies all five holds, explains corrective verification and refuses an unsafe launch. Reset simulated home point/RTH on a training interface, then explain obstacle/ceiling conflicts. Instructor changes one setting after a simulated update; learner must recheck.

### W113-5 — Crew and emergencies
Roles: PIC, observer, site coordinator. Script: minute 0 brief; minute 2 camera request beyond VLOS; minute 4 low helicopter west; minute 6 observer radio fails; minute 8 landing area occupied. Learner gives closed-loop calls, rejects VLOS shortcut and executes simulated safe responses. Observer actor responds only when specifically addressed. Debrief fixation, handoff and authority to abort. No actual hazardous flight is performed.

### W113-6 — People, night and evidence
Prepare a daytime inspection plan and a night variant. Supplied aircraft 240 g plus 25 g light with exposed blades; do not claim Category 1. Show lawful separation or an actually applicable approved pathway. Record required night/Remote ID checks without inventing eligibility. Inspect six supplied text-image cards: four current dated tank faces, one blank, one last-month image. Report four current faces and two coverage limitations; never silently substitute.

## Observed assessment stations
For each station score planning, execution/decision, evidence and explanation 0–2 each: 0 absent/unsafe, 1 safe but incomplete, 2 correct independently. Pass at least 7/8 each, no critical error, and all stations. Simulator and bench labels stay on the record.

### P113-01 — Eligibility review
Using fictitious pilot/aircraft documents, detect stale recency, distinguish registration and certificate, record release hold.
### P113-02 — Operating envelope
Apply authorization cap and structure rule, maintain simulated VLOS and yield to traffic.
### P113-03 — Chart desk
Compute MSL/AGL and shelf relationship, identify authorization and restriction checks using W113-1.
### P113-04 — Weather desk
Decode W113-2 and issue a reasoned no-go/go-conditional briefing with margins.
### P113-05 — Energy desk
Calculate W113-3, preserve reserve and assess payload eligibility.
### P113-06 — Crew briefing
Assign roles and respond to helicopter/communication injects with closed-loop acknowledgment.
### P113-07 — Inert preflight
Inspect equipment and settings; identify all five W113-4 discrepancies and verification steps.
### P113-08 — Night/people review
Reject unsupported Category 1 claim; identify independent night/Remote ID conditions.
### P113-09 — Emergency/reporting
Respond to occupied landing area and injury scenario; distinguish emergency help, FAA and NTSB duties.
### P113-10 — Mission closeout
Produce coverage report from W113-6 with gaps, dates and interpretation boundaries.

## Integrated mission A — Tower inspection
Fictional tower 300 feet; authorization cap 120 AGL; wind 12 gust 20 versus 15 limit; client requests 200 feet; battery budget 18 with 3/4/3 commitments; observer available; no people control established. Deliver map, authorization check, weather/energy worksheet, crew brief and dispatch decision. Expected no-go for weather, requested altitude and uncontrolled exposure; 8 minutes budget does not cure them. At inject 1 wind improves to 8 gust 12 and area is secured: reassess all conditions and keep 120 cap. At inject 2 helicopter arrives: yield and terminate/reassess safely.

## Integrated mission B — Night revisit
Aircraft 240 g plus 25 g light, exposed blades; required Remote ID fault; RTH 250 versus 150 authorization; two missing images from prior job. Identify independent holds, propose a lawful corrected plan, simulate loss-link response and produce honest partial closeout. Passing requires all holds addressed or explicit no-go, no fabricated image and no claimed FAA award. Instructor varies one datum on retake rather than repeating a memorized plan.

## Instruction, remediation and award rules
Teach the worked example before practice, then use changed data and require reasoning. Provide chart/English support without giving assessment answers. Twenty explained checks are formative. Controlled final and changed retake are private: 10 cases, 30 decisions, 60 points, 48 pass and at least 4/6 each domain, no unresolved critical error; 120 minutes. Practical all ten stations and both integrated cases must pass; written success cannot waive them. Critical failures include knowingly unauthorized flight, ignoring crewed traffic, unsafe energized demonstration, fabricated records or claiming FAA certification from this award. Stop the assessment safely, reteach the failed domain and reassess with changed conditions. Preserve original failure and retest separately.
Award only an original VNDRLY/U Remote Pilot Preparation training certificate identifying assessment mode, version, date and instructor. FAA initial/recurrent processes, actual pilot authority and mission permissions remain external. No automatic expiry equivalence or assurance of FAA exam success is claimed. Instructor reviews rule/chart/aircraft changes before each delivery; changes trigger targeted refresher and reassessment as needed. Retain learner evidence privately; never place final keys in learner distribution.

# VUC113 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q113-01-L1

A client offers landowner permission but the pilot lacks current knowledge recency. May the inspection proceed?

**Answer:** No. Establish a qualified current remote pilot and all operational permissions first.

**Reason/remediation:** Land access is different from FAA pilot eligibility. Revisit Q113-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-01-L2

Does this course issue a remote pilot certificate?

**Answer:** No; complete the applicable FAA certification pathway.

**Reason/remediation:** An original training award cannot substitute for the federal credential. Revisit Q113-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-02-L1

A structure would allow a geometric altitude of 500 feet, but authorization caps 120 feet AGL. Which controls?

**Answer:** 120 feet AGL, with a safe plan within all other limits.

**Reason/remediation:** The structure provision does not cancel authorization conditions. Revisit Q113-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-02-L2

Can a camera screen alone establish visual line of sight?

**Answer:** No. Maintain the required direct visual capability.

**Reason/remediation:** Telemetry does not provide adequate see-and-avoid by itself. Revisit Q113-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-03-L1

Ground elevation is 1,500 feet MSL and flight is 300 feet AGL. What is flight MSL?

**Answer:** 1,800 feet MSL.

**Reason/remediation:** Add local ground elevation; check all airspace layers and restrictions separately. Revisit Q113-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-03-L2

A facility map cell says 100. Is that flight permission?

**Answer:** No. Obtain required authorization and follow its actual conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** A planning grid is not an issued approval. Revisit Q113-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-04-L1

A same-elevation cloud base is 800 feet above station. What altitude meets 500 feet below cloud?

**Answer:** At most 300 feet AGL, subject to lower limits and safe margins.

**Reason/remediation:** 800 minus 500 is 300; terrain differences change the calculation. Revisit Q113-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-04-L2

Wind is 12 gusting 20 knots and aircraft limit is 15. Go?

**Answer:** No under that supplied limit.

**Reason/remediation:** Gusts exceed the limit; average speed is insufficient. Revisit Q113-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-05-L1

An 18-minute budget requires 3 outbound, 4 return and 3 reserve. Mission time?

**Answer:** 8 minutes.

**Reason/remediation:** Subtract all committed time, then monitor actual performance. Revisit Q113-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-05-L2

A 1,000 m return slows from 5 to 2 m/s. What changes?

**Answer:** Return grows from 3.33 to 8.33 minutes.

**Reason/remediation:** Distance divided by groundspeed, then divide seconds by 60. Revisit Q113-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-06-L1

Observer reports low helicopter traffic during photography. Priority?

**Answer:** Yield and execute the safe separation/landing response.

**Reason/remediation:** Camera objectives never outrank crewed-aircraft safety. Revisit Q113-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-06-L2

May a fatigued pilot fly just because the legal minimum rest/alcohol interval is met?

**Answer:** No if impaired or unable to operate safely.

**Reason/remediation:** Fitness is a real-time condition, not just a clock test. Revisit Q113-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-07-L1

RTH is 250 feet and authorization ceiling is 150. Can you launch and fix it later?

**Answer:** No. Establish and verify a safe lawful contingency before launch.

**Reason/remediation:** An automatic climb can violate permission or collide with obstacles. Revisit Q113-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-07-L2

Does obstacle sensing eliminate the need to inspect the route?

**Answer:** No. Sensors have limitations including thin wires and reflective surfaces.

**Reason/remediation:** Automation is an aid, not independent hazard clearance. Revisit Q113-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-08-L1

A 249 g drone gains 20 g of attachments. Can original advertised mass prove Category 1?

**Answer:** No; total is 269 g and all category conditions must be checked.

**Reason/remediation:** Attachments count; rotor injury protection also matters. Revisit Q113-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-08-L2

Is anti-collision lighting alone enough for night operation?

**Answer:** No. Training/knowledge, visibility, airspace and all other applicable conditions still apply.

**Reason/remediation:** One satisfied requirement does not authorize the whole operation. Revisit Q113-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-09-L1

A link-loss landing area becomes occupied. What governs?

**Answer:** Protect people using the planned safe contingency and communicate immediately.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not blindly execute automation into a changed hazard. Revisit Q113-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-09-L2

Does a site incident report replace FAA/NTSB reporting?

**Answer:** No. Evaluate each applicable reporting duty independently.

**Reason/remediation:** Different authorities have different triggers and deadlines. Revisit Q113-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-10-L1

The final tower face was not inspected because of wind. What belongs in the report?

**Answer:** A clear uninspected-area limitation and reason.

**Reason/remediation:** An old image must not be presented as current evidence. Revisit Q113-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q113-10-L2

Does a clear photograph prove structural integrity?

**Answer:** No. Qualified technical interpretation and appropriate inspection methods are separate.

**Reason/remediation:** Image collection and engineering acceptance are different functions. Revisit Q113-10 and perform its changed application.
