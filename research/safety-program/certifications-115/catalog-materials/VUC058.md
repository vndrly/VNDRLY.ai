# VNDRLY/U Source Inspection — Electrical Equipment

VUC058 · Original available-source materials · 2026-11-18

## Purpose and boundaries

Learn to establish what an electrical supplier has built, which purchase requirements it meets, what the inspection actually observed, and what remains unresolved before shipment. This is supplier-surveillance training with passive practical work, not qualification as an electrician, energized-work authorization, acceptance-testing certification or API SIEE certification. The common inspection foundation Q01–04 is included in this package. Electrical-specific Q05–24 then applies it to equipment and records. Every E58 numerical acceptance band is a fictional classroom requirement; none is a substitute for a project specification, equipment manual or applicable code.

The current API program page links a November 2026–July 2028 publication list. Its linked BOK filename says “through July 2026,” a documentary inconsistency retained in the source register. We reviewed the available outline and list, not all referenced full standards. No complete proprietary-code or external-exam equivalence is asserted. [E01–02]

Prerequisites: industrial safety, controlled drawing reading, units, fractions, percentages and basic electrical terminology. Instructors diagnose gaps before scored work. Voltage describes potential difference, current is charge flow, resistance opposes current, and power is rate of energy transfer. AC, DC, phase, frequency, grounding and insulation are distinct properties. Do not treat a familiar voltage label as evidence that an installation is safe to touch. This course uses paper cases, inert assemblies and ordinary dimensional tools only.

## Q05 — Electrical safety at the supplier

A source inspector can be injured while merely trying to read a label. Establish the visit's permitted access, test-area barriers and responsible electrical authority before approaching equipment. A dark lamp, stopped motor or open control switch is not proof of safe isolation. Electrical work requires the applicable employer procedure and qualified personnel; this training does not grant that role. OSHA identifies verification of deenergization, including possible backfeed or induced voltage, as a qualified-person task. [E03]

Treat a supplier's live test bay as an exclusion area unless the responsible organization has explicitly established a safe observation arrangement. If that arrangement is absent, stop the observation and obtain records or a safely scheduled inspection. Never request defeat of an interlock for a better view. Record the restriction rather than inventing an observation. Motors also introduce rotation; breakers may contain stored mechanical energy; capacitors and test specimens may retain charge. A component's small size is not a reliable hazard indicator.

**Worked case:** the supplier says a control panel is safe because its display is dark, but its auxiliary supply is not identified. Decline internal access, identify the unresolved energy boundary and refer to the responsible qualified person. **Changed case:** a verified safe arrangement is established, but a separate test is starting nearby. Reassess the observation area; the first isolation does not cover the neighboring operation. P05 assesses recognition and communication, not actual isolation performance.

## Q06 — Drawings, terminals and circuit identity

Use the single-line diagram to understand the main distribution arrangement, the schematic to understand circuit logic, and the terminal/wiring schedule to trace physical connections. They answer different questions. Match revision, equipment tag, circuit designation and terminal number before drawing conclusions. A wire color alone is insufficient to establish function. Cross-references must lead to the actual destination, including external interfaces.

Make a connection matrix with origin, destination, wire identification, drawing reference and evidence. Review the matrix against the final configuration, not an early design only. Distinguish a passive visual trace from a continuity test and from a functional test. A correctly labeled conductor may be connected to the wrong terminal; an electrically continuous path may still implement the wrong function.

**Worked case:** inert model drawing D requires TB1-3 to K1-A1; actual cord goes to K2-A1. Record both endpoints and the revision. The label is not enough to pass the circuit. **Changed case:** the cord is corrected but the terminal schedule still says K2. Physical and documentary correction are separate closure items. In P06 the learner traces a harmless cord board without electrical power or test injection.

## Q07 — Equipment identity, ratings and materials

Start at the purchase data sheet and nameplate, then reconcile the bill of material and manufacturing/test records. Tag, serial, model, voltage, phase, frequency, current and relevant duty ratings must correspond. A higher number is not automatically an acceptable substitution: changing voltage or a protective device can change compatibility and coordination. Record deviations for the authorized technical owner rather than selecting equipment yourself.

Materials also matter: enclosure material, conductor material, insulation designation and specified environmental features need supporting evidence. Do not assume a stainless-looking surface proves alloy or corrosion suitability. Where certification or listing is required, verify the actual assembly and its conditions, not merely a component logo.

**Worked case:** order requires a 480 V, three-phase, 60 Hz motor; delivered plate is 400 V, three-phase, 50 Hz. Identify both mismatches, hold acceptance and seek technical disposition. Do not run it to see whether it works. **Changed case:** rating evidence matches, but serial on the routine test belongs to the previous unit. Ratings can close while test applicability remains unresolved. P07 physically compares inert labels to the packet.

## Q08 — Enclosures and environmental suitability

An enclosure protects against specified exposures, not every possible environment. Compare the actual required enclosure designation, construction and installed penetrations with the evidence. IP and NEMA/UL type designations are not freely interchangeable; type requirements include features beyond the IP ingress classification. [E04] A supplier's crosswalk is not automatic authorization for a substitution.

Inspect doors, latches, gaskets, unused entries, cable glands, drainage and ventilation as specified. A correctly rated empty enclosure can lose its intended performance through unapproved modifications or incompatible accessories. Do not drill or repair an enclosure as an inspection shortcut. Preserve the exact discrepancy, location and photograph/reference.

**Worked case:** a synthetic order calls for Type 4X evidence; the packet only states IP66. Record that the required designation is not established. **Changed case:** suitable assembly evidence arrives, but an added entry lacks the required closure. Separate documentation from final construction; do not sign a blanket pass. On the safe model, identify the seeded open-entry card and compare the actual gland/entry schedule.

## Q09 — Hazardous-location equipment

Use the approved area-classification documentation and the equipment's applicable markings and approval conditions. Class, division or zone, material group, temperature suitability and installation conditions cannot be replaced by the word “explosionproof.” OSHA requires suitability for the specific location and ignitable material, not simply a generic hazardous-area label. [E05] This course does not train independent area classification or supply all selection tables.

Source inspection establishes whether the required evidence and as-built details match the approved design. Review cable entries, blanking elements and specified seals against that design. Do not assume that an ordinary weatherproof enclosure is suitable for an explosive atmosphere. Do not mix division and zone assumptions without the approved engineering basis. Escalate unknown markings instead of interpreting them by appearance.

**Worked case:** classification drawing and order specify a particular gas-group approval; the delivered label omits the required group. Request the actual approval/applicability evidence and hold the suitability conclusion. **Changed case:** the correct equipment approval is supplied but one substituted gland is not covered. Close the equipment-document issue only. P09 uses printed fictitious markings and cannot award hazardous-area design authority.

## Q10 — Purge and pressurization packages

A purge/pressurization system has functions, prerequisites and failure responses. Review the approved sequence, supply requirements, sensing points, alarms and permitted energization state. A pressure indication alone cannot establish the complete protective sequence. Determine which tests were actually performed and which were simulated. The specific approved equipment instructions govern; this course provides no universal purge duration or setpoint.

Create a cause/effect matrix: initiating condition, expected action, actual evidence and open discrepancy. Include startup permissives and loss-of-protection behavior where required. If a bypass was used during testing, identify its scope, removal and effect on the evidence. Source inspectors do not enable operation or defeat the protective function.

**Worked case:** six required sequence outcomes have four unique valid records, one duplicate and one untested result. Coverage is 4/6, or 66.6667%; two outcomes remain unsupported. **Changed case:** one missing unique record is provided, making 5/6 or 83.3333%, while the other remains bypassed. Do not round coverage into completion or infer a pass from a lit pressure lamp.

## Q11 — Grounding, bonding and cable support

Trace the specified protective paths and support arrangement on the approved drawing. Grounding/bonding, neutral conductors and signal reference connections are not interchangeable merely because a continuity indication is available. Inspect the actual specified connection, conductor identification, physical protection and evidence of required testing. Do not disconnect protective conductors for this course or invent conductor sizing rules.

Cable support must preserve the specified routing, attachment and separation. Sharp edges, damaged insulation, unsupported sections or interference with doors are findings, even when a cable has the correct label. Mechanical presence does not prove low-resistance bonding, and a low reading does not prove that the conductor is the required size or route.

**Worked case:** passive board requires eight support points, seven are present; the unsupported span is not accepted by calculating an average spacing. **Changed case:** the eighth support is added but the route now crosses the specified separation boundary. Reinspect the changed route. P11 requires actual tracing and safe dimensional measurement on an inert cable model, with results tied to a drawing.

## Q12 — Junction boxes and control panels

Compare the final assembly to its schematic, terminal schedule, component list and required inspection/test plan. Check terminal accessibility, identification, separation, wire routing, specified connections and closure of unused openings. Component approval does not automatically establish approval of the assembled panel. A neat appearance cannot substitute for connection evidence.

Distinguish visual checks, point-to-point checks and operational logic tests. Each needs appropriate identity, method and acceptance basis. Record factory-installed jumpers and temporary test connections so final configuration is clear. Do not remove a conductor to improve appearance or introduce a jumper to obtain a passing sequence.

**Worked case:** twelve required circuits have ten distinct test records and two duplicate attachments. Supported coverage is 83.3333%, with two gaps. **Changed case:** all twelve records arrive, but terminal TB4 was changed after the tests. The original count is complete; the changed circuit needs applicable follow-up evidence. P12 asks for a final-configuration connection matrix and a narrowly worded finding.

## Q13 — Transformer construction and identity

For a liquid-immersed transformer, reconcile the unit serial, rating plate, approved arrangement, accessories and test packet. Nameplate ratio, winding connection and tap position affect interpretation of test results. A simple voltage ratio calculation is useful only under explicitly stated assumptions; actual winding connections and test methods must be considered by the responsible specialist.

Review tank condition, identified fluid, accessory schedule, shipping condition and required leak/test records. Fluid identity, fluid condition and equipment performance are separate questions. Do not open a tank, sample liquid or operate a tap changer as a classroom task. Unknown oil condition or a damaged seal is referred through the specified process.

**Worked case:** a fictional single-phase ideal transformer at the stated tap has 2400 V input and 240 V output, ratio 10: 1. A test at a different tap cannot be compared without the applicable expected ratio. **Changed case:** the specified tap ratio is 12: 1; 2400 V implies 200 V under the same ideal assumption. Neither calculation proves insulation, losses or safe service. P13 reviews supplied records only.

## Q14 — Transformer test evidence

A test packet must identify the transformer, configuration, method, instrument, conditions, raw results and acceptance basis. Separate ratio, winding resistance, insulation, losses and other required tests; passing one does not prove the others. Verify that the plan distinguishes routine, type and special tests and what evidence is acceptable for this order. Do not demand an unrelated destructive test or accept a generic type report as the actual unit's routine record.

Winding resistance results depend on method and temperature; the inspector checks the specified basis rather than applying a remembered correction indiscriminately. A report's final green box is weaker evidence than an identifiable set of comparable measurements. Missing units and undocumented tap positions require clarification.

**Worked case:** synthetic expected ratio is 10.00 and allowed deviation is ±1.0%; measured 10.12 differs by 0.12/10×100=1.2%, outside the fictional band. **Changed case:** 10.08 differs by 0.8%, within the band, but instrument identity is missing. State the numerical comparison and remaining validity gap separately. No actual transformer test is performed in this course.

## Q15 — Switchgear construction and ratings

Review the approved switchgear configuration: sections, compartments, bus arrangement, grounding, doors, barriers, feeder interfaces and protective devices. Continuous-current rating, short-circuit capability and voltage rating describe different properties. Matching one is not proof of overall suitability. The designer determines required system duties; the inspector verifies specified evidence against the supplied assembly.

Access restrictions apply even when equipment is not running. Racking mechanisms, springs and shutters can create mechanical hazards. Use passive diagrams and inert models. Check that inspected final configuration matches the test evidence and that substitutions received the required disposition. A component's interrupting rating does not automatically establish the assembly's complete rating.

**Worked case:** order requires a 2000 A main bus; supplied label says 1600 A. The shortfall is 400 A, or 20% of the requirement. Hold the mismatch; do not assume actual load excuses it. **Changed case:** rating evidence is corrected, but a final barrier is absent. Ratings and construction closure remain separate. P15 requires a compartment/record map, not operation of switchgear.

## Q16 — Breakers, interlocks and tie arrangements

Interlocks enforce particular permitted states. Use the approved logic to determine what is allowed for incoming breakers, tie breakers and other devices; do not assume every installation prohibits or permits the same combination. The test must identify initial state, action, expected response, observed response and final restoration. A control simulation may not establish the entire mechanical/electrical trip path.

Review bypass and temporary-setting records. A successful operation performed with an interlock disabled does not prove the interlock. Where a prohibited state occurs, preserve the result and obtain authorized correction/retest. Do not instruct a supplier to force a mechanism or repeat an unsafe operation for a photograph.

**Worked case:** E58 logic prohibits both incomers plus the tie being closed simultaneously. Paper test shows that state was permitted. Record failure against that exact fictional rule. **Changed case:** corrected simulation prevents the state, but the final mechanism test is absent where required. Close only simulated logic evidence. P16 uses state cards, never actual breaker operation.

## Q17 — Instrument transformers and metering

Check CT and VT identity, ratio, polarity/designation, intended connection and applicable test evidence. Current and voltage transformers are not interchangeable. A CT secondary opened while primary current is flowing can produce dangerous voltage; only appropriately qualified personnel follow the actual manufacturer's procedures. [E06] No CT wiring, shorting or energized measurement is a classroom task here.

For supplied idealized records, a 400: 5 CT has a ratio of 80. A secondary record of 3 A corresponds to 240 A primary under the stated ideal assumption. Scaling alone does not establish accuracy, polarity or burden suitability. A meter configuration using the wrong ratio can display a plausible but wrong result.

**Worked case:** physical CT is 400: 5 but meter setting is 600: 5. At the same fictional 3 A secondary, the configured display would imply 360 A, 120 A too high. Escalate the mismatch; do not change live settings. **Changed case:** ratio is corrected, but polarity evidence is missing. Arithmetic is now consistent while metering acceptance remains incomplete. P17 requires a scale/configuration review and a safety response.

## Q18 — Motor control centers

An MCC integrates power distribution, motor control, protective devices and interfaces. Check lineup identity, incoming compartment, bus and grounding arrangement, unit schedule, enclosure, isolating devices and required interlocks against the approved packet. A spare bucket is not evidence that an installed unit has the correct protection or wiring.

Match each unit to the intended motor/load and its specified components and settings. A supplier's common catalog option may differ from the order. The inspector records this, while the authorized engineer determines coordination and settings. Separate factory configuration from site commissioning obligations.

**Worked case:** eight units are required; seven unique matching unit records and a duplicate are provided. Coverage is 87.5%, not 100%. **Changed case:** the eighth record is supplied but refers to a replacement contactor different from the final installed item. Resolve actual configuration before closing that unit. P18 builds a lineup-to-unit-to-record matrix and carries open items into handover.

## Q19 — Induction motors and performance context

Match motor serial, rating, enclosure, mounting, direction reference, bearings, terminal arrangement and specified accessories to the purchase requirements. Nameplate output power is not electrical input. Efficiency is output divided by input on a compatible basis. A motor meeting a power rating can still fail a mounting, environmental or testing requirement.

Synchronous speed in revolutions per minute is 120 times frequency divided by pole count. Induction-motor speed under load is below synchronous speed in ordinary motoring operation; slip is their difference divided by synchronous speed. These calculations explain record consistency, not universal acceptance. Use the actual approved test standard and duty for acceptance.

**Worked case:** 60 Hz and four poles gives 1800 rpm synchronous. Actual 1770 rpm gives 30/1800×100=1.6667% slip. **Changed case:** six poles at 60 Hz gives 1200 rpm; actual 1180 gives the same 1.6667% slip. Do not compare the 1180 rpm record to a four-pole purchase without resolving the configuration. P19 reviews records and inert labels, not a running motor.

## Q20 — Insulation measurements and limitations

Insulation resistance testing applies a test voltage and measures the resulting response; it is not an ordinary harmless visual inspection. The method, test voltage, duration, connections, environmental conditions and post-test safety arrangements must come from the approved procedure and qualified personnel. Read the record without attempting the test. A large displayed resistance alone does not establish every insulation property. [E07]

For a supplied time-resistance record, polarization index is the ten-minute reading divided by the one-minute reading. Its applicability and acceptance are equipment/method dependent. Do not invent a universal pass threshold or compare unlike temperature/voltage conditions as if they were identical. An insulation-resistance test also does not replace a required dielectric withstand test.

**Worked case:** fictional readings are 100 MΩ at one minute and 250 MΩ at ten minutes: PI=2.5. This is arithmetic only until the approved applicability and criteria are supplied. **Changed case:** readings 200 and 300 MΩ yield 1.5, despite the larger one-minute resistance. P20 distinguishes calculation, comparable evidence and acceptance authority without applying test voltage.

## Q21 — Functional tests and protective settings

Use a required-function register, with each input, expected output, configuration and evidence linked to the test plan. A relay pickup simulation, a contact output and actual breaker opening are different extents of testing. Identify the extent honestly. Settings need an approved basis, correct revision and final as-left record; a screenshot from an unrelated unit is not evidence.

Review failed and interrupted tests as well as successful ones. Correction must be tied to the affected function and required retest, including dependencies if configuration changed. Do not average passing functions into acceptance of a failed protective function. Time measurements must use the same start/stop definitions as their criterion.

**Worked case:** fictional required operation time is at most 0.50 s; recorded 0.62 s exceeds it by 0.12 s. Hold the affected result. **Changed case:** 0.48 s meets that numerical limit, but the record is a contact simulation and the required final-device operation is absent. Report the narrower achieved scope. P21 prepares a cause/effect and settings evidence sheet.

## Q22 — Inspection tools, dimensions and coating

Select a tool appropriate to the quantity, range and required resolution. Record tool identity, verification status, reference and observed result. A calibration sticker does not excuse damage or an unsuitable range. For electrical test equipment, qualified specialists also consider application-specific safety ratings and leads; this course only reviews that evidence and never selects a live probing arrangement.

Actual learner measurements use benign dimensions on a passive model. Compare a known reference first, measure the named feature consistently and preserve units. A result near the acceptance boundary requires the specified decision rule; do not subtract an unsupported “tolerance” to make it pass. Coating appearance alone does not prove film thickness or substrate preparation; review specified records separately.

**Worked case:** reference 50.00 mm reads 50.30 on a tool with fictional allowable error ±0.10 mm. Error is +0.30, exceeding the allowed magnitude by 0.20. Do not use it for acceptance. **Changed case:** a verified tool reads the model feature within its supplied band, but coating records are absent. Close dimension only. P22 requires actual safe measurement and a recorded tool check.

## Q23 — Nonconformance and final configuration

Write each finding with requirement, actual evidence, affected identity, consequence for inspection and assigned disposition owner. Distinguish proposed correction from verified correction, and correction from acceptance of any deviation. A source inspector must not approve a design change outside delegated authority. Preserve rejected or superseded evidence with status so the dossier's history remains intelligible.

After change, assess which earlier records still apply. Replacing a protective device may affect functional tests and settings; changing an enclosure penetration may affect its claimed protection. A physical repair alone does not establish all dependent requirements. Verify final records against final configuration and keep open conditions visible.

**Worked case:** twelve findings have eight verified closures: 66.6667%, four open. **Changed case:** two closures reopen after a configuration change and one open finding closes, leaving seven closed and five open, 58.3333%. Do not report nine closures by ignoring reopened items. P23 requires a chronological register and a restricted recommendation with named owners.

## Q24 — Preservation, shipment and receiving handover

Inspection completion, shipment authorization and field energization are separate decisions. Identify who controls each. Review specified shipping protection, moisture controls, packaging, lifting information, loose accessories and receiving/storage instructions. Do not operate heaters, perform lifting or open oil-filled equipment as part of this course. Preservation evidence must match the actual shipping condition.

Build a unique dossier index: item, required record, revision, acceptance status and unresolved issue. Duplicate files do not increase coverage. State witness versus review, deviations, retest obligations and preservation/receiving actions. A recipient should be able to tell what is ready and what still needs action without reading vague “subject to completion” wording.

**Worked case:** twenty required records contain seventeen valid unique items, two duplicates and one unrelated file. Coverage is 85%, three missing. **Changed case:** two missing records arrive, so nineteen are valid, 95%; the last requirement remains open. Recommend only the release scope actually supported and authorized. I58 combines the complete paper dossier with required passive observations, ending in a written and spoken handover.

# Common source-inspection foundation Q01–04

Retained S references; VUC058 delivery/award rules govern.

## Q01 — Source inspection and accountability

Source inspection checks evidence while equipment is being made, when defects or missing information can still be addressed before shipment. The inspector works to an assigned scope rather than taking over the manufacturer's quality system. Identify the purchaser, supplier, sub-supplier and technical decision makers. Clarify who can approve a deviation, release a hold point, authorize shipment and accept equipment at destination. These may be different people.

An inspection visit does not prove every manufacturing activity was observed. State the time, items and records examined. If an unobserved stage is important, determine what evidence the approved plan requires; do not sign a retrospective witness statement. A supplier's responsibility for making conforming equipment does not disappear because a purchaser's inspector attended. Likewise, the inspector must not conceal a known defect by calling the visit a courtesy.

Use a three-part statement: requirement, evidence and conclusion within scope. For example, “Order F56 requires identified heat records for all six plates; four are linked; two remain unresolved.” That is more useful than “material looks good.” Keep technical acceptance separate from commercial schedule discussion.

**Worked case:** supplier asks for an unconditional release after a short visit to one of three fabrication bays. Record the actual bay/items inspected and remaining scope; do not issue a broader conclusion. **Changed case:** records for the other bays arrive. Review their applicability and completeness under the plan, labeling record review accurately rather than claiming personal observation.

## Q02 — Purchase requirements and document hierarchy

Read the purchase order, specification, drawings, data sheets and approved inspection plan as a connected package. Record revisions and any stated order of precedence. A later email does not automatically supersede a controlled drawing. If requirements conflict, raise a technical query to the authorized owner before the affected activity. Preserve the original conflict and the disposition so later inspectors can understand the basis used.

Create a requirement register with the equipment tag, clause or drawing reference, evidence needed, stage and responsible reviewer. Distinguish required from optional documents, but do not downgrade a requirement because it is difficult to obtain. Where a code is named, establish the contractual edition and applicable scope; this course does not supply every code provision. A public examination reference list is not a project specification.

Check units and terminology early. “Test pressure” and “design pressure” are different fields, and a value without units can be ambiguous. Do not repair an ambiguous order by assuming customary units. Resolve it and record the approved interpretation. Changes may affect already completed work as well as future activity.

**Worked case:** drawing revision D specifies nozzle orientation 90°, while the shop traveler still uses revision C at 0°. Hold the affected configuration for controlled resolution. **Changed case:** the revised traveler is issued after fabrication. Future documentation is corrected, but the existing nozzle still needs dimensional verification and authorized disposition.

## Q03 — Inspection plans and notification

Translate the requirements into stages that can actually be inspected. Material identity may need checking before cutting; weld preparation before welding; internal cleanliness before closure. Define hold, witness, review and surveillance points according to the contract's definitions. Record notification timing and the authority required to waive or release a point. Silence is not automatically a waiver unless the governing agreement explicitly makes it so.

Discuss the plan at a pre-inspection meeting with relevant parties. Confirm scope, schedule, document status, sub-supplier controls, test readiness and communication routes. Write actions and owners. The meeting should expose missing prerequisites, not merely produce attendance signatures. A schedule change may require revised notification so the inspector can attend an irreversible stage.

Maintain a stage register rather than relying on memory. If a required hold was bypassed, preserve the actual condition and seek an authorized recovery or disposition. A photograph can be useful evidence, but it may not replace a specific required observation. Do not manufacture a release date after the fact.

**Worked case:** ten internal-inspection holds are required; eight have evidence and two units were closed early. Eight of ten are evidenced, with two missed holds. **Changed case:** supplier offers a final external photograph for each closed unit. The photographs do not establish the internal condition; obtain the designated disposition instead of changing the inspection plan retrospectively.

## Q04 — Supplier quality controls and sub-suppliers

Assess whether the controls needed for this order are actually being used. Look for current work instructions, identification, calibrated equipment, personnel qualifications, inspection records and control of nonconforming items. A quality certificate or polished manual is not proof that a specific traveler was followed. Sample actual jobs and preserve the limits of the sample.

Sub-supplier activities can carry important requirements, such as heat treatment, material testing or coating. Determine how the main supplier transfers requirements and verifies returned evidence. A subcontracted test report should identify the correct item, method, date and acceptance basis. The main order's traceability must survive the handoff. Do not assume a sub-supplier's generic certificate covers all delivered items.

Use findings to separate isolated record errors from potential common-cause issues. If several records use the same invalid tool, the affected population may be larger than the sample. Escalate through the approved quality process; the inspector must not invent a statistical sampling plan or infer lot acceptance from a convenient small sample.

**Worked case:** three of twelve reviewed travelers lack the required tool identity. The sample defect rate is 25%; this does not prove exactly 25% of all travelers are defective. **Changed case:** the same unidentified tool appears across another production lot. Identify the potentially affected scope and obtain an authorized review rather than limit the finding to the first twelve records.

# VUC058 workbook, instructor guide and credential rules

## Delivery plan

48 planned hours: 16 instruction and formative checks, 8 labs, 12 stations, 6 integrated dossier/remediation, 6 final. Breaks and extra remediation are additional; record actual time. Instructor competence must cover supplier inspection, electrical drawing/record interpretation and the benign practical tools. Electrical specialists resolve questions beyond that competence. This course does not create an electrical qualified person.

Start with a diagnostic: identify volts versus amperes, compute 3/12 as a percentage, distinguish schematic from terminal schedule, and explain why a dark display is not safe isolation. Teach missing concepts before scored work. Demonstrate one full requirement→evidence→conclusion sequence, then have learners perform the changed case independently. Do not reveal controlled exam keys during teaching. Accessible drawings, larger print, oral responses and reachable fixtures are acceptable when the same outcomes remain observable.

Use an inert cord/terminal board, blank enclosure model without springs or electrical components, printed rating plates, safe plastic cable/support model, ruler/caliper with known reference, and synthetic E58 documents. No power supply, battery, capacitor, spring-loaded breaker, actual CT, transformer test set, insulation tester, energized panel, chemicals or live machinery is needed or permitted in these practicals. Seed defects with safe labels and removable cords, not damaged live equipment. Instructor inspects every fixture before use and records its identity/reference dimensions. P06, P07, P08, P11 and P22 require actual passive observations/measurement; paper answers cannot replace them.

## E58 controlled training packet

Order E58-01 contains junction box JB1, panel CP1, transformer T1, switchgear SG1, MCC1 and motor M1. All values below are fictional exercise requirements. Provide drawing D, terminal map TB1-3→K1-A1, enclosure Type 4X evidence requirement, motor 480 V/3-phase/60 Hz, main bus 2000 A, CT400: 5 with required matching metering setup, and a required-functions register. Training purge sequence has six outcomes; panel has twelve circuits; MCC has eight units. The transformer ratio exercise specifies a single-phase ideal model at a defined tap and fictional ±1% comparison band. Training relay time maximum is 0.50 s. Actual dimension fixture: 50.00 mm reference, tool allowable error ±0.10 mm, feature requirement 30.00±0.50 mm. Actual reference values must be measured/confirmed by the instructor; printed values are not fabricated observations.

Issue separate current and changed record cards with unique IDs. Initial cards contain only the evidence stated in each lab. Missing records are intentional: learners must identify them, not invent data. A change card closes only its described issue. All pages are synthetic and must never be inserted into production business records.

Worked response: ratio 10; deviation 1.2% fails fictional band; bus short 400A=20%; prohibited state fails stated logic. ## W06 — Instrument transformers, MCC and motors

Input: CT400: 5, secondary record 3A, meter 600: 5; eight MCC units with seven unique records; four-pole 60Hz motor 1770 rpm.
