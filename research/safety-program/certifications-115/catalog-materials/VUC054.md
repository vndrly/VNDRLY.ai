# VNDRLY/U Bolted Flange Joint Assembly

VUC054 · Original available-source instruction · 2026-11-18

## Purpose and limits

This course teaches preparation, controlled manual assembly, inspection and records for bolted flange joints using an approved job basis. It includes benign hands-on assembly and simulated powered-tool planning. It does not award API BFJA, ASME qualification, live line-breaking authority or hydraulic-tool authorization. API's public BFJA outline/effectivity and ASME's program description distinguish relevant knowledge and practical learning; full controlled standards were not reviewed.[S01–03] All B54 numerical criteria are fictional teaching values. Obtain the actual joint procedure, component specifications and tool instructions before real work.

Learners need industrial safety, reading, units and basic arithmetic. Diagnose these through the first exercises and supply prerequisite teaching. The assembler must recognize missing or conflicting information, stop the affected step, preserve the joint's condition and seek authorized direction rather than improvise a pressure-boundary repair.

## Q01 — The joint as a system

A bolted joint combines flanges, gasket, fasteners and the assembly process. Fastener load compresses the gasket while the components respond to load and service conditions. Correct torque does not compensate for an incompatible gasket, damaged face or wrong bolt. Conversely, correct parts do not guarantee correct assembly. Treat the complete joint and final configuration as the unit of work.

Identify the joint tag and service from approved documents. Establish the boundaries of the assembler's role: verify parts and conditions, follow the procedure, record actual work and report deviations. Engineering selects or approves the technical basis; operating personnel control isolation and return to service. Do not convert a training certificate into authority to change the assembly specification.

**Worked case:** B54 joint J1 has the specified fasteners but an unidentified gasket. Keep preparation incomplete and obtain the correct identity; do not proceed because the bolts are right. **Changed case:** gasket identity is resolved but the flange rating is unverified. The independent rating question remains. State which evidence closed and which did not.

## Q02 — Work package and joint identity

The work package should identify the system, joint, drawing revision, component requirements, gasket, lubricant, tools, assembly sequence, target values, inspections and release points. Read the whole package before starting. Similar adjacent joints may have different service or component requirements. Marking a joint for work must agree with the approved system identity and isolation scope.

Resolve contradictions before affected work. A handwritten torque on a box is not necessarily the approved target. A newer drawing may supersede the material list; obtain the designated resolution rather than choose whichever document is convenient. Preserve the final authorized revision in the assembly record so the result is reproducible.

**Worked case:** J1 drawing revision C specifies gasket G2; an older work card says G1. Record the conflict and request resolution. **Changed case:** supervisor confirms G2 verbally but the controlled work package remains unreconciled. Follow the actual document-control process and obtain the required authorization/evidence. Do not invent a signature or silently edit history.

## Q03 — Isolation and line-breaking boundaries

Before a pressure-boundary joint is disturbed, the applicable operating and hazardous-energy controls must establish a safe work condition. Stored pressure, temperature, chemicals, trapped sections and possible reaccumulation require attention. A stopped pump, closed valve or zero on one gauge alone does not prove that every hazard is controlled. OSHA's hazardous-energy rule provides a US general-industry framework; actual applicability and site procedures govern.[S04]

The assembler follows their assigned authorized role and verifies the required work clearance; this course does not provide a universal isolation or flange-opening sequence. If isolation evidence conflicts with the observed condition, stop and contact the responsible authority. Do not loosen a nut to test whether a line is pressurized. Keep people out of potential release and movement paths through the approved controls.

**Worked case:** the permit names J2 while the marked joint is J1. Do not begin opening. Resolve the physical/document identity before work. **Changed case:** the tag is corrected but a blocked-in section's condition is unknown. Retain the energy-control question. Classroom work uses an unconnected, unpressurized fixture with no process inventory.

## Q04 — Flange types, ratings and facing compatibility

Recognize that size, facing and pressure class describe different aspects of a flange. A nominal size is not a complete dimensional or rating specification. Pressure class is not a universal allowable pressure in psi; applicable material, temperature, standard and configuration matter. Raised-face, flat-face and ring-type arrangements use different sealing geometries and component requirements. Do not force visually similar parts together without the approved compatibility basis.

Read markings and correlate them with certificates and drawings. Large-diameter or wellhead equipment can follow different dimensional systems from ordinary piping flanges. A matching bolt count does not establish interchangeability. Refer missing markings, damage or a proposed substitution before assembly.

**Worked case:** two flanges share bolt count and approximate outside diameter but have different specified facing systems. Hold compatibility unresolved. **Changed case:** faces match, yet material/rating records do not. Geometric agreement alone still cannot establish service suitability. Record the exact unresolved property rather than “looks wrong.”

## Q05 — Fasteners, nuts and traceability

Fastener selection includes material/grade, diameter, thread, length, condition and associated nut/washer requirements. Compare actual marks and records with the work package. Do not mix metric and inch threads because they initially appear to engage. Cross-threading, damaged threads, bent studs or unidentified substitutions require disposition. Smooth initial engagement is useful evidence of condition, not proof of material grade.

Keep fasteners identifiable through cleaning and assembly. Reuse is a decision under the actual procedure, not a universal right because a stud looks intact. Check required engagement and projection against the specified configuration; this course supplies no universal exposed-thread count. An assembler must not grind or modify a fastener to make an unsupported arrangement fit.

**Worked case:** B54 needs eight matched studs; six have valid identity and two are unmarked. Record two unresolved components and obtain authorized replacements or disposition. **Changed case:** identity is recovered, but one thread is visibly damaged. Traceability resolution does not clear physical condition. Separate each finding and final verification.

## Q06 — Gasket selection and handling

The gasket must match the specified service, geometry, material and installation method. Nonmetallic sheet, spiral-wound and metallic ring arrangements are not interchangeable simply because their diameters are close. Check identification, dimensions, storage and visible condition against the approved basis. Keep sealing surfaces free of contamination and avoid bending or damaging the gasket during handling.

Do not stack gaskets, reuse a removed gasket or add sealant unless the actual approved procedure explicitly supports that configuration. Such changes can alter seating and load behavior. Manufacturer guidance is product-specific; Garlock's referenced guide covers named product families and is not a universal instruction for every gasket.[S05]

**Worked case:** the specified new gasket is missing, and someone offers a previously compressed one of the same size. Hold the substitution and obtain the approved component. **Changed case:** a new correctly identified gasket has a damaged edge. Identity is correct, condition is not established acceptable. Record the damage and obtain disposition rather than trim it casually.

## Q07 — Cleanliness and flange-face condition

Preparation must leave the sealing surfaces in the condition required by the joint procedure. Remove residual material using approved methods that do not damage the face or alter its required finish. Inspect accessible surfaces for contamination, corrosion, scratches, deformation and other relevant damage. Record location and dimensions where the method supports them. A shiny appearance is not proof of an acceptable sealing finish.

Do not invent a permissible scratch depth or machine a face without authorization. Surface finish and defect acceptance depend on the actual joint/gasket basis. Protect cleaned faces while other work proceeds. If preparation exposes a previously hidden defect, treat it as a new finding rather than conceal it beneath the gasket.

**Worked case:** an inert teaching face has residue across one quadrant and a marked radial scratch. Identify both and request the supplied disposition; cleaning does not erase the scratch. **Changed case:** residue is removed, but the scratch's depth cannot be measured by the available tool. Record the measurement limitation and obtain an appropriate assessment. Do not label an unmeasured defect within tolerance.

## Q08 — Alignment, support and gaps

Joint alignment includes more than whether bolts enter holes. Compare orientation, gap and parallelism with the approved procedure and inspect the support condition. Uncontrolled loads or forced fit can affect assembly and later service. Do not use bolts as an improvised alignment tool unless the actual engineering procedure specifically provides a controlled method. The assembler does not redesign supports or move connected equipment on personal judgment.

Measure at defined positions with an appropriate tool and record the reference. Uneven gaps can indicate an issue needing investigation, but the readings alone do not identify every cause. Keep alignment acceptance separate from final tightening and any piping-stress or equipment-alignment requirements.

**Worked case:** B54 supplied gap readings are 3.0, 3.2, 3.1, 3.8 mm; fictional maximum spread 0.5 mm. Spread 0.8 exceeds by 0.3 mm. Stop the affected assembly step and seek authorized correction. **Changed case:** readings 3.1, 3.2, 3.0, 3.3 give 0.3 mm spread, meeting that comparison. Missing support approval remains unresolved; a passing gap is not overall installation acceptance.

## Q09 — Lubrication and friction

Applied torque is influenced by friction in threads and bearing surfaces. The approved lubricant, application locations and condition must match the target's basis. Changing lubricant can change achieved load at the same torque. Do not substitute grease, mix products or apply lubricant to a surface not covered by the procedure merely because it makes assembly easier. Check material/service compatibility and product identity.

The classroom relationship T=KFd illustrates sensitivity, where T is torque, F preload, d nominal diameter and K a supplied empirical factor. It is not a field torque-selection formula without a validated basis. Convert diameter to metres for N·m and newtons. A generic K value is not a measured fact about the actual joint.

**Worked case:** supplied K0.20, F10000N, d0.01 m yields 20N·m. At unchanged 20N·m and K0.15, the simplified implied load is 13333.33N, 33.33% higher. **Changed case:**K0.25 gives 8000N, 20% lower. The lesson is to preserve the approved lubrication/target pairing, not to choose field torque from these synthetic examples.

## Q10 — Load, elongation and gasket compression

Bolts act elastically within their appropriate working range, but the joint's response also involves gasket compression and component stiffness. A torque reading is not a direct universal measurement of preload. Stretch-based or tensioning methods require their own geometry, calibration and procedure. Do not assume equal torque guarantees equal load or that more tightening always improves sealing; excessive load can damage components.

For an idealized classroom elastic bolt, elongationδ=FL/(AE), with consistent units and a supplied effective area. Real threaded assemblies and measurement methods need more detailed treatment. This calculation teaches proportionality and units, not a target for a pressure boundary.

**Worked case:**F10000N, L100 mm, A100 mm², E200000N/mm² givesδ0.05 mm. **Changed case:**F12000N gives 0.06 mm. If measured elongation differs, first examine reference, geometry, temperature and method under the approved process; do not automatically add torque. Gasket stress and service performance require the actual design basis, not this bolt-only model.

## Q11 — Manual tools and calibration

Select a tool suitable for the required torque, direction, access and fastener. Verify identification, calibration/verification status, range and condition before use. A certificate for another serial number does not establish the tool in hand. Check sockets and adapters for correct fit and approved use. Do not use damaged tools or unapproved handle extensions.

Follow the manufacturer's loading technique and storage instructions. A reading or click is meaningful only when the tool is used as intended. An in-line extension can alter effective lever length; a perpendicular arrangement may behave differently. Use the actual approved correction method rather than assume every adapter has no effect. Classroom calculations are not permission to modify a field tool setup.

**Worked case:** supplied wrench effective length 300 mm, in-line extension 60 mm, desired joint torque 24N·m. Under the stated ideal geometry, setpoint 24×300/360=20N·m. **Changed case:** an approved configuration has zero additional effective length; setpoint remains 24. If geometry or manufacturer approval is unknown, stop and obtain the correct basis instead of applying either answer automatically.

## Q12 — Powered torque and reaction hazards

Powered torque tools introduce reaction forces, pinch points, hoses, pressure and stored energy. Match tool, socket/cassette, pump and accessories to the manufacturer's approved system. Verify the reaction arrangement, clearances and communication before any authorized operation. Keep hands out of the reaction path; do not use a body part or improvised object to restrain the tool. Enerpac's safety guidance emphasizes proper fit and reaction-arm use for its tools.[S06]

This course uses de-energized identification and planning exercises only. Actual hydraulic/pneumatic operation requires equipment-specific training and the employer's authorization. Do not infer a pump-pressure setting from a generic torque value; use the correct tool's current pressure/torque data and limits. A hose or coupling problem requires the manufacturer's safe depressurization and service process, not a hand search for a pressurized leak.

**Worked case:** a tool's chart belongs to another model and the proposed reaction point is uncertain. Record both gaps and do not energize. **Changed case:** the correct chart arrives, but socket fit is wrong. One corrected document does not make the setup safe. Demonstrate recognition on inert equipment and state the required competent follow-up.

## Q13 — Tensioning and special-joint limits

Hydraulic tensioning applies load through a different process from torque tightening. Tool engagement, available stud length, hydraulic system, load transfer and the approved sequence matter. A torque target cannot be relabeled as a tensioner pressure. Multi-tool or special-joint methods require their specific procedures and qualifications. Large-diameter, ring-joint or wellhead assemblies may have requirements beyond the benign manual fixture used here.

The assembler must identify when the work exceeds their demonstrated scope. Ask for the actual equipment and joint method rather than extrapolate from another system. Record tool coverage, sequence and final verification according to the approved plan; do not invent compensation factors for load transfer.

**Worked case:** a work package requires tensioning but provides only a manual-torque record and no tensioner setup. Hold the method unresolved. **Changed case:** a tensioner is available but thread engagement evidence is missing. Tool presence is not readiness. This course's completion record distinguishes manual fixture performance from unperformed powered/special-joint qualification.

## Q14 — Controlled assembly sequence

After the approved preparation and release checks, position the correct gasket and components without damage, establish the specified initial condition and follow the documented tightening sequence and stages. Track each location so no bolt is omitted or counted twice. The sequence and passes must match the actual procedure; this course's eight-position training pattern is only a benign fixture exercise.

Monitor the specified condition between stages and stop on unexpected movement, damaged components, incorrect readings or loss of tool control. Do not conceal a skipped stage by signing the final column. Manufacturer guidance for the cited gasket families uses staged tightening and a final sequential pass, but actual product/joint instructions govern.[S05]

**Worked case:** B54 classroom target 8N·m with supplied stages 25%, 50%, 100% gives 2, 4, 8N·m. Fixture positions 1–8 are numbered clockwise; exercise cross-pattern 1, 5, 3, 7, 2, 6, 4, 8 is followed at each stage, then the supplied sequential verification. **Changed case:** bolt 6's second-stage record is absent. Stop and obtain the instructor's controlled recovery instruction; do not invent the missing reading. No value here is a field assembly target.

## Q15 — In-process observations and interruptions

An interruption can leave the joint at an intermediate condition. Preserve which steps were completed, by whom, with which tool and settings. Protect the assembly against contamination or accidental alteration. Before resuming, reconcile the actual condition with the approved restart instructions. A new shift must not infer completion from a paint mark alone.

Record unexpected friction, damaged threads, gasket movement, unequal gaps or tool issues as observations. Do not diagnose every resistance change as a bad bolt or compensate with unapproved torque. If the tool is found out of tolerance, identify the potentially affected work and obtain the required assessment rather than silently replacing it and continuing.

**Worked case:** a tool check fails after four of eight joints were assembled. Identify those four by records and retain the scope for technical disposition. **Changed case:** the replacement tool is valid, but affected joint evidence has not been reviewed. Future work may have a valid tool; past work is not automatically accepted. Keep the two decisions separate.

## Q16 — Post-assembly verification

Final verification checks the completed configuration and required records against the procedure. Confirm joint identity, components, gasket position where inspectable, specified gaps/engagement, completed sequence and any required inspection sign-off. A checked form is not proof if the actual work or identity is unsupported. Preserve any unresolved deviation before handing the joint to the next stage.

An assembly record and a pressure/leak test answer different questions. Testing requires its own approved plan, boundaries, equipment and authorization. The assembler must not pressurize the classroom fixture or conduct an improvised leak test. A test pass also does not authorize concealment of an assembly deviation that requires separate disposition.

**Worked case:** twelve joints are listed, ten have valid complete assembly records, one has an unresolved gasket deviation and one has the wrong tag. Valid coverage 83.33%; two remain open. **Changed case:** tag corrected with valid evidence yields 11/12=91.67%, but the gasket issue remains. Report the exact open joint rather than rounding the job to complete.

## Q17 — Leaks and abnormal conditions

A leak or abnormal condition is a reason to follow the operating response and safe isolation process, not to add torque reflexively. Temperature, pressure, component damage and stored energy can make intervention hazardous. The cited Garlock guidance does not recommend retightening its flange connections after temperature or pressure is applied.[S05] This course authorizes no live retightening or hot bolting.

Report location and observed condition from a safe position, protect people through established controls and contact the responsible operator. After the system is made safe under its actual procedure, the authorized investigation can review components, alignment, lubrication, assembly records and service changes. Preserve failed parts or records as directed; do not erase the evidence by an undocumented repair.

**Worked case:** a joint leaks during an authorized test and someone requests “one more turn.” Do not comply; follow the test/emergency controls and responsible authority. **Changed case:** pressure has been removed but residual temperature and trapped inventory are unverified. Safe intervention is still not established. A single zero gauge does not answer all hazards.

## Q18 — Disassembly, inspection and reassembly records

Disassembly requires the actual authorized work and isolation plan. The course does not prescribe a universal first bolt or opening direction. Once safe access is established by the responsible process, preserve component identity and observations for the investigation. Record damage, gasket condition and relevant assembly history without assuming the cause from one mark.

Reassembly is a new controlled configuration. Determine required replacements, approved reuse, face preparation, support and procedure changes. An old successful record cannot be reused as proof of the new assembly. Retain the relationship between original failure, disposition, actual work and verification.

**Worked case:** a gasket was replaced after a leak, but the record still carries the original assembly date/tool. Create the required traceable reassembly evidence; do not present the old record as current. **Changed case:** all new work is recorded, but the failure investigation imposes a support check that remains pending. Keep that condition open through handover.

## Q19 — Records, traceability and quality control

The record should identify joint/system, procedure revision, components, lubricant, tool serial/status, assembler, dates, stage results, interruptions, deviations and verification. Record actual actions, not an intended schedule. Use the designated correction method so changes remain traceable. Blank or illegible entries need resolution, not invented values.

An independent review may compare records with the physical joint and work scope. Help that review by retaining exact IDs and evidence references. Quantity totals should distinguish prepared, assembled, inspected and tested joints. Do not treat these stages as interchangeable or assume all counts refer to the same joints.

**Worked case:** twenty prepared, eighteen assembled, sixteen verified and fourteen tested. Report each stage and its actual identity map; no single “twenty complete” claim is supported. **Changed case:** two more records verified, but they concern joints already counted. Duplicates do not increase verified coverage. Reconcile unique identities before reporting progress.

## Q20 — Handover and continuing competence

Handover communicates the verified configuration, evidence and remaining restrictions to the designated owner. The assembler can state which assembly tasks were completed under which basis; operating release belongs to the actual authority. Identify unresolved components, interrupted work, failed tool checks and required technical dispositions before the system advances.

Maintain competence within the methods actually demonstrated. A manual fixture assessment is not proof of hydraulic tensioning, energized equipment operation or every special-joint design. Changes in tools, joint types, procedures or observed performance may require additional instruction and assessment. Keep certificates and skill records accurate rather than broadening their wording to cover untested tasks.

**Worked case:** a learner passes manual fixture assembly and de-energized powered-tool hazard identification. Record both outcomes with their limits; do not call the learner a qualified hydraulic operator. **Changed case:** the employer later supplies equipment-specific training and actual assessment. Record that separate evidence and authorization, not a retroactive claim that this course already covered it. Honest scope is part of competent assembly practice.

# VUC054 workbook, practical delivery and credential

## Instructor, equipment and delivery

Instructor must be competent in the actual manual assembly method and the training fixture, with access to qualified bolting/engineering specialists for questions beyond that scope. Assess safety, reading, units and arithmetic prerequisites; supply instruction before assessment. Plan 40 hours: 10 instruction/checks, 8 labs, 14 stations, 4 integrated assembly/remediation and 4 final. Breaks and extra remediation are additional; record actual attendance, not just the plan.

Use a purpose-built stable, unconnected, unpressurized eight-position training fixture, compatible reusable training gasket, identified fasteners, benign approved training lubricant, correct sockets, a suitable verified low-range manual torque tool, ruler/caliper/feeler gauges, reference cards and printed records. Instructor confirms fixture/component/tool suitability for the supplied 8N·m maximum and records the manufacturer's setup instructions. If that suitability cannot be established, use a different documented benign fixture with its approved values and reissue all exercise references; never apply 8N·m blindly. Guard pinch points and secure the fixture. No fluid, pressure, live process, powered hydraulic operation, hot work or line breaking occurs.

Training-only reusable gasket/fixture is not an example permitting field gasket reuse. Clearly label it. Actual observed stations P04–P08, P11, P14 and P16 plus integrated I54 are mandatory. Remote delivery requires supervised equivalent equipment and known references; otherwise affected outcomes remain pending. Other stations use supplied records or de-energized equipment/images. Safe accessible tools/response formats may be substituted while preserving outcomes. No unassessed powered-tool endorsement is awarded.

## B54 approved classroom work card

Joint J1; procedureT54 revision 1; eight positions numbered 1–8 clockwise. Instructor fills actual fixture, tool serial, component IDs, lubricant and reference dimensions before issue. Supplied training stages 2/4/8N·m use pattern 1, 5, 3, 7, 2, 6, 4, 8; final sequential verification 1–8 at 8N·m. These are fixture-only values. Instructor verifies the tool is suitable at every stage or supplies a separately approved low-range tool. Require gasket centering and supplied initial condition before stages. Monitor hands/fixture, gaps and component condition throughout. Unexpected movement or damaged parts stops the exercise.

For an interrupted/missing stage, the learner stops and reports actual state. Instructor inspects the fixture and supplies a documented safe recovery instruction; do not improvise a universal field recovery rule. For reassessment, disassemble/reset only under the instructor's safe fixture instructions, issue changed identification/defect cards and require a fresh complete record. The instructor's known reference list is withheld during scored performance but is available for debrief.

Forms:

- Preparation: learner/date, joint/system, procedure/revision, isolation/fixture status, parts/marks, gasket/condition, faces, gaps/support, lubricant, tool serial/range/status, hold release.
- Stage sheet: bolt position, stage target, actual completion/reading as method permits, tool, operator, time, interruption and disposition. Never invent a continuous measured torque when the tool only supplies a setpoint/click indication.
- Deviation: observation, requirement, affected joints, safe hold, authority, disposition, actual correction and verification.
- Handover: unique joint IDs, assembly records, inspection status, testing status, open conditions, owner and release boundary.
- Assessment: original response, score, critical error, feedback, remediation, changed attempt, actual equipment/reference, assessor/date/version.

Labs use five dimensions 0–2: identity, calculation/method, comparison, safety/authority and traceable communication. Pass 8/10 with no zero or unresolved critical error. Correct all formative answers with reasoning and revisit the chapter/changed case. Keep unsuccessful attempts and targeted instruction.

## I54 — Complete manual fixture assembly

Learner receives the B54 card, actual identified fixture/components and seeded documentation/condition discrepancies. First resolve the mismatches through the instructor's controlled issue process; do not assume they are harmless because it is training. Demonstrate preparation, component checks, gasket position, gap measurement, tool checks, staged manual assembly and final verification. Record actual work, including any interruption. Give handover with unperformed powered methods and field release explicitly excluded.

Assessor scores six dimensions 0–2: identity/preparation, component/face checks, measurement/tool setup, sequence/control, final record, and safety/authority. Pass 10/12 with no zero or unresolved critical error. All mandatory actual stations must also pass. For changed assessment reset the fixture under its instructions, change component/record defects and require a fresh sequence. Never seed a physical hazard; simulated defects use clearly safe inert samples or cards.

# VUC054 sources and coverage

Reviewed 2026-11-18. Original available-source instruction; full controlled API/ASME standards were not reviewed. All B54 torque, gap, load, geometry and scoring data are synthetic. The course does not reproduce proprietary tables or claim external qualification.

- **S01 [API BFJA program](https://www.api.org/products-and-services/individual-certification-programs/certifications/apibfja)** and [public 2026–2028 topic outline](https://www.api.org/-/media/Files/Certification/ICP/ICP-Certification-Programs/BFJA/202603_BFJA_BOK_2026_Final.pdf). Subject context: joint components, lubrication, tools and assembly inspection stages. The outline contains an apparent “Inspector” wording inconsistency under the Assembler title; no inference of interchangeable credentials is made. External examination and eligibility remain API processes.

This is a reference list, not proof of full-text review or current project applicability.
- **S03 [ASME bolted flange joint assembly program](https://www.asme.org/learning-development/learning-experiences/bolted-flange-joint-assembly-program).** Distinguishes levels of component knowledge, manual preparation/assembly and higher-level methods. Supports keeping academic and practical scope explicit. This original course awards neither ASME qualification nor its microcredentials.
- **S04 [OSHA 1910.147](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147).** General-industry hazardous-energy control framework. Actual employer procedure/applicability governs; the course conducts no process isolation or line breaking and does not train every authorized-employee function.

No torque table, universal lubricant, washer rule or field sequence is copied. B54 fixture values are independently supplied teaching data.
- **S06 [Enerpac hydraulic torque wrench safety handbook](https://enerpac-stg.kibocommerce.com/en-US/resources/education/safety-handbook-for-hydraulic-torque-wrenches).** Publisher-hosted guidance supporting correct fit, approved reaction arrangements and model-specific instructions. No operating pressure values or live actuation procedure are reproduced. The practical is de-energized hazard identification only.
