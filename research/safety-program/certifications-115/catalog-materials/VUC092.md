# VNDRLY/U Onshore Fire/Emergency Response Team Member — Further Training

VUC092. Original available-source materials, 18November 2026. Existing trained members only; not initial firefighter instruction. Actual professional live-fire delivery and individual assessment are required for the full learner award. No hazardous activity was performed during authoring. Full external specification unavailable; no OPITO approval or exact parity claimed.

## Q92-1 — Existing capability and the team-member role

Further training is for an existing onshore response team member whose prior training, practice and assigned duties can be authenticated. It refreshes performance under controlled conditions; it does not turn a novice into an operational firefighter. Tell the instructor about changed equipment, long gaps or participation concerns before assessment. Current fitness and respiratory-program requirements are checked through the proper qualified process, not by peer pressure.

A member works within the team leader's brief and the brigade's scope. Understand the task, location, method, limits, communications, escape and withdrawal triggers before accepting it. Ask for clarification when an instruction is ambiguous. Reporting a capability limit is a safety action, not disobedience. Do not freelance to another team, operate an unfamiliar system or assume incident command because you arrive first.

Worked example: member has practiced exterior tasks but is assigned unfamiliar interior work. State the limitation and request appropriate instruction/resource reassignment; do not learn by entering. Changed example: new radio model is issued just before the exercise. Complete familiarization and checks before the task rather than assume identical controls.

## Q92-2 — Recognizing hazards and reporting change

Fire scenes combine heat, smoke, energy, structural and process hazards. Members observe and communicate changes while carrying out the assigned task. Do not wait for the leader to notice every condition. Report what you see, where and how it affects your task or escape. Keep observations separate from diagnoses; an unusual sound or smoke change is a reason to report and reassess, not a basis for an invented tactical remedy.

Use the current preplan and briefing to identify excluded areas, material uncertainties, process isolation and surrounding exposures. Do not approach an unknown container or suspected hazardous area just to identify it. If a condition exceeds the task limits, stop/withdraw as trained and inform the leader. The absence of visible flame does not establish a safe atmosphere or stable structure.

Worked example: a marked route becomes affected by a controller's simulated smoke card. The member alerts the leader and follows the approved withdrawal/alternate arrangement, not the previous route by habit. Changed example: a process valve is described as isolation requested. Do not repeat that as isolated; report the exact unverified status.

## Q92-3 — Protective equipment and readiness checks

Inspect and use the protective equipment specified by the actual task and program. Check identity, condition, compatibility and correct fit using the manufacturer's procedure. Do not hide a damaged item because the team is waiting. A successful buddy check supports but does not replace your own checks or formal respiratory fit/medical requirements. Personal protective equipment reduces specified risks; it does not make every environment acceptable.

Know the actual equipment's donning, operational checks, limitations, alarms and doffing/maintenance arrangements. Complete unfamiliar-equipment practice in a safe environment before assessment. Keep contaminated and clean equipment separate. If a component is damaged or incompatible, remove it from service through the authorized route and inform the leader of the resulting availability limit.

Worked example: a glove is damaged and a replacement interferes with another item. The member does not accept the ensemble as ready until suitable compatible protection is obtained. Changed example: facepiece seal is affected by interference. Resolve through the respiratory program; shortening the task does not repair the seal.

## Q92-4 — Breathing apparatus, contact and withdrawal

Follow the actual breathing-apparatus procedure and report starting condition, checks and changes as required. Maintain team contact and monitor your own equipment and condition. Tell the leader early about unexpected consumption, alarm, leakage or difficulty. Do not remove respiratory protection in a potentially hazardous area to speak more clearly or because visible smoke has reduced. Use the trained emergency/withdrawal procedure.

Nominal cylinder duration is not guaranteed work time. Classroom A92 uses an abstract indication 250 units, threshold 170 and consumption 10 units/minute: the simple allowance is 8 minutes. At 20 units/minute it is 4 minutes. These are arithmetic illustrations only, not actual pressures, reserve rules or entry permission. Real work rates, route length, alarms and the limiting member's condition govern under the actual program.

Worked example: your indication drops unexpectedly while your buddy's appears normal. Inform the leader and follow the team's stop/withdrawal procedure; do not average readings or remain to protect the schedule. Changed example: contact with the buddy is lost. Apply the trained lost-contact/emergency procedure immediately; do not continue the assigned task alone.

## Q92-5 — Equipment handling and coordinated movement

Refresh handling of the actual hose, branch/nozzle, connections and other assigned equipment under qualified instruction. Inspect before use and confirm compatibility and operating procedure. Secure footing and team positions matter; unexpected movement or reaction can injure people. Do not energize equipment before the team is ready or change operating settings without the agreed communication. Follow the actual equipment and facility limits rather than a generic pressure from a classroom example.

Route equipment to maintain access and escape and avoid sharp edges, traffic and entanglement. Coordinate lifting and movement with the team. If a coupling, hose or control is damaged, report and make safe through the prescribed procedure; do not attempt an improvised live repair. Release stored energy as required before disconnection or maintenance by authorized personnel.

Worked example: a hose coupling is visibly damaged during preuse inspection. Reject it and report unavailable capability; do not wait to see whether it leaks under pressure. Changed example: the team is repositioning and a member is not ready. Hold the movement/operation and obtain acknowledgment rather than treating silence as readiness.

## Q92-6 — Media, application and task limits

Know the medium and application method selected by the approved plan and leader. Material compatibility and equipment suitability matter. Do not substitute water, foam or another agent because it is nearby. Unknown material or unverified electrical/process isolation requires clarification. An operator's request for isolation is not confirmation of completion. Coordinate with the leader rather than touching process controls without authority.

During controlled application, perform the assigned technique within your existing training and the facility procedure. Observe the effect and report unexpected behavior. Do not change strategy independently. Foam proportioning and product compatibility are specific; the arithmetic of a percentage does not validate the system or material. Environmental controls remain part of the task.

Worked example: fictional 3% solution of 500 litres consists of 15 litres concentrate and 485 litres water. This is a learning calculation, not a mixing instruction for a real product. Changed example: 2% of 800 litres is 16 litres concentrate and 784 water. Even correct quantities do not permit use of an unapproved concentrate or incompatible equipment.

## Q92-7 — Radio discipline and personal accountability

Use clear messages with identity, location, condition and needed action. Acknowledge critical instructions and report completion or inability. Do not hide misunderstanding behind a quick affirmative response. Emergency traffic follows the local procedure and should identify the emergency and last known position without unnecessary narrative. If radio communication fails, use the trained backup/withdrawal arrangement rather than continue silently.

Check in, stay with the assigned crew and report changes of task/location/status. After withdrawal, confirm your identity at the accountability point. Do not answer for someone you have not verified. A tag or duplicate name is not a person. If a buddy is unaccounted for, report immediately with the last known information and follow the emergency process.

Worked example: return listM1, M2, M2, M4 for assignedM1–M4 has onlythree unique identities; M3 is unresolved. Do not sign all returned. Changed example: M3 is confirmed in rehabilitation. Update the location/status through the authorized channel; that does not meanM3 is ready for the next assignment.

## Q92-8 — Controlled live task and withdrawal performance

Before the evolution, repeat the assigned task, limits, escape and stop signals. During the task, maintain team coordination and watch for change. Stop unsafe action and report it even when the exercise objective is unfinished. A member is assessed on safe independent performance within the team, not on whether the prop flame goes out at any cost.

When withdrawal is ordered or required by the actual procedure, acknowledge and move with the team by the approved safe route. Keep equipment and contact under control as trained, then report at the accountability point. Do not detach to recover a tool or improve a photograph. If a problem prevents withdrawal, communicate it as an emergency promptly. Never defeat a facility safety interlock or ignore the independent safety officer.

Worked example: a simulated supply-loss card is announced at a safe decision point while the real safety supply remains intact. The member follows the leader's controlled withdrawal and confirms identity. Changed example: a supervisor says finish the last bit after the emergency stop. Follow the stop and report the conflicting instruction; assessment pressure does not override safety authority.

## Q92-9 — Casualty support and emergency signals

A member must know how to call for help and assist only within trained duties. Report identity, location, last task and observable condition. Keep the rest of the crew informed and controlled. Do not launch an unprotected rescue or improvise a casualty carry. The designated rescue/medical personnel and command structure coordinate the response with suitable protection and equipment.

In practice, use a mannequin and approved handling method only with trained personnel and suitable supervision. No real person is trapped, deprived of air or exposed to create realism. A real injury or symptom immediately changes the event from exercise to actual emergency. Provide accurate information and access for medical responders; do not diagnose away a symptom or delay care to complete assessment.

Worked example: your buddy is unresponsive in a non-live card scenario. Send the trained emergency message and follow the leader/rescue plan while maintaining your own safety. Changed example: a participant reports real dizziness during the live evolution. Call the actual stop/medical response and follow the facility plan; do not classify it as a scripted card because another casualty scenario was expected.

## Q92-10 — Contamination, hygiene and rehabilitation

Smoke, debris and response agents can contaminate equipment and people. Follow the actual clean/dirty boundary and decontamination arrangements. Do not put contaminated gloves or gear into clean vehicles or eating areas. Unknown contamination needs appropriate identification and specialist advice; do not choose a cleaning chemical by guesswork. Record possible exposure through the employer process.

Report fatigue, heat discomfort, breathing difficulty or other symptoms early. Use the designated rehabilitation and medical arrangements. Being willing to continue does not establish readiness. Remain unavailable while medical review or required recovery is incomplete, and notify the leader of that status. Support colleagues in reporting concerns without ridicule. Psychological support after distressing events is also part of responsible recovery.

Worked example: gear receives a simulated contamination tag. Route it to the designated controlled area, not the clean cab. Changed example: after a rest period you are still awaiting medical assessment. Do not accept another task; state that you remain unavailable until the appropriate release.

## Q92-11 — Equipment recovery, records and learning

At task completion, follow the actual shutdown and equipment-restoration procedure. Inspection, cleaning, testing and replenishment are assigned to competent personnel. Tag defects and keep them separate from serviceable equipment. Do not return an item to stock just because the incident is over. Record what you actually used, observed and reported, including failures and exposure concerns.

Debrief your own actions with the team. Describe a skill gap or plan problem accurately and propose a practical correction. A log should distinguish observation, assisted practice and independent performance. Do not sign for a task performed by another member. Changes to equipment or duties require appropriate familiarization and review before future response.

Worked example: you watched a colleague operate a branch/nozzle but did not perform that task. Record observation only. Changed example: you performed it but required a safety prompt. Preserve that assisted result, practice the gap and complete a changed independent attempt; do not rewrite the first attempt as passed.

## Q92-12 — Further-training assessment and bounded award

This package requires individual performance within two changed professionally controlled live-fire scenarios and a non-live emergency/contamination exercise. Prior competence and current readiness are prerequisites. The facility controls props, fuels, limits, safety staffing and shutdown. Written teaching and dry rehearsal support the practical work but do not replace it. Every learner must perform the required member tasks, not stand in a group while others demonstrate.

Assessment considers preparation, protection, apparatus use, equipment handling, task understanding, communication, crew integrity, withdrawal, emergency support and recovery. Critical unsafe action prevents a pass regardless of total marks. Remediate and reassess with changed conditions after safe practice. If live delivery is not available, record only the completed knowledge/development component; do not issue the full further-training award.

Worked example: learner passes theory and attends both scenarios but never handles the assigned equipment. Practical evidence is incomplete. Changed example: learner independently recognizes the stop trigger and withdraws before completing the prop objective. That is correct safe performance. The resulting VNDRLY record states actual assessed scope and does not claim OPITO renewal, leader qualification or additional employer authority.

Qualified personnel handle fitness/medical and respiratory-program requirements privately. Provide accessible instruction and safe familiarization; do not waive essential individual performance. Instructors must be competent in actual systems, live-fire training and assessment. An independent safety/controller function, qualified prop/system operators, adequate trained team/backup and medical support remain separate from the assessed learner.

Use a professionally operated fire-training facility and its approved live-fire procedures. Resources include suitable approved props and media, inspected actual PPE/respiratory and firefighting equipment, radios/backup, accountability system, safe mannequin, clean/dirty and rehab areas, scenario cards and forms. ## I92 required integrated scenarios

ScenarioA live: approved facility evolution within candidate's existing scope. Candidate receives leader briefing, states limits, checks own protection/equipment, demonstrates coordinated equipment handling and assigned application, maintains contact/reports conditions, and follows planned withdrawal at controller's safe cue. Candidate personally confirms identity and completes recovery/rehab reporting. Independent safety/operator maintains all real safeguards. Observation alone is not a pass.

ScenarioB live: changed approved task/prop configuration and individual equipment role. Before dependent action, give incompatible-material or unverified-isolation card; candidate must query/hold rather than act. Once actual readiness is properly released, perform approved live role, then changed withdrawal/communication cue at safe point. Candidate preserves crew integrity, uses trained backup if needed and gives accurate accountability. No real supply loss or apparatus fault is induced.

ScenarioC non-live: missing-buddyR92 card, mannequin support under trained method, contamination tag and pending-medical status. Candidate sends emergency message, works within leader/rescue plan, segregates gear and reports unavailable status. Real symptom discussion tests actual override. No untrained lifting or deliberate exposure.

Twelve dimensions matchP92-1 through 12. Score 0 = absent/unsafe, 1 = major coaching, 2 = safe but incomplete or prompted, 3 = independent acceptable, 4 = accurate independent with reasoning. Require≥39/48, every dimension≥3, all stations and all three scenarios pass, no critical error. Both live scenarios independently meet applicable safety/member criteria; written score cannot compensate. Critical: unsupported entry, removed/bypassed protection, ignored stop, freelancing, unreported missing buddy, false accountability, unsafe discharge/handling, real-emergency delay or fabricated evidence. Stop danger immediately. Retain failure, teach/practice and reassess changed task independently.
