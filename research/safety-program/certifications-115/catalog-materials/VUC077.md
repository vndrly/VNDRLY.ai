# VUC077 — Well Control in Design and Lifecycle Management

## Purpose and entry requirements

This original VNDRLY/U course teaches engineers, planners and integrity personnel to make and document defensible well-control decisions throughout a well's life. It is not an IWCF award, professional engineering license, complete well-design qualification or authorization to approve a real well. Entrants need basic well design/intervention knowledge, introductory well-control competence and awareness of crew resource management. Diagnose gaps before advanced work. Use the individual project and changed oral defense to demonstrate reasoning, not attendance alone.

All numerical data below belong to fictional D77. Assigned allowable values are exercise inputs, not industry defaults. The course covers the published compulsory design/lifecycle topics and the listed optional subjects at decision-review level. Advanced material selection, structural analysis, relief-well engineering and jurisdictional approvals require qualified specialist work. Real field documents must be reviewed against applicable current law, standards, installed equipment and subsurface evidence.

## Q01 — Design decisions as lifecycle commitments

A well design is a set of future operating possibilities and constraints. The design team must consider construction, production or injection, interventions, suspension and abandonment before selecting the initial architecture. A choice that simplifies drilling can make later isolation, surveillance or access difficult. Begin with the well's purpose, expected fluids and pressures, credible changes, environment, service life and eventual disposition. Record why alternatives were selected or rejected, who owns unresolved assumptions and what evidence is needed before each release gate.

Separate the operator's accountability, technical design authority, independent examination, contractor execution and regulator's role. A reviewer signature is evidence of a defined review, not transfer of every responsibility. Deliver a design basis, barrier strategy, operating envelope, verification plan and lifecycle handover requirements together. Keep revisions connected so a drawing change cannot leave an obsolete pressure limit in the field procedure.

**Worked case:** an existing producer is proposed for injection. The team cannot approve conversion solely because the tree pressure rating exceeds the proposed surface pressure. Fluid compatibility, pressure/thermal loads, annular isolation, monitoring and abandonment consequences need review. **Changed case:** ownership changes but service does not. Transfer actual records and unresolved integrity issues; a new company name does not reset equipment condition or remove restrictions.

## Q02 — Subsurface evidence and pressure windows

Use offset data, geology, seismic interpretation, pore-pressure estimates, formation-strength information, faults and uncertainty to build a depth-referenced hazard model. An offset well is evidence, not a guarantee: depletion, compartmentalization and local formations can differ. Distinguish measured observations, interpreted values and conservative assumptions. State datum and true vertical depth for pressure calculations; measured length belongs in volume and friction models.

For the original D77 screening interval at 10,000 ft TVD, assigned pore-pressure equivalent is 12.0 ppg and formation-strength equivalent 14.5 ppg. Their simplified pressure equivalents are 6,240 and 7,540 psi using 0.052 × ppg × TVD. The raw difference is 1,300 psi. Assigned uncertainty reduces the usable comparison to pore upper bound 12.4 and strength lower bound 14.0: 6,448 and 7,280 psi, difference 832. Neither number is a final operating window; surge, swab, circulation and prescribed margins still matter.

**Worked case:** the forecast enters a depleted zone beneath a higher-pressure layer. Evaluate isolation and the conflicting limits rather than averaging pressures. **Changed case:** new data move pore upper bound to 13.2 while strength lower remains 14.0. The raw bounded difference falls to 416 psi. Escalate the changed design basis before committing to the old plan.

## Q03 — Holistic architecture and combined loads

Casing, cement, wellhead, completion and pressure-control equipment interact. Establish load cases for construction, pressure testing, production, injection, thermal cycling, evacuation, intervention and abandonment. Burst, collapse, axial force, buckling and combined effects cannot be accepted through one nominal pressure number. Consider how an internal pressure change affects surrounding annuli and how installation tolerances affect the available barrier envelope.

An original screening card gives internal pressure 6,200 psi, external pressure 1,800 and an already-derived allowable differential of 5,000 for one stated load case. Differential is 4,400, utilization 88%, numerical residual 600. This comparison checks only that supplied criterion. It does not calculate material strength or combined-load capacity. If external pressure falls to 800, differential becomes 5,400, utilization 108% and excess 400: reject that case even though internal pressure is unchanged.

**Worked case:** the design passes a production burst check but lacks an evacuation/collapse assessment. Hold design release pending all credible load combinations. **Changed case:** a proposed alternate connector has the same size and nominal rating but unknown seal/temperature performance. Request qualified compatibility and load-envelope evidence; do not accept catalog similarity as interchangeability. Preserve assumptions in the design dossier so later changes can be tested against them.

## Q04 — Uncertainty, sensitivity and decision gates

A single best estimate hides the uncertainty that drives failure. Keep a register of uncertain inputs, source quality, plausible bounds, consequence, owner, required evidence and decision deadline. Explore physically credible combinations rather than adding unrelated extremes without explanation. Sensitivity analysis identifies which input changes the decision most; it does not prove a low-probability event impossible. A model should be reconciled with actual observations as drilling or operation proceeds.

For D77, an assigned dynamic equivalent-density increment of 0.6 ppg added to a selected 13.0 ppg fluid yields 13.6 ppg at the specified comparison location. Against lower strength bound 14.0 the raw residual is 0.4 ppg, or 208 psi at 10,000 ft. If the dynamic increment is instead 1.0, the model reaches 14.0 and leaves zero raw residual before any required margin. The team cannot turn an uncertain friction estimate into a fixed safe limit.

**Worked case:** the uncertainty owner promises better data after the casing decision. Identify whether the decision is reversible and whether the missing information could invalidate it; hold or select a justified alternative before the gate. **Changed case:** measurements narrow the range but come from an uncalibrated sensor. Verify measurement quality before claiming uncertainty reduction. Keep both the original forecast and the revised basis rather than overwriting inconvenient history.

## Q05 — Barrier envelopes and independence

Draw the full path from each pressure source to every potential receptor, including internal tubular paths, annuli, behind-casing communication, control lines and connections. Define the required barrier functions for each lifecycle phase and transition. A barrier element is a component of the envelope; two components do not automatically provide two complete independent barriers. Identify common dependencies such as cement placement, shared controls, power or monitoring.

For each claimed barrier, specify function, operating limits, verification method, acceptance criteria, current evidence, owner and response to impairment. State changes during displacement, testing, maintenance or equipment removal may temporarily remove a barrier. Plan the permitted transition and compensating arrangements through the applicable approval process before work begins. Do not retroactively call an unplanned loss of protection a planned transition.

**Worked case:** two valve symbols appear in series, but both depend on a failed common hydraulic supply and neither has current sealing evidence. The review cannot count two proven independent barriers. It must establish actual state and hold the affected transition. **Changed case:** valves are verified but an annular path bypasses both. Repair the envelope analysis; the number of valves inside the tubing does not isolate that path. A barrier drawing becomes useful when it predicts where fluid can travel and what evidence stops that conclusion.

## Q06 — Cement integrity and placement evidence

Cement design addresses zonal isolation and the expected mechanical, thermal and chemical service. Placement depends on hole geometry, centralization, fluid compatibility, displacement efficiency, losses, contamination and temperature/pressure history. Laboratory suitability of a slurry is necessary evidence for its purpose but does not prove it was placed correctly downhole. Record planned versus actual volumes, returns, pressures, density, mixing and timing; investigate discrepancies before relying on the cement as a barrier.

Original C77 has an assigned annular volume of 180 bbl and an exercise planning allowance of 15%, giving 207 bbl. This allowance is not a universal cement excess. If the validated slurry yield is 1.5 ft³ per sack and 1 bbl is 5.615 ft³, the theoretical requirement is 774.87 sacks, rounded up to 775 for this arithmetic exercise. Actual job design must include its full volumes and mixing constraints. A revised annular estimate of 200 bbl with the same assigned allowance becomes 230 bbl and 860.9667 theoretical sacks, or 861 rounded up.

**Worked case:** pumped volume matches plan but losses occurred. Do not infer cement top or isolation from pumped volume alone; obtain the applicable evaluation and remedial decision. **Changed case:** a bond-related log is available but conditions limit its interpretation. Combine qualified interpretation with placement and test evidence; no single attractive trace proves every possible flow path sealed.

## Q07 — Verification methods and acceptance before testing

Choose a verification method for the function and differential direction it must prove. Function testing, positive pressure testing, negative/inflow testing, logging and monitoring answer different questions. A positive test does not automatically demonstrate behavior when pressure reverses. Define the configuration, instruments, stabilization requirements, duration, acceptance criteria, communication paths and response to inconsistent readings before conducting the test.

Original T77 is a desktop record, not permission to pressure-test a real well. Its assigned criterion after stabilization is pressure decrease no greater than 20 psi over ten minutes with no unexplained flow. A record from 3,000 to 2,992 psi declines 8 and satisfies only that numerical criterion. A separate unexplained flow observation prevents acceptance. Another record from 3,000 to 2,970 declines 30 and fails the assigned pressure criterion even if no visible flow is reported.

**Worked case:** the test team explains a contradictory gauge as a nuisance after seeing the result. Hold acceptance and investigate configuration/instrument behavior; do not invent a new criterion to fit the trace. **Changed case:** the planned next phase reverses the differential. The reviewer requests verification suitable for that phase rather than treating the previous positive test as universal proof. Retain raw traces and unsuccessful attempts alongside the final interpretation.

## Q08 — Designing for influx and loss of the normal envelope

Design must make detection and response feasible, not merely state that the rig will handle a kick. Assess likely influx properties, pressure/volume evolution, formation limits, flow paths, BOP capability, choke/kill systems and surface handling. Establish the operating envelope and triggers for suspension or specialist support. Kick tolerance depends on geometry, fluid, influx assumptions and the chosen method; a single generic barrel threshold is not a complete design.

In a simplified static shoe comparison, assigned allowable total pressure is 5,000 psi at 7,000 ft TVD with uniform 12 ppg above it. Hydrostatic contribution is 4,368 psi and raw surface allowance 632. At 13 ppg the contribution is 4,732 and allowance 268. Equipment with a larger working-pressure rating does not increase the formation-based allowance. Gas distribution, friction and other weak points can further constrain the actual response.

**Worked case:** a larger influx than modeled threatens the verified handling path. The office team supports the site's approved response with validated data and specialists; it must not issue improvised valve instructions. **Changed case:** losses undermine the assumed hydrostatic barrier. Reassess the method and barrier state rather than simply increasing fluid density. Document stop criteria, escalation ownership and the evidence required before returning to normal operation.

## Q09 — Lifecycle surveillance and annular-pressure interpretation

A well's integrity state changes with pressure, temperature, corrosion, wear, intervention and aging. Establish surveillance that can detect relevant failure modes early enough to act. Trends require context: operating phase, fluid levels, temperature, tubing/annulus relationships, instrument calibration and prior interventions. Annular pressure can have different causes; a momentary low reading is not proof that a persistent source has disappeared.

Original A77 has an assigned annular limit of 800 psi and a separate action threshold of 650. Readings of 400,520 and 680 show that the latest value is 30 above the action threshold despite remaining 120 below the absolute assigned limit. Apply the specified response at the threshold; do not wait for the maximum. If pressure returns after an authorized diagnostic bleed, preserve the time/volume/rebuild record and assess the source. This course does not authorize bleeding an actual annulus.

**Worked case:** a dashboard is green because it compares only with the absolute maximum. Correct the operational interpretation and response logic through controlled change. **Changed case:** an annulus no longer holds its expected pressure and needs repeated top-ups. Low pressure can also indicate loss of integrity. Investigate and record additions, fluid and cross-annulus behavior instead of assuming lower means safer.

## Q10 — Risk management and defensible acceptance

Begin with credible initiating events and consequences, then identify prevention, detection, control and mitigation. A risk matrix is a communication aid; its colored cell does not replace engineering requirements or prove that additional controls are unnecessary. Distinguish inherent risk, actual controls with evidence, and residual risk under stated assumptions. Include uncertainty, human performance, simultaneous operations and common causes. Assign owners and expiry/review triggers to temporary arrangements.

For an original planning exercise, a scenario likelihood category 3 and consequence category 5 gives a product score of 15. A proposed control is said to reduce likelihood to 1, yielding 5, but no reliability or implementation evidence is supplied. The arithmetic is correct; the reduction is not justified. Categories are ordinal screening labels, not measured probabilities or a quantitative expected-loss calculation.

**Worked case:** a schedule benefit is offered as the sole reason to proceed with an unresolved barrier anomaly. Require the technical and governance basis for safe disposition; production pressure does not restore a barrier. **Changed case:** a lower score is justified but a mandatory criterion still fails. Hold that criterion until corrected or a legally permitted, properly authorized alternative is established. The acceptance record must name the decision maker, evidence, limitations and review point, not merely contain signatures.

## Q11 — Human and organizational performance

Office design decisions reach the worksite through drawings, programs, meetings, software and shift handovers. Every interface can lose assumptions or blur responsibility. Use plain-language operating limits, explicit hold points and closed-loop confirmation of critical changes. Invite frontline challenge before approval; personnel familiar with actual equipment may identify a control or access mismatch invisible in a design model.

The CSB Macondo investigation emphasizes management of safety-critical elements and their assurance, not reliance on personal-injury statistics as proof of major-hazard control. Apply that distinction to original D77: a team reports months without recordable injuries while barrier verification actions are overdue. The first metric cannot cancel the second. Review leading evidence such as unresolved test failures, impairment duration and quality of changes, while preserving incident/near-miss learning.

**Worked case:** a contractor uses revision B while the office has approved revision C with a different limit. Stop the affected operation at its safe state, reconcile documents and verify the team's understanding before release. **Changed case:** the right revision is present but the operator cannot explain the abort condition. Rebrief and verify comprehension. Avoid treating a signed attendance sheet as proof that a critical instruction can be executed under pressure.

## Q12 — Corrosion and material-selection decisions

Material suitability depends on the actual environment: fluid composition, water, gases, temperature, pressure, stress, erosion and foreseeable changes. Uniform wall loss is only one mechanism. Localized attack, cracking, galvanic effects and nonmetallic degradation may invalidate a simple corrosion allowance. Specify traceable material, service limits and inspection/monitoring evidence; substitutions require more than matching dimensions.

Original M77 has measured minimum wall 8.0 mm, an assigned required minimum 6.0 and a hypothetical constant uniform loss rate 0.2 mm/year. The simple time to that minimum is ten years. At 0.4 mm/year it is five. This is a sensitivity illustration, not an inspection interval or remaining-life approval. Local pits, measurement uncertainty, combined loads or changing chemistry require qualified analysis and may make either number irrelevant.

**Worked case:** CO₂/H₂S or water content differs from the design basis. Refer the change to qualified materials and well-integrity personnel and revisit the envelope before accepting continued service. **Changed case:** a replacement seal is rated for the nominal pressure but lacks compatibility at expected temperature. Hold acceptance pending exact service evidence. Retain lot/material records, approved deviations and inspection results so later reviewers can identify what was actually installed.

## Q13 — Maintenance as preservation of barrier function

Link each safety-critical function to equipment, failure modes, preventive tasks, tests, acceptance criteria and records. Scheduling maintenance is not completion; a closed work order without test evidence may conceal an unavailable function. Consider access, isolation, spare-part suitability, competence and the temporary barrier state created by maintenance. Restore and verify the complete affected system before handing it back.

Original backlog B77 contains twelve due tasks, four completed and eight outstanding. Two outstanding tasks concern required barrier verification. Overall completion is 33.33%, but the percentage alone does not express the risk of those two. Prioritize through the actual failure consequences and operating restrictions. Deferral requires documented review, compensating measures where valid, ownership and a time/condition limit; repeatedly extending a date is not a control.

**Worked case:** a valve is replaced and its actuator moves correctly, but required sealing verification was omitted. Keep the affected function restricted until the required evidence exists. **Changed case:** the part is correct but maintenance has left a monitoring line isolated. Include configuration restoration in acceptance; a local repair can impair another protective function. Records should allow the next shift to distinguish restored, restricted and unknown status without guessing.

## Q14 — Integrity assurance and independent challenge

Assurance asks whether the management system and its evidence actually establish well integrity. Sample traceable records from design assumptions through installed configuration, tests, surveillance, anomalies and corrective action. Independence means the reviewer can challenge the decision without simply approving their own unexamined work. It does not mean the operator can delegate away responsibility. Apply the jurisdiction's actual examination and verification arrangements rather than claiming one global legal scheme.

For original audit S77, eighteen of twenty sampled records have complete traceability, giving 90%. The missing two concern a barrier transition and a material substitution. Do not report the well safe merely because the percentage is high. Evaluate the consequence of each missing record and whether the missing evidence reflects a physical or procedural failure. Close findings only after the promised correction is verified.

**Worked case:** an examiner's concern is marked closed because a response email was sent. Check whether the evidence resolves the concern and whether the affected operation remained within its restrictions. **Changed case:** the evidence is sound but refers to another well serial number. Traceability fails until the correct configuration is established. A defensible assurance report separates sampled findings, unexamined areas, residual uncertainties and recommendations.

## Q15 — Shallow hazards and early construction

Early hole sections may lack the formation strength and pressure-control configuration assumed for deeper operations. Assess shallow gas, water flow, unstable sediments, faults and nearby existing wells using appropriate surveys and offset records. Plan location, casing, detection, response, diversion where applicable, evacuation and equipment exposure with specialist input. Do not transpose a conventional shut-in sequence into a shallow weak-formation situation without the approved design.

MMS/BSEE Safety Alert 212 describes a shallow-gas event where an older well log contained relevant information not recognized in the new well plan. The training lesson is to reconcile all available evidence, not assume seismic alone resolves the hazard. Diverter arrangement and exposure of personnel/equipment are part of the system review. This course supplies no universal diverter size, line routing or shallow-hole depth rule.

**Worked case:** D77 seismic interpretation shows low concern, but an offset log flags a potentially gas-bearing shallow interval. Hold the hazard closeout and obtain qualified reconciliation and the appropriate revised plan. **Changed case:** a plan is approved but the actual equipment layout places vulnerable services in the predicted exposure area. Review the installed arrangement and revise controls before drilling. A documented survey is not useful if its implications never reach the rig.

## Q16 — Suspension and abandonment from the start

Design for eventual isolation of every potential flow path and the evidence needed to demonstrate it. Suspension is a managed lifecycle state, not indefinite abandonment without a plan. Permanent abandonment must address subsurface isolation, annuli, materials, verification and residual responsibilities under the applicable jurisdiction. Preserve as-built geometry and condition so future work does not depend on an optimistic original drawing.

Original P77 has two proposed internal plugs but a behind-casing path is not addressed and one placement record is absent. The number of plugs is not enough to release the transition. Map the source-to-receptor routes, determine the actual barrier requirements, resolve placement and verification, and obtain the responsible approval. Avoid universal plug-length or test-pressure claims; geology, regulation and condition determine the design.

**Worked case:** pressure rebuilds after a nominally successful test. Retain the hold and investigate the source rather than discarding the new observation. **Changed case:** a suspended well has no current surveillance owner after an organizational change. Restore accountability, records and risk-based review before treating it as safely unattended. The final handover must identify what remains, how it was verified, and who carries the continuing obligations.

## Q17 — Tertiary response and source-control readiness

Plans for loss of normal control must connect technical options, logistics, command, personnel protection and environmental response. Specialist measures may include source control, capping, containment or relief-well planning as applicable. Their feasibility depends on the actual well, access, pressure/temperature/flow envelope and available resources. A telephone number or equipment brochure is not proof that an option can be deployed in time or safely.

Original R77 assigns mobilization 18 hours, transport 30 and setup/verification 12 with no overlap in this exercise: total 60 hours. A changed transport constraint adds 24, giving 84. These are planning arithmetic, not promised emergency response times. Evaluate weather, access, interfaces, specialist availability and dependencies, then identify alternatives and protective measures. Concurrent stages require an explicit justified schedule rather than silently subtracting time.

**Worked case:** the nominated capping equipment does not match the installed interface. Escalate the feasibility gap before operation, not after a blowout. **Changed case:** a relief-well concept exists but no subsurface targeting or rig access analysis supports it. Record it as unverified capability and obtain specialist work. Learners prepare an escalation and readiness review; they do not acquire authority to design or execute a relief well.

## Q18 — Lifecycle change, project report and handover

Changes in use, fluid, temperature, pressure, completion, ownership or service life can invalidate prior assumptions. A management-of-change record should identify the initiating change, affected barriers and load cases, technical evidence, approvals, implementation, training, document updates and verification before closure. Small changes can accumulate into a different operating envelope even when no individual work order looks major.

The final D77 project asks for a supported disposition, not a production-ready engineering design. Synthesize subsurface uncertainty, pressure/load screening, cement/test evidence, barrier paths, surveillance, maintenance, specialist limits and abandonment. Recommend proceed only for a clearly defined phase whose evidence is adequate; otherwise state the exact hold and evidence required. Do not conceal missing evidence with a polished executive summary.

**Worked case:** a project report recommends holding conversion because materials compatibility and annular isolation remain unverified, while allowing only already-approved surveillance within the existing envelope. That is a useful bounded decision. **Changed case:** new evidence resolves compatibility but not isolation. Revise only the resolved item and retain the other hold. Handover includes actual configuration, limits, evidence status, unresolved items, named owners and restart requirements. An oral defense with changed facts tests whether the candidate understands the report rather than reproducing a template.

## I77 controlled project and oral defense

The project is separate from the written final. Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Why review abandonment during initial design?

**Answer:** Architecture can constrain future isolation and verification.

**Reason/remediation:** Lifecycle choices must be considered before irreversible construction. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

Does an independent review transfer operator accountability?

**Answer:** No; it provides defined independent challenge and evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Roles and retained accountability must remain explicit. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

Why distinguish TVD from MD?

**Answer:** Vertical head uses TVD intervals; volumes and friction use actual path geometry.

**Reason/remediation:** Mixing them can conceal an invalid pressure balance. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

May an offset pressure be treated as certain?

**Answer:** No; assess depletion, compartmentalization and data relevance.

**Reason/remediation:** An offset supports an estimate but does not eliminate local uncertainty. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

Does a passed burst screen prove collapse capacity?

**Answer:** No; each credible load case and combination needs assessment.

**Reason/remediation:** A single supplied differential criterion has limited scope. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

Can a connector be substituted solely by size/rating?

**Answer:** No; verify exact service, seals, temperature and combined loads.

**Reason/remediation:** Nominal similarity does not prove interchangeability. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

What belongs in an uncertainty register?

**Answer:** Input, source quality, bounds, consequence, owner and decision deadline.

**Reason/remediation:** Uncertainty needs an actionable decision connection. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

Does an uncalibrated measurement automatically narrow uncertainty?

**Answer:** No; establish reliability before treating it as better evidence.

Why map annular paths?

Does higher BOP rating raise formation strength?
