# VUC108 — Cathodic Protection Technician

Original VNDRLY/U intermediate training. Requires demonstrated tester foundations; external certification and task authorization remain separate.

## Q108-01 — Technician scope and survey design

The technician converts a collection of readings into a defensible assessment and supervised maintenance recommendation. Begin with the asset boundary, material, coating, electrical continuity, applicable protection criteria and drawing revisions. Identify neighboring systems and responsible owners before planning interruption or current injection. Separate the question being asked—protection effectiveness, coating anomaly, interference, instrument fault or installation quality—from the instrument available. A close-interval survey and a coating survey answer different questions; neither automatically measures remaining wall thickness.

Create a test plan naming locations, sequence, equipment, reference checks, source states, synchronization, acceptance basis and restoration responsibilities. Define hold points for unexpected voltage, unsafe access, unknown bonds, damaged instruments, uncontrolled interference or uncertain identity. Agree communications and authority for every source change. Supervising testers means checking their method and evidence, not signing unread worksheets.

Worked plan: a pipeline has a low potential at one station and a nearby rail crossing. Collect validated repeat potentials, time-varying current/source records, coating information and continuity evidence before proposing an output increase. A blanket adjustment could worsen interference or overprotection elsewhere. This course requires tester-level skills or equivalent demonstrated preparation. It does not confer external CP2, electrical qualification, operator qualification or independent specialist design authority.

## Q108-02 — Electrochemical interpretation

An Evans-type plot compares anodic and cathodic reaction behavior; their intersection represents a mixed corrosion condition. Activation polarization reflects reaction kinetics, while concentration polarization reflects reactant transport limits. Flow, oxygen availability and temperature can change demand even if the power supply is unchanged. Passive films can reduce dissolution over a range but may break down locally; low average current is not proof that pitting is absent.

The Nernst relation describes equilibrium potential for a specified reaction: E = E0 - (RT/nF) ln Q. At 25°C a base 10 form uses approximately 0.05916/n volts per decade of reaction quotient. In an original two-electron example, increasing Q from 1 to 100 decreases equilibrium potential by 0.05916 V. This equilibrium relation does not calculate the operating potential of a complex polarized field structure without additional information.

Faraday's relation gives ideal mass m = ItM/(nF). For iron as Fe 2+, 0.05 A for 86400 s with M55.85 and F96485 gives 1.250 g. To estimate uniform thickness, divide mass by density and exposed area using consistent units; the uniformity assumption is often false in service. A localized anodic region may lose metal much faster than an average suggests. Worked interpretation: a measurement shows low electrolyte resistivity but no oxygen or chemistry information. It supports a conductivity observation, not a unique corrosion-rate prediction. Explain which mechanism is supported and which remains uncertain.

## Q108-03 — Reference scales, errors and uncertainty

Reference conversion is an accounting operation between known electrochemical scales. If a structure potential measured against reference A is E_A and the references have potentials r_A and r_B against the same standard, E_B = E_A + r_A - r_B. Use verified chemistry and temperature, not a memorized generic offset. In teaching card R108, r_A=+0.316 V and r_B=+0.256 V under specified common conditions. A reading -0.900 V against A becomes -0.840 V against B. The structure has not changed; only the reference scale has.

A meter with finite input resistance loads a high-resistance source. In a simple divider model, displayed voltage is true voltage times R_meter/(R_source+R_meter). A1 V source with 1 megaohm source resistance and 10 megaohm meter displays 0.909 V. Increasing contact quality and choosing suitable impedance can reduce error; applying a blanket numerical correction without knowing the source model is not defensible.

Distinguish calibration, functional check, reference comparison, repeatability and uncertainty. Good repeatability can coexist with systematic error. Record contact, temperature, cell condition, lead integrity and time response. If an acceptance boundary is close to the result and uncertainty is material, report the uncertainty and seek the approved decision rule. Do not round a marginal value into compliance. Maintain raw values and the conversion formula so another practitioner can reproduce the result.

## Q108-04 — Potential surveys and synchronized interruption

Plan an ON/OFF survey around all influential current sources, their interruption capability, timing and the structure's electrical continuity. Confirm synchronization at the field location rather than assuming programmed clocks stayed aligned. Capture a time trace when switching transients or dynamic interference obscure immediate OFF. A delayed sample may include depolarization; an early spike may not represent the polarized potential. Locate the reference consistently and record coordinates, chainage, contact and surface conditions.

A close-interval potential survey follows a structure at the specified spacing to reveal spatial variation. Long metallic structures have longitudinal voltage drop, and remote earth gradients affect readings. Breaks in contact, inaccessible sections and jumps over obstacles must remain visible in the record. Offshore contact surveys require appropriate marine reference, contact verification, depth/position and approved diving/ROV arrangements; a land procedure cannot simply be transplanted underwater.

Original route: chainages 0, 50,100,150 m have qualified OFF values -0.93,-0.91,-0.79,-0.90 V on one reference scale. The 100 m point deserves investigation under the supplied project criterion; averaging the four would hide it. A second source stayed ON at 100 m, so the first priority is measurement qualification, not automatic protection adjustment. Repeat with controlled sources and preserve both runs. If the revised value remains weak, correlate coating, continuity and current distribution before recommending corrective work.

## Q108-05 — Coating surveys, coupons and probe evidence

DC voltage-gradient surveys seek spatial electrolyte-voltage patterns associated with current entering coating defects under a controlled signal. AC voltage-gradient and current-attenuation methods use an applied signal and appropriate receiving equipment. Their interpretation depends on geometry, signal path, soil contact, parallel conductors and operating setup. An indication is not a direct measurement of wall loss. Relative severity categories require the actual approved method and criteria; do not invent a universal percentage-to-repair rule.

For a simple attenuation teaching example, signal current falls from 100 mA to 70 mA over a segment. The magnitude drop is 30 mA or 30% of the starting value. A branch connection can also remove current, so this alone does not identify a coating holiday. Establish topology and repeatability, then combine independent evidence. A voltage-gradient polarity reversal can help locate a feature but must be checked against reference placement and source timing.

A coupon approximates an exposed surface of known area. Current divided by area gives average coupon current density: 2 mA over 10 cm 2 is 0.2 mA/cm 2 or 2 A/m 2. Geometry and shielding limit extrapolation to the asset. An electrical-resistance probe infers metal loss from resistance changes with temperature compensation and its calibration model; it is not interchangeable with a coupon potential measurement. Review calibration, baseline and trend. A sudden jump after maintenance may be an instrument change, not instant corrosion. Record that distinction before issuing a condition statement.

## Q108-06 — Current requirement and distribution tests

A temporary current-requirement test applies controlled current to assess response at representative points. The approved plan must address temporary anode placement, polarity, source capacity, cables, safe access, neighboring assets and restoration. Do not inject current into an unidentified structure. Stabilization and polarization history matter: the response after a short interval may not represent long-term demand.

In a synthetic test, 1 A produces qualified OFF potentials -0.80 and-0.76 V at near/far points; 2 A produces-0.91 and-0.82 V; 3 A produces-0.99 and-0.87 V. Under fictional project card C108 requiring each point at or more negative than-0.85 V but not beyond-1.10 V, only the 3 A trial meets both point values. This is evidence for the tested state, not a final system design or a universal criterion. Examine other points, adverse effects, seasonal changes and uncertainty before recommending operation.

Current spans use known pipe resistance over a defined length and measured longitudinal voltage drop to estimate current, subject to geometry, material, temperature and alternative paths. For a verified 0.002 ohm span and 6 mV drop, I=3 A. A coating-conductance model relates current change to potential change and area: G=I/deltaV, with area-normalized conductance when justified. In a simple 0.6 A/0.2 V/300 m 2 example, G=3 S total and 0.01 S/m 2. Do not treat that model as valid when current escapes to unknown branches.

## Q108-07 — Rectifier diagnostics and controlled maintenance

Distinguish AC supply, transformer/rectification, DC output, anode circuit and structure circuit. First confirm the observation with validated external instruments and history. Low voltage and low current, normal voltage with low current, or abnormal ripple suggest different diagnostic paths but do not uniquely identify a failed component. Fault isolation requires the approved procedure and qualified electrical personnel. Never reach into an energized cabinet or defeat interlocks to save time.

Record input/output indications, source settings, environmental conditions, alarms and independent verification. On an isolated simulator, 24 V and 4 A correspond to 6 ohms apparent circuit resistance and 96 W DC output. If later output is 24 V and 2 A, apparent resistance is 12 ohms; investigate circuit/contact/anode/environment changes rather than immediately turning up voltage. AC input power and DC output power are not identical, so output alone cannot establish efficiency.

Diode tests require a de-energized, verified safe component and the manufacturer's method. A half-wave fault can increase ripple and reduce effective output, but a visual panel reading may miss it. Separate authorized maintenance from design changes. After repair, verify polarity, output, representative potentials, neighboring effects and restoration of protections. Record before/after conditions and obtain owner acceptance. An increased current reading is not sufficient closeout if the protected structure or foreign line has not been checked.

## Q108-08 — Interference and AC exposure

DC interference may be steady or dynamic. Current can enter a foreign structure near a source and discharge elsewhere; the discharge region can become anodic. Coordinate simultaneous observations on affected assets and source states. Use synchronized time plots and a defined polarity convention. A negative shift near one point and positive shift elsewhere can be part of the same interference circuit. Do not solve one owner's reading by transferring corrosion to another owner.

Inductive coupling arises from changing magnetic fields, conductive coupling through shared earth paths and capacitive coupling through electric fields. AC touch hazards and AC corrosion assessment require specialized criteria and engineered mitigation; a satisfactory DC potential does not prove either issue controlled. Preserve decouplers, grounding and surge devices unless an approved safe procedure specifies otherwise. No universal safe-touch threshold is assigned by this course.

Original data: foreign point F1 changes from-0.78 to-0.96 V when source S runs; F2 changes from-0.82 to-0.65 V. The shifts are-180 mV and+170 mV. Record both; the more positive F2 response warrants coordinated investigation. An improvised bond may create dangerous current or defeat isolation. Any mitigation trial needs approval, ratings, monitoring and restoration. Verify all affected points after a change, not only the one that initially failed. A return to the previous state is also a controlled action with confirmation.

## Q108-09 — Casings, isolation and electrolyte investigation

A casing can provide mechanical protection while complicating corrosion assessment of the carrier pipe. Metallic contact, electrolyte coupling and shielding in the annulus can produce different behaviors. Compare carrier/casing potentials and controlled responses, inspect available isolation evidence and use approved diagnostic methods. Similar potentials alone do not prove metallic contact; a difference alone does not establish protection of the hidden carrier surface. Do not drill, fill, disconnect or bypass a casing system without an engineered plan.

Isolation devices should be assessed with methods appropriate to the actual circuit, including protective components and parallel paths. Maintain safety grounding functions. Locator traces, drawings and measured response help establish topology, but uncertainties must be documented. A pipe-to-soil potential taken outside a casing may not represent the annular environment.

For electrolyte investigation, use multiple Wenner spacings and orientations with depth assumptions recorded. In a shallow-pin example, 1 m spacing/20 ohms gives 125.66 ohm-m; 4 m/8 ohms gives 201.06 ohm-m. These are apparent resistivities sampling different volumes, not direct proof of two discrete layer values. Soil-box samples require moisture, compaction and geometry records. pH, chlorides, sulfates and other chemistry can inform specialist interpretation, but no single test establishes corrosion rate. Plan resampling when identity or preservation is uncertain.

## Q108-10 — Installation quality, supervision and reporting

Installation oversight checks approved materials, anode configuration, lead connections, polarity, cable protection, backfill, isolation and coating repair before concealment. Deep anode work raises drilling, groundwater, gas venting and environmental issues requiring the design and jurisdictional controls. Galvanic anode selection and life are not determined by mass alone; alloy performance, utilization, resistance and demand matter. Concrete CP has its own electrical continuity, material and overprotection concerns, especially where prestressing is involved. Refer unfamiliar systems to the responsible specialist.

Use hold points: verify components before installation, connections before burial, drawing/as-built consistency before commissioning, and representative performance before acceptance. A missing test-lead label is a commissioning defect, not an invitation to guess. Track each nonconformance, corrective action and reinspection evidence. Train testers to preserve raw data, units, time and invalid readings, and independently sample their work.

A technician report should separate observations, calculations, interpretations, uncertainty and recommendations. State the exact criterion supplied by the responsible authority and whether measurement conditions support its use. Rank unresolved conditions by consequence and urgency without inventing a repair approval. Worked closeout: a coating anomaly, a weak qualified potential and a carrier/casing ambiguity occur together. Recommend coordinated investigation with the three evidence streams attached; do not report wall loss, metallic short or successful repair without the required evidence. Handover includes restored source settings, temporary equipment removal, foreign-owner communication and assigned follow-up.

# VUC108 delivery and practical workbook

## Preparation and teaching plan

Demonstrate tester-level instrument use, polarity, unit conversion and safe stop decisions before entry. Instructor diagnoses gaps with the VUC107 stations or equivalent evidence; prior attendance alone is not enough. Original 48-hour plan: 14 instruction/worked examples, 18 safe practical work, 8 integrated investigations, 4 review/remediation, 2 controlled exam and 2 feedback/reassessment planning. Increase practice time when independent performance is not achieved. This is an original schedule, not an external course duration.

Instructor must have demonstrable CP field-evaluation competence and access to qualified electrical personnel for any electrical tasks. Use isolated low-voltage current-limited rigs; no energized mains cabinets, live pipe attachments, excavation or actual foreign-line bonding. Required equipment: verified meters/shunts/clamp, reference/master cells, safe interrupted-source model, logger/timebase, soil-resistivity instrument/cleared shallow training plot, soil box/pH equipment, locator loop, inert casing/isolator model, coating-survey training model and simulated rectifier fault records. Provide manufacturer operating instructions for the selected equipment. Precheck safe ratings and expected values. Missing equipment defers the affected observed performance.

R108 reference card: A=+0.316 V and B=+0.256 V versus a common standard, same specified temperature/chemistry. These values are supplied solely for conversion exercises, not a universal Ag/AgCl offset. C108 fictional project criterion: each qualified OFF point must be at or more negative than-0.85 V and no more negative than-1.10 V on the supplied scale. Equality is allowed for the arithmetic exercise; real uncertainty decision rules remain asset-specific. No value here substitutes for an applicable standard or design authority.

## Pass, remediation and credential

Ten dimensions 0–4: 0 unsafe/absent, 1 major help, 2 safe but incomplete/prompted, 3 independent correct traceable, 4 anticipates and resolves uncertainty with evidence. Require 32/40, each at least 3, eight practical stations and two observed reviews, both independent cases satisfactory and no unresolved critical error. Critical errors: dangerous instrument use, unauthorized source/bond change, bypassed safety, fabricated/hidden data, unsupported whole-asset compliance or wall-loss claim, or concealing unresolved polarity/identity. Stop the unsafe simulation, record it, remediate the cause and observe a changed demonstration.

Complete 20 explained checks and 5 labs. Final 10 cases/30 decisions, 60 points, 120 minutes; pass 48, each domain at least 4/6, no critical error. Private changed retake and keys are separate. Retain failed attempts, coaching and reassessment evidence; a competent independent reviewer handles appeals. Written success cannot replace practical performance.

Award VNDRLY/U Cathodic Protection Technician Training only when all original requirements pass. Include identity, version/hash, assessor/date, practical scope and limitations. It is not AMPP CP2, design approval or employer appointment. External certification has its own experience/application/exam requirements; no synthetic scenario counts as employment experience. Employer schedules continuing practice and review following changes, absence or adverse performance. No learner is awarded by authoring this package.
