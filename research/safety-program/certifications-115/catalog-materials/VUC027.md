# VUC027 — instructor, evaluation and credential guide

## Entry, scope and instruction

Audience: workers preparing for crane signaling duties. Entry requirements: understand the task's communication language, perceive and communicate the signals by the assessed method, understand basic directions and participate safely in demonstrations. Provide accessible teaching and reasonable accommodations without removing the essential communication/performance requirement. No prior crane-operator credential is implied. The employer's qualified evaluator or an appropriate third-party qualified evaluator determines actual qualification; this written package alone does not do so.

Instructor/evaluator: documented knowledge and experience in the actual signal methods, crane functions/dynamics and applicable requirements, with ability to evaluate clear signals and stop unsafe activities. Use an authorized operator for any real crane activity. The evaluator must distinguish teaching/coaching from independent assessment. Do not use an unqualified observer's signature as evidence of qualification.

Outcomes S01–S12: role/qualification; equipment functions/perspective; full standard signal vocabulary; visibility/handoff; ordered voice signals; radio/electronic setup; load motion/deflection; equipment limits; site/weather/electrical awareness; specialized-operation boundaries; stop/landing/recovery; integrated signaling. Twelve coursebook chapters include instruction, worked cases and practice; 24 formative checks have explanations and remediation. Separate final and practicals assess the same outcomes independently.

Planned 16 hours excluding breaks, a VNDRLY instructional plan rather than a statutory hour requirement. Day 1: S01–S04 instruction and demonstrations 180 minutes; individual chart production/recognition practice 120; S05–S06 voice/radio instruction 90; communication practice/correction 90 =480. Day 2: S07–S11 instruction/model scenarios 120; guided integrated practice 120; individual practical assessment 120; final 60; remediation/changed reassessment and records 60 =480. Extend for class size and learner needs. Do not shorten observed signal demonstrations to keep the timetable.

Equipment: full-size official chart in reference/hand-signals.html and the two local PNG sheets; crane function diagrams or models with cab, boom, main/auxiliary lines, labeled tracks and tower trolley; scenario cards; route/exclusion markers; radios and spare power; hands-free receiver; controlled visual obstruction; recording sheet. For real equipment, use an approved low-risk training plan, inspected crane/configuration, verified support/route, qualified operator, appropriate rigging team and exclusion. Models and simulated transmission failures are preferred for unsafe-condition teaching. Never deliberately two-block, side-load, approach electrical lines or suspend people for a test.

Pre-session record: instructor/evaluator____; date____; actual signaling methods____; learner communication/access needs____; chart revision/source____; equipment/model IDs____; actual crane/operator if any____; route/limits____; exclusion/emergency plan____; signal/handoff agreement____; radio channel/test____. A simulated crane does not entitle the record to claim actual crane operation. Signaling competence may be evaluated through a suitable practical setup by the qualified evaluator; document the setup transparently.

## Written/oral final and practical standards

Final: 36 constructed responses, 72 points, 60 minutes plus accommodations. An equivalent orally administered final is permitted with the same prompts, independent answers and recorded scoring. Each answer: 2 correct safe decision and reason, 1 safe incomplete, 0 wrong/absent/unsafe. Pass 58/72 and at least 4/6 in each domain. Critical unsafe endorsements override score: continuing with lost required communication; ignoring emergency stop; knowingly issuing ambiguous commands to multiple cranes; directing side drag/two-blocking or an unapproved personnel lift; entering an unsecured fall/crush/electrical hazard to regain visibility. Record the exact response and reason.

All eight practical stations must pass. Each station has five criteria scored 0 unsafe/not demonstrated, 1 safe with prompting/incomplete, 2 independently correct. Require 8/10 and all starred criteria at 2. Additionally, P02's complete chart checklist must pass every item in recognition and production; P04/P05 must demonstrate each claimed voice/radio method. A good average cannot hide inability to give stop. Stop unsafe action immediately and preserve the observed result.

After failed knowledge or performance, teach the specific gap, practice it and reassess with changed prompts/routes. Use the protected alternate cases for written/oral reassessment and changed model positions/equipment for practicals. Preserve attempts and exact deficiencies. Subsequent deficient workplace performance requires retraining/reassessment before further signaling. Do not issue a full award when a required station is missing. The local private examiner folder separates files; it is not a claim of implemented system access control.

## P01 — Task brief and qualification boundaries

Task: inspect synthetic qualification documents and a blind-landing work order. Identify the needed signaling method, assigned roles, safe route/position and unresolved prerequisites.

Criteria: 1 recognizes need for signaling; 2 distinguishes attendance, employer and third-party evaluation; 3 identifies documented method and employer applicability; 4*holds an unqualified/undocumented assignment; 5 briefs roles/stop/handoff. Expected: a hand-only record is insufficient evidence for radio duties. Record the actual reasoning and proposed next step.

## P02 — Full chart recognition and production

Task: evaluator prompts every official chart signal in random order, first by demonstrated motion for recognition, then by named function/task for production. Use the chart during teaching, not the independent test. The evaluator uses the original illustrations to judge form and direction.

Criteria: 1 identifies function accurately; 2 correct hand/arm orientation; 3 discernible motion from operator viewpoint; 4*unambiguous stop/emergency stop and no dangerous substituted function; 5 maintains the intended signal and concludes appropriately. In addition to 8/10, every checklist entry must be independently correct in both directions after any documented changed reassessment.

Individual checklist (recognition R / production P / correction record): stop____; emergency stop____; hoist____; lower____; raise boom____; lower boom____; swing____; extend telescope____; retract telescope____; raise boom/lower load____; lower boom/raise load____; dog everything____; move slowly with action____; main select then action____; auxiliary select then action____; travel/tower travel____; crawler both tracks forward/back____; crawler one track with identified locked track____; trolley travel____. Use a model to verify resulting crawler/trolley direction; do not accept motion memorization with the wrong intended equipment response.

Supplemental recognition/production entries: one-hand telescope extension____; one-hand telescope retraction____. Teach from S03's cited manufacturer description and demonstration; require correct thumb-versus-heel chest contact and intended function. Include these two entries in the individual pass requirement rather than assuming two-handed proficiency transfers.

## P03 — Visibility, position and handoff

Task: choose a safe position on the marked model route, direct a short sequence, then respond to a simulated obstructed view and hand off to another designated signaler while stopped.

Criteria: 1 sees necessary route and is discernible to operator; 2 remains outside marked danger areas; 3 uses operator perspective; 4*stops before required communication is lost and prevents conflicting signalers; 5 deliberately transfers responsibility and resumes only after acknowledgment. Expected: no walking under the represented load or continuing from memory behind an obstacle.

## P04 — Ordered voice commands

Task: give independent hoist, swing, telescope and trolley commands for supplied model movements. Each has function/direction, distance or speed and function-stop. Repeat with a second named crane and changed signaler position.

Criteria: 1 correct crane/line identification; 2 correct function and operator-perspective direction; 3 intelligible distance/speed; 4*clear timely function-stop and no ambiguous recipient; 5 observes response and stops/clarifies a deliberately wrong simulated response. Expected: commands are delivered as the movement proceeds, not a rushed recital. Record actual spoken sequence.

## P05 — Radio setup and interruption

Task: inspect/setup radios, demonstrate a dedicated signaling arrangement and hands-free reception, test at required positions, then direct a model task with a simulated transmission failure.

Criteria: 1 battery/spare/channel/device check; 2 on-site/location clarity test; 3 suitable hands-free receiving arrangement; 4*safe stop during interruption with no guessed continuation; 5 re-establishes communication and proper understood command before restart. Expected: a near-cab test alone does not cover a known dead zone. Record each location and method assessed.

## P06 — Dynamics and equipment hazards

Task: use models/diagrams to identify swing lag, boom deflection, side pull, wrong line, two-blocking risk and crane versus trolley/track travel. Choose the safe signaling response to each.

Criteria: 1 explains momentum and controlled stopping; 2 recognizes deflection/alignment effects; 3 distinguishes equipment functions/line selection; 4*stops unsafe side pull/two-blocking rather than driving to an interlock; 5 obtains responsible resolution before restart. Each named hazard requires a documented response, not one generic 'stop' for the whole station.

## P07 — Site and specialized-operation decisions

Task: inspect a synthetic site drawing with electrical boundary, pedestrian path, blind landing and a weather change. Then evaluate two-crane and proposed person-riding-load scenarios.

Criteria: 1 safe position/exclusion/escape selection; 2 identifies applicable electrical plan/roles rather than guessing voltage; 3 accounts for visibility/wind and actual limits; 4*rejects unauthorized personnel lifting or unplanned coordination; 5 identifies qualified planning and communication prerequisites. Expected: do not confuse a signalperson certificate with approval of every lift. All hazards are simulated, not introduced into an active operation.

## P08 — Integrated route, stop, landing and record

Task: brief and direct a complete approved model or actual low-risk route using hand signals, then voice/radio. Include a controlled stop, handoff or method change while stopped, simulated unexpected response and stable landing verification.

Criteria: 1 complete prebrief/method verification; 2 accurate sustained commands with observation; 3*honors emergency stop from anyone and resolves discrepancy; 4 controlled landing without premature release or danger-area entry; 5 records assessed methods and actual setup honestly. Expected: independent application, not evaluator-led movements. A record must identify simulation versus actual equipment and does not claim crane operator competence.

## Instructor correction scripts and records

Wrong function: stop the exercise; have learner name the intended equipment component, demonstrate the correct signal slowly, compare with the chart, then use a changed task from another position. Wrong perspective: mark the operator's viewpoint and rehearse from opposite sides, then reassess without floor arrows. Missed radio stop: re-teach the three-part command and loss protocol, practice on unloaded models, then inject a different interruption. Invisible signal: compare from the actual viewing distance and adjust position without entering danger space. Never correct a live hazardous action by waiting to see whether it succeeds.

Assessment record: learner____; date/version____; evaluator identity/qualification basis____; employer____; evaluation path (employer/third party as applicable)____; method(s)____; oral/written scores by domain____; total____; critical response review____; P01–P08 criterion scores____; complete chart R/P checklist____; actual crane/model/radio setup____; observation notes____; deficiencies/remediation/changed reassessment____; result____; signatures____. Employer documentation must be available at site and identify the qualified signaling methods; this blank form alone certifies nobody.

VNDRLY achievement wording: 'VNDRLY/U Signalperson — completed instruction, knowledge assessment and documented practical signaling methods: ____.' Include version/date and actual evaluator/setup. Do not label it CCO certification, OSHA-issued license or automatically portable employer qualification. Full course completion requires all knowledge/check and practical requirements. An incomplete learner receives only a truthful participation/limited-scope record. No invented universal renewal period replaces applicable employer or external program requirements.

# VNDRLY/U Signalperson — learner coursebook

This original course teaches crane signaling and its limits. It does not award CCO certification or authorize crane operation. A qualified evaluator must assess the individual and document the signaling methods actually demonstrated. Actual workplace assignment remains the employer's responsibility. The official OSHA hand-signal illustrations are included as unmodified reference sheets; use the white-background reference viewer for readable display.

## S01 — Know when signaling is needed and who may do it

The signal person's job is to communicate the intended safe movement while observing the load path and relevant hazards. It is not to operate the crane remotely by assumption, calculate unknown capacity on the fly or replace the lift director. Before a task, establish the operator, designated signaler, lift direction and receiving team's responsibilities. Identify how a worker can call a stop and how the team will resolve a changed condition.

In construction crane work, signaling is required when the operator cannot fully see the point of operation, when travel visibility is obstructed or when site-specific safety concerns make signaling necessary. A camera does not automatically remove the need; evaluate the actual visibility and reliability of the operation. Select a suitable method and ensure the signaler is qualified for it before work.

OSHA provides third-party qualified evaluation and employer-qualified evaluation paths. The employer-only assessment is not portable to another employer. The documentation must identify each signaling method and be available at the worksite. Qualification includes knowledge and practical ability, basic equipment dynamics and applicable rules. Unsafe later performance requires retraining and reassessment before resuming the role. A course attendance record alone does not establish that ability.

Worked case: an employee has only demonstrated hand signals but is assigned to direct a blind lift by radio. Do not treat the hand-signal record as radio qualification. Verify or complete the appropriate knowledge and practical evaluation and document that method before assigning the role. If the planned signaler is absent, the need for a signaler does not disappear.

Practice: examine three synthetic records—attendance only, employer hand-signal qualification and documented hand/radio qualification. Identify what each establishes, what it does not and what must be verified for the actual employer and task.

## S02 — Translate the task into crane functions

Hoisting changes the hook/load elevation through the load line. Booming changes boom angle. Swing rotates the upper works or boom about the crane's rotation axis. Telescoping changes the length of an extendable boom. Trolley travel moves the trolley along a tower-crane jib; crane travel moves the crane itself. These are different functions even when more than one can move the load toward a target. A command must name the intended function, not just point vaguely toward the destination.

Know which line is in use. Main and auxiliary hoists can have different capacities and geometry. The signal selecting a line is followed by the intended action, not interpreted as movement by itself. Verify the operator and signaler agree which line/load is being addressed. Do not direct simultaneous line use or a specialized attachment without the approved procedure and clear communication.

Directions use the operator's perspective. Standing opposite the operator makes your own left and right unreliable as commands. Before the exercise, place a model crane and identify the cab's reference direction, then practice translating targets from several signaler positions. Never change the meaning halfway through a movement because you walked around the load.

Worked case: the hook needs to rise while the boom should remain at its planned angle. A raise-boom signal could change radius and clearance rather than simply raise the hook. Use the hoist function and stop at the agreed point. If the operator responds with the wrong function, stop and resolve the misunderstanding rather than trying to compensate with another signal.

Practice: label the functions on a crane diagram and explain what each does to the hook path. Use a tabletop model to show the difference between trolley travel and whole-crane travel. Do not infer actual capacity from the model.

## S03 — Learn the standard hand-signal vocabulary

Use the official illustrated sheets in `reference/hand-signals.html` during instruction. They contain the complete OSHA Appendix A chart. The small memory cues below are not replacement illustrations. The instructor demonstrates each motion, the learner names it, and then the learner produces it from a task prompt. Practice from the operator's viewing position so a technically correct shape is also discernible.

| Function | Short recognition cue |
|---|---|
| Stop | One horizontal arm sweeps, palm down |
| Emergency stop | Both horizontal arms sweep, palms down |
| Hoist / lower | Upward / downward index circles |
| Raise / lower boom | Horizontal arm, thumb up / down |
| Swing | Horizontal pointing arm |
| Extend / retract telescope | Waist-level thumbs outward / inward |
| Raise boom, lower load | Thumb up; fingers open/close |
| Lower boom, raise load | Thumb down; fingers open/close |
| Dog everything | Hands together at waist |
| Move slowly | Other hand in front of action hand |
| Main / auxiliary selection | Tap head / tap elbow, then action |
| Travel / tower travel | Upright fingers, pushing motion |
| Crawler both tracks | Fists rotate away / toward body |
| Crawler one track | One fist indicates locked track; other rotates |
| Trolley travel | Palm up, thumb direction, horizontal motion |

Do not reduce stop to a stationary raised hand or use dog-everything as an ambiguous substitute for the required stop. Combined boom/load signals have a specific coordinated meaning; they are not permission to give unrelated conflicting commands. Move-slowly modifies the action signal. Watch the actual response and maintain the appropriate signal while movement is requested.

Worked drill: the evaluator shows a lowering hook on a model while boom angle stays fixed. The learner must produce lower, not lower boom. After a safe stop, the evaluator changes the task to reducing boom angle while maintaining the intended load motion. The learner identifies and produces the appropriate charted combined signal and explains the different meaning.

Practice every listed chart entry individually in both recognition and production. For crawler and tower functions, use a model with labeled tracks/trolley so the learner can explain the resulting movement. Correct confused signals before increasing speed or combining a route sequence.

Additional one-handed telescope vocabulary: the manufacturer reference identified in the source register describes extension with a fist in front of the chest and the thumb tapping the chest; retraction uses the outward-pointing thumb while the fist's heel taps the chest. These are not the two-handed inward/outward thumb cues. Demonstrate and assess both separately, and establish the appropriate signal agreement for the actual work. Do not mistake the one-hand form for a special command to extend only one boom section. The manufacturer reference is instructional corroboration, not a claim that this course has audited every current ASME provision.

## S04 — Make hand signals visible and control the handoff

A clear signal at the wrong location is ineffective. Select a position with a view of the necessary load path and a reliable view to the operator, outside swing, crush, fall and travel hazards. Consider sun behind the signaler, dark backgrounds, rain, gloves, reflective clothing and obstructions. A visible hand above a wall may not communicate a full arm motion. Test visibility from the actual operator location before movement.

One designated person directs a crane at a time except that anyone may give stop/emergency stop. Plan handoff at a safe stopped point: identify the incoming signaler, obtain the operator's acknowledgment and make clear when responsibility transfers. Do not let two people compete with slightly different directions. If an obstruction requires another position, stop before moving out of effective communication unless the approved method maintains it.

The standard chart is the baseline. Nonstandard hand signals require the appropriate pre-operation agreement where standard signals are infeasible or do not cover the operation. Do not invent a signal during a lift because the operator seems to understand. A new communication technology needs demonstrated effectiveness or an applicable equally effective consensus-standard basis, not merely a convenient screen.

Worked case: the signaler steps behind a column but keeps signaling. The operator cannot see the full action. Stop operations requiring signals, re-establish a suitable position/method, and resume only after the proper signal is understood. Continuing from the last visible command risks moving beyond the intended limit.

Practice: use safe floor markers and an empty model hook to choose three signaling positions, then identify a visibility failure. Demonstrate the stop, relocation and deliberate handoff. No learner stands in a real load's fall zone to test visibility.

## S05 — Build concise voice commands in the correct order

Before work, the operator, signaler and lift director where present agree on voice signals and communicate effectively in the language used. Repeat the agreement when a participant changes, confusion occurs or the command system changes. Accent, noise or unfamiliar terminology must be resolved by a workable communication method, not by pretending to understand.

The required command sequence is function and direction; distance and/or speed; function and stop. For a training example: 'Hoist up; two feet, slowly; hoist stop.' The stop is given at the planned stopping point as the movement is monitored, not spoken prematurely as one rushed sentence that the operator cannot apply. If more than one crane is addressed, identify the intended crane before function/direction or use an equally effective identification method.

Avoid conversational shorthand such as 'bring it this way' or 'a little more.' Those phrases hide function, direction and amount. Specify the intended function and maintain timely updates consistent with the agreed system. Do not keep issuing movement instructions while distracted from the load. An unclear transmission requires a safe stop and clarification, not guessing the missing word.

Worked case: two cranes share a coordination channel and a voice says 'swing left.' Neither operator should have to infer which crane is meant. The team must establish identification and use it consistently. A signaler facing the opposite direction must still use the operator's perspective. Practice with 'Crane Blue: swing right; slowly, toward the marked limit; swing stop' only after that limit and terminology are agreed and visible to the directing team.

Practice: write commands for hoist, lower, swing, telescope and trolley movements with a final function-stop command. Speak each into a recorder, then have a partner identify the crane, function, direction and amount without seeing the prompt. Correct missing information before the practical test.

## S06 — Set up radio and electronic signaling

Test the actual transmitting and receiving equipment on site before operations. Check battery condition, spare power, channel, microphone, headset and reception at intended positions. Verify that wind, machinery noise, structures and other users do not obscure commands. The operator's reception must be hands-free. A personal phone held to the ear is not the required reception arrangement simply because its audio is clear.

Use a dedicated signal channel under the applicable rule, with its defined coordination exceptions. A shared general conversation channel invites missed or mistaken commands. For multiple-crane coordination, identify recipients reliably and prevent conflicting directions. Define the loss-of-communication response and test it safely before a load moves. Do not switch channels mid-operation without stopping and re-establishing the system.

Video may assist visibility but introduces field-of-view, latency, blind-zone and failure concerns. A picture of the load is not necessarily a picture of the whole route or receiving area. Verify the complete signaling arrangement, not only that a camera displays an image. If required transmission is interrupted, safely stop until the channel and a proper understood signal are restored.

Worked case: a radio passes a test next to the cab but fails behind the structure where the signaler will stand. The operation is not ready. Change equipment, positioning or the approved communication plan and repeat the on-site test at the necessary locations. Do not plan to shout from behind the obstruction as an untested backup.

Practice: set up the training radios, conduct a location-by-location test, demonstrate hands-free reception and deliberately simulate a dead transmission with the equipment unloaded. Record the observed stop and restored communication before resumption.

## S07 — Understand load motion, deflection and stopping

A suspended load behaves like a pendulum. Starting, stopping or changing swing can create motion that continues after the crane function stops. Sudden corrective commands can amplify the swing. Anticipate the route and request controlled movements early enough to avoid chasing the load back and forth. The operator manages the equipment; the signaler communicates the safe intended movement and warns of hazards.

Boom deflection under load can change hook position and radius. A hook positioned over an unloaded object may move as load comes onto the boom. Observe the actual line and load rather than assuming the initial position remains unchanged. Keep the hoist line appropriately vertical; do not direct side dragging or use the crane to pull a snagged load free. A tilted line can impose side loads and produce sudden movement when the obstruction releases.

Load shape matters as well as weight. A large flat panel can catch wind and rotate, while a compact object of the same weight may behave differently. Center of gravity, rigging arrangement and clearances affect stability. A signaler does not approve unknown weight or capacity by intuition. Obtain the established lift information and stop if actual behavior differs from the plan.

Worked case: during take-up, the load moves sideways before leaving its support. Stop and have the responsible team investigate alignment, snagging or changed geometry. Do not order more hoist to force it clear. A safe trial can reveal a discrepancy but cannot substitute for verified capacity and rigging.

Practice: use a small unloaded teaching pendulum or model to compare gradual and abrupt movement. Explain the lag and identify when to request a stop. Do not deliberately generate excessive swing with a real crane as a classroom demonstration.

## S08 — Recognize equipment limits and avoid two-blocking

Two-blocking occurs when the moving hook block or similar lower assembly contacts the upper block/boom-tip assembly. Hoisting upward is one possible cause, but geometry changes such as telescoping can also consume clearance. Watch the relevant clearance and understand the actual configuration. Anti-two-block equipment is a safeguard; it is not a target to routinely reach or a reason to ignore a developing hazard.

Know the operating envelope established by the qualified team and operator: intended functions, radius, boom configuration, load and site limitations. The signaler does not override a load chart, warning or operator safety decision. If a command is unsafe for the equipment, stop and resolve the issue. Confusing main and auxiliary line selection can move the wrong hook toward an obstruction; identify the line before the action.

When crane travel is involved, look beyond the load. Ground conditions, slope, clearance, tail swing, adjacent equipment and the operator's travel view matter. Use the charted travel signal for the actual equipment and operator's perspective. Crawler one-track commands differ from both-track travel and must be demonstrated correctly on a model before any real movement. Tower trolley motion is not a command for the crane to travel along its base.

Worked case: while the boom extends, hook-to-tip clearance shrinks. The signaler must recognize the developing condition and stop the relevant operation, allowing the operator and plan to resolve it. Waiting until a device trips or attempting a guessed simultaneous correction can create an avoidable event.

Practice: identify two-blocking, side loading, wrong-line selection and travel-obstruction hazards from original diagrams or a safe model. State the stop and the information needed before resuming. No live equipment interlock is defeated for assessment.

## S09 — Read the site, weather and electrical hazards

Walk the planned route with the responsible team before signaling. Identify overhead services, structures, swing radius, pedestrians, traffic, receiving edges, floor openings, poor lighting and escape space. Choose positions that let you see necessary clearances without entering the load path. Establish barriers and coordinate with other activities so a delivery or pedestrian cannot unexpectedly enter the operating area.

Power-line safety requires the employer's applicable work-zone assessment and controls. Do not estimate voltage from appearance or assume covered lines are safe. For equipment operations within the scope of 1926.1408, the rule's 20-foot assessment trigger and clearance/control options are not a universal permission to work at any guessed distance. Verify the chosen applicable method, boundaries and responsible personnel before movement. Higher voltages and closer operations have additional requirements.

A dedicated power-line spotter may need specific positioning, visual aids and continuous operator communication. Do not casually combine that dedicated function with competing load-direction tasks. The plan must establish compatible roles and reliable timely information. If the boundary cannot be maintained or the electrical status is uncertain, stop and obtain responsible resolution.

Weather can change visibility, communication and load control. Apply equipment/plan limits and observed conditions; do not invent one universal wind speed for every load. Lightning, gusts, rain and ice may require suspension and safe securing. A sail-like load can become difficult before another nearby lift does.

Worked case: an approaching truck blocks the signaler's safe escape route while a panel is being moved. Stop safely, restore the controlled area and revise traffic coordination. Do not step beneath the panel to regain visibility. Practice identifying two alternative safe positions and the condition that would make each unusable.

## S10 — Blind lifts, multiple cranes and personnel lifting boundaries

For a blind lift, plan how the signaler observes the required route and landing while maintaining communication. A relay arrangement needs deliberate responsibilities and transfer, not a chain of people independently changing commands. The operator must know who is directing at each stage. Stop before a handoff or loss of view makes the next movement uncertain.

Multiple-crane work needs its own qualified plan and direction. Crane identification is essential, but correct wording alone does not validate load sharing or coordination. The signaler must understand the approved sequence and stop criteria without independently redesigning it. A movement by one machine can alter demand on another. If responses diverge, stop under the coordinated plan and resolve the condition.

Personnel lifting is a specialized operation with additional prerequisites, equipment, platform, inspection/test and communication requirements. Ordinary cargo-lift training does not authorize it. The signaler verifies that the applicable personnel-lift plan and roles are established and does not improvise a person-carrying arrangement. This course's practicals use no suspended people.

Worked case: a worker proposes riding a material load to guide its placement because radio reception is poor. Reject that improvisation. Restore an effective signaling method and use the legally appropriate access/lifting arrangements under responsible planning. A communication problem cannot be solved by creating an unauthorized personnel lift.

Practice: review three scenario cards—blind landing, two cranes and proposed personnel platform. List the information you need, identify your signaling responsibility and identify the approval/design decisions outside this course's authority. Stop is a valid and necessary outcome when prerequisites are missing.

## S11 — Stop, clarify, recover and land

Stop early enough to prevent a hazard, using the agreed standard command. If the operator does not respond as intended, do not pile on more movement signals. Stop and establish why: visibility, wrong function, misunderstood direction, equipment response or another person signaling. Resume only when the problem is resolved and a proper command is understood.

At landing, observe the receiving area and clearances. Direct controlled movement while keeping people out of pinch/fall hazards. The receiving team establishes stable support; do not signal release of rigging based only on the load touching something. If the object rocks, dunnage shifts or rigging is trapped, stop and let the responsible team establish a safe correction. The signaler must not enter beneath an unsecured load to inspect it.

During suspected electrical contact or other emergency, stop/warn and follow the established emergency response while keeping people away from the potentially energized equipment/load and surrounding hazard. Call appropriate emergency/utility assistance. Do not touch the equipment or attempt an improvised rescue that exposes additional people. Detailed equipment escape/rescue actions require the site emergency plan and trained personnel.

Worked case: an unidentified worker gives emergency stop while the designated signaler is directing a swing. The operator must stop safely. The signaler resolves the reported hazard rather than dismissing the person as unauthorized. The one-directing-person rule does not suppress emergency warnings.

Practice: demonstrate ordinary stop, emergency stop, communication-loss stop and a restart after a deliberately resolved scenario. Explain why restart is a new deliberate decision rather than automatic continuation of the last command.

## S12 — Put the entire signaling task together

Begin with the task brief and the actual qualification scope. Identify the crane/operator, method, load line, route, landing, hazards, limits and stop/handoff arrangements. Inspect/test the communication equipment, select a safe viewing position and confirm operator perspective. Use the correct command for each planned movement and observe its effect continuously.

In the training route, direct a model or approved low-risk crane exercise through take-up, small hoist movement, controlled swing or trolley movement as applicable, a planned stop and landing. The evaluator introduces only safe verbal/simulated changes: obstruction, radio interruption, wrong response or a new signaler. Recognize the change, stop and resolve it without giving ambiguous competing instructions.

Complete both hand and voice/radio tasks if both methods are to appear on the achievement record. Demonstrate every charted signal separately, including main/auxiliary selection, combined boom/load signals, telescope, crawler and tower functions. Model-based function demonstrations must be identified as such; actual worksite qualification must be determined by the appropriately qualified evaluator and employer for the methods used.

Worked closeout: a learner accurately names every signal but cannot produce a distinguishable stop or reliably identify the addressed crane by radio. That learner has not passed the practical. Provide targeted instruction, change the scenario and reassess independently. Memorizing a chart is only one part of competent application.

Record the exact method, assessment basis, observations and evaluator. Do not state 'all crane work qualified' or imply that an employer-only assessment transfers to another employer. A defensible record describes what the individual actually demonstrated and what remains outside the assessed scope.

# Learning checks

Attempt before reading answers; correct misconceptions through named chapter and changed practice.

## S01-L1

Does course attendance alone qualify a signaler?

**Answer:** No; required knowledge and practical evaluation plus method-specific documentation are needed.

**Reason/remediation:** Review S01 and distinguish learning participation from evaluated competence.

## S01-L2

Can an employer-only evaluation automatically transfer to another employer?

**Answer:** No; that OSHA evaluation path is not portable.

**Reason/remediation:** Verify the evaluation path and employing organization.

## S02-L1

What is the difference between hoist and raise boom?

**Answer:** Hoist moves the hook by the load line; raise boom changes boom angle and geometry.

**Reason/remediation:** Use the intended function, not an approximate load direction.

## S02-L2

Whose perspective governs directions?

**Answer:** The operator’s perspective.

**Reason/remediation:** Practice from both sides of the model before giving commands.

## S03-L1

How do main/auxiliary selection signals relate to the movement signal?

**Answer:** Select the intended line, then give the regular action signal.

**Reason/remediation:** Selection alone is not a movement command.

## S03-L2

What distinguishes ordinary stop from emergency stop on the chart?

**Answer:** Stop uses one horizontal sweeping arm; emergency stop uses both, palms down.

**Reason/remediation:** Demonstrate the actual movement from the official chart, not a static pose.

## S04-L1

Your position loses the operator’s view behind a column. What now?

**Answer:** Stop the operation requiring signals and restore suitable communication before an understood restart.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not continue on the last visible command.

## S04-L2

Can two signalers direct the same crane simultaneously to be helpful?

**Answer:** No; one directs at a time, with the emergency stop exception.

**Reason/remediation:** Plan a deliberate handoff while safely stopped.

## S05-L1

State the three elements in a voice command.

**Answer:** Function/direction; distance and/or speed; function/stop.

**Reason/remediation:** S05: preserve the order and deliver stop at the appropriate movement point.

## S05-L2

What is missing from “bring it this way”?

**Answer:** Unambiguous function, operator-perspective direction and movement amount/speed with function-stop.

**Reason/remediation:** Translate conversational intent into agreed crane commands.

## S06-L1

Where must radio effectiveness be tested?

**Answer:** On site at the positions needed for the operation, before work.

**Reason/remediation:** A near-cab test does not prove reception behind a structure.

## S06-L2

What receiving arrangement does the operator require?

**Answer:** Hands-free reception in the suitable tested signaling system.

**Reason/remediation:** A handheld phone is not equivalent merely because audio is clear.

## S07-L1

Why can a load keep moving after a swing command stops?

**Answer:** Suspended-load momentum/pendulum motion persists.

**Reason/remediation:** Anticipate controlled stopping; do not amplify motion by abrupt corrections.

## S07-L2

The load drags sideways during take-up. Safe response?

**Answer:** Stop and investigate alignment, snagging or changed geometry with the responsible team.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not order extra hoist to force it free.

## S08-L1

What is two-blocking?

**Answer:** Contact between lower moving hook/block assembly and upper boom-tip/block assembly.

**Reason/remediation:** Recognize clearance loss from hoisting and geometry changes.

## S08-L2

Does an anti-two-block device replace signaler observation?

**Answer:** No; do not drive to the safeguard or defeat it.

**Reason/remediation:** Stop a developing clearance hazard before contact.

## S09-L1

Can line voltage be inferred safely from appearance?

**Answer:** No; verify applicable electrical information and controls through responsible planning.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not turn a guessed voltage into an approach clearance.

## S09-L2

Why can one load require stopping in wind before another nearby load?

**Answer:** Shape, surface area, equipment and plan limits differ.

**Reason/remediation:** No single universal wind number is invented for all lifts.

## S10-L1

Does crane identification alone make a two-crane lift ready?

**Answer:** No; qualified planning/direction and coordinated sequence are also required.

**Reason/remediation:** Communication is necessary but does not establish load sharing.

## S10-L2

May a worker ride a cargo load to solve a blind-view problem?

**Answer:** No; reject the improvised personnel lift and establish proper communication/access.

**Reason/remediation:** A visibility problem does not waive personnel-lift requirements.

## S11-L1

Who may give emergency stop?

**Answer:** Anyone aware of a safety problem.

**Reason/remediation:** The designated-signaler rule does not suppress emergency warnings.

## S11-L2

A landed load rocks and traps the sling. Release or pull free?

**Answer:** Neither; stop and establish stable support and an approved safe removal.

**Reason/remediation:** Touching the landing is not proof of stability.

## S12-L1

A learner names signals but cannot produce a discernible stop. Pass?

**Answer:** No; remediate and independently reassess practical performance.

**Reason/remediation:** Recognition does not substitute for application.

## S12-L2

What signaling scope belongs on the record?

**Answer:** The methods actually evaluated, setup/evaluator and limitations, without invented operator or external certification.

**Reason/remediation:** Record actual evidence, not a broad title.
