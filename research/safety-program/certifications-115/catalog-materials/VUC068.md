# VUC068 — Overhead Crane Operation

## Purpose and qualification boundary

This original VNDRLY/U course develops the decisions and practical skills needed to inspect, plan, move and secure loads with an overhead or gantry crane. The recorded scope identifies cab, pendant or remote control and the actual equipment demonstrated. It does not issue CCO certification, certify a rigger or crane inspector, or replace employer designation and equipment-specific qualification. Knowledge completion and actual operating performance are recorded separately. A simulator is useful preparation but cannot silently replace the actual performance required by this package.

Use the actual manufacturer's instructions, equipment markings, approved procedures and applicable current rules. Examples marked O68 are fictional training information. They do not establish capacity, operating limits or engineering acceptance for real equipment. Read the lesson, work the case, complete its checks and demonstrate the matching application before independent assessment.

## Q01 — Operator role and scope

Being able to press a pendant button is not the same as being ready to operate. The designated operator must understand equipment identity, permitted work, signals, inspection, load control, stop conditions and securing. Riggers, maintenance personnel, inspection personnel and lift supervisors have distinct responsibilities. Clarify who supplies verified load information and who can release equipment after a defect. Do not assume another person's presence transfers your operating responsibilities to them.

Before a shift assess readiness: familiarity with this crane and controls, comprehension of warnings/signals, fatigue and impairment, access to the manual and any restrictions. A new attachment or unfamiliar control mode may require additional instruction even on a familiar crane. Stop and obtain competent help when the plan or equipment condition is uncertain. Do not let an urgent production target become a substitute for authorization.

Worked case: an operator qualified on a pendant crane is given a radio controller for a different bay without identification or familiarization. Hold movement and verify the crane/controller pairing, controls and required qualification. Changed: the crane is familiar but a lifting magnet is newly fitted. The new attachment and load-retention risks require their own instructions and competence.

## Q02 — Bridge, trolley, hoist and control perspective

The bridge moves along the runway, the trolley moves across the bridge, and the hoist raises or lowers the load. A gantry supports its bridge on legs, which introduce ground-level travel and pinch exposures. Main and auxiliary hoists can have different ratings and purposes. Do not combine their capacities by arithmetic unless the equipment and lift are specifically designed and authorized for the arrangement.

Control labels must be legible and understood. A floor operator who walks to the other side of a load can reverse their personal left/right perspective without changing the crane's labeled direction. Establish orientation using the actual direction markings and a safe unloaded familiarization. Do not guess with a load attached. Keep the pendant cable clear of entanglement and do not use it as a towing line. With radio control, verify the selected crane, command ownership and approved handover; an unguarded transmitter can create unintended movement.

Worked: a remote marked Bay 4 is found beside the Bay 3 crane. Its location is not evidence of pairing. Verify identity by the approved procedure with a protected area before operation. Changed: directions feel reversed after walking around the load. Reorient using fixed markings and safe observation; do not relabel controls to match a momentary viewpoint.

## Q03 — Work area and route

Plan the complete load path and the operator's safe path separately. A pendant operator may be able to see the load but have no safe place to walk beside it. Consider machinery, stored stock, vehicles, doors, pedestrians, pits, slippery floors, overhead services and other cranes. The load must not force the operator into a pinch zone between itself and a wall. Establish exclusion and warning arrangements before motion, not after someone appears beneath the load.

Verify the landing area can support the load and leave space for safe rigging removal. Identify the load's dimensions and any protrusions, not merely the hook's path. Other cranes on a shared runway need coordinated operations and maintenance protection. Outdoor gantries introduce wind and rail-related conditions; follow the equipment-specific restrictions. A warning horn or anti-collision feature supplements the plan, not permission to push through an occupied route.

Worked O68 case: a load is 1.8 m wide and the planned clear passage is 2.2 m. Static geometric spare width is only 0.4 m, or 0.2 m each side if centered. That calculation alone does not prove a safe route because swing, protrusions and positioning uncertainty remain. Changed: an attached fitting adds 0.3 m to one side. Reassess the actual envelope and choose a safely validated route before lifting.

## Q04 — Pre-use inspection and records

Follow a repeatable inspection from the safe operator position: markings and warnings, visible structure/runway concerns, hook/block, rope or chain, attachments, controls/cables, guards, leaks, abnormal sounds and required functional checks. Review unresolved defects and the status of required inspections. Do not climb onto the crane to inspect an inaccessible part without the separately authorized access, isolation and fall-protection arrangements.

A functional check is a controlled prescribed action, not an overload experiment. Test controls and protective functions only by the manufacturer-approved method with the area clear. If the upper-limit check or another required check fails, stop and report rather than continuing because the load will stay low today. Distinguish an operator pre-use inspection from detailed periodic inspection, load testing and maintenance release. The operator must not manufacture a missing inspection record.

Worked: a pendant button sticks and sometimes returns slowly. A normal-looking hook does not offset that defect. Remove the crane from the affected operation under the defect procedure and communicate the specific symptom. Changed: the button works after tapping the housing. Intermittent recovery does not prove repair; preserve the restriction pending competent resolution.

Check the pendant's suspension and strain relief: electrical conductors must not be used as the structural support for the pendant. Check warning lights/horns and their visibility/audibility in the actual environment, not only whether a lamp glows nearby. Locate and keep access to the main runway disconnect clear. An operator locates it and knows the emergency procedure; that is different from being authorized to perform electrical work. Record inaccessible disconnects, damaged guards, leaks and failed warnings as specific deficiencies. Do not prescribe an arbitrary sound level or guard dimension without the governing specification.

## Q05 — Hoist rope, chain, hooks and attachments

Identify whether the hoist uses wire rope or load chain and apply the actual manufacturer's criteria and instructions. Rope damage can include broken wires, crushing, kinks, corrosion and distorted reeving. Chain damage can include twist, elongation, wear, deformation and damaged connections. A sling-chain criterion is not automatically a hoist-chain criterion. Observe safely; never feed fingers into moving chain or rope to feel a defect.

Check hook condition, seating, latch where required, swiveling behavior and block/attachment identity. The load is carried in the intended bowl or connection, not on the hook tip or latch. Do not weld, heat, straighten or grind a damaged hook to make it look acceptable. Inspect below-hook equipment under its own instructions. A lifting beam's rating does not make its slings, shackles or the crane equally capable.

Worked case: the crane's main hook is rated above the planned load, but one shackle's identification is unreadable. The rigging arrangement has an unresolved capacity basis; replace or obtain valid identification through the authorized process before use. Changed: a chain is visibly twisted. Do not attempt a loaded lift to pull it straight; correct the condition safely under the equipment instructions.

For equipment covered by OSHA 1910.179, lowering must preserve at least two complete rope wraps on the drum. Follow any more restrictive manufacturer requirement and the actual permitted hook travel. This is not a chain-hoist rule. Identify the permitted lower travel before a lift into a pit; a deeper destination is not permission to unwind beyond the limit.

## Q06 — Brakes, limits and protective functions

Understand the difference between a normal operating control and a protective limit. The operator controls routine stopping; an upper limit is not a normal method of ending every hoist movement. Likewise, end stops and bumpers are not targets to strike to achieve a repeatable travel position. Confirm the actual check procedure and stop distance without deliberately creating an overtravel hazard.

An overload device, warning or automatic positioning feature has defined limitations. It does not determine whether an unknown load is safe by permitting the operator to pull until it trips. Never bypass it, increase a setting or use an emergency stop as a maintenance disconnect. Brake holding and control behavior must be checked by the prescribed safe method; creeping, unintended motion or failure to stop requires the appropriate hold and qualified attention.

Worked: an operator wants to run the empty block into the upper limit at full speed for each production cycle. That confuses protective function with normal positioning and can damage equipment. Use normal control and the approved inspection procedure. Changed: the block drifts after releasing the command. Stop/report the fault; do not accept drift as normal merely because the load eventually stops.

The operator shift-start upper-limit check for covered equipment is unloaded and carefully performed at inching/slow speed. It is distinct from qualified testing that establishes a limit's trip setting. Do not substitute a loaded test or a high-speed routine check. A failed result is reported immediately and the crane is not used for the affected operation until properly resolved.

## Q07 — Weight and limiting capacity

Obtain verified weight of the actual load, including contents, packing and attachments that remain with it. Confirm the rated crane/hoist and every component in the selected load path. Read units carefully: a metric tonne is 1, 000 kg, while a US short ton is 2, 000 lb. The word ton alone is ambiguous. Follow the actual equipment convention for what its rating includes; do not import a mobile-crane deduction rule without checking.

O68 fictional training convention: the stated hoist rating limits all load and below-hook lifting accessories attached to its hook. A 5, 000 kg rating with payload 4, 300 kg, beam 450 kg and slings/shackles 180 kg sees 4, 930 kg. Its arithmetic margin is 70 kg, but all other components, configuration and operating conditions still need verification. A narrow margin is not permission to ignore uncertainty in the payload.

Worked changed case: the payload estimate is uncertain by 150 kg. Its possible total is 5, 080 kg, exceeding the rating by 80 kg. Obtain a reliable weight or replan; do not select the favorable estimate. Main-hoist and auxiliary-hoist ratings cannot simply be added to handle a load neither can carry independently without an explicitly engineered and approved arrangement.

Where verified dimensions and material density are an authorized weight basis, calculate volume with consistent units, then mass = density × volume. A solid O68 rectangular steel training piece 2.0 × 0.5 × 0.1 m has volume 0.10 cubic metre; using an assigned density 7, 850 kg per cubic metre gives 785 kg before attachments. A hollow item is not a solid block: subtract validated void volume and include actual contents. If dimensions, density or contents are uncertain, preserve that uncertainty instead of presenting a precise calculation as a verified actual weight. This calculation cannot establish an unknown alloy or hidden liquid level.

## Q08 — Rigging geometry and load stability

Position the hook vertically over the load's center of gravity using the approved lifting points and rigging arrangement. A misplaced hook can cause the load to shift suddenly as it clears its support. Keep sling angles, hitch configuration, edge protection, component compatibility and unequal leg loading within the qualified rigging plan. The operator needs enough understanding to recognize missing information and unsafe arrangements; this course is not a replacement for rigger qualification.

For an ideal static classroom two-leg symmetric lift, leg tension is W/(2 sin theta), with theta measured above horizontal. At a smaller angle the tension increases. Real selection must account for actual geometry, component ratings and manufacturer instructions. Do not assume three or four legs share perfectly just because their lengths look equal. Protect slings from cutting/crushing and keep body parts out of pinch points as slack is taken up.

Worked O68 example: W is 3, 000 kg and theta is 30 degrees. Each ideal leg tension is 3, 000 kg-force equivalent, not 1, 500. At 60 degrees it is approximately 1, 732.05. Changed: the center of gravity is offset; the symmetric calculation no longer establishes actual leg demands. Stop for a verified rigging arrangement before lifting.

## Q09 — Initial lift and brake check

Before taking strain, ensure the load is free to move and is not bolted, snagged, frozen or otherwise restrained. Never use an overhead crane to discover whether an attachment is still connected by pulling harder. Position the hook correctly, clear people from exposure and take slack smoothly. Conduct the prescribed initial lift/check at a safe small height over the support, checking balance, rigging seating, brake/control behavior and clearance.

If the load tilts unexpectedly, slips, snags or moves differently than planned, lower safely and correct the cause before traveling. Do not let a coworker hold a heavy load level by hand or stand on it. Keep hands clear while tension changes. The initial check is not a proof load and does not establish an unknown object's weight. Excessive force or trial lifting cannot replace reliable planning information.

Worked: a machine begins to tilt because one mounting bolt remains installed. Stop the lift, return it safely to support and resolve restraint under an authorized method; do not use the crane to break the bolt. Changed: the mounting bolts are removed but liquid shifts inside. Reassess actual contents, center of gravity and stability before another attempt.

For covered equipment, each load approaching rated capacity requires a brake check by lifting a few inches and applying the brakes. Use the actual procedure and safe exposure controls; do not invent a universal percentage definition or use this check to justify an overloaded lift.

## Q10 — Travel, swing control and operator position

Plan smooth starts and stops for bridge, trolley and hoist motions. Suspended loads act like pendulums: abrupt changes can create swing even when capacity is adequate. Use the taught equipment-specific control technique and avoid aggressive reversing to chase the load. Keep enough clearance for the entire load and avoid side pulls. The training rule is no intentional side pulling; any real exceptional operation requires the governing qualified authorization and equipment suitability, not operator improvisation.

Maintain a safe operator position with visibility and an escape route. Do not walk backwards into unseen hazards, stand between the load and fixed objects, or route a pendant cable through a pinch zone. Lift only high enough for the planned safe clearance; unnecessary height increases consequences and may reduce visibility. If required view is lost, stop and use an approved signaling arrangement rather than continuing from memory.

Worked: a floor operator follows the load into a narrow aisle and becomes trapped between it and a press. Stop and replan the operator/load paths. Changed: the aisle is empty but the load oscillates beyond its marked envelope. Restore controlled safe conditions; an empty aisle does not make loss of control acceptable.

## Q11 — Signals, handoffs and shared control

Choose and test the communication method before movement. Use the applicable standard hand signals and ensure both parties understand their meaning; agree any necessary alternate communication in advance. Establish who directs the operation and how control transfers. When a load is hidden from the operator, the person directing it must have the needed view and a reliable communication path. Stop if the signal is unclear or required communication fails.

Radio-crane control and voice radio signaling are different systems, both requiring identification and reliability. Prevent competing control sources and unintended activation during handover. Secure the inactive transmitter under the approved procedure. Emergency stop directions must be respected even when given by someone other than the normal signaler. Do not treat a warning horn as permission for workers to remain beneath a moving load.

Worked: the signaler leaves the landing zone and another worker gives an unfamiliar hand movement. Stop safely and establish an understood signaler/handoff before moving. Changed: the replacement calls out directions for the neighboring crane. Require unambiguous crane identification; do not infer which operation was intended.

## Q12 — Landing and below-hook devices

The landing plan needs suitable supports, clearance for rigging removal and protection against tipping or rolling. Lower smoothly and verify stable support before slackening or disconnecting. Do not put hands under a load to position a support at the last moment. A load can remain unstable after its weight leaves the hook, particularly long cylinders, uneven machines or stacked material. Use the prescribed blocking/restraint plan.

Magnets, vacuum lifters, clamps, grabs and lifting beams require specific instructions and compatible loads. Surface condition, material, thickness, orientation, temperature, power supply and attachment condition can change their capability. A magnet that holds a small demonstration plate does not prove it can retain the intended production load. Do not lift several stacked sheets unless the device and approved procedure explicitly support that operation. Plan for loss of attachment power/retention and keep people out of the exposure area.

Worked: a clamp is intended for vertical plate handling but the proposed turn changes its loading direction. Hold for the correct rated device and approved procedure. Changed: a vacuum alarm activates during the initial check. Follow its safe response and land safely; do not mute the alarm to finish the transfer.

## Q13 — Unusual loads and coordinated lifts

Hot metal, hazardous contents, long flexible loads, load turning and two-hoist/two-crane lifts require special planning and often additional equipment or authorization. A routine operator qualification does not establish readiness for all of them. Identify how load share, center of gravity, geometry and stability change throughout the movement. Small differences in hoist motion can transfer load unexpectedly between systems.

Do not use two individually undersized hoists on the assumption that each takes exactly half. The supporting structure, each hoist, rigging and control scheme must all be suitable for the engineered arrangement. A designated leader and tested communications are essential where more than one operator is involved. This curriculum uses tabletop cases for such special operations unless a separately validated training plan expressly includes them; the basic credential does not imply unrestricted special-lift competence.

Worked O68 case: a 6, 000 kg load is proposed for two 3, 000 kg hoists with no load-sharing analysis. Even ideal half-sharing leaves no allowance for actual accessory loads or imbalance. Hold for qualified planning and suitable equipment. Changed: one hoist moves first. The distribution can change immediately; stop the scenario and reassess rather than estimating by eye.

## Q14 — Malfunctions, isolation and maintenance boundaries

Unexpected movement, failure to stop, unusual mechanical noise, damaged electrical components or a retention failure require a safe response and defect control. Protect the load/area, use the actual emergency procedure and summon qualified assistance. Do not open an energized cabinet, adjust a brake under load or defeat a limit as an operator's quick fix. Power loss may leave gravity and stored energy hazards; a stopped motor is not proof of a safe maintenance state.

Maintenance isolation must address the actual energy sources and prevent other cranes on a shared runway from creating exposure. Follow the employer's authorized lockout/tagout arrangements and verification. Only authorized persons perform their assigned isolation/repair functions. Before returning to service confirm required guards, protective functions, tools/personnel clearance and testing/release. Record the defect and repair distinctly from the release decision.

Worked: a hoist intermittently continues after the command is released. Stop use and document the symptom. A restart that seems normal is not evidence of correction. Changed: maintenance fixes the control but leaves a guard removed. The repair alone does not complete safe return to service.

Know the fire/emergency plan, alarm and evacuation route before the shift. In US extinguisher labeling, A addresses ordinary combustibles, B flammable liquids, C energized electrical equipment, and D combustible metals; a general extinguisher is not suitable for every hazard. Select only the equipment specified for the hazard and act only within training and the employer's response policy. Do not use water on energized electrical equipment or remain to fight a growing fire. Raise the alarm, protect escape and evacuate when required. If authorized to use an extinguisher for a small incipient fire, follow its instructions with an escape route maintained; this lesson is not firefighter qualification. Inspect location/access and visible service condition of required extinguishers and report deficiencies.

## Q15 — Shutdown and securing

Complete the actual equipment's securing sequence: land and stabilize the load, remove unnecessary attachments as instructed, place the hook/controls in the specified safe state, park in the designated location, disable/secure controls and communicate restrictions. Do not leave a suspended load unattended. Ensure a parked hook or pendant does not create a strike hazard or obstruct traffic. Outdoor equipment may require additional wind, rail-clamp or storm precautions specific to its design.

Different equipment has different parking and isolation arrangements; do not invent one universal height or switch position. A controlled stop and an isolation for maintenance serve different purposes. Keep access to emergency disconnects clear. Secure remote transmitters so another person cannot inadvertently command the crane. Record any abnormal event and the actual final state, particularly if normal parking cannot be achieved safely.

Worked: the operator finishes with an empty hook at face height in an aisle and a powered transmitter on a bench. Correct both strike and unintended-command hazards using the prescribed parking/control-storage procedure. Changed: a fault prevents normal parking. Protect the affected zone, apply the authorized abnormal securing response and hand over the restriction instead of pretending normal shutdown occurred.

## Q16 — Integrated planning and honest qualification

A complete transfer requires several conditions at once: authorized operator, suitable equipment and inspection status, verified load/rigging, controlled route and landing, understood communications, functioning required protection and a safe securing plan. A correct weight calculation cannot override a sticking control or occupied route. Each material change must be assessed before proceeding; do not reuse a stale plan because the first lift went well.

Worked O68 case: a 4, 000 kg rated hoist carries payload 3, 250, beam 400 and slings 150 kg. Total 3, 800 gives a 200 kg arithmetic margin. The load is still not ready if the landing support is damaged. Changed: supports are repaired but payload becomes 3, 500 kg; total 4, 050 exceeds rating by 50. Each issue is resolved independently and changes can create new failures.

The independent assessment observes inspection, planning, unloaded control, initial check, controlled transfers, landing, securing and records. Award only the control mode/type actually demonstrated. Missing actual performance must be plainly identified on a knowledge-only record. The purpose is reproducible safe decisions and operation, not a certificate produced by silently averaging away a failed critical action.

# VUC068 — Delivery and assessment workbook

## Preparation, equipment and schedule

Plan 48 hours: 10 instruction/checks, 6 application labs, 18 supervised operating stations, 8 integrated practice/assessment, 3 controlled written final and 3 remediation/records. Record actual attendance and additional practice; time is not evidence of proficiency. Instructor must be competent and authorized for the crane/control mode and training tasks. Use qualified rigging, inspection, maintenance and technical personnel for their own responsibilities. Before instruction diagnose units/weight arithmetic, control orientation, stop recognition and load-path reasoning; teach missing foundations before actual operation.

Provide a suitable inspected overhead/gantry crane with actual manuals and capacity markings; protected training area; validated stable loads and landing supports; identified rated rigging and appropriate below-hook devices; radios and applicable signal reference; safe control storage; inspection/defect records; accessible emergency arrangements; and safe fault photographs/simulator cases. Cab operation additionally requires the approved access and rescue arrangements. No one climbs onto the bridge, enters an electrical enclosure or performs energized maintenance for this course. No rubber-tired gantry performance is represented as the CCO overhead practical. Record the actual type and control mode.

The responsible team validates crane support/runway, inspection status, route/load capacities, device checks, personnel protection and emergency plan before actual training. Faults remain photographic/tabletop or isolated training components; never install a fault, disable a protective function or create a live emergency. Actual operating instructions take precedence over fictional O68 paper data. Use generous validated capacity margin and no hazardous contents, personnel lifting or molten material for the ordinary practical.

Demonstrate a known task with explanation, let learners practice a changed safe task with feedback, then assess independently using different load/path arrangements. Keep assessor answers and final papers controlled. Do not send training notifications or change business records; all examples and forms are synthetic.

Stop unsafe action immediately. Critical errors include capacity violation, unsupported load/rigging assumption used to lift, side pulling, unsafe exposure, bypassed protection, ignored stop/lost required signaling, unauthorized control or fabricated evidence. ## I68 original independent practical

Create distinct teaching set T and assessment sets A/B. Each assessment set uses two stable verified loads with known center-of-gravity/lifting arrangements and at least two landing locations. Establish actual equipment/control identity, manual revision, all component ratings, load/accessory weights, safe route, operator position, signals, exclusion, supports, emergency plan and securing instructions. A qualified instructor validates the entire course before the learner attempts it. Do not copy the proprietary CCO course dimensions or timed scoring.

Use an open protected area and lightweight visual route markers. Landing rectangles provide at least 0.5 m clearance around each test-load footprint. Route width/height and turns must allow controlled movement with ample clearance; no person acts as an obstacle. Any simulated obstruction must be outside the real safe envelope. Record the validated route, actual distances and required safe clearances. Schedule about 90 minutes plus familiarization; elapsed time is coaching information, not a score incentive to rush.

Independent sequence: verify authority/equipment and records; perform pre-use inspection and authorized checks; calculate and brief the actual lift; establish correct hook position and rigging; perform initial check lift; transfer load to a prepared landing using bridge and trolley movement; complete a second transfer with a safe direction change; return a load to its designated support; remove rigging safely; park/secure and complete handover. Three transfers must all pass. Include an unloaded orientation step and a controlled planned stop signal. Where cab operation is claimed, observe actual approved cab operation/access; where remote or pendant is claimed, observe that mode. Do not award unobserved modes.

Require every transfer to remain within the approved envelope, with no side pull, uncontrolled swing, collision, unplanned ground contact, person beneath the load or ignored stop. The load must be fully supported stably inside its landing rectangle before release, and controls must produce the intended movements. A safe voluntary pause is allowed. Assessor intervention to prevent an unsafe act fails the independent attempt. Stop and record equipment/weather interruption separately from learner failure; restore safe conditions and restart the complete affected transfer. New equipment requires new identity/familiarization and a fresh full integrated assessment.

Set B changes verified load, pickup/landing positions and safe route while preserving equivalent difficulty and controls. Require a new calculation and briefing, not memory of joystick movements. Maintain all actual results, including failures. No author-created record claims anyone has already operated this course.

## Written final, remediation and credential

Private Form A and changed Form B each contain 16 original three-decision cases, 96 points. Pass at least 77/96 and at least 4/6 per domain with no unresolved critical error. Allow 150 minutes, calculator and coursebook, not keys or help. Keep the keys private. This differs deliberately from the external CCO exam and must not be represented as its questions or grading. Form B includes the original situation so changed questions remain answerable independently of memory.

Require corrected formative checks, six completed labs, every station, I68 and written final before awarding the VNDRLY operational training credential. If actual performance is unavailable, issue only a knowledge-completion record listing the missing practical work. State observed crane/control mode and task limitations, not all crane types. External CCO certification, inspection certification and employer authorization remain separate. A one-year VNDRLY review reminder is a training policy, not an external certification expiration rule; reassess earlier after relevant equipment/task change, unsafe performance or incident.

Remediation: identify the failed decision/action, reteach its explanation, use a changed practice example and reassess with the alternate written/physical set. Preserve both attempts. Accommodations support access and communication without removing required safe performance. An unsupported pass, copied observation or erased critical error invalidates the credential decision.

## Q01-L1

Does passing a written overhead course establish competence with every control mode?

**Answer:** No. Record actual demonstrated modes and required employer authorization.

**Reason/remediation:** Knowledge and actual equipment performance are distinct evidence. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

A new below-hook attachment is installed. What must the operator verify?

**Answer:** Attachment suitability, instructions, competence and the revised lift plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Familiarity with the crane does not establish attachment competence. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

What are the three basic powered motions?

**Answer:** Hoist, trolley and bridge travel.

**Reason/remediation:** They move the load vertically and along two distinct horizontal axes. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

Does a transmitter resting beside a crane establish its pairing?

**Answer:** No. Verify identity by the approved safe procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** Location can change without changing pairing. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

A 1.6 m load crosses a 2.4 m opening. What is centered static spare space per side?

**Answer:** 0.4 m.

**Reason/remediation:** (2.4−1.6)/2=0.4; this is geometry only, not a complete safety clearance. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

Why plan the operator path separately?

**Answer:** The operator can be trapped or lose footing/visibility even if the load fits.

**Reason/remediation:** Load clearance alone does not protect the person controlling it. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

Why is damaged pendant strain relief a defect?

**Answer:** Conductors must not become the structural support; damage can compromise control/electrical safety.

**Reason/remediation:** A working button does not demonstrate safe cable support. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L2

Can a normal pre-use check replace a missing required periodic inspection?

**Answer:** No. Resolve the distinct inspection requirement.

**Reason/remediation:** Different inspection duties are not interchangeable. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L1

May a damaged hook be heated straight by an operator?

**Answer:** No. Remove/report and use the authorized disposition/replacement process.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance after improvised repair does not establish strength. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L2

Does hoist-chain inspection use any convenient sling-chain criterion?

**Answer:** No. Use the actual hoist chain and manufacturer/applicable criteria.

**Reason/remediation:** Different components and service conditions have different requirements. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L1

Should the upper limit be the routine stop for every lift?

**Answer:** No. Use normal control; test the limit only through the prescribed safe procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** A protective limit is not a production positioning target. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L2

Does overload protection determine an unknown load weight safely by pulling until it trips?

**Answer:** No. Establish reliable load information before lifting.

**Reason/remediation:** A protective device does not replace a valid lift calculation. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L1

O68 payload 2, 200 kg plus fixture 350 and rigging 150: total?

**Answer:** 2, 700 kg.

**Reason/remediation:** All attached loads under the O68 convention count. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L2

Solid 1×0.4×0.1 m piece at assigned density 7, 850 kg/m³: mass?

**Answer:** 314 kg.

**Reason/remediation:** Volume 0.04 m³ times 7, 850; actual density/dimensions must be valid. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L1

Ideal 2, 000 kg symmetric two-leg load at 30 degrees above horizontal: leg demand?

**Answer:** 2, 000 kg-force equivalent per leg.

**Reason/remediation:** 2, 000/(2×0.5); the angle increases leg tension. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L2

Why position the hook over the center of gravity?

**Answer:** To avoid unintended shift/tilt and side loading at lift-off.

**Reason/remediation:** Visual symmetry of the object does not necessarily locate its center of gravity. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L1

The load is still bolted down. May the crane break it free?

**Answer:** No. Safely resolve restraints through an authorized method before lifting.

**Reason/remediation:** Breakout may create shock, unknown demand and uncontrolled release. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L2

What if the initial check lift shows tilt or slipping?

**Answer:** Lower safely and correct the cause before travel.

**Reason/remediation:** The check is intended to reveal a condition requiring correction, not permission to continue. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L1

Why is an abrupt direction reversal risky?

**Answer:** It can increase swing and dynamic demands.

**Reason/remediation:** Smooth equipment-specific control is needed rather than chasing the pendulum. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L2

May an operator stand between the load and a fixed press to improve the view?

**Answer:** No. Establish a safe position and communication.

**Reason/remediation:** Visibility does not justify a crush exposure. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L1

What if required signals become unclear?

**Answer:** Stop safely and re-establish understood communication.

**Reason/remediation:** Continuing from a guessed command can move into an unseen hazard. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L2

How should a control transmitter be handed over?

**Answer:** Use the approved ownership/disable/storage and identification process.

**Reason/remediation:** Two active or ambiguous control sources can cause unintended motion. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L1

When can rigging be removed at landing?

**Answer:** After the load is stably supported and the safe coordinated release procedure is followed.

**Reason/remediation:** Weight off the hook alone does not prove stability against tipping/rolling. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L2

Does a magnet demonstration on one plate establish capacity on every stack?

**Answer:** No. Verify actual device/material/surface/thickness/stack conditions under instructions.

**Reason/remediation:** Retention capability depends on the specific load and configuration. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L1

Can two 2 t hoists automatically lift 4 t together?

**Answer:** No. Verify engineered load sharing, accessories, structure and coordinated operation.

**Reason/remediation:** Adding nameplate capacities does not establish a valid system. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L2

Why can a load turn change the lift plan?

**Answer:** Center of gravity, load share and attachment direction can change.

**Reason/remediation:** A safe starting arrangement may not remain safe through rotation. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L1

Does emergency stop equal maintenance isolation?

**Answer:** No. Follow authorized energy isolation and verification.

**Reason/remediation:** Stored energy and other equipment can remain hazardous. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L2

Is water suitable for an energized electrical fire?

**Answer:** No. Raise alarm and follow trained response/evacuation and suitable equipment policy.

**Reason/remediation:** Electrical exposure and unsuitable extinguishing media can worsen the hazard. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L1

Can an empty hook be left at head height in a walkway?

**Answer:** Not as a safe parked state; follow the specified parking arrangement clear of exposure.

**Reason/remediation:** An empty hook can still injure people. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L2

What if a fault prevents normal parking?

**Answer:** Use the authorized abnormal securing response and preserve the restriction in handover.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not record normal shutdown when it did not occur. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L1

Can sufficient hoist capacity offset a failed required control check?

**Answer:** No. Each readiness condition must be satisfied.

**Reason/remediation:** Safety requirements are not averaged into a score. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L2

Does a successful first transfer validate a changed second load automatically?

**Answer:** No. Reassess material changes in load, route, rigging and conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** The earlier plan describes the earlier operation. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.
