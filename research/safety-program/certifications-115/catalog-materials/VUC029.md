# VNDRLY/U Commercial Driving — CDL and Endorsements

VUC029 · Original instructional edition · Source review 17 November 2026

This program teaches commercial driving decisions and performance, with separate passenger, school bus, tank, hazardous-material and multiple-trailer modules. A VNDRLY completion record is not a CDL, endorsement, medical certificate, carrier authorization, ELDT registry submission, or state skills-test result. A learner needs the actual legal prerequisites and qualified instruction before operating. The delivery guide specifies equipment, practice, assessments and records. The source crosswalk identifies federal curriculum units; coverage does not establish registered-provider status. Use the full standard Class A curriculum rather than assume a Class B upgrade exemption. Vehicle-specific manuals and current route/state rules govern real operations.

## D01 — License, vehicle and work authorization

Start with the vehicle and operation, not the job title. Record the power unit and trailer ratings, actual weights, combination rating/weight, passenger design capacity, cargo classification and intended route. Federal Class A covers combinations at least 26,001 lb using the applicable greater rating/actual weight, with towed unit(s) over 10,000 lb using the greater rating/actual weight. Class B covers a single vehicle at least 26,001 lb and any trailer not over 10,000 lb. Class C covers vehicles outside A/B that meet the passenger or specified hazardous-material criteria: designed for at least sixteen people including the driver, placard-required material, or specified select agents/toxins. A vehicle below CDL thresholds can still be a regulated CMV under other rules. An empty trailer does not erase its rating.

Match the license class, endorsements and restrictions to the configuration. T concerns doubles/triples; P passengers; N tanks; H hazardous materials; X combines N and H; S school buses. Air brakes are not a separate endorsement: restrictions can prohibit air brakes or full-air systems. Automatic-transmission, non-fifth-wheel Class A, passenger-vehicle-class and intrastate restrictions also matter. Inspect the actual license record rather than guess from a wallet card's color.

Before training, verify CLP status, medical/self-certification category, supervising driver's qualifications, permitted occupants and state prerequisites. A P/S learner permit does not permit ordinary revenue passengers or children. An N learner permit permits only an empty tank and excludes an unpurged hazardous-material residue tank. First A/B, B-to-A and first P/S/H applicants generally need the applicable registered-provider ELDT before the relevant state test. H requires theory before its knowledge test; A/B/P/S include actual behind-the-wheel training. There is no federal minimum hour substitute for demonstrated proficiency. States can add requirements.

**Worked decision: ** A 32,000-lb-rated truck towing a 12,000-lb-rated equipment trailer is not a Class B assignment merely because the trailer is empty. Establish the combination data and Class A eligibility, then check braking and coupling restrictions. If a dispatcher offers only a Class B driver, hold dispatch and obtain the correct driver or a genuinely compliant different configuration. Do not relabel the load.

## D02 — Fitness, medical status and professional duties

Driving readiness includes adequate sleep, attention, vision, physical ability and valid qualifications. Check medical certification and applicable variances through the carrier process. A prior certificate does not resolve a new condition affecting safe driving. Tell the medical examiner about medications and symptoms; do not conceal sleep problems or use stimulants to compensate for fatigue. Sedating nonprescription medicine can impair driving even without a controlled-substance label. A prescription alone is not permission to operate while impaired.

Know the carrier's Part 382 testing and Clearinghouse procedures: pre-employment controlled-substance testing, random, reasonable suspicion, applicable post-accident, and return-to-duty/follow-up requirements. A prohibited driver cannot drive until the required process is satisfied. Refusal and positive results are not repaired by changing employers. Do not use alcohol within the prohibited pre-duty period or while on duty; a CMV alcohol concentration of 0.04 or greater is a serious disqualification threshold, not a safe drinking target. The rule also prohibits other alcohol-related conduct below that number. Report traffic convictions and loss of privileges through the required channels.

Use three points of contact for cab access, face the vehicle and keep hands free. Adjust seat, belt and mirrors before moving; secure loose cab objects. Learn the vehicle's blind areas from the driver's position. Buckle the belt before movement and require applicable occupant restraints. Stop safely before reading a message, entering navigation, searching an app or addressing a complex conversation. Hands-free equipment does not remove cognitive distraction. Never text or hold a mobile telephone while operating contrary to the CMV rules.

**Worked decision: ** A driver has two hours legally available but is nodding off. Legal hours do not make the person alert. Park safely, notify dispatch, and arrange rest or relief. Record the actual status; do not invent a breakdown to disguise fatigue. If pressured to drive unlawfully, state the safety issue, preserve messages and use the carrier escalation and FMCSA coercion complaint process. Retaliation concerns can also fall under OSHA's transportation whistleblower process; prompt reporting matters.

## D03 — Systems, controls and defect recognition

With the vehicle secured, identify steering, accelerator, service brake, parking brake, transmission selector, ignition, emergency controls, lights, wipers, horn and gauges. Explain what each indicates, including oil pressure, coolant temperature, charging, air pressure and warning lamps. Locate emergency equipment and the correct manual. Do not discover a transmission mode or retarder switch on a steep descent.

The engine converts fuel to power; cooling, lubrication and exhaust systems control heat, friction and emissions. A loss of oil pressure can damage the engine rapidly. Stop safely and follow the warning/manual rather than wait for noise. Exhaust entering the cab is a health hazard. Never run an engine in an enclosed area without an approved ventilation arrangement. Do not remove a hot cooling-system cap. Electrical defects can disable safety systems or start a fire; isolate through approved procedures, not improvised wire bridges.

The driveline transfers torque through transmission, shaft and axles. Steering and suspension keep the vehicle controllable and load supported. Inspect for visible looseness, cracks, missing fasteners, damaged springs/air bags, leaking components and tire interference. Do not crawl under an unsupported vehicle or touch hot/moving components. Wheel fastener streaks, damaged rims, bulges, cuts, exposed cord and mismatched tire requirements warrant evaluation. Tire pressure is checked with an appropriate gauge under the specified conditions, not a kick.

Brakes convert motion to heat; heat reduces available braking. Air storage, compressor/governor, valves, chambers and foundation brakes work as a system. Hydraulic brakes depend on fluid pressure and appropriate assist. ABS helps prevent wheel lock; it does not create traction or shorten every stop. Stability systems and driver assistance do not override loading, weather or attention requirements. A warning light is a reason to investigate, not cover it.

**Worked decision: ** The low-oil-pressure warning remains on after starting. Secure and shut down according to the manual, report the indication and obtain maintenance clearance. Adding fluid blindly or revving until the light goes away is not diagnosis. Drivers may perform only trained, authorized servicing; brake adjustment and structural repair are not routine improvisation.

## D04 — Inspection before, during and after the trip

Choose level, protected ground where possible; park, secure and establish a safe inspection boundary. Review the previous defect report and repair certification where applicable. Walk a consistent route so no side or connection is forgotten. Check leaks and obvious damage before starting. Inspect the engine area safely, steering, suspension, tires/wheels, brakes and lines, lights/reflectors, frame/body, doors, cargo restraints, coupling and trailer supports. Verify registration, inspection and operational documents, fire extinguisher, warning devices and other required equipment.

In the cab, inspect glass, mirrors, belt, seat, visibility and controls. Test lights with assistance or a safe reflection method. Start using the manufacturer's procedure; observe gauges and warnings. Perform the correct hydraulic or air-brake checks, parking and service-brake checks in a controlled area. Never release securement during a stationary brake test without the prescribed chocks and precautions. A spoken list is not an actual inspection: point to the component, inspect it and describe the condition and disposition.

During travel, monitor gauges, handling, unusual sounds, odors and warning lamps. Make safe stops to check cargo and equipment as required; general cargo reinspection includes the first fifty miles and subsequent duty-status changes or three hours/150 miles, whichever occurs first, subject to the rule's specific exceptions. Do not enter a prohibited sealed load or create exposure to inspect cargo; establish the applicable exception and other checks. After the trip, report defects accurately with vehicle identity, location and symptoms. Passenger carriers have different daily-report obligations from property carriers; do not apply the property no-defect reporting exception universally.

**Worked defect report: ** Unit T17, left rear outer tire, visible sidewall bulge at valve position, found before departure, vehicle held, maintenance notified at 06: 20. This is actionable. “Truck bad” is not. If an inspector places the vehicle or driver out of service, obey the order and clearance process. Dispatch cannot waive it. Preserve the report and repair evidence; do not erase a defect because someone promises to look later.

## D05 — Air-brake use and controlled checks

Understand service, parking and emergency functions separately. The compressor supplies reservoirs; the governor controls operating pressure; dual circuits provide separation but not permission to continue with a failed circuit. Low-pressure warning provides time to stop before spring brakes may apply. Spring brakes can hold a parked vehicle; they must not be relied on as an ordinary high-speed stopping method. Do not compound service and spring-brake applications where the vehicle procedure prohibits it.

Before testing, use the exact vehicle manual and current state testing procedure, secure against movement and explain each step to the instructor. Build pressure to the specified governor cut-out, shut down as directed, and stabilize before timing leakage. Typical CDL screening limits are no more than 2 psi/minute for a single vehicle and 3 for a combination with brakes released; with service brakes applied, 3 and 4 psi/minute respectively. Verify configuration-specific limits, especially additional trailers. Confirm the low-air warning activates before pressure falls below 60 psi and spring-brake controls operate within the specified range, commonly 20–45 psi. These are training inspection checks, not a maintenance adjustment procedure or permission to accept a manufacturer defect.

Perform the manufacturer/state buildup and governor checks. Secure then test the parking brake gently in a designated area, and test service braking at low speed with a clear path. Check for pulling, delayed response and abnormal pedal travel. Failure means stop and report; do not keep driving to see whether the system improves. Never get under the vehicle to adjust brakes without qualification and proper safeguards.

Air-brake application includes lag before full braking develops; preserve extra space. Avoid repeated unnecessary brake applications that consume pressure. Select a safe gear and speed before a downgrade. Use the approved auxiliary braking method and intermittent service braking procedure for that vehicle and surface; never coast downhill in neutral. Retarders can cause traction loss on slippery surfaces. **Case: ** The combination's applied leakage is 6 psi in one minute after stabilization. It fails the typical 4-psi check. Repeating the test without repairing the fault does not convert it to a pass.

## D06 — Starting, shifting, steering and positioning

Before moving, establish a clear route, inspect surroundings, set mirrors and belt, confirm doors/securement, select the proper gear and release brakes in the correct sequence. Move smoothly at a speed allowing an immediate controlled stop. Look far enough ahead to plan while checking mirrors and the near path. Avoid staring at the hood or one mirror. Make small steering corrections and monitor trailer tracking.

Manual-transmission operation requires the actual shift pattern, ranges and engine speed guidance. Coordinate clutch, throttle and gear selection; use double-clutching where required. Choose a lower gear before entering a steep grade or tight turn. A missed shift on a descent is not an occasion to coast faster while searching. Automated transmissions also require knowledge of modes, creep, grade behavior and manual override. Efficient shifting saves fuel, but fuel economy never justifies lugging, overspeed or loss of control.

Rear wheels cut inside the front-wheel path on turns; rear overhang swings outward. Plan both. For a right turn, use a lawful setup that protects the inside from a vehicle trying to squeeze through, while checking the left/front swing and curb clearance. Do not enter an opposing lane without lawful control and adequate clearance. A wide left turn can send the trailer over a center island or curb if the tractor turns too soon. Stop when uncertain rather than continue a blind squeeze.

For merging, build appropriate speed within available distance, signal, observe mirrors and blind areas and select a real gap. Do not force another vehicle to brake abruptly. On exit ramps, slow before the curve to a safe CMV speed; passenger-car advisory speed may still be too high for the load. Avoid braking hard while turning. **Case: ** A delivery entrance requires the trailer to pass a pedestrian bollard and the tractor to swing near parked cars. Walk the route and obtain controlled assistance before entry; if the geometry does not fit, use another delivery plan. Confidence does not change the turning radius.

## D07 — Backing and docking without blind commitment

Avoid backing when a forward-exit plan is available. Before backing, secure and get out to inspect the whole route, overhead clearance, surface, dock height, people and escape space. Recheck whenever the scene changes. Prefer a sight-side setup and a slow, controlled maneuver. Agree on one spotter, signals and a visible safe position; anyone can call stop. Stop immediately if the spotter disappears. The spotter must not stand between vehicle and dock or within the projected sweep.

For straight backing, align the units before starting, use both mirrors and make small early corrections. For offset backing, plan the target lane and use controlled countersteering to move the trailer, then straighten before final alignment. For alley docking, establish the approach angle and watch both tractor swing and trailer rear. A pull-up is a safety correction, not a failure of pride. For parallel parking, establish front and rear reference points and check the blind side; never use only a remembered steering count for a different vehicle.

Stop, secure and get out again if a cone, person or obstacle cannot be located. Keep pedestrians outside the maneuvering area. Before docking, verify dock capacity, trailer support and the site's restraint/chock, communication and release procedure. A green light is meaningful only within the agreed system. Do not move while a forklift, person or bridge plate is still in the trailer. Confirm the load and doors are safe before pulling away.

**Worked case: ** Halfway into a blind-side parallel space, the right rear corner disappears and the spotter moves behind the trailer. Stop. Secure, relocate the spotter to an agreed visible safe position and inspect clearance. Do not ask the spotter to squeeze between trailer and wall. The range exercises require straight, left/right offset, 45/90-degree alley and both-side parallel maneuvers; passing one does not prove the others.

## D08 — Coupling, uncoupling and trailer control

Use the actual fifth-wheel and trailer manuals. Inspect coupling components, locking mechanism, kingpin, apron, air/electrical lines and landing gear. Choose a firm level location, secure the trailer against movement and align straight. Set trailer height so the tractor engages correctly; a high trailer can cause a false or high-hook connection. Back slowly under controlled conditions, then verify the lock physically from a safe position. Check for no unintended gap and correct jaw engagement around the kingpin shank. A tug test is an additional check, never a substitute for visual verification.

Connect and secure air/electrical lines in the correct arrangement without kinks, abrasion or ground contact. Charge/test the system according to the vehicle procedure; confirm trailer brakes and lights. Raise landing gear fully and secure its handle. Complete the walk-around before movement. Do not enter between units that can move, reach into an energized coupling or trust an audible click alone.

For uncoupling, use stable supporting ground and secure the trailer. Lower landing gear to the proper support position, manage suspension as specified, disconnect and stow lines, release the coupling only after loads and brakes are controlled, and pull out in stages while checking trailer stability. Never drop a loaded trailer onto weak soil or let a person hold landing gear. A sliding fifth wheel or sliding tandem adjustment requires its own manufacturer procedure, secured area and positive lock verification.

**Case: ** The tractor tugs against the trailer but there is a visible gap between fifth-wheel plate and trailer apron. Do not depart. Secure and correct the coupling under supervision. Also confirm the kingpin is actually captured; a tractor can resist a tug temporarily without a proper lock. For pintle, drawbar or converter-dolly systems, use their specific procedures; fifth-wheel competence alone does not establish competence with every connection.

## D09 — Seeing, communicating and preserving space

Scan far ahead, near the vehicle and into mirrors in a repeating pattern. Look for pedestrians behind parked vehicles, wheels beginning to turn, a vehicle drifting, brake lights several vehicles ahead and work-zone lane changes. Mirrors show only portions of the space; a recently clear lane can become occupied. Signal early enough to be useful, verify the response and keep observing. Eye contact does not guarantee that another person will yield. Use horn and lights appropriately without treating them as right-of-way commands.

Choose speed for available sight distance, traction, curve, load and stopping room, not merely the posted maximum. Count a time gap to a fixed landmark and increase it for length, poor weather, darkness or uncertainty. A useful starting training rule is about one second per ten feet of vehicle length below forty mph, with another second above forty; conditions may require substantially more. A sixty-foot combination at higher speed therefore begins with seven seconds in good conditions, not seven seconds in ice or fog. If someone cuts in, ease back to rebuild space instead of retaliating.

Check overhead and side clearance. A posted bridge height is not a substitute for knowing vehicle height or assessing resurfacing, load movement and the actual lane. Leave a way out when stopped; avoid parking so close that escape requires reversing into traffic. On narrow bridges and curves, slow before the restriction, establish position and wait if opposing movements cannot safely coexist.

**Worked hazard scan: ** A ball rolls from behind a parked vehicle beside a school. Reduce speed and prepare to stop before a child appears. Do not concentrate on the ball and miss an oncoming vehicle. In a construction zone, follow temporary signs/flaggers, watch workers and abrupt lane edges, and resist pressure from following traffic. Driving assistance may not recognize the changed lane geometry; the driver remains responsible.

## D10 — Darkness, weather, grades and emergencies

At night, keep lights, mirrors and glass clean, use appropriate beams and reduce speed so stopping distance stays within usable visibility. Do not look directly into glare. Fatigue and impaired road users increase risk; stop for rest before attention fails. Fog, heavy rain and blowing dust can hide a stopped queue. Slow smoothly and leave the travel lane at a safe location if visibility is inadequate; stopping in a live lane creates another hazard.

In cold weather inspect traction equipment, fluids and visibility systems. Install chains only in a safe designated area using the actual tire/chain instructions, verify clearance and retension as specified. Do not work beside live traffic or use chains as permission for unsafe speed. In heat, monitor cooling and tires; do not release hot pressurized coolant. Crosswinds particularly affect tall/light vehicles and exposed bridges. Delay travel when safe control is doubtful.

Before a downgrade, identify grade, load, safe speed, gear, braking method and escape routes. Avoid relying on repeated heavy braking; fade can remove the margin needed later. If brakes fail, use the planned escape route or ramp while maintaining control, warn others and follow trained emergency procedures. Never bypass an available escape ramp to save recovery cost.

Prevent skids with smooth inputs and appropriate speed. In a skid, ease the input that caused loss of traction and steer to regain the intended path without abrupt overcorrection; follow the ABS/vehicle-specific braking procedure. Never deliberately induce a jackknife in ordinary training. For a tire blowout, hold steering firmly, avoid abrupt braking and slow under control before leaving the roadway. If wheels leave the pavement, reduce speed and return only when safe with a controlled steering input, not a sharp swerve. A rollover often begins before the driver feels extreme lean; slow before curves and avoid abrupt evasive changes.

**Case: ** Rain begins after a dry spell just before a downhill curve. Reduce speed and establish gear/space before the curve, use appropriate braking and delay if traction is inadequate. Do not switch on a strong retarder without considering the manufacturer's slippery-surface guidance. Emergency cases are discussed or simulated safely; no learner must create a real blowout, rollover or skid to pass.

## D11 — Rail crossings, route planning and inspections

Plan using CMV-appropriate route data and official restrictions, not a car navigation shortcut. Establish height, width, length, gross and axle weights, bridge restrictions, permits, fuel/rest stops, weather and secure parking. Confirm destination access with the site. An overweight permit does not waive every bridge, route or axle restriction. Observe weigh-station instructions and lawful inspection directions; buses also need the applicable state rules checked.

At rail crossings, identify signs, signals, tracks, sight distance, low-ground-clearance risk and far-side storage. Never enter unless the entire vehicle can clear without stopping. Required-stop vehicles, including applicable passenger and hazardous-material operations, generally stop fifteen to fifty feet from the nearest rail under the federal rule, listen/look and proceed only when safe, subject to the rule's specific exceptions. Do not shift gears while crossing. Do not drive around a lowered gate or follow another vehicle onto occupied storage space. A second train may follow the first on another track.

If immobilized on tracks, get people away from the tracks and approaching train path; contact the railroad using the blue emergency-notification sign and crossing identifier, and emergency services. Do not remain in the vehicle trying repeated restarts while a train approaches. At a drawbridge, follow required stopping rules, signals and attendant directions; never assume a barrier's position proves the deck is safe.

During a roadside inspection, stop safely, secure, remain professional and provide the required documents. Explain a defect honestly. Do not argue that an electronic log or dispatch authorization overrides an out-of-service order. Record the result, notify the carrier and arrange authorized repair or relief. **Case: ** Navigation proposes a low-clearance road to save ten minutes. The driver rejects it, confirms an approved route and updates arrival expectations. This is competent trip planning, not poor productivity.

## D12 — Duty records, fatigue and trip arithmetic

For ordinary interstate property operations, plan around eleven hours driving after ten consecutive hours off and the fourteen-consecutive-hour driving window. A thirty-minute non-driving interruption is required after eight cumulative driving hours without one. The applicable sixty-in-seven or seventy-in-eight on-duty limit is another independent constraint. A thirty-four-hour restart may reset the property weekly calculation when properly used. Passenger rules differ: ten driving hours after eight off and no driving after fifteen on-duty hours; off-duty time does not count in that fifteen. Do not import the property restart or break rule into passenger operations without checking applicability.

Record all work for all employers as required. Loading, inspection, fueling and required waiting may be on duty even though the wheels are stopped. ELD edits must preserve the original record and explain corrections; never invent personal conveyance to advance a load. Know how to annotate an ELD malfunction and maintain required paper records while the carrier resolves it. A short-haul exception changes particular requirements only when every condition is met; it is not a universal hours exemption. Oilfield and other industry exceptions require documented applicability, not an oil-company logo.

**Worked property log: ** After ten hours off, duty starts 06: 00. Inspection/loading 06: 00–07: 00, drive 07: 00–11: 00, on-duty unloading 11: 00–11: 30, drive 11: 30–15: 30. The uninterrupted thirty-minute non-driving interval qualifies as the break interruption. Eight driving hours have accumulated; the fourteen-hour window ends 20: 00, and at most three driving hours remain, further limited by cycle hours and fatigue. A two-hour ordinary meal later does not move 20: 00 to 22: 00.

For a paper exercise, draw four duty-status lines, use the correct home-terminal time basis, total twenty-four hours and reconcile miles/locations and supporting documents. For a cycle recap, subtract hours dropping outside the rolling period and add today's on-duty hours; don't just look at driving hours. Split sleeper and adverse-condition provisions require their exact conditions and recalculation. Do not use an exception as a remedy for predictable dispatch delay. When uncertain, stop and obtain a qualified compliance decision before further driving.

## D13 — Cargo, weight, security and environmental care

Establish gross and axle weights, center of gravity, cargo dimensions and vehicle ratings before loading. A legal gross weight can coexist with an overloaded axle. Distribute and secure cargo to prevent movement, leakage or loss. Inspect deck, anchor points, tiedowns and blocking; reject damaged or unidentified equipment. Protect restraints from sharp edges and prevent rolling items from moving. A tarp contains or protects material but is not automatically a load restraint.

Use the applicable general and commodity-specific securement rules. Aggregate working-load limit normally must be at least half the cargo weight, with contribution depending on attachment arrangement; minimum tiedown counts and commodity rules still apply. A direct restraint can contribute differently from an over-the-top tiedown. Do not count each leg twice. **Example: ** A 10,000-lb article requires at least 5,000 lb qualifying aggregate WLL under the general rule, but two rated straps do not establish compliance without their condition, arrangement, length-related count and appropriate blocking. Heavy equipment, coils, logs, concrete pipe and other commodities have additional rules.

Load/unload only in a controlled area with stable support and communication. Stay clear of forklifts and suspended loads; do not assume the driver is a qualified rigger. Secure doors before opening and stand out of a shifted-load fall path. After loading, verify paperwork, quantities and restrictions, then perform required enroute checks. Seal numbers are evidence of custody, not proof of internal securement.

Protect cargo information, keys and access codes; park securely and report tampering. Do not confront thieves. Fuel and service without contaminating drains or soil; stop a safe controllable source only within training, notify the responsible party and follow spill procedures. Do not wash a spill away. **Case: ** A pallet shifts against a trailer door. Keep people clear and obtain a safe unloading plan rather than forcing the door while standing beneath the load.

## D14 — Crash, breakdown and fire response

First assess your condition and immediate dangers, secure the vehicle if possible, warn others and request emergency help with exact location, direction, vehicle and cargo information. Protect people from traffic and secondary collision. In a minor crash move vehicles off the roadway when lawful and safe; do not move an injured person except for immediate danger or trained direction. Account for passengers and arrange assistance. Do not step into traffic to collect photographs.

Use hazard flashers and required warning devices. Under the ordinary federal stopped-vehicle rule, place devices within ten minutes: on a two-way road, approximately ten feet on the traffic side and one hundred feet ahead/behind; on a divided or one-way road, ten, one hundred and two hundred feet toward approaching traffic. Curves, hills and other obstructions require adjusted advance warning, generally one hundred to five hundred feet, so approaching drivers see it. Do not use flame-producing devices where prohibited or near flammable cargo. If deploying would expose you to immediate danger, prioritize life safety and emergency assistance.

Use an extinguisher only if trained, the fire is small and appropriate for the equipment, escape is available and hazardous cargo is not threatened. Do not open a hot engine enclosure fully into a fire or approach a heated cargo tank. Withdraw and give responders shipping information. Protect records and cooperate with investigators without guessing causes or admitting facts you do not know.

Part 382 post-accident testing depends on the qualifying event, citations and time limits. Notify the carrier immediately and remain available; obtain necessary medical care first. Do not consume alcohol during the prohibited post-crash period. Record who was notified and when rather than decide that “no visible injury” means no testing. **Case: ** A rear-end crash produces a disabled vehicle and a citation. The driver activates the carrier's post-accident decision process promptly, not the next morning, while protecting the scene and obtaining medical assistance.

## D15 — Passenger vehicles and accessible service

Identify the bus's dimensions, axle and tire capacities, rear overhang, ground clearance and passenger limits. Adjust the driver's seat and all mirrors, including views around doors and the rear. Inspect emergency exits, release mechanisms, seats, belts, aisle, steps, handholds, interior lighting, temperature controls and restroom emergency equipment where installed. Inspect luggage compartments for secure latches, shifted articles, suspicious packages or tampering. Cycle an accessible lift using its manual and a protected area; a defective interlock or restraint requires correction, not a makeshift bypass.

Brief passengers before departure: restraint use, exit locations and operation, extinguisher location, emergency contact method, safe movement in the aisle and restroom alarm where fitted. Explain simply and check comprehension rather than recite rapidly. Load only at a safe stop with the vehicle secured. Keep passengers behind the standee line and within the vehicle's authorized seating/standing configuration. Close and verify doors before moving. Secure luggage so it cannot fall, block exits or shift into occupants. Handle bags with a stable stance and assistance for heavy items.

Ask a passenger with a disability what assistance is needed; speak to the person, not only a companion. Explain actions before touching mobility equipment. Use the lift/ramp within its rated capacity and manufacturer's sequence, secure the mobility device to approved points, and apply the appropriate occupant restraint separately. A wheel lock is not a transport tiedown. Accommodate service animals and communication needs under applicable ADA rules; do not impose invented proof requirements or demand a passenger transfer merely for convenience. Maintain a clear evacuation route and individualized assistance plan without publishing private medical details.

Manage disruption by calm instruction and, if necessary, a safe stop and assistance. Do not turn around to argue while driving. Avoid fueling with passengers aboard and never fuel in an enclosed space. Follow idling restrictions and prevent exhaust exposure near queues. Passenger HOS, weigh-station and inspection requirements are checked specifically. **Case: ** A rider's wheelchair fits the lift but the securement station has a damaged strap. Do not improvise a restraint from luggage webbing. Arrange safe compliant equipment or alternate service while treating the rider respectfully.

## D16 — Passenger emergencies, security and evacuation

Prepare an evacuation plan for each vehicle: usable exits, assistance roles, accessible evacuation, safe assembly location, headcount and communication. Explain that the safest choice may be remaining aboard if outside traffic is more dangerous, but fire, hazardous exposure, unstable location or imminent collision can require prompt evacuation. Secure the bus, shut down as appropriate, notify emergency services and choose exits away from traffic or the hazard. Give short instructions; designate capable helpers without requiring an untrained passenger to perform a dangerous lift.

Conduct a controlled drill with a stationary secured bus. Demonstrate exit release and a safe descent; do not jump from an elevated exit merely to prove speed. Move occupants to a protected assembly point, account for everyone and communicate missing persons to responders. Keep passengers away from traffic and the bus's hazard zone. Do not reenter for luggage. For a medical emergency, stop safely, call for qualified help and provide care only within training. Preserve emergency access.

Inspect unattended compartments before departure and after stops. Report unusual wires, packages, damage or behavior through the security plan without touching or opening a suspicious object. For a threat or hijacking, prioritize life, avoid provocative confrontation, follow trained emergency communication and cooperate with responders. Do not teach improvised weapons or pursuit as a driver duty. Keep passenger information secure.

At rail crossings and drawbridges, use the applicable required-stop procedure, ensure complete far-side clearance and prevent distraction during the crossing. A quiet-bus instruction can improve hearing. At a breakdown, assess whether a protected location is available and arrange replacement transport; do not leave passengers stranded without a plan. **Case: ** Smoke enters a coach cabin while stopped on a shoulder. The driver chooses a safe-side exit, communicates the threat and location, moves occupants away, accounts for them and prevents reentry. The driver does not spend evacuation time searching the luggage bay for the source.

## D17 — School-bus loading, mirrors and student safety

School-bus work adds danger-zone and student-management duties beyond ordinary passenger service. Identify the actual mirror systems and demonstrate their views with cones around the secured bus, including the front, sides and rear. Mirrors reduce blind areas but do not prove a child has left the danger zone. Teach riders to remain away from the bus until directed, cross only under the approved procedure and never retrieve a dropped object near or under the bus without telling the driver.

Review each authorized route and stop before driving. Identify visibility, traffic speed, turnarounds, rail crossings, shelter and safe student paths. Report a hazardous stop through the school process; do not casually move it to an unapproved location. At the stop, use the state's required warning-light sequence, stop arm/crossing gate and door procedure. Secure the bus, monitor traffic and count students as they board or leave. Keep the danger zone under observation; verify every child is accounted for, the doorway is clear and no clothing or bag is caught before closing and moving. Never wave traffic through a stopped-bus situation contrary to law.

Keep students seated and manage behavior without taking eyes off driving. Set clear expectations, use calm corrections and stop safely for a serious disruption. Record bullying or threats and notify the designated school staff; do not punish by abandoning a student. Field-trip riders may not know the routines, so brief them too. At the end of every route, physically walk the bus and inspect seats/under seats for sleeping children and left items. An electronic child-check device supplements, not replaces, the walk-through.

**Case: ** Seven children exit but the driver sees only six reach the expected safe location. Keep the bus secured, establish where the missing child is and use the approved assistance procedure. Do not move because traffic is impatient. If a child drops a phone near the front tire, movement remains prohibited until the danger zone is positively clear.

## D18 — School-bus emergencies and special maneuvers

Inspect school-specific equipment: stop and crossing arms, mirrors, exits and alarms, extinguisher, first-aid kit, seats, interior lighting, heaters/defrosters and required safety equipment. Learn the actual bus height, tail swing and wheelbase; changing buses changes reference points. Avoid backing whenever route planning can eliminate it. Where authorized backing is necessary, secure the area and use a controlled procedure with a visible trained spotter when available. Stop when sight or clearance is uncertain. Do not put a student behind the bus as a spotter.

High winds affect the broad bus body; slow or delay according to conditions and policy. Use strobe lights only under applicable rules and vehicle procedures. At rail crossings, quiet the bus, stop as required, look/listen using the approved door/window method, confirm far-side space and cross without shifting or stopping. Do not let a student's question interrupt the safety sequence.

Plan evacuation for regular and occasional riders, including mobility or communication needs. After a crash assess injuries and hazards, secure, call help, decide whether remaining or evacuating is safer, and account for every student. Use appropriate exits, assembly locations and trained assistance. Provide first aid only within qualification. Preserve information for school staff and law enforcement; release students only through the authorized reunification process. A parent-like stranger at the scene is not automatic release authority.

Security includes checking the bus before service, controlling access, reporting threats and suspicious items, and responding to an attack without escalating danger. Follow the district plan and emergency instructions; this course is not tactical-response training. **Case: ** A disabled bus is safely off the road in cold weather with no smoke or traffic intrusion. The driver maintains supervision and temperature safety while arranging relief rather than automatically evacuating children into the road. If fire develops, that decision changes immediately. Document the hazard-based decision, not a universal rule to always stay or always leave.

## D19 — Tanks, surge and bulk handling

A tank's liquid can move after the vehicle starts braking or turning. Forward/backward surge changes forces during stops; side-to-side movement and a high center of gravity increase rollover risk. Baffles reduce some longitudinal surge but do not eliminate side forces; compartments change distribution and loading choices. An apparently empty tank can retain hazardous residue and vapor. Do not enter a tank or treat cleaning as ordinary driving work.

Determine tank endorsement applicability from the current regulatory definition, not only whether the body looks like a tanker. The federal tank-vehicle definition includes liquid/gas tanks individually rated over 119 gallons and aggregating at least 1,000 gallons, permanently or temporarily attached to the vehicle/chassis. It excludes the specified empty storage-container tank not designed for transportation, temporarily carried on a flatbed. Verify all facts and other CDL applicability before applying the exception. Plan load using material density, allowable fill/outage, compartment ratings, gross/axle limits and cargo compatibility. Allow for expansion; “fill until it looks full” is unsafe. A small volume of a dense liquid may overload an axle before a large tank is volumetrically full. Keep load data and compartment distribution in the dispatch documents.

Inspect tank body, mounts, valves, caps, vents, hoses, closures, emergency controls and required markings using the actual equipment checklist. Do not block a vent or defeat a shutoff. For loading/unloading establish site communication, correct product/connection, securement, exclusion area, required bonding/grounding where applicable and emergency shutdown. Use only the approved written transfer procedure and qualified personnel. Verify capacity before transfer and monitor as required; never leave an attended transfer because a gauge seems steady. Stop a mismatch before opening valves.

Slow before curves and ramps, make smooth inputs, preserve additional stopping space and avoid sudden steering. A posted car advisory is not a tank speed guarantee. **Worked exercise: ** The training load is 3,000 gallons at a supplied 8.5 lb/gal, giving 25,500 lb of liquid before tank/vehicle weight. The vehicle's remaining payload allowance is 24,000 lb, so the proposed load is 1,500 lb too heavy even before axle/distribution checks. Reduce the approved quantity or change equipment; do not round the density down. Water-only training equipment must be controlled by the instructor; no hazardous transfer is required to practice the decision.

## D20 — Hazard classes and shipping communication

Hazardous-material transport uses a system, not a single diamond sticker. Recognize classes: explosives; gases; flammable liquids; flammable solids/spontaneously combustible/dangerous-when-wet materials; oxidizers/organic peroxides; toxic/infectious materials; radioactive materials; corrosives; and miscellaneous regulated hazards. Divisions, subsidiary hazards and packing groups refine the risk. Packing groups I, II and III indicate different degrees of danger where assigned; not every material has one. Never identify cargo by smell or an unlabeled container's shape.

The trained shipper classifies and prepares the shipment; the carrier/driver must not accept an obviously noncompliant offer. Read the shipping description in the required sequence: identification number, proper shipping name, hazard class/division and packing group when applicable. Check quantity, package type, emergency response information, emergency telephone and certification where required, plus special entries applicable to the material. Confirm physical packages, marks, labels and vehicle placards agree. A safety data sheet is useful but not automatically a compliant shipping paper.

Marks identify information such as a UN/NA number or proper shipping name; labels communicate package hazards; placards communicate transport-unit hazards. Table 1 placarding uses specified hazards regardless of quantity; Table 2 has an aggregate gross-weight threshold generally 1,001 lb for applicable nonbulk materials, but exceptions and bulk rules matter. Never use that threshold to excuse Table 1 or bulk placarding. Use the actual §172.504 table, material entry and exceptions before deciding. Do not remove placards from an uncleaned residue tank simply because unloading ended.

Keep papers recognizable and accessible: tab or place them first, within restrained driver's reach and visible/door holder while at controls; door holder or driver's seat when away. **Case: ** The paper says a Class 3 liquid while package labels identify a corrosive with different identification number. Hold acceptance and obtain a corrected, reconciled shipment from the responsible qualified party. Guessing which label is “probably right” can misdirect responders.

## D21 — Hazardous-material loading, compatibility and security

Inspect packages before acceptance: leaks, damage, closures, required marks/labels and securement. Do not load a leaking or unapproved package. Keep people out of the loading path, secure the vehicle and use the specified handling equipment. Prevent movement, crushing and contact with incompatible cargo. Consult the actual §177.848 segregation table, including its symbols, exceptions and subsidiary hazards; the same general class does not guarantee compatibility. Food, feed and toxic materials require particular separation controls. Do not substitute an ordinary pallet gap for required separation.

Bulk cargo requires the correct tank/package specification, inspection/test status, compatible product and actual transfer procedure. Identify shutdown and emergency control locations before starting. Some transfers require qualified attendance and specific connection/ignition controls. Do not connect a hose solely because the fitting fits. Know how to stop transfer from a safe location; never practice a release to prove readiness. Intermediate bulk containers, portable tanks and bulk cylinders are not interchangeable with a cargo tank's procedures.

Passenger service has restrictive hazardous-material rules. Ordinary freight acceptance on a bus is not permitted merely because there is luggage space. Section 177.870 contains limited exceptions and quantities; it prohibits specified poisonous hazards and imposes radioactive-material restrictions. Personal medical devices require their own applicable provisions and secure handling, not a blanket ban or blanket acceptance. Ask the qualified compliance person to resolve the actual item before loading. A classroom example never authorizes transport of explosives or radioactive material on a bus.

Security training includes general awareness and, where required, the employer's actual security plan: access controls, personnel responsibilities, route/stop communications, cargo custody and suspicious activity reporting. Protect information about high-risk loads and do not accept an unverified reroute from an unknown caller. **Case: ** A caller instructs the driver to divert a bulk load to a different gate and provides a new contact number. Stop safely, independently verify with dispatch using a trusted number, and preserve the request. Do not enter a secluded location to “sort out the paperwork.”

## D22 — Hazardous-material travel, emergencies and reporting

Plan lawful designated routes, tunnel restrictions, rail stops and parking before departure. Certain explosives require a written route plan; highway-route-controlled radioactive quantities have specialized routing provisions. A hazardous-material safety permit applies to specified materials/quantities and adds carrier duties, communications and related controls. Do not confuse an H endorsement, PHMSA registration and HMSP: they serve different purposes. Operating convenience alone does not justify ignoring route restrictions.

Know applicable attendance and parking rules. For Division 1.1/1.2/1.3 explosives, continuous attendance generally applies with tightly defined exceptions; a safe haven is formally approved, not simply a truck stop. Other covered hazardous loads on public roads/shoulders also have attendance duties. The attendance definition includes being awake/on the vehicle or within 100 ft with unobstructed view and appropriate qualification. Parking separation and fire rules matter; for covered loads do not park within 300 ft of open fire. Applicable smoking separation is 25 ft for listed hazards and specified residue tanks. During fueling, engine off and a person controls the process at the fill point. Inspect each tire at trip start and each parking event; obtain correction for defects and address overheating under the rule with qualified help, not by driving until it cools.

For a release, stop safely, withdraw uphill/upwind where appropriate, keep others away and call emergency services. Do not touch, taste, sniff or walk into a vapor cloud. Use shipping information and the current ERG from a safe place: identify the material by number/name, open its orange guide, check highlighted entries and green tables where applicable, and use the appropriate spill size/day-night and fire guidance. Isolation distances are not guessed from memory. Give responders the material, amount, container, location, exposure and observed conditions. Do not perform leak repair or rescue beyond training.

Immediate NRC reporting under §171.15 applies to specified serious outcomes and threats, with notice as soon as practical and no later than twelve hours after occurrence; written DOT5800.1 reporting under §171.16 generally has a thirty-day deadline for reportable incidents, with exceptions and supplemental duties. The carrier's designated reporter must apply the actual criteria. Driver notification is immediate and must not wait for a form. Record facts, times and contacts without speculation. **Case: ** A cargo tank leaks beside a drainage channel. The driver withdraws, warns, calls emergency services and dispatch, provides the shipping papers and does not enter the liquid to close an inaccessible valve. No audible alarm is needed to make the release dangerous.

## D23 — Doubles, triples and longer combinations

Multiple trailers amplify steering inputs: a small tractor maneuver can become a large rear-trailer movement. Rearward amplification, offtracking, longer stopping distance and additional connections require lower-risk route and lane decisions. Keep the heavier trailer in the correct position under the approved configuration, commonly ahead of the lighter trailer; never invent an arrangement contrary to manufacturer/carrier rules. Check axle and gross limits for every unit and the combination.

Inspect each fifth wheel/pintle, dolly, drawbar, safety device, air/electrical connection and landing gear. Couple using the actual system procedure on stable ground, verifying each mechanical lock visually and by the prescribed test. Ensure shutoff valves are in the correct operating configuration and the rear of the combination is properly closed; test air supply and brake operation at all trailers, not only the first. A connected electrical plug does not establish braking continuity. Uncouple in the approved sequence with every unit supported and secured; a dolly can roll or tip.

Avoid abrupt steering, high-speed curves, unnecessary lane changes and backing a configuration not designed or authorized for it. Allow extra space and choose lanes early. If a connection or brake fails, stop and obtain qualified assistance; do not drag a disabled dolly to a convenient yard. **Case: ** The second trailer's brake test does not respond even though the tractor gauge shows normal pressure. Hold departure and investigate the entire supply/control path. Normal tractor pressure is not a pass for the rear trailer.

A T endorsement alone does not satisfy all longer-combination-vehicle requirements. Where Part 380's LCV provisions apply, verify the required separate training, instructor qualifications, experience and certificate, and the legal route/configuration. This package teaches T knowledge and controlled multiple-trailer practice; it does not claim the separate AppendixF LCV qualification or authorize every triple combination. Record that boundary explicitly when advising a learner.

## D24 — Integrated dispatch and responsible completion

Use a dispatch decision in layers: qualified and fit driver; correct license/endorsements/restrictions; safe inspected vehicle; lawful secured cargo or passenger arrangement; viable route; adequate duty time and weather margin; complete documents; clear destination and emergency communications. One failed layer means resolve it before departure. A high written-test score does not cancel a mechanical defect or missing endorsement.

**Integrated exercise: ** A proposed combination has a correctly licensed driver, but the delivery route includes an unverified bridge, the load sheet exceeds the rear axle rating, and the ELD shows only forty minutes until a limiting clock. The correct answer is not to choose the most urgent problem and ignore the others. Hold dispatch, obtain verified routing, correct and reweigh the load, then replan timing or relief. Document all three resolutions. Do not edit the axle weight or clock to fit the plan.

For a passenger variation, add accessible equipment, headcount, briefing and evacuation needs. For a school-bus variation, add approved stops, danger-zone checks and student accounting. For a tank/hazardous variation, add product compatibility, fill limits, shipping/placarding, transfer controls, routing, security and release response. For doubles, add each connection and rear-unit brake check. Skills transfer only where they truly match; a competent tractor driver still needs bus-specific practice.

Prepare for state tests using the current state instructions and actual representative vehicle. The Texas handbook reviewed is revised March 2026. Texas's June 1, 2026 notice requires CDL/CLP knowledge examinations in English; do not promise a translated state examination based on an older handbook. VNDRLY can provide learning support while maintaining required safety communication and accurately distinguishing its own assessment from state testing. State eligibility, medical, security and administrative requirements must be verified at application time.

At completion the instructor records precisely what was taught and demonstrated, vehicle/class and modules, actual practice hours, theory/practical results and remaining legal steps. Do not issue a broad “all endorsements qualified” record when a learner completed only the core. A materials package can be complete before any person takes it; a learner credential requires that person's evidence. Neither statement establishes a state license or employer permission.

# Applied regulatory decisions — VUC029

Use with D01, D12, D14, D20–22. These are original explanations and exercises; the supplied government extracts let an instructor verify exact conditions without substituting memorized slogans for decisions.

## Security eligibility and licensing

An H endorsement involves TSA security-threat assessment and state licensing requirements in addition to knowledge and training. A VNDRLY examination does not establish security eligibility. Use the state and TSA official process, never an unofficial service promising a guaranteed result. Do not collect a learner's sensitive identity documents in an ordinary course worksheet. Record only that the responsible authority's requirement remains pending or has been verified through the lawful process. Restrictions, eligibility and administrative rules must be checked at application time.

## Hours exceptions are conditional

The ordinary property calculation in D12 is the starting point. A qualifying sleeper split uses at least seven consecutive hours in the sleeper and another period of at least two hours, totaling at least ten; the paired periods and recalculation must meet §395.1. Do not treat any two-hour lunch as a window reset. A short-haul record exception requires all applicable radius, reporting-location, release-time and record conditions; it does not permit unlimited driving. An adverse-driving extension concerns qualifying conditions not reasonably known before dispatch or the duty period, not ordinary predictable congestion. Oilfield waiting-time and restart provisions apply only to the stated operations and conditions. Read the supplied §395.1 before using any exception; if facts are missing, plan within the ordinary rule.

**Exercise: ** A driver knew about a scheduled road closure before leaving but plans to call its delay “adverse conditions.” Reject that justification and replan. For a different truly unexpected event, establish the rule's facts and every remaining clock before claiming an extension. Do not simply add two hours to every limit.

## Post-accident testing decision

Under §382.303, a fatality triggers applicable testing of the surviving CMV driver without requiring a citation. For injury with immediate treatment away from the scene or disabling damage requiring tow-away, the moving-violation citation and its timing matter. Alcohol citation window is eight hours; controlled-substance window thirty-two hours. The employer documents why an applicable alcohol test was not done within two hours and ceases alcohol attempts at eight hours; controlled-substance attempts end at thirty-two hours with required documentation. Necessary emergency care is not delayed. The driver remains available, communicates immediately and follows the supplied employer instructions.

**Exercise: ** A nonfatal tow-away crash is followed by a moving-violation citation to the CMV driver five hours later. Apply the alcohol and controlled-substance decision promptly; do not assume the delay cancels both. A fatal crash without a citation gives a different reason for testing. Preserve actual times and never backdate a sample.

## Shipping, marking and segregation practice

Use §172.101 to identify the actual entry, then applicable shipping-paper, packaging, label and placard requirements. A hazard class alone does not tell you every special provision. For a fictional nonbulk Table 2 load above its threshold with no exception, placards are required; for a Table 1 load the Table 2 weight threshold does not apply. Bulk and residue rules require separate review. The carrier cannot cure an unknown material by choosing the most alarming diamond.

Use the §177.848 table supplied in the reference. Locate the row and column for both hazards, then read the symbol explanation: an X generally prohibits the indicated common loading/transport/storage arrangement; an O requires the specified separation to prevent commingling if leakage occurs. A blank is not permission to ignore other hazards, subsidiary labels or material-specific prohibitions. Use all accompanying notes. **Exercise: ** The instructor gives a Class 3/Division 5.1 pair and then changes one item to an unidentified material. Read and record the actual table result for the first pair; hold the second until identified. Do not extrapolate from packaging color.

Where §172.704 applies, hazmat employees need general awareness, function-specific, safety and security-awareness training, and in-depth security-plan training when required. Initial/recurring training, testing and records are employer duties. A CDL H course alone does not automatically cover a worker's packaging, shipping or transfer functions. Include the actual employer/product task before authorizing that work.

## Permit and route exercise

Section 385.403 identifies permit-triggering materials and quantities: highway-route-controlled Class 7; specified explosive quantities; particular poisonous-by-inhalation zones/package sizes; and specified large bulk methane-containing gas shipments. Use the actual section, not a universal “all hazmat needs HMSP” claim. Section 385.415 requires permit identification, applicable written route plan and an answered carrier contact familiar with the route, plus prescribed communications at duty-tour start/end and permitted-load pickup/delivery. Maintain required communication records; an unanswered voicemail is not the designated live contact. Special radioactive pretrip inspection requirements also apply where specified.

**Exercise: ** A fictional shipment is sixty pounds net of Division 1.1 material. That exceeds the stated fifty-five-pound permit trigger. Verify carrier permit and all applicable route, document, attendance and security duties before dispatch. A driver's H endorsement alone does not clear it. Repeat with a shipment outside all listed permit triggers: ordinary hazardous-material rules still apply even if HMSP does not.

## Incident worksheet and report thresholds

For §171.15 consider death, hospital admission, public evacuation for an hour or longer, a major artery/facility closure for an hour or longer, specified radioactive/infectious events, marine-pollutant quantities and continuing danger. The person in physical possession has reporting duties; the carrier's designated reporting process must actually fulfill them. Alerting dispatch without ensuring the required report is made is not proof of compliance. Federal and state environmental reporting can add duties.

For the P22 fictional ninety-minute evacuation, prepare these mock facts: reporter/name/contact, represented carrier, occurrence time/location, known injuries, material class/name/quantity if known, event type and continuing danger. Apply immediate notice as soon as practical and no later than twelve hours. Then prepare the written-report data for the thirty-day-from-discovery deadline, retain the report for two years as required, and review supplemental updates when later injury, identification or cost information meets the rule. Do not fill an unknown quantity with a guess. The actual DOT5800.1 form and official submission process remain with the responsible reporter; no training exercise is submitted.

The planned day cannot all support driving: the cycle reaches sixty after six more on-duty hours, at 11: 00 on this timeline, and further driving is prohibited. Later non-driving work does not authorize more driving. Demonstrate a truthful mock ELD correction and malfunction paper record, never a business-record edit.

Score: totals twenty-four hours; correct driving/on/off totals; window/break interpretation; *cycle limit applied before driving; *truthful records without invented personal conveyance.

## P12 — Cargo and roadside incidents (D13, D14)

Use an inert fictional 8,000-lb article and restraint-rating cards. Calculate 4,000 lb minimum qualifying aggregate WLL under the general rule, then check arrangement, count, condition and commodity requirements. A scale card shows an overloaded rear axle despite a legal gross total: hold it. Lay out triangles on a closed range for two-way and divided roads and conduct mock crash/OOS calls.

Score: weight/restraint reasoning; *holds axle overload; correct safe warning layout; *protects people without hazard entry; clear report and testing notification. Never deploy into live traffic for assessment.

## P13 — Passenger inspection and accessibility (D15)

Use an actual secured passenger vehicle with lift and approved mobility securement, its manuals and an adult volunteer or suitable inert trainer. Inspect bus-specific components, cycle the lift, check interlocks, and secure mobility equipment and the separate occupant restraint correctly.

Score: complete physical inspection; *safe actual lift use; correct device/occupant securement; respectful assistance/communication; *holds defects rather than bypassing. Record each interlock and exit checked. Watching someone else does not pass.

## P14 — Passenger loading, luggage and briefing (D15)

Adult roleplayers only. Choose a safe stop, secure the bus, load/unload, stow luggage, brief safety and respond to a mock disruption and medical-device question. An inert card marked unknown hazardous freight must not be accepted without resolution.

Score: *safe loading/door sequence; clear aisle/exits and secure baggage; complete briefing; calm management without driving distraction; *proper restricted-cargo and accessibility decisions. Check standee line and actual vehicle capacity.

## P15 — Passenger evacuation, road and rail (D16)

Conduct a stationary evacuation drill with safe exits and an accessible assistance plan. Inject a headcount mismatch. Then observe actual passenger-vehicle road maneuvers and rail-crossing procedure on an approved route with a qualified instructor and only authorized training occupants.

Score: *safe exit and assembly decisions; accountability/missing-person report; security/breakdown judgment; *safe actual passenger-vehicle driving; correct actual rail procedure. Record separate observations. Required performance cannot be replaced by discussion.

## P16 — School danger zones and stops (D17)

Use an actual secured school bus, cones to demonstrate mirror coverage, and adult roleplayers, never children. Adjust all mirrors and identify blind areas. Execute the approved light/arm/door loading and unloading sequence, account for every rider and respond to a missing-child card by holding. Physically walk the bus after the route exercise.

Score: actual mirror coverage; correct stop/door sequence; *positive accounting before movement; *physical end-route check; appropriate stop-hazard/bullying reporting. A recorded child check without the walk fails.

## P17 — School inspection and maneuvers (D18)

Inspect school-specific equipment. On a closed range perform authorized backing and turns using actual tail-swing reference points. Before a new attempt the instructor changes an obstacle location. Discuss wind, strobe and security limits.

Score: full equipment inspection; correct reference points; *safe backing without a child spotter; *stops for changed clearance; safe weather/security decision. Actual equipment and maneuvers are required.

## P18 — School evacuation and rail (D18)

Conduct a stationary drill with adult roleplayers and an accessibility plan. Account for riders and control reunification using fictional identity cards. Demonstrate an actual school-bus rail procedure on an approved road with a qualified instructor. Simulate crash notification without contacting emergency services.

Score: *appropriate evacuation/exit choice; safe assistance/assembly; *headcount and controlled release; accurate crash communication; *actual rail sequence. Memorized speech does not replace operating the bus.

## P19 — Tank planning and controls (D19)

Use a safe clean empty tank trainer, its manual, and a water-only demonstration where approved. Locate and inspect closures, vents and emergency controls; draw the approved connection sequence. Calculate 2,000 gallons at 9.2 lb/gallon against an 18,000-lb payload allowance: 18,400 lb, so hold the 400-lb overload. Explain surge with a safe transparent trainer or video, never a rollover demonstration.

Score: component inspection; *correct capacity/weight decision; compatibility/fill/distribution reasoning; *safe shutdown sequence without release; sound driving plan. Record actual trainer operation. A tabletop-only attempt cannot be labeled physical transfer competence.

## P20 — HM descriptions and placarding (D20)

Use supplied current government sections and fictional cards: A is nonbulk Table 2 Class 3, 1,200-lb gross, no exception; B is a Table 1 material, twenty pounds; C has conflicting paper/package UN numbers. Locate the table and document decisions, shipping-description checks and paper storage.

Score: correct A decision; correct B decision; *holds C; correct description/emergency-information checks; accessible storage. A and B require placards on the stated facts. A real shipment also requires the actual material entry and applicable exceptions to be established.

## P21 — HM segregation and transfer/security (D21)

Use current §177.848, inert package cards and unpressurized training controls. The instructor supplies two actual table classifications and their subsidiary hazards from the reference; the learner records the intersection/symbol, meaning and required separation. Repeat with an unidentified material: hold pending classification. Independently verify a mock destination change through trusted dispatch. Demonstrate shutdown on the safe trainer.

Score: recorded actual table lookup; *no guessed compatibility; *safe controls without interlock bypass; trusted security verification; proper passenger-cargo restriction decision. No dangerous cargo or public notifications.

## P22 — HM ERG and incident reports (D22)

Use the current ERG and government reporting extracts. Give a fictional identified material and a ninety-minute public evacuation, with no actual release. Locate its guide and applicable green-table/fire instructions, identify missing spill facts, then prepare a mock NRC call and DOT 5800.1 information worksheet. Unknown fields remain unknown.

Score: *safe withdrawal/no entry; correct ERG navigation; accurate location/facts/callback; immediate twelve-hour outer limit and thirty-day discovery rule; *no actual calls or invented facts. Also assess route, tire, fueling and attendance decisions. This is not a regulatory submission.

## P23 — Multiple-trailer systems (D23)

Use actual approved equipment on a closed range with manuals and a qualified instructor. Inspect, couple and uncouple correctly; verify every lock, line and valve and test rear-unit braking. Perform only authorized low-speed forward turns, not improvised backing. Explain the T/LCV boundary.

Score: *units and dolly secured; *all mechanical locks verified; full brake/air continuity; smooth path/clearance; correct qualification limit. Record every connection. A diagram cannot establish physical multiple-trailer proficiency.

## P24 — Integrated dispatch and handover (D24)

Packet one has a valid license but an unresolved brake defect, unverified bridge, insufficient duty time and mismatched hazardous paper. List all holds, the responsible correction and objective release evidence. Packet two has all gates valid; explain a properly scoped approval without inventing credentials.

Score: *all failed gates found; correction/evidence for each; coherent route/time/load plan; accurate scoped handover; *no release before all failures resolved. This is a fictional decision exercise, not live dispatch.

# Formative practice and explanations

Attempt each question before reading its answer. Correct mistakes by reviewing the named domain and solving a changed case with the instructor.

## D01-L1

A trailer is empty. What weight information still affects licensing?

**Answer:** Its rating and the applicable greater actual/rated combination and towed-unit weights.

**Reason/remediation:** Empty does not remove rated weight; reconstruct D01 classification.

## D01-L2

Does a VNDRLY course completion authorize state CDL issuance?

**Answer:** No; state testing/licensing and applicable registered-provider ELDT remain separate.

**Reason/remediation:** Separate material completion, learner achievement and legal authority.

## D02-L1

What should a fatigued driver do despite remaining legal hours?

**Answer:** Stop safely, notify dispatch and obtain rest or relief.

**Reason/remediation:** Hours are ceilings, not evidence of alertness.

## D02-L2

Why check a new nonprescription medicine before driving?

**Answer:** It can impair alertness; resolve safe use through appropriate medical guidance.

**Reason/remediation:** Legal sale does not establish fitness.

## D03-L1

What do you do with persistent low oil pressure?

**Answer:** Secure and shut down per manual, report and obtain qualified correction.

**Reason/remediation:** Continued running can damage the engine; review warning response.

## D03-L2

Does ABS remove the need for additional following space?

**Answer:** No; traction and stopping limits remain.

**Reason/remediation:** Assistance cannot defeat physics.

## D04-L1

Give the minimum useful elements of a defect report.

**Answer:** Vehicle, component/location, observed condition, time and hold/notification disposition.

**Reason/remediation:** Specific reports support repair and safe release.

## D04-L2

Why inspect enroute after an initially good inspection?

**Answer:** Loads, tires and equipment can change during travel.

**Reason/remediation:** Initial condition is not continuous assurance.

## D05-L1

A combination loses 6 psi/minute on a stabilized applied test; disposition?

**Answer:** Hold and correct; it exceeds the typical 4 psi/minute criterion.

**Reason/remediation:** Check the actual configuration standard and do not average away failure.

## D05-L2

Why select the downgrade gear before descent?

**Answer:** Avoid uncontrolled coasting/missed shifts and preserve braking capacity.

**Reason/remediation:** Review heat and fade.

## D06-L1

Explain offtracking and tail swing.

**Answer:** Rear wheels follow inside the turn while rear overhang can swing outward.

**Reason/remediation:** Check both envelopes, not only tractor front.

## D06-L2

How should an unfamiliar transmission be learned?

**Answer:** Secured orientation and supervised controlled practice using its manual before road use.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not discover controls in a hazard.

## D07-L1

The spotter disappears during backing. Action?

**Answer:** Stop, secure as needed and restore an agreed safe visible position before moving.

**Reason/remediation:** Loss of communication removes the maneuver control.

## D07-L2

Why is a pull-up appropriate?

**Answer:** It corrects alignment safely rather than continuing an uncertain blind path.

**Reason/remediation:** Accuracy never outranks clearance.

## D08-L1

Why is a tug test alone insufficient?

**Answer:** It cannot prove proper jaw/kingpin engagement and absence of a high hook.

**Reason/remediation:** Visual lock verification and system checks are also necessary.

## D08-L2

What ground matters when dropping a loaded trailer?

**Answer:** Stable, sufficiently supporting ground with correct landing-gear support and securement.

**Reason/remediation:** Soft ground can let a supported-looking trailer collapse.

## D09-L1

Calculate the starting good-condition gap for a 60 ft combination above 40 mph.

**Answer:** About 7 seconds; increase for adverse conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Six length seconds plus one speed second is a starting rule, not a guarantee.

## D09-L2

Why is eye contact not permission to proceed?

**Answer:** The other person may not yield or understand; verify the actual path remains clear.

**Reason/remediation:** Communicate and observe rather than assume.

## D10-L1

Why slow before a curve instead of braking hard in it?

**Answer:** Preserve traction and stability and avoid lateral/longitudinal demand combined.

**Reason/remediation:** Load and surface affect safe speed.

## D10-L2

What is the first control response to a blowout?

**Answer:** Maintain firm steering, avoid abrupt braking and slow under control.

**Reason/remediation:** Sudden inputs can turn a tire failure into loss of control.

## D11-L1

When may you enter a rail crossing?

**Answer:** Only after applicable stopping/look/listen requirements and full far-side clearance are satisfied.

**Reason/remediation:** A green roadway signal does not create train clearance.

## D11-L2

What identifies a crossing to the railroad during an obstruction?

**Answer:** The emergency-notification sign information and crossing identifier.

**Reason/remediation:** Give a precise location from safety.

## D12-L1

Property duty begins 06: 00 after qualifying rest. Does an ordinary 2 h meal move the 14 h endpoint?

**Answer:** No; the ordinary window still ends 20: 00.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not apply a split-sleeper exception without its conditions.

## D12-L2

Does 30 minutes on-duty unloading interrupt the 8 h driving accumulation?

**Answer:** Yes if it is 30 consecutive minutes without driving under the ordinary property rule.

**Reason/remediation:** The break need not be off duty, but the time remains on duty.

## D13-L1

Why can a legal gross load still be rejected?

**Answer:** An axle, tire, vehicle rating, securement or route limit can independently fail.

**Reason/remediation:** All applicable constraints must pass.

## D13-L2

Is a tarp automatically a tiedown?

**Answer:** No; establish rated restraint function and applicable securement requirements.

**Reason/remediation:** Covering and restraint serve different purposes.

## D14-L1

What should happen before photographing crash damage in traffic?

**Answer:** Secure/protect people and summon help; do not enter traffic for photos.

**Reason/remediation:** Evidence collection does not outrank life safety.

## D14-L2

Does no visible injury end the post-accident testing inquiry?

**Answer:** No; notify the carrier and apply actual event/citation criteria promptly.

**Reason/remediation:** Some qualifying events do not depend on visible injury.

## D15-L1

Why are wheelchair brakes insufficient for transport?

**Answer:** They do not replace approved mobility-device securement and separate occupant restraint.

**Reason/remediation:** Use actual rated equipment and procedures.

## D15-L2

List key passenger briefing subjects.

**Answer:** Belts, exits, emergency contact, extinguisher and safe aisle/restroom procedures as equipped.

**Reason/remediation:** Brief before motion and confirm comprehension.

## D16-L1

Must every bus breakdown cause immediate evacuation?

**Answer:** No; compare cabin and outside hazards and maintain supervision, changing the decision if danger develops.

**Reason/remediation:** Evacuation is a hazard decision.

## D16-L2

What follows movement to an assembly point?

**Answer:** Account for everyone, report missing persons and prevent reentry.

**Reason/remediation:** Leaving the bus does not complete evacuation management.

## D17-L1

A child drops an item near a bus wheel. What do you do?

**Answer:** Keep the bus secured, prevent unsafe retrieval and positively clear the danger zone.

**Reason/remediation:** A mirror glance alone cannot account for a hidden child.

## D17-L2

What completes the route child check?

**Answer:** A physical walk-through including seats/under seats and required documentation.

**Reason/remediation:** An electronic reminder does not inspect the bus.

## D18-L1

Who may serve as a school-bus backing spotter?

**Answer:** An appropriate trained person under the approved procedure, never a student placed behind the bus.

**Reason/remediation:** Keep children out of vehicle movement hazards.

## D18-L2

Why brief field-trip passengers on evacuation?

**Answer:** They may not know the regular route routines or exits.

**Reason/remediation:** Familiarity cannot be presumed.

## D19-L1

Compute 3,000 gal at 8.5 lb/gal.

**Answer:** 25,500 lb before vehicle/tank and other load weight.

**Reason/remediation:** Keep volume and mass units distinct.

## D19-L2

Do baffles eliminate tank rollover risk?

**Answer:** No; side forces, high center of gravity, surge and driving inputs remain.

**Reason/remediation:** Reduce speed before turns.

## D20-L1

Put a basic shipping description in order.

**Answer:** Identification number, proper shipping name, class/division, packing group when applicable.

**Reason/remediation:** Check additional required entries rather than treating the sequence as the whole paper.

## D20-L2

Can the 1,001 lb Table 2 threshold excuse Table 1 placards?

**Answer:** No; apply the correct table, bulk rules and exceptions to the actual shipment.

**Reason/remediation:** One threshold is not universal.

## D21-L1

What do you do with incompatible or uncertain mixed freight?

**Answer:** Hold loading and resolve the actual segregation requirements with qualified personnel.

**Reason/remediation:** An improvised gap does not establish compliance.

## D21-L2

An unknown caller changes a hazardous-load destination. Response?

**Answer:** Independently verify through trusted dispatch channels before diverting.

**Reason/remediation:** Protect custody and avoid social engineering.

## D22-L1

What is the driver priority at an unknown release?

**Answer:** Withdraw, warn, request emergency help and provide verified shipping information from safety.

**Reason/remediation:** No unqualified leak entry or rescue.

## D22-L2

How do NRC and written report timing differ?

**Answer:** Specified immediate reports as soon as practical no later than 12 h; applicable written report within 30 days of discovery.

**Reason/remediation:** Driver emergency notification is immediate, not delayed to either deadline.

## D23-L1

Why test brakes on every trailer?

**Answer:** Normal tractor pressure does not establish air/control continuity to rear units.

**Reason/remediation:** A disconnected or closed line can leave a trailer without proper braking.

## D23-L2

Does T alone establish LCV qualification?

**Answer:** No; applicable separate Part 380 LCV training and route/configuration authority remain.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not convert an endorsement into every operational qualification.

## D24-L1

Name independent dispatch checks that can stop a trip.

**Answer:** Driver fitness/authority, vehicle, load/passengers, route, duty time and documents.

**Reason/remediation:** One passing check cannot compensate for another failed gate.

## D24-L2

What must a completion record identify?

**Answer:** Actual modules, vehicle/class, theory/practical evidence, hours and remaining legal steps.

**Reason/remediation:** Scope the claim to demonstrated work.
