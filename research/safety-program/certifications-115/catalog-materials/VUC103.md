# VNDRLY/U Offshore Muster Coordinator — Workplace Competence

VUC103 • Original materials •18November 2026

Purpose: coordinate reliable installation-wide people accounting under the emergency plan. This is independent VNDRLY training/assessment, not OPITO approval or incident-command authority. Full proprietary criteria were unavailable. The public 7058 entry paragraph names the checker role; this apparent wording inconsistency is not used to invent coordinator prerequisites. This original workplace route requires local induction, role/system familiarization and safe drill participation.

Planned allocation 32 hours: 10 instruction, 6 labs, 4 guided practice, 10 workplace-drill observation/review, 2 written assessment. Allow additional scheduling time to obtain authentic evidence. Objectives: establish baseline, activate points, reconcile identities, escalate missing people, report accurately, track movement/capacity, coordinate assistance and hand over a traceable picture.

## Q103-01 — Coordinator role and command interface

The muster coordinator combines reports from all muster points and other authorized locations into a single, identity-based account of people. The role is broader than checking one point: it resolves cross-point duplicates, tracks missing people, communicates the current picture to command and preserves accountability during relocation or evacuation. The coordinator does not independently direct rescue tactics or override the incident commander's authority.

Know the installation plan, reporting hierarchy, alternate arrangements and deputy role. Establish who verifies medical, response-team, transport and visitor information. Reports must state source and time. A point checker may confirm local presence, while a medic confirms a person in treatment; the coordinator reconciles those facts without counting one person twice. Report uncertainty explicitly so command can make informed decisions.

Worked example: Point A reports twelve and Point B reports eight against twenty people on board. The totals appear complete, but one identity occurs at both points and one is missing. The coordinator must compare identities rather than announce all accounted. A mathematically tidy sum is not a reliable emergency picture.

## Q103-02 — Baseline, versions and readiness

Establish the authoritative people-on-board baseline and the process for approved arrivals, departures and role changes. Record version, time and source. Prepare point assignments, checker contacts, assistance arrangements, current response-team roster and medical interface. Confirm backup forms and communications before duty. A live dashboard can still display an outdated roster; check its update status and known outages.

Control changes during an incident. A claim that someone left earlier must be supported by the authorized departure record before removing that person from the baseline. Keep unresolved claims visible. Unlisted arrivals are protected and recorded while their status is reconciled; they are not excluded from accountability simply because the baseline is incomplete. Retain an audit trail so a later reviewer can reconstruct the count at each decision time.

Worked example: baseline revision 5 lists twenty-four people, and a late update adds one contractor but lacks authorization. Record the contractor as unlisted/unreconciled and seek the authorized source. Do not silently change the expected count or reject the person's presence. Once the update is verified, issue revision 6 and reconcile all point reports to that baseline.

## Q103-03 — Activating and supervising the muster network

On alarm, activate the reporting arrangements allocated to the coordinator. Confirm which checkers are at their points, which points are usable and what fallback arrangements are active. A missing checker report can reflect failed communications or an unsafe location; it does not prove zero people at that point. Assign an owner to resolve the gap and inform command of its consequence.

Set a concise reporting rhythm appropriate to the emergency plan and event. Request initial reports promptly, then updates for changed identities, movements, hazards and assistance needs. Avoid flooding checkers with duplicate demands from multiple channels. If a point relocates, retain its identity and records through the transfer and establish the receiving contact. Never instruct a checker to remain in a threatened area merely to complete a count.

Worked example: Point East is unavailable. The checker reports moving under the approved plan to West. The coordinator records the alternate arrangement, informs command and requests named transfer/receipt evidence. Treating East as missing and West's arrivals as new people would create both a false deficit and duplicates.

## Q103-04 — Cross-point identity reconciliation

Build the global picture from unique identifiers and current verified locations. Keep local presence, confirmed elsewhere, in-transit and unconfirmed categories distinct. Resolve overlap by asking both sources for identity, time and movement evidence. Do not select the more convenient count without checking. A person can legitimately move between points, but the record must show when and where the current status was confirmed.

Use set-based thinking: the global count is the number of unique confirmed identities, not the sum of report rows. Compare that set with the authorized baseline to identify missing and extra identities. Unlisted people require a separate resolution path. Preserve original reports and append corrections. A total can exceed the baseline because of duplicates, an unlisted arrival, or both; the cause matters.

Worked example: A has P01–P10 and B has P10–P19 against P01–P20. Each point reports ten, yet the union has nineteen unique people. P10 is duplicated and P20 unconfirmed. If Medic verifies P20, the global count becomes twenty, but P10's current location still needs reconciliation for accurate movement planning.

## Q103-05 — Missing-person and discrepancy escalation

A missing-person report should identify the person, last confirmed location/time, likely task if known, source reliability, relevant hazards and actions already taken to verify records. Distinguish unconfirmed because a report is missing from a confirmed absence at a checked location. Both can require urgent attention, but they are different evidence states. Give command the facts promptly; do not wait to perfect every field before reporting a serious gap.

Coordinate verification through safe, authorized channels. Do not order a checker or attendee into a hazard to find a missing colleague. Search and rescue decisions belong to command and trained response roles. When new evidence locates the person, verify the source and update command and affected checkers. Preserve the earlier discrepancy and correction times so a stale missing-person message does not continue to drive action.

Worked example: a coworker says P20 probably went to medical. Record this as an unverified lead and ask the medical interface for confirmation. Do not change P20 to accounted merely because the explanation sounds plausible. Once Medic confirms identity and location at a stated time, update the picture and obtain acknowledgment of the correction.

## Q103-06 — Command reporting and communication resilience

Report baseline version, unique confirmed total, category/location breakdown, unresolved identities, unlisted people, response-team status, assistance needs and point hazards. State the timestamp and limitations. A concise summary should let command understand both the number and the remaining uncertainty. Do not use all accounted while any required identity is unresolved, even when raw totals match.

Obtain readback for critical counts and instructions, correct errors, and log acknowledgment. Use approved fallback when a channel fails; a sent update is not necessarily received. Maintain a single controlled global record while allowing local fallback forms. Reconcile updates after service returns without importing duplicates or replacing actual event times with upload time. Protect sensitive information and use appropriate channels for medical details.

Worked example: command reads twenty-nine when the coordinator reports nineteen confirmed and one unconfirmed. Correct the statement immediately, including the baseline and unresolved identity. A simple yes to end the exchange would mislead the emergency decision maker.

## Q103-07 — Relocation, evacuation and transport accountability

Maintain accountability through every movement stage: selected, departed, received and verified at destination. Use named lists and responsible handover roles. Coordinate with command, checkers and the relevant transport operator, who retains authority over capacity and safe operation. A seat allocation or boarding list is not proof of departure, and departure is not proof of receipt.

Reconcile each movement against the previous location and global baseline. Do not remove a person from all records while in transit. If destination reports fewer unique identities than departed, retain the specific discrepancy and last-known time. Report changes to command before relying on an apparently complete evacuation total. People at medical or in response teams may have separate arrangements that must join the global record.

Worked example: twelve depart A, destination confirms eleven unique plus a duplicate. Eleven are received and one remains unconfirmed in transit. The installation total should not drop by twelve and rise by twelve automatically. Preserve the named gap until authorized evidence resolves it.

## Q103-08 — Assistance, capacity and changing conditions

The coordinator helps command understand practical constraints on moving and accounting for people. Track approved assistance needs, available helpers, medical movement requirements and point capacity according to the local plan. Do not improvise medical treatment or physical handling. A planned helper who is absent is a resource gap needing resolution, not a completed arrangement.

Report threatened muster/refuge locations and unavailable transport capacity promptly. Capacity calculations use confirmed available means, not nominal equipment on a diagram. Never solve a shortfall by overloading. Time estimates and route availability can change; state assumptions and relay changes to the responsible decision maker. The coordinator provides accountability and resource facts while command decides the response.

Worked example: twenty-six people remain, two fictional means each have sixteen approved places, but one is unavailable. Available capacity is sixteen, leaving ten without that option. Report the gap and assistance needs; do not describe thirty-two nominal places as sufficient. Request confirmed alternative arrangements through command.

## Q103-09 — Relief, records and recovery

A coordinator handover transfers the whole accountability picture, not just a final number. Include baseline/version, unique identities by current status, unresolved discrepancies, point conditions, assistance, movements awaiting receipt, unacknowledged messages and next update. The incoming coordinator reads back critical gaps and accepts responsibility. Inform command and checkers of the change.

Preserve original reports, corrections, event times and source references under the site's privacy and retention rules. Do not delete a mistaken all-accounted report; correct it and ensure recipients received the correction. After the drill, compare records with observed movements and identify system weaknesses. Recovery does not erase the need to confirm that all transferred people were received and all response personnel have a known status.

Worked example: the system is restored and its dashboard says twenty-five, while the controlled paper record says twenty-four plus one unresolved. Reconcile identities and source times before handing over. The cleaner electronic display cannot automatically overrule the documented uncertainty.

## Q103-10 — Workplace demonstration and continuing practice

The assessor must observe coordination across multiple reporting sources in approved workplace drills. Evidence includes readiness, activation, identity reconciliation, discrepancy escalation, communication, movement tracking, assistance coordination and relief. Classroom exercises teach the method but do not prove competence using the actual installation arrangements. No genuine emergency is required or created.

Use changed drills to demonstrate that the learner can cope with a lost point report, duplicate identity, baseline change and movement discrepancy. Record coaching separately from independent performance. A failed critical report requires remediation and changed reassessment. Continuing drills and review after roster-system, role or plan changes maintain competence; a certificate alone does not keep the emergency picture accurate.

Worked example: a candidate reconciles one muster point perfectly but has never coordinated a cross-point duplicate or transfer. The coordinator evidence is incomplete. Arrange approved multi-point exercises rather than awarding the broader role on the strength of checker experience alone.

# VUC103 multi-point workplace workbook

## Delivery and evidence setting

Use the installation's approved emergency-drill arrangements, current people-on-board system, point checker contacts, communications, fallback forms, movement records and command interface. Duty holder approves safe timing and drill routes. A competent coordinator-role assessor directly observes; controller and role players cover two or more points, medical, response-team contact, command, transport and relief. No live emergency, unsafe search, blocked escape route or unauthorized alarm is created. Hazards and unavailable equipment are simulated by cards; actual participant movement follows approved safe drill routes. Stop the exercise for a real emergency.

Classroom resources: synthetic identity cards, named rosters, shared board, paper backup, isolated reader/dashboard mock-up, training radio/voice channel, clocks and forms below. Keep real personal data out of practice datasets. Explain stop word, fitness demands, breaks, accessibility, privacy and real-emergency override. Do not punish a learner with unnecessary distress or require physical handling beyond approved arrangements.

Workplace result requires two approved multi-point drill cycles W1/W2 using actual local systems and role interfaces. Simulation-only work earns only a clearly limited theory/simulation record. No authentic workplace observation is claimed by this materials package. The two-cycle rule is VNDRLY's original evidence requirement, not a claim about inaccessible external criteria.

## Fictional network Cedar

Point North and Point South report to Coordinator, who reports to Command. Medic verifies medical locations; Response verifies assigned team identities and status. Only authorized Records can amend baseline arrivals/departures. Primary channel E; fallback B requires two-way test and readback. Checkers verify local identities; Coordinator reconciles globally; Command decides search, relocation and evacuation. Transport operator retains capacity and movement safety authority. No one enters a hazard or operates real transport in this exercise.

A report includes point/time/roster revision, named local identities, duplicates, unlisted people, unresolved identities, hazards and assistance needs. Global board categories are local point, medical/response location confirmed, in transit and unconfirmed. A person appears once in the global identity count even when multiple source reports exist. Preserve original reports and correction history.

## Scenario A — overlapping points and baseline change

Baseline revision 1: P01–P20. At 09:00 North reports P01–P10. South reports P10–P19. Raw rows 20; unique identities 19; P10 overlaps; P20 unconfirmed. Coordinator must identify both problems and report 19 confirmed of 20, with location overlap and missing identity. At minute 3 a coworker says P20 probably at medical; this is only a lead. At minute 5 Medic verifies P20 at medical at 09:05. Global identity count becomes 20, but P10 location still needs reconciliation. At minute 6 South corrects that P10 left South and was physically verified at North at 09:02; current location North is now confirmed with source/time.

At minute 8 X1 arrives at South unlisted. Record safe presence as unlisted/unreconciled and seek Records authority. At minute 10 Records supplies authorized revision 2 adding X1; baseline 21, North 10(P01–P10), South 10(P11–P19, X1), Medical 1(P20), total 21. At minute 12 Command reads 31 instead of 21. Correct/readback required. At minute 14 E fails before a follow-up acknowledgment; test B and repeat current picture. At minute 16 relief arrives while an assistance request for X1 has not been confirmed. Transfer that gap explicitly.

Controller does not supply the correct unique count unless debriefing. A false all-accounted declaration while P20 is unconfirmed is critical even if row totals match. Preserve action and report timing. No real missing person is created; identities are role-play cards in a safe room.

## Changed remediation C

Baseline C01–C18. A reports C01–C09, B reports C09–C17:18 rows but 17 unique, C09 overlap, C18 unconfirmed. Later Medic verifies C18 and B corrects C09 current location to A. Authorized Records adds X2 and verifies C18 departed from the installation after medical transfer: baseline remains 18 but identities change. ## Workplace W1/W2 plan

W1 observes actual baseline preparation, network activation, at least two point reports, cross-point identity reconciliation, command report and relief. W2 changes at least three conditions and covers communication/system fallback, authorized baseline change, missing/elsewhere verification, named transfer/receipt, assistance/capacity reporting and restored-record reconciliation. Dangerous conditions are inject cards, never created physically. If actual site arrangements cannot demonstrate a required function safely, arrange another approved workplace drill and keep the evidence gap open.

Retain local ERP/roster versions, approval, observation times, privacy-safe identity trace, checker and command acknowledgments, original/corrected reports, movement records and debrief. Witness information supports direct assessor observation; it does not replace observation of critical global-accounting decisions. No retrospective invented logs, signatures or event times are permitted.

## Observed station dimensions

### P103-01 — Baseline readiness

Task: Establish approved baseline, contacts, point assignments and fallback records.

Required evidence: Versions and authority clear; unverified changes separated. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

### P103-02 — Network activation

Task: Collect readiness reports, resolve a missing point and coordinate an alternate.

Required evidence: Unknown is not zero; safe relocation and receiving contact confirmed. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

### P103-03 — Global reconciliation

Task: Merge named lists and resolve cross-point overlap.

Required evidence: Unique totals and missing IDs correct; current location limits retained. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

### P103-04 — Missing-person escalation

Task: Prepare command report from uncertain lead and later verification.

Required evidence: Facts/source/time distinguished; no unsafe search instruction. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

### P103-05 — Communications

Task: Report global picture, correct wrong readback and use fallback.

Required evidence: Acknowledgment of current report recorded; no false all accounted. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

### P103-06 — Movement tracking

Task: Reconcile named departures, destination receipt and medical diversion.

Required evidence: Transit discrepancy preserved until verified; no duplicate movement count. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

### P103-07 — Assistance and capacity

Task: Identify actual available capacity and missing assistance resource.

Required evidence: Shortfall accurately reported; no overload or improvised handling. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

### P103-08 — Relief and records

Required evidence: Outstanding items/readbacks preserved; authentic audit trail. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

Required evidence: All role functions directly observed with traceable independent evidence. Record behavior/time, source and assessor rationale.

## Scoring and decision

Ten dimensions 0–4: 0 unsafe/absent, 1 major help, 2 safe but incomplete/prompted, 3 independent accurate traceable, 4 independent with timely anticipation. Require 32/40, each at least 3, no critical error, all functions observed across two approved workplace drills. Critical global-accounting and movement decisions must pass independently in both where applicable. False all-accounted statements, concealed missing identity, unsafe search/route instruction, overload, fabricated evidence or unverified baseline deletion are critical. Stop unsafe simulated branches, record, remediate and reassess changed conditions; do not average them away.

Complete 20 formative checks with corrected reasoning and 5 labs. Final: 10 cases, 30 decisions, 60 points, pass 48 and at least 4/6 each, no critical error, 90 minutes. Changed final and keys are restricted instructor files. Obtain independent changed evidence after remediation; preserve unsuccessful records. An independent competent reviewer addresses appeals against documented criteria.

Award only VNDRLY/U Offshore Muster Coordinator — Workplace Assessed after authentic workplace and knowledge evidence passes. Record identity/site, scope, package hash, drill dates, assessor and renewal/review date. Set two-year original VNDRLY renewal with earlier review after material changes/performance concerns; continue local drills. Theory/simulation-only records must not say workplace assessed. No OPITO registration, incident-command appointment or actual learner completion is asserted by this authored package.

## Q103-01-L1

May the coordinator independently direct a hazardous search?

**Answer:** No. Provide facts to command and trained response roles.

**Reason/remediation:** Accounting responsibility does not grant tactical rescue authority. Revisit Q103-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q103-01-L2

Do point totals alone prove all accounted?

**Answer:** No. Reconcile unique identities across sources.

**Reason/remediation:** One duplicate can conceal one missing person. Revisit Q103-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q103-02-L1

An unsigned update adds a contractor. Silently change baseline?

**Answer:** No. Record unlisted presence and obtain authorized baseline confirmation.

**Reason/remediation:** Presence and baseline authority are distinct. Revisit Q103-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q103-02-L2

A dashboard is live but its roster is old. Which matters?

**Answer:** The authoritative roster version and update status.

**Reason/remediation:** A working screen can show stale data. Revisit Q103-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q103-03-L1

No report from a point: zero attendees?

**Answer:** No. Treat the point status as unknown and resolve the reporting gap.

**Reason/remediation:** Communication failure is not a count. Revisit Q103-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q103-03-L2

A threatened point relocates. What must be retained?

**Answer:** Identity records, authorized destination, receiving contact and transfer/receipt evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Silent relocation creates false deficits and duplicates. Revisit Q103-03 and perform its changed application.
