# VNDRLY/U Maritime Access — TWIC Readiness

Program VUC030 · Original course materials · Revision 2026-11-17

## Purpose and boundaries

This course prepares maritime workers and gate personnel to apply for, safeguard and correctly use a Transportation Worker Identification Credential, recognize security concerns and work within authorized access arrangements. A TWIC is issued by TSA following its assessment. Completing this course cannot issue, renew or replace that card, clear an applicant, create a right to enter a facility, qualify a facility security officer or replace a merchant mariner credential. The course certificate must say **VNDRLY Maritime Access Readiness**, never “TSA TWIC certified.”

The intended learner is an adult worker, contractor, supervisor or access-control trainee. No existing TWIC is required for classroom work. Reading assistance and interpretation are permitted; identity documents and actual criminal-history information are not collected in class. Use fictional records. Actual operational access requires the employer's role authorization, applicable security plan and credential checks. Security personnel need additional assigned-duty training. Workers must learn their facility's reporting route before deployment.

By completion, the learner can distinguish credential eligibility from access authorization; prepare an accurate enrollment plan; manage renewal and loss; choose an authorized inspection or escort route; recognize observable security concerns; make a factual incident report; respond to MARSEC changes; and protect sensitive information. Ten chapters connect those decisions to realistic cases. Instructor and assessment materials follow in the separate guide.

## D01 — The credential, the workplace and the boundary

A maritime facility can contain public, secure and restricted spaces. Their boundaries come from the applicable approved security arrangements, not from a worker's opinion about whether an area looks dangerous. A public reception area may be outside the secure area. A restricted control room can require a further permission beyond entering the secure yard. Some facilities designate their entire facility as restricted. Read signs and obtain the approved briefing; do not infer permission from an open door.

TWIC establishes that TSA issued a credential after its security threat assessment. It does not establish that its presenter is the cardholder, that the card remains valid, that the person has a job at this location or that the requested room is authorized. These are separate decisions. An access-control process therefore checks identity, credential validity, business purpose, local authorization and any screening or escort conditions. A positive card response is one input, not the whole decision.

For facilities and vessels subject to the maritime security requirements, people needing unescorted access to secure areas generally need TWIC unless an applicable exception applies. Not every person on every waterfront property automatically falls into that rule. The owner/operator determines covered spaces and applies the security plan. The Facility Security Officer, or FSO, administers facility security responsibilities; the Coast Guard Captain of the Port, or COTP, has the relevant local regulatory role. TSA handles credential assessment and issuance. The training provider handles only this course.

**Worked case:** A pump technician has an unexpired TWIC but no work order and asks to enter a restricted pump-control room. The gate worker keeps the technician in the designated waiting area, checks the sponsor and authorized work location, and follows the security plan. The technician is not admitted solely because the card exists. If approved only for the loading dock, the technician cannot self-extend permission to the control room.

**Application:** Sketch fictional public reception, secure yard and restricted control-room boundaries. For each, write who authorizes entry, what identity process applies and whether escorting is available. Explain why a course completion certificate is not an access credential. A common mistake is treating contractor status as blanket permission; correct it by stating the exact destination and job before choosing an access route.

## D02 — Enrollment without identity or privacy mistakes

Use the official TSA enrollment service to find current application instructions, fees, appointments and acceptable documents. Do not follow an unsolicited payment link or give a supervisor your personal account password. Enrollment is a personal official application, not a classroom exercise to submit with invented data. The class rehearses the process using a fictional applicant and stops before any submission.

Prepare a private checklist covering legal name and prior names, the required address history, birth and citizenship/immigration information, identity evidence, occupational purpose and current contact details. The official document list controls which originals or acceptable certified records and combinations establish identity and status. A workplace badge alone does not establish every required element. If names differ between records, obtain the appropriate legal linking evidence; do not alter a document or choose whichever spelling is convenient. Ask the enrollment provider to resolve uncertain document combinations before travelling.

Review the application before certification. A mistaken date, an omitted prior name or an address copied from memory can cause delay and may undermine the assessment. Some fields are voluntary under the governing application rule; the official notice explains consequences of omission. Do not let a trainer turn an optional field into a local requirement. Information given to the government must be truthful and complete where required. Questions about eligibility belong with TSA and, where appropriate, qualified legal assistance, not a public class discussion.

An initial enrollment normally includes identity review, fingerprints and photograph through the authorized provider. An online pre-enrollment entry is not a completed assessment or an issued card. A person with a qualifying HME or FAST credential may be offered a comparability option; it still involves the TWIC process, and the reduced-fee card can have an expiration linked to the other credential. Compare the actual current terms rather than assuming a full new validity period. A mariner should accurately identify the occupational purpose and check the Coast Guard's separate credential requirements.

**Worked case:** Jordan's identity evidence uses a former surname, while the appointment uses the current legal surname. Jordan checks the official list, brings the required name-change linkage and reviews the enrollment record before signing. Jordan does not upload those records into the company training chat. The instructor marks the preparation method, not the applicant's private eligibility.

**Application:** Prepare a fictional appointment plan with a document-check step, contact method, transportation allowance and a decision point for unresolved identity evidence. Explain the difference between application receipt and approval. Never promise a start date based solely on an appointment.

## D03 — Assessment decisions, redress and continuing responsibilities

TSA applies the applicable criminal-history, immigration, security and other eligibility standards. A trainer cannot determine the result from a rumor about one offense or another worker's experience. Different grounds have different effects, and some can support a waiver while others cannot. The accurate answer to an uncertain personal case is to use TSA's process and the actual notice, with qualified assistance if needed.

If an adverse notice arrives, read its stated basis, instructions, deadline and address. An appeal challenges an error or the applicability of the determination; a waiver asks TSA to consider relief where the rules allow it despite an otherwise disqualifying condition. They are not interchangeable. Preserve a copy of the notice and evidence, establish the applicable response date immediately and keep proof of timely submission. Do not assume that asking a supervisor for help files a response, that all cases allow a waiver or that an appeal automatically restores access. Immediate revocation procedures can apply.

The worker has ongoing responsibilities after issuance. The card remains TSA property. Under 49 CFR 1572.19(d), the specified continuing-responsibility events require surrender within 24 hours. These include the listed disqualifying criminal-process events, specified mental-capacity determinations, loss or renunciation of the stated citizenship/permanent-resident status, and specified immigration violations or removal orders. Read the exact rule and relevant definitions rather than assuming every arrest or medical visit has that effect. If unsure whether an event fits, promptly obtain official guidance rather than deciding that an unexpired date overrides it. The course must never encourage hiding an event to preserve employment. Employers handle access decisions through authorized channels without broadcasting private allegations.

Plan renewal before expiration and check whether the official online renewal route is available for the individual. Follow the current service process for identity or contact changes. Receipt of a new card can invalidate the old one; follow return/disposition instructions rather than lending or retaining it as a spare access card. Protect the card from damage and keep its PIN private. A photograph of a credential is not its electronic credential and cannot reproduce its authorized inspection process.

**Worked case:** A worker receives a notice based on a court record they believe belongs to someone else. The worker identifies the response deadline, obtains appropriate record-correction evidence and submits through the stated redress route. The supervisor schedules work consistent with actual access status. Neither claims that course completion overrules TSA's decision.

**Application:** Using a fictional notice with a supplied deadline, build a response checklist: basis, desired correction or permitted waiver, evidence source, assistance, submission method, deadline and receipt. The exercise tests process comprehension, not legal advice or disclosure of a learner's history.

## D04 — Presenting and inspecting a credential

At the access point, present the actual credential as instructed and cooperate with the authorized inspection. The process may involve visual inspection, electronic inspection or an approved physical access control system, called PACS, depending on the facility's regulatory requirements and plan. Workers do not choose a weaker route because a queue is long. Inspectors use the assigned procedure and equipment, not an improvised phone photograph or an unapproved app.

Visual inspection includes matching the presenter's face to the photograph, checking that the expiration has not passed and examining the credential for apparent tampering or forgery. It is not enough to glimpse the card color. When electronic inspection is required, authentication checks the credential's genuineness, validity checks expiration and cancellation status, and identity verification matches the person using the prescribed biometric process or authorized fallback. These are three separate questions. A reader's inability to complete one is not proof that all three passed. A poor-quality or absent biometric template is different from evidence that the presenter is the wrong person: do not use a quality-related fallback to excuse an actual identity mismatch.

The Canceled Card List, or CCL, includes cards invalidated for more than one reason, including loss or damage. A canceled response therefore is not a public accusation of criminal conduct. It is an access-control problem requiring the approved response. Record the operational result and escalate privately. Do not repeatedly swipe another person's card, guess a PIN or manually override an electronic failure without a specifically authorized procedure.

For required electronic checks, the CCL must meet the applicable freshness rules: at MARSEC Level 1, data no more than seven days old; Levels 2 and 3, no more than one day old. A rise in MARSEC level requires the specified update within 12 hours. Use the newest available list and follow tighter local requirements. This is an operator/system responsibility; a worker's visible card expiration does not cure stale reader data. An approved PACS can use linked credential information with the required initial verification, subsequent identity and validity controls and records. Do not assume any ordinary employee badge qualifies as that system.

**Worked case:** An unexpired credential returns canceled at a required electronic checkpoint. The worker says it was recently reported lost and then found. The inspector does not announce a criminal allegation or accept the printed date as an override. The worker is held outside the secure boundary while the authorized special-circumstance or escort process is assessed. A found canceled card is not automatically restored.

**Application:** Explain authentication, validity and identity using three separate fictional failure cards. State what evidence would be required before access could proceed. Practise speaking respectfully: “This check has not cleared; please wait here while I contact the authorized security lead.”

## D05 — Escorting, visitors and authorized limits

Escorting is active control of a person's authorized presence in a secure area. It is not simply issuing a visitor sticker or saying that someone will meet them later. The plan determines approved routes, escort responsibilities, any monitoring arrangements and how contact is maintained. In restricted areas, the rule generally requires a TWIC holder as a side-by-side companion for a person without TWIC, subject to the specified regulatory exceptions. Never extend a less restrictive secure-area monitoring arrangement into a restricted area without authority.

The operator decides whether an escort is available. A worker cannot demand one as a right. Verify the visitor's identity under the applicable personal-identification requirements, business purpose, destination, responsible sponsor and escort authorization. Under the applicable 101.515 identification standard, the credential must be tamper resistant and contain the full name, a current facial photograph and issuing authority, from the specified government, employer, union or trade-association source. An unverified handwritten name slip is not enough. Communicate prohibited actions and what to do if separated. The escort must remain able to observe whether the visitor is doing something outside the authorized activity. Being busy with a radio or another assignment does not remove that obligation.

During movement, keep the visitor on the approved route and out of areas not covered by the authorization. If the visitor needs a different location, stop at a safe authorized point and obtain the change. Handover to another escort requires positive acknowledgment and continuity of control. At departure, account for the visitor and close the visit record as required. If separation occurs, report immediately and follow the security response; do not silently reconstruct an imaginary continuous escort in the log.

Do not use a TWIC as a substitute for personal protective equipment, induction, a work permit or specialized competence. A welder with a card still needs the actual hot-work controls. A driver with an approved delivery still may not enter a vessel without the vessel's arrangements. Federal officials, law enforcement and emergency responders have specific regulatory provisions; ordinary workers should refer these situations promptly to trained security staff and avoid obstructing legitimate emergency response.

**Worked case:** A visitor without TWIC is escorted to a warehouse and requests a shortcut through a restricted controls building. The escort declines the shortcut and contacts the sponsor if a route change is necessary. If the escort is called away, the visitor waits in the authorized location with continuity of escort maintained; the visitor is not left wandering while a replacement is sought.

**Application:** Conduct a fictional escort from reception to a secure workshop, with a surprise restricted-door request and an escort handover. Record where the visitor is at each handover and who accepts responsibility. Identify the moment at which the original authorization would no longer cover the requested activity.

## D06 — Lost cards, failures and special circumstances

Report a lost, stolen or damaged TWIC to TSA immediately and initiate the official replacement process. Inform the employer's authorized access contact so the next shift is planned correctly. Do not borrow another card, use an image as a substitute or keep using a card after receiving instructions to surrender it. Protect any report confirmation, but do not place sensitive application records in an open gate log.

33 CFR 101.550 provides specific discretionary routes for special circumstances. For the relevant lost, stolen, damaged or expired-card route, the person must previously have had unescorted access and be known to have held a TWIC; provide evidence of reporting the loss/damage/theft to TSA or applying to renew an expired card; provide the required alternative personal identification; and have no suspicious circumstances associated with the request. The owner/operator may permit access for no more than 30 consecutive calendar days under the provision. Meeting one condition is not enough. This is not a universal grace period and does not oblige a facility to grant access.

Forgetting a card at home for another reason does not automatically satisfy that provision. An approved escort may be necessary if the facility offers it. A reported-lost card that appears on the CCL needs the exact applicable procedure; do not confuse that limited provision with permission to disregard all cancellations. Poor biometric performance also has specified handling: where the rule permits, visual identity verification plus correct PIN can support the prescribed fallback. Inability to obtain a biometric match is not permission to share PINs or use another person's biometric.

Reader outage, communications failure and emergency conditions are different events. Follow the approved contingency procedure and security lead's direction. Document the malfunction and access decision; never manufacture an electronic pass. The rules include emergency-response provisions, but a production deadline or delayed truck is not by itself an emergency. New-hire provisions, if applicable, require their own authorization and conditions; a gate worker cannot create new-hire status with a handwritten badge, and those provisions are not a way to assign unqualified security duties.

**Worked case:** Sam reports a stolen card, supplies reporting confirmation and acceptable identity evidence, and has verified prior unescorted access. An authorized lead evaluates all conditions and local policy; the gate worker does not promise admission. On day 31, yesterday's discretionary permission cannot simply be extended under the 30-day rule. Replacement, escort or another lawful route must be resolved before access.

**Application:** Sort four fictional cases: forgotten card, reported theft, expired card with renewal evidence, and canceled card with unexplained identity mismatch. For each, identify missing evidence, decision authority, waiting location and a possible authorized route without guaranteeing entry.

## D07 — Security awareness based on observable behavior

Security awareness means noticing actions and conditions that could compromise the maritime operation. Examples include someone attempting to follow a cleared person through a controlled door, requesting another person's badge, photographing prohibited controls after being told the rule, trying multiple unauthorized entry points or leaving a suspicious item near critical infrastructure. Describe the action, location and time. Nationality, race, religion, language or clothing associated with a community is not a substitute for observable security facts.

Use respectful, authorized challenge procedures from a safe position. Do not pursue a person into danger, search belongings without the assigned authority or attempt to dismantle a suspicious device. If there is an immediate threat, move to safety, raise the alarm and contact emergency/security responders through the site procedure. Keep other people clear without handling the object. A worker trained in awareness does not become a bomb technician, armed responder or investigator.

Do not help someone circumvent a control as a favor. Common social-pressure requests include “the boss knows me,” “just hold the door” or “use your badge because mine is in the truck.” Verify through known official contacts rather than a telephone number supplied solely by the visitor. A convincing uniform or familiar name cannot replace the required checks. If a legitimate task conflicts with the control, the security lead resolves the access arrangement.

Cyber and physical access interact. A message asking for a PIN, a screenshot of a reader error or a full credential scan can be a phishing attempt. Use approved reporting tools; do not plug a found storage device into a gate computer. Do not publish reader locations, security-plan pages or access logs to public assistants or personal messaging groups. This course introduces safe behavior; it does not replace separately required assigned-role cybersecurity training.

**Worked case:** A delivery driver says an urgent repair requires holding the secure door for an unbadged colleague. The worker keeps the door controlled, asks the colleague to use reception and verifies the job through the known sponsor. The incident report says what was requested and what happened, not that the driver's accent proved a threat.

**Application:** Rewrite a biased or speculative report as an objective observation. Include time, exact location, actions, direction of movement, vehicle details actually observed and whom you informed. Label uncertainty rather than filling gaps with guesses.

## D08 — MARSEC changes, emergency action and reporting

MARSEC Levels 1, 2 and 3 correspond to increasing security conditions and protective measures under the applicable plan. Level 1 is the baseline; Level 2 adds measures for heightened risk; Level 3 involves further measures for a probable or imminent security incident. The worker follows the notified measures and supervisor's instructions rather than inventing restrictions. A change can affect entry points, screening, escorts, work activity and communications. Confirm the instruction, its effective time and your specific duties.

A security breach means measures have been circumvented, eluded or violated even if no transportation security incident has resulted. The absence of damage does not erase a breach. Report immediately through the assigned channel. Emergency life safety comes first: move to the designated safe area or muster point, follow evacuation instructions and account for yourself. Do not stop to collect a forgotten badge from a threatened area or independently re-enter to search for a coworker.

Under 33 CFR 101.305, covered owners/operators must report suspicious activity that may result in a transportation security incident, and breaches of security, without delay to the National Response Center. Its toll-free number is 1-800-424-8802. For an actual transportation security incident, the specified reporting route includes the local COTP for covered vessels/facilities or the cognizant District Commander for covered OCS facilities, followed by plan actions. The frontline worker must know the internal immediate reporting route and whether their role is the designated external caller. Internal escalation must not become an excuse for delaying required reports. Any person may report relevant suspicious activity to the NRC. For immediate life-threatening emergency assistance, use the site emergency route/911 as appropriate.

Give the caller's name and contact details, location, observed incident or activity, and available information about involved persons. Do not postpone a report until every fact is known. Separate observed facts from reports received from others, note time and preserve relevant records through authorized means. Do not make public allegations or upload sensitive scene photographs to a general group.

**Worked case:** A controlled gate is found propped open and an unknown person has entered. No theft is visible. The worker reports the breach immediately, maintains a safe position and follows the security response. The designated lead makes required external notifications without waiting for proof of loss. The worker records when the condition was discovered and the actions taken, not an invented time when the gate was opened.

**Application:** Deliver a 45-second report of that case, then answer clarifying questions. Practise saying “unknown” for an unknown fact and supplying an update when new information is verified.

## D09 — Records, sensitive information and handover

Useful records establish what happened, when, who made a decision and which authorized procedure applied. They do not need gratuitous personal details. Record a credential/access result in the approved system, the authorized destination, escort if applicable and exceptions or unresolved issues. Do not copy full identity records or PINs into a generic shift note. Protect access logs and security information according to their classification and the applicable plan; some electronic TWIC/PACS records are sensitive security information under 49 CFR Part 1520.

33 CFR 105.225 sets facility recordkeeping requirements. Relevant categories include training, drills/exercises, incidents and breaches, MARSEC changes, equipment maintenance and required electronic access records. Retention rules differ by category: many records have a two-year requirement, while declarations of security have their own specified retention. The course does not authorize deleting records at the end of a shift or moving them into an uncontrolled spreadsheet. Use the organization's retention schedule consistent with the applicable rule and any investigation hold.

A handover must identify current MARSEC instructions, out-of-service equipment, approved temporary arrangements with expiry limits, unresolved visitors or escorts, incidents needing follow-up and the responsible contact. Transmit only what the receiving role needs. A new operator must not assume a handwritten “OK” carries indefinitely into their shift. Read back critical conditions and confirm the actual authorization when unclear.

Errors should be corrected transparently using the approved audit trail. Preserve the original event where required, identify the correction and its reason, and never backdate a check that did not occur. If you discover a previous shift admitted someone without a required step, report it as a concern and preserve evidence. Making the record look complete after the fact does not repair the access failure.

**Worked case:** A replacement-card exception is near its end date and the reader has a logged maintenance fault. The outgoing worker identifies both conditions, their authorizing lead, applicable expiration and instructions for the next shift. The incoming worker acknowledges them and verifies any unclear continuation before admission. Neither shares the worker's unrelated personal application history.

**Application:** Produce a five-line handover with one routine visitor, one active escort, one expiring temporary arrangement, one equipment fault and one incident reference. Remove information that belongs only in a protected case file.

## D10 — Integrated maritime access decisions

The repeatable decision sequence is: identify the requested activity and boundary; establish identity; apply the required credential process; confirm local purpose and authorization; establish screening/escort or a specifically permitted exception; communicate conditions; document the result; and maintain control until departure or handover. At any unresolved safety or security condition, hold at the safe authorized location and escalate. Do not use a later step to excuse a failed earlier one.

Consider a morning shift at a fictional terminal. MARSEC has risen to Level 2. A returning contractor reports a stolen card, a first-time driver presents a valid TWIC but no sponsor, and a visiting technician requires access to a restricted room. The contractor needs the special-circumstance conditions evaluated by authorized staff; the driver needs a verified business purpose; the technician needs the required local permission and any escort arrangement. None can be admitted by applying one blanket rule to the entire queue. The operator also needs reader/CCL readiness consistent with the increased level.

Now a medical emergency occurs inside the terminal. The worker promptly follows the emergency access and notification procedure rather than imposing the routine contractor queue on responders. Another worker accounts for the waiting visitors and maintains the unaffected boundary. Afterward, the record distinguishes emergency responders, ordinary visitors and the equipment status. It does not retrospectively label everyone an emergency entrant.

Performance means making these decisions calmly under pressure, explaining the reason without arguing, and staying within role authority. A wrong but confident clearance is worse than a safe escalation. A thoughtful escalation should include the missing fact and the decision needed, not merely “something is wrong.” After resolution, tell the next responsible person the conditions and close the loop.

**Capstone exercise:** Apply that morning-shift scenario using the supplied fictional plan and records. Submit an access decision for each person, a MARSEC readiness check, a short emergency communication and a handover. Explain the boundary between your recommendation and the security officer's authorization. Correct any missed condition with a changed case before final assessment.

## Learner field reminder

My card is not blanket access. My course certificate is not a TWIC. I use the authorized inspection and escort process. I report loss immediately. I do not lend credentials or share PINs. I describe behavior, not stereotypes. I report breaches promptly, protect records and ask the authorized lead when a condition is unresolved.

# VUC030 instructor delivery and application packet

## Delivery plan and instructor preparation

Eight instructional hours, excluding breaks: D01–D03 teaching/cases 90 minutes; D04–D06 teaching/cases 90; D07–D10 teaching/cases 75; ten application stations 120; final examination 60; remediation planning and close 45. This is a VNDRLY plan, not a TSA-mandated course duration. Extend individual practice until demonstrated proficiency; attendance alone does not earn completion. Use small groups with individual responses, and stagger stations so each learner performs the critical decisions rather than merely watching a group leader.

The instructor should understand maritime security access requirements, the difference between worker awareness and designated security duties, and the current official rules cited in the source crosswalk. Before delivery, check cited sections, TSA enrollment instructions and the scenario plan. Obtain a suitably qualified maritime security reviewer for local operational adaptation. Do not expose a real facility security plan in open classroom materials. The fictional plan below is enough to evaluate readiness, but actual employers must supply authorized site induction and role-specific instruction.

Resources: coursebook, current official reference extracts, fictional cards plainly marked TRAINING ONLY, printed scenario plan, area map, clock, mock reader result cards, blank logs, role cards, examiner packet and a quiet examination space. Do not collect live fingerprints, actual PINs, SSNs, immigration records or criminal histories. No live gate penetration, suspicious-object placement or emergency calls are authorized by these exercises. All calls are simulated. Use ordinary index cards, not reproductions intended to resemble usable government credentials.

Before class, explain privacy and respectful challenge rules. Ask learners to distinguish public, secure and restricted areas using a sketch. Teach through a normal case, then alter one condition and ask what changes. After each learning check, have the learner explain why the tempting unsafe shortcut fails. For language or reading support, preserve the decision difficulty and record accommodations. No learner should be forced to disclose a personal eligibility issue.

## Fictional terminal plan used in all stations

Harbor Delta Terminal is entirely fictional. Reception R is public. Yard Y and workshop W are secure. Control room C is restricted. Normal entry requires identity, the assigned TWIC process, a confirmed sponsor and destination authorization. The training security lead is FSO Morgan; internal extension 200 is simulated. Reception is the safe waiting point. Escort changes require direct acknowledgment. Visitors without TWIC entering C require an authorized TWIC-holding side-by-side escort under this scenario. No other restricted-area exception is supplied. Authorized escorts are Lee and Patel; reception staff cannot self-authorize escorting.

The scenario uses required electronic inspection for unescorted secure entry. Mock reader data must be current under the applicable MARSEC rule. No learner may override a failure. Morgan evaluates special circumstances and determines whether local policy permits them. The exercise provides no new-hire authorization. The emergency scenario directs the gate worker to contact the simulated emergency lead, follow the emergency responder procedure and keep routine visitors at R. The muster point is M outside the exercise's threatened area. External calls are described verbally only. This plan does not authorize or describe any real site's security measures.

## Assessment method and critical gates

Each station has five criteria scored 0 absent/unsafe, 1 safe but incomplete or prompted, 2 independent and complete. Pass each station with at least 8/10 and all starred criteria at 2. Any critical unsafe action stops the station; explain the issue, teach the relevant chapter, then reassess with a changed case. Preserve the first result and the reassessment separately. Never average an unsafe access decision into a pass.

Critical gates: no fabricated credential or entry authorization; no credential/PIN sharing; no unauthorized bypass of failed inspection; no abandonment of required escort; no handling of suspicious devices or dangerous pursuit; prompt safety/security escalation; no real personal-data exposure in training; no claim of TSA issuance from course completion. These gates apply to final written responses as well as station behavior. A safe response may use different wording if its decision and rationale are correct.

Ask them to identify the current authorized destination, boundaries and missing permission. Criteria: identifies R/Y/W/C correctly; distinguishes credential from purpose; *does not grant C access; names the authorized decision-maker; communicates the safe waiting/verification step. Change-case retest: a vendor is approved for Y but asks to board a vessel not included in the scenario authorization. Require vessel-specific permission rather than assuming Y covers it.

## P02 — Prepare enrollment privately

Provide fictional records with a legal name change and a checklist containing an optional field. Ask the learner to prepare an appointment plan without submitting anything. Criteria: uses the official document list; identifies legal name-link evidence; distinguishes pre-enrollment from issuance; *does not submit invented data or expose personal records; identifies how to resolve uncertainty through the authorized service. Retest: the document is expired and the name matches; learner must check acceptable alternatives rather than merely repeating the name-change solution.

## P03 — Respond to an assessment notice

Provide a fictional adverse notice dated May 2, with an explicit response deadline of July 1, indicating possible mistaken identity. No real legal determination is involved. Ask for a response plan. Criteria: identifies the actual July 1 deadline; separates an error appeal from a potentially permitted waiver; identifies appropriate evidence/assistance; *does not promise restored access or approval; preserves submission/receipt records privately. Retest: a notice identifies an accurate record and states a waiver route is available; learner must recognize the changed purpose instead of claiming mistaken identity.

## P04 — Inspect the inspection result

Supply three mock results: authentic card with expired validity; valid card with failed identity match; passing card checks with no confirmed sponsor. Ask whether each permits entry. Criteria: distinguishes authenticity/validity/identity; detects the failed element in each; *withholds unauthorized clearance; uses the known escalation contact; records an objective result without accusations. Retest: all card checks pass but destination is unauthorized. A card pass still cannot create access authority.

## P05 — Escort and hand over

Learner role-plays authorized escort Lee taking a visitor from R to W. The visitor requests C; then Lee is asked to leave and Patel arrives. Criteria: confirms visitor purpose/route; *does not grant the unauthorized C detour; maintains required observation; *obtains positive handover without leaving the visitor uncontrolled; closes the movement record. Retest: Patel does not acknowledge accepting responsibility; Lee must maintain control and escalate rather than treating physical presence as acceptance.

## P06 — Special circumstances

Supply a reported-stolen-card confirmation, acceptable alternative ID and verified prior unescorted access, but no operator decision. Ask learner to recommend next steps and explain day 31. Criteria: checks all relevant conditions; identifies operator discretion; *does not self-authorize access; recognizes the 30-consecutive-calendar-day limit; distinguishes forgetting from qualifying loss/renewal circumstances. Retest: worker only forgot the card, with a photo on a phone. Require the approved escort/other lawful route, not the loss exception.

## P07 — Recognize and report safely

Role-player requests use of the learner's badge to follow a truck through the gate. A separate card describes an unattended suspicious item. Criteria: describes actions without profiling; *refuses credential sharing; keeps a safe position; *does not touch/investigate the item or pursue dangerously; promptly reports relevant observations. Retest: an apparently familiar supervisor makes the bypass request. Familiarity does not remove the control.

## P08 — MARSEC and emergency communication

Level rises from 1 to 2 at 08:00. Mock CCL data is three days old. At 08:10 a simulated medical-response vehicle arrives. Criteria: identifies the higher-level data requirement and update deadline; notifies the responsible lead; *does not claim stale data is compliant simply because the physical card is current; *follows the supplied emergency procedure without imposing the ordinary visitor queue; gives a concise factual communication. Retest: level remains 1 but CCL data is eight days old; it still requires correction, not reliance on the absence of escalation.

## P09 — Records and handover

Provide a draft note including a PIN, irrelevant medical history, an expiring approved arrangement and an unresolved reader fault. Ask the learner to produce a corrected handover. Criteria: *removes PIN/private extraneous data from the general note; retains the relevant authorization and expiry; records fault and responsible contact; preserves an auditable correction rather than falsifying the original; obtains receiving-worker acknowledgment. Retest: an access check was omitted on the previous shift. Require reporting and transparent correction, not backdating a pass.

## P10 — Integrated shift

Use D10's three visitors and emergency, with instructor Morgan responding only when the learner states the missing facts and decision needed. Criteria: *keeps each person's authority separate; identifies the contractor's evidence and local decision needs; resolves sponsor and restricted-route questions; *maintains emergency/safety and boundary controls; produces a factual end-of-shift record. Retest: make the contractor's alternative ID insufficient and the technician's escort unavailable. The learner must hold/escalate the unresolved cases rather than repeating the original approvals.

## Final examination and completion rule

The controlled instructor packet contains a 30-item final, answer key and ten changed reassessment cases. It is separate from the public coursebook. Allow 60 minutes, official reference extracts and a plain calculator if needed; do not provide formative answers or the examiner key during the final. Each item earns 2 for a correct safe decision and reason, 1 for a safe but incomplete answer, 0 for wrong/absent/unsafe. Pass at least 48/60 and at least 4/6 in each of ten domains, with no unresolved critical-gate failure. The final does not substitute for the ten observed stations.

Before retest, document the misconception, teach the specific content and check a new formative example. Use the changed three-part domain case in the protected packet for the failed domain; apply the same 6-point/domain threshold and critical gates. Repeated failure requires further instruction, not coaching through the same answers. The instructor signs only observed assessment evidence.

Award the VNDRLY readiness completion only when all chapters/checks are completed with corrections, all stations pass and the final passes. Record learner identity through the organization's approved method, course revision, dates, instructor, scores, attempts, accommodations, practical evidence and certificate identifier. Certificate wording: “Completed VNDRLY/U Maritime Access — TWIC Readiness training and its classroom application assessments.” Add: “Not a TSA-issued TWIC, access authorization, FSO qualification or merchant mariner credential.” No external endorsement is implied. Revisit training when duties, rules or site arrangements change or performance shows a gap; this course creates no regulatory card-expiry extension. Store protected assessment keys separately from learner materials with controlled access. Do not issue any real completion records merely because this authoring package exists.

# Formative checks and corrective explanations

Attempt before reading the answer. Explain each corrected decision to the instructor and use a changed example before continuing. Domain IDs refer to coursebook chapters.

## D01-L1

Does a TWIC alone authorize entry to any secure room?

**Answer:** No. Identity, current credential status, business purpose and local destination permission must also be satisfied.

**Explanation/remediation:** Separate eligibility from actual access authority; revisit the pump-technician case.

## D01-L2

Who issues the TWIC and who administers facility security?

**Answer:** TSA issues the credential; the owner/operator and designated FSO implement facility security under the applicable requirements.

**Explanation/remediation:** The course provider cannot issue the government card or grant facility access.

## D02-L1

An applicant has different legal surnames on two records. What should they do before enrollment?

**Answer:** Check the official acceptable-document requirements and obtain appropriate legal name-link evidence.

**Explanation/remediation:** Do not alter records or guess which name to use; practise the appointment checklist.

## D02-L2

Does completing online pre-enrollment mean a card is approved?

**Answer:** No. The remaining enrollment and TSA assessment steps still apply.

**Explanation/remediation:** An appointment or receipt is not an issuance decision.

## D03-L1

How does an appeal differ from a waiver?

**Answer:** An appeal challenges an error or applicability; a waiver requests permitted relief despite a disqualifying condition.

**Explanation/remediation:** Use the actual notice and official process; not every ground permits a waiver.

## D03-L2

Can an unexpired printed date override a new TSA revocation?

**Answer:** No. Follow actual credential status, applicable reporting/surrender duties and authorized access instructions.

**Explanation/remediation:** Expiration is only one validity condition.

## D04-L1

Name the three separate electronic inspection questions.

**Answer:** Is the credential authentic, is it valid, and is the presenter the correct holder?

**Explanation/remediation:** Authentication, validity and identity are separate; one pass does not prove the others.

## D04-L2

Why should a canceled-reader result not be announced as proof of criminal conduct?

**Answer:** Cancellation can result from loss, theft, damage or other reasons; handle it as an access result and escalate privately.

**Explanation/remediation:** Avoid unsupported allegations while maintaining the boundary.

## D05-L1

Can an escort leave a visitor while finding a replacement?

**Answer:** Not while abandoning the required control. Maintain continuity and obtain a positive authorized handover.

**Explanation/remediation:** A sticker or a nearby employee does not create an accepted escort assignment.

## D05-L2

May a visitor take a restricted-area shortcut because the secure-yard destination is approved?

**Answer:** No. Restricted-area authorization and applicable escort conditions must be met.

**Explanation/remediation:** Use the authorized route and escalate a requested change.

## D06-L1

What must a holder do upon discovering a lost TWIC?

**Answer:** Notify TSA immediately, follow replacement instructions and inform the authorized workplace access contact.

**Explanation/remediation:** Do not borrow a card or treat a phone photo as an electronic credential.

## D06-L2

Is the special-circumstance period a guaranteed 30-day grace period for everyone?

**Answer:** No. It is conditional operator discretion for the specified circumstances and cannot exceed 30 consecutive calendar days.

**Explanation/remediation:** Check prior access, known TWIC possession, report/renewal evidence, alternative ID and suspicious circumstances.

## D07-L1

What belongs in an objective security observation?

**Answer:** Observed actions, time, location, relevant descriptions, uncertainty and notification, without stereotypes or invented motives.

**Explanation/remediation:** Replace labels with evidence of conduct.

## D07-L2

What should an awareness-trained worker do with a suspicious item?

**Answer:** Keep safely clear, warn or protect others as instructed and notify authorized responders; do not handle or investigate it.

**Explanation/remediation:** Awareness training does not qualify a device specialist.

## D08-L1

Does an access-control breach require visible damage before reporting?

**Answer:** No. Circumvention or violation of security measures can be a breach without resulting damage.

**Explanation/remediation:** Prompt reporting allows the responsible lead to act before harm develops.

## D08-L2

What changes when MARSEC rises?

**Answer:** Apply the notified plan measures and duties, including applicable increased inspection-data requirements; confirm timing and responsibility.

**Explanation/remediation:** Do not invent new restrictions or ignore a change because the queue is busy.

## D09-L1

Should a general shift note include a card PIN?

**Answer:** No. Use approved protected records and include only information needed for the receiving role.

**Explanation/remediation:** PINs and unnecessary personal histories do not belong in an open handover.

## D09-L2

How should an omitted check be corrected in the record?

**Answer:** Report it and make an authorized transparent correction preserving the audit trail; do not backdate a check that never occurred.

**Explanation/remediation:** A completed-looking record cannot repair a missing inspection.

## D10-L1

What is the first step in the integrated access decision?

**Answer:** Identify the requested activity and boundary, then apply identity, credential and local authorization requirements.

**Explanation/remediation:** Starting with the destination prevents a valid card being treated as blanket access.

## D10-L2

What should a useful escalation say?

**Answer:** State the missing fact or failed condition, current safe status and the specific decision needed from the authorized lead.

**Explanation/remediation:** Clear escalation resolves the condition without self-authorizing an exception.

## Explicit limits and adaptation

The source catalog names TWIC, but TWIC is not an instructional certification that VNDRLY can issue. The complete VNDRLY counterpart teaches the preparation, use and security-awareness decisions associated with that credential. Only TSA can make its assessment/issuance decision, and only the authorized operator can grant local access. This distinction appears in the learner introduction, cases, practical critical gates, final and certificate wording.

The package does not purport to qualify an FSO, security screener, armed responder, mariner, cybersecurity specialist or immigration/legal adviser. Those roles require their own qualifications and assigned training. It does not replace a facility's mandatory drills/exercises or cybersecurity program. New-hire, recurring-access and emergency exceptions are not blanket self-service permissions; actual applicable provisions and authorized plans must be used.

Every domain has two original formative questions with explanations, three distinct final questions and one changed three-part retest. All ten domains have an observed application station. Source review covers the decisions actually taught; it does not claim every clause in every cited part is taught as a separate occupational qualification. Operational customization adds the actual reporting contacts, authorized routes, inspection systems, escort arrangements and protected record procedures without inserting sensitive real plan details into this public package.
