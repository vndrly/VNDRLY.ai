# VNDRLY/U Source Inspection — Rotating Equipment

VUC057 · Original available-source instruction · 2026-11-18

The package retains Q01–12 from the fixed-equipment source-inspection course as the common supplier, material, fabrication and examination foundation, with their source register. It adds Q13–28 below. The VUC057 workbook, assessments and award rules govern the combined course; no fixed-equipment specialist award is inferred. Learners need industrial safety, technical reading, units and arithmetic; diagnose and teach gaps before assessment.

The public API rotating-equipment outline/effectivity provides subject context through July 2027. Full listed standards were not reviewed. All R57 limits and numerical tests are fictional. This course teaches inspection evidence and benign measurements, not machine design, live operation, balancing, electrical testing or specialist NDT authorization. [R01–02]

## Q13 — Centrifugal pump configuration

A pump record must identify the actual casing, rotating assembly, impeller configuration, materials and ordered duty. Compare nameplate, data sheet, drawing and test report rather than treating the model name as complete identity. A different impeller or speed can change performance. Trace changes after testing into the final record and determine the required approved follow-up.

Review the prescribed material, dimensional, assembly and test evidence. A valid casing test does not establish pump hydraulic performance, and a satisfactory performance point does not prove every material requirement. Keep these outcomes separate. Do not use classroom efficiency arithmetic to approve a different pump design or alter an impeller.

KSB distinguishes pump hydraulic output from input and identifies the operating point as part of an efficiency statement. [R03] Record the power measurement boundary; motor electrical input and pump shaft input are not interchangeable. An efficiency without that boundary can mislead the reviewer.

**Worked case:** report P1 uses impeller A, while final assembly has B. Preserve the discrepancy and obtain approved applicability/retest disposition. **Changed case:** the configuration is reconciled, but the report's input power is electrical and the guarantee is shaft-based. Resolve the measurement boundary before comparing efficiencies.

## Q14 — Positive-displacement pumps

For positive-displacement equipment, identify the actual pumping mechanism, displacement basis, speed, materials, seals and required protective arrangements from approved documents. Do not assume a centrifugal pump curve or operating method applies. Source inspection verifies the required configuration and test evidence; it does not authorize operating a pump against a blocked path or experimenting with pressure protection.

A simplified displacement-times-speed calculation can explain theoretical flow, but actual delivered flow depends on the machine and conditions. Keep theoretical and measured values separate. If a report claims efficiency or slip, confirm definitions and units. Do not treat a classroom percentage as a universal acceptance threshold.

**Worked case:** supplied displacement 0.5 L/revolution at 600 revolutions/minute gives theoretical flow 300 L/min. Measured 270 gives a ratio 90% and difference 30 L/min under those stated conditions. **Changed case:** at 500 revolutions/minute theoretical flow is 250 L/min; measured 230 gives 92%. Neither result approves the actual order without its specified criteria, test conditions and protection evidence.

## Q15 — Compressor types and performance boundaries

Identify whether the package is reciprocating, rotary screw, centrifugal or axial and map its stages, driver and auxiliaries. Inspection records must match the actual machine and prescribed test. A procedure for one type cannot automatically establish another's acceptance. Review materials, internal assembly evidence, clearances and required tests through the approved plan rather than inventing universal tolerances.

Gas performance data require a clear basis. Gauge and absolute pressures differ; inlet temperature, gas composition, reference conditions and flow definition affect comparisons. A normal-volume value cannot be compared directly with actual inlet volume without the approved conversion. The source inspector checks the supplied basis and escalates inconsistencies; this course does not perform compressor design or surge-control tuning.

**Worked case:** synthetic inlet 1 bar gauge and discharge 7 bar gauge, with supplied atmospheric pressure 1 bar, give absolute pressures 2 and 8 bar and ratio 4. Using 7/1=7 would be wrong. **Changed case:** inlet 2 bar gauge, discharge 8, atmosphere 1 gives 3 and 9 absolute, ratio 3. A correct ratio alone does not establish capacity, efficiency or safe operation.

## Q16 — Turbines and drivers

Map the driver to its ordered equipment train, rotation, speed range, coupling and auxiliaries. A turbine's casing, rotor, controls and protective functions require their own evidence. An electric motor also has identification, electrical and mechanical requirements that need appropriately qualified review. Do not infer driver suitability solely from a matching power number.

Review test scope carefully. A no-load demonstration may not prove the ordered loaded performance, and a simulation of a trip input may not prove the complete installed shutdown path. Identify what was actually tested and what remains. No energized motor test, steam admission, overspeed demonstration or turbine operation is performed in this course.

**Worked case:** specified rotation is viewed from the driven end, but the report does not state viewing direction. The word clockwise is insufficient; resolve the reference. **Changed case:** direction is clarified, but driver serial differs from the train's test record. Keep identity unresolved even though the direction comparison now makes sense.

## Q17 — Gears and train relationships

Review gear unit identity, ratio, rotation references, materials, assembly records and required testing. The nominal ratio describes a speed relationship, not complete mechanical acceptance. Bearings, lubrication, contact/clearance evidence and actual operating conditions remain important. Do not prescribe backlash or tooth-contact acceptance from a generic classroom example.

For a supplied simple ratio, show the calculation and its assumptions. Multi-stage or special arrangements need their actual configuration. A changed gear set can affect the train and test applicability. Preserve evidence of the final configuration rather than reusing a report for an earlier ratio without authorization.

**Worked case:** supplied reduction ratio 3: 1 with 1800 rpm input gives 600 rpm output. **Changed case:** actual configuration is 2.5: 1, giving 720 rpm at that input. The mismatch requires technical disposition; do not simply relabel the output record or authorize running at a different speed to hide it.

## Q18 — Bearings, shafts and fits

Inspection of bearing and shaft records begins with the correct parts, dimensions and approved assembly method. A bearing designation, clearance or fit must correspond to the actual location and operating basis. Handling damage or contamination remains a condition issue even when identity is correct. Do not approve heating, pressing or machining from this course; actual manufacturer methods and qualified personnel govern.

Review dimensions with consistent units and named features. A housing bore minus an outside diameter is only meaningful if the specific fit and measurement conditions are defined. Shaft runout, alignment and bearing clearance are different measurements. Avoid calling one a substitute for another. Record instrument/reference, temperature where relevant and measurement location.

**Worked case:** fictional permitted measured clearance 0.10–0.20 mm; actual 0.22 is 0.02 above maximum. **Changed case:** corrected measurement 0.18 meets the supplied band, but the bearing lot identity is missing. The numerical pass does not cure traceability. Practical measurements use inert models, not actual interference-fit assembly.

## Q19 — Seals and support systems

Match the seal arrangement, materials and support-system configuration to the approved drawing and data sheet. A seal body alone does not establish that its required supply, return, cooling, monitoring or protective functions are present. Different arrangements require different conditions; do not infer a universal support pressure or piping configuration from a familiar installation.

Trace lines and instruments on the controlled drawing and safe model. Verify actual tag, direction and connection evidence where required. A swapped connection can make otherwise correct components ineffective. Review the required leak/function evidence and final preservation. A seal-related test should identify the conditions and acceptance basis; “no leak” without scope is incomplete.

**Worked case:** required support line S2 is absent from the assembly record, although the seal serial matches. Hold support completeness unresolved. **Changed case:** S2 is documented but its connection is mapped to the wrong port. Presence is not correct configuration; obtain authorized correction and verification.

## Q20 — Lubrication and auxiliaries

Map reservoir, filters, pumps, coolers, lines, instruments and required standby functions to the approved auxiliary-system plan. Verify lubricant identity and the prescribed cleanliness, flushing and test evidence. A clean-looking sample does not prove a specified cleanliness result. A flushing record must refer to the actual circuit and configuration, including any changes afterward.

Review records for condition, units, sampling location and applicable acceptance basis. Do not invent an oil cleanliness class, mixing permission or operating pressure. Replacing a filter does not automatically validate prior contaminated operation. Source inspection identifies evidence gaps and required dispositions; it does not commission the live system.

**Worked case:** twelve required circuit records exist, but two are duplicates, leaving ten unique circuits supported: 83.3333%. **Changed case:** records for the two missing circuits arrive, then one circuit is opened for modification. Determine the required post-change evidence; a previously valid record does not automatically cover the changed configuration.

## Q21 — Baseplate, soft foot and alignment

Inspect the supplied baseplate, mounting and alignment evidence against the actual approved procedure. SKF describes soft foot as improper support contact and provides equipment-specific measurement methods. [R04] A good shaft-alignment reading does not by itself establish satisfactory support, and support correction can alter alignment. Keep the sequence and remeasurement requirements tied to the actual method.

Training uses a stable, unpowered bench model with safe supports, gauges and no motor. Follow its approved setup instructions. Record reference positions, units, as-found and as-corrected values. Never loosen a live machine foot or reach into rotating equipment to obtain a reading. Actual thermal-growth targets and coupling tolerances must come from the machine's approved basis.

**Worked case:** fictional model offset maximum 0.10 mm; measured 0.14 exceeds by 0.04. **Changed case:** corrected offset 0.08 passes, but a support check remains unperformed. Alignment is not overall mounting acceptance. A separate angular reading must retain its own units and criterion rather than being compared directly with offset.

## Q22 — Balance and vibration evidence

Balance records and operating vibration records address related but different questions. A balance certificate should identify the actual rotor/configuration and applicable basis; an overall vibration value should identify machine state, measurement location/direction, units and processing. A single number cannot diagnose every cause or prove all required testing.

Fluke distinguishes peak from RMS and displacement, velocity and acceleration. [R05] Do not compare unlike quantities directly. For an ideal sine wave only, peak divided by √2 gives RMS; that is not a universal conversion for arbitrary machinery vibration. Spectrum, phase or diagnostic conclusions require appropriate specialist competence and data.

**Worked case:** supplied ideal sine velocity peak 4.2426407 mm/s corresponds to 3.0 mm/s RMS. A fictional RMS maximum 2.8 is exceeded by 0.2. **Changed case:** a report states 3.0 mm/s without identifying peak/RMS or bandwidth. Do not apply the conversion or acceptance rule until the measurement definition is established. No rotor balancing or live vibration collection is performed in this course.

## Q23 — Performance-test calculations and conditions

A performance report needs the ordered duty, actual configuration, method, instruments and test conditions. Compare like-for-like values under the approved acceptance basis. Correct arithmetic cannot rescue wrong density, speed, units or measurement boundaries. Preserve raw results and the approved corrections instead of presenting only a favorable adjusted value.

For an incompressible classroom pump example, hydraulic power is ρgQH. Use kg/m³, m/s², m³/s and metres to obtain watts. Efficiency relative to shaft power is hydraulic output divided by shaft input. KSB's definitions support these boundaries. [R03] This is calculation practice, not a pump acceptance standard or authorization to alter test conditions.

**Worked case:** ρ=1000, g=9.81, Q=0.02 and H=30 give 5886 W. Shaft input 8000 W gives 73.575%. **Changed case:** supplied density 850 with the same other values gives 5003.1 W and 62.53875%. If the actual test used another density, reconcile its approved basis; do not choose whichever calculation passes a guarantee.

## Q24 — Mechanical running tests

Review the approved running-test plan, duration, conditions, monitoring, safety arrangements and acceptance criteria before a required witness. The test team controls operation and emergency response. The inspector must not bypass guards, trip functions or exclusion zones to obtain evidence. No live running occurs in this course; use synthetic time-series and event records.

Distinguish elapsed time from qualifying operation. If the supplied requirement demands uninterrupted operation under specified conditions, downtime or out-of-condition periods cannot simply be added to claim the hold. Where interruptions are permitted, apply the actual approved rule. Record stops, alarms, adjustments and post-test inspection requirements rather than smoothing them out of the report.

**Worked case:** fictional requirement is 120 continuous qualifying minutes. Record shows 70 minutes, stop, then 60. Total 130 is not 120 continuous. **Changed case:** authorized new run shows 125 continuous minutes, but one required sensor channel is unverified. Duration passes; monitoring completeness remains open. Do not invent missing readings from neighboring channels.

## Q25 — Controls, electrical evidence and protective functions

Map each required alarm, trip, permissive and control function from input to required output using the approved cause-and-effect record. A software indication alone may not establish the final actuation path. Identify what a simulation actually exercises and which portions remain untested. A disabled protection needs its authorized status and restoration evidence; never treat a bypass as a successful test.

Electrical test records must identify equipment, method, conditions, instruments and qualified personnel under the actual requirements. This course reviews records and a passive model only; it does not perform insulation, high-voltage, energized or live functional testing. Do not substitute guessed thresholds for approved electrical criteria.

**Worked case:** eight required cause-and-effect checks, six valid, one duplicate, one bypassed. Supported coverage is 75%, two gaps. **Changed case:** the missing unique test is validly completed and the bypass remains. Seven of eight, 87.5%, still does not establish all functions. Identify the actual remaining protection and owner.

## Q26 — Surface condition and shipment preservation

Review the ordered surface preparation/coating and preservation requirements for the actual train and auxiliaries. A visually uniform finish does not establish all specified preparation or thickness. Preserve inspection access until required checks are complete. Safe training coupons may be measured; actual blasting, chemical treatment or solvent use is outside the practical scope.

Shipment protection can include openings, machined surfaces, moisture control and handling restraints as specified. Verify the actual condition and instructions rather than assuming the supplier's standard packaging meets the order. Temporary transport restraints must be identified for the receiver; they must not be mistaken for operating components. No live shaft rotation or preservation charging is performed by the learner.

**Worked case:** ten required protection points, nine verified, one open port: 90% coverage and one open issue. **Changed case:** port covered correctly, but required receiving/removal instruction missing for a restraint. Physical protection improves while the handover gap remains. Shipment readiness must address both.

## Q27 — Defects, rework and affected test scope

Record nonconformances against exact requirements and equipment IDs. Determine which disposition authority is needed and what work/test evidence may be affected by a change. An impeller swap, bearing replacement, opened lubrication circuit or revised control function can have different follow-up requirements. The inspector does not decide every change is harmless or demand every test again without the actual basis.

Preserve original results and approved rework records. A corrective action addressing recurrence does not automatically accept earlier equipment. Verify the required conditions before closure, including any repeated test or examination. If a correction creates another discrepancy, track it rather than closing the original finding as proof the package is complete.

**Worked case:** twelve findings, eight verified closed, four open. Closure 66.6667%. **Changed case:** two verified items reopen after configuration changes and one open item closes: seven verified, five open, 58.3333%. Report actual affected tests and identities, not just a declining percentage.

## Q28 — Dossier and restricted release

Reconcile the final equipment train against its ordered configuration and unique supporting records. Include component identities, assembly and alignment, required examinations, tests, controls, preservation, deviations and manuals as applicable. A test of a previous configuration requires an approved applicability decision before it can support the shipped train. Duplicate reports do not increase coverage.

State the actual release type and authority. Shop inspection or shipment permission is not field commissioning, operational acceptance or specialist qualification. Carry unresolved conditions, receiving checks and temporary preservation items to the responsible owner. Give a handover that another inspector can follow without interpreting a vague “all satisfactory.”

**Worked case:** twenty-four required dossier items, twenty-one valid and three unresolved: 87.5% evidence completeness. **Changed case:** two valid missing records arrive, yielding 23/24=95.8333%; the last gap remains. Any conditional release needs the actual authorized decision, restrictions and owner. Do not round the dossier to complete.

# Common source-inspection foundation Q01–12

Retained S references; VUC057 delivery/award rules govern.

## Q01 — Source inspection and accountability

Source inspection checks evidence while equipment is being made, when defects or missing information can still be addressed before shipment. The inspector works to an assigned scope rather than taking over the manufacturer's quality system. Identify the purchaser, supplier, sub-supplier and technical decision makers. Clarify who can approve a deviation, release a hold point, authorize shipment and accept equipment at destination. These may be different people.

An inspection visit does not prove every manufacturing activity was observed. State the time, items and records examined. If an unobserved stage is important, determine what evidence the approved plan requires; do not sign a retrospective witness statement. A supplier's responsibility for making conforming equipment does not disappear because a purchaser's inspector attended. Likewise, the inspector must not conceal a known defect by calling the visit a courtesy.

Use a three-part statement: requirement, evidence and conclusion within scope. For example, “Order F56 requires identified heat records for all six plates; four are linked; two remain unresolved.” That is more useful than “material looks good.” Keep technical acceptance separate from commercial schedule discussion.

**Worked case:** supplier asks for an unconditional release after a short visit to one of three fabrication bays. Record the actual bay/items inspected and remaining scope; do not issue a broader conclusion. **Changed case:** records for the other bays arrive. Review their applicability and completeness under the plan, labeling record review accurately rather than claiming personal observation.

## Q02 — Purchase requirements and document hierarchy

Read the purchase order, specification, drawings, data sheets and approved inspection plan as a connected package. Record revisions and any stated order of precedence. A later email does not automatically supersede a controlled drawing. If requirements conflict, raise a technical query to the authorized owner before the affected activity. Preserve the original conflict and the disposition so later inspectors can understand the basis used.

Create a requirement register with the equipment tag, clause or drawing reference, evidence needed, stage and responsible reviewer. Distinguish required from optional documents, but do not downgrade a requirement because it is difficult to obtain. Where a code is named, establish the contractual edition and applicable scope; this course does not supply every code provision. A public examination reference list is not a project specification.

Check units and terminology early. “Test pressure” and “design pressure” are different fields, and a value without units can be ambiguous. Do not repair an ambiguous order by assuming customary units. Resolve it and record the approved interpretation. Changes may affect already completed work as well as future activity.

**Worked case:** drawing revision D specifies nozzle orientation 90°, while the shop traveler still uses revision C at 0°. Hold the affected configuration for controlled resolution. **Changed case:** the revised traveler is issued after fabrication. Future documentation is corrected, but the existing nozzle still needs dimensional verification and authorized disposition.

## Q03 — Inspection plans and notification

Translate the requirements into stages that can actually be inspected. Material identity may need checking before cutting; weld preparation before welding; internal cleanliness before closure. Define hold, witness, review and surveillance points according to the contract's definitions. Record notification timing and the authority required to waive or release a point. Silence is not automatically a waiver unless the governing agreement explicitly makes it so.

Discuss the plan at a pre-inspection meeting with relevant parties. Confirm scope, schedule, document status, sub-supplier controls, test readiness and communication routes. Write actions and owners. The meeting should expose missing prerequisites, not merely produce attendance signatures. A schedule change may require revised notification so the inspector can attend an irreversible stage.

Maintain a stage register rather than relying on memory. If a required hold was bypassed, preserve the actual condition and seek an authorized recovery or disposition. A photograph can be useful evidence, but it may not replace a specific required observation. Do not manufacture a release date after the fact.

**Worked case:** ten internal-inspection holds are required; eight have evidence and two units were closed early. Eight of ten are evidenced, with two missed holds. **Changed case:** supplier offers a final external photograph for each closed unit. The photographs do not establish the internal condition; obtain the designated disposition instead of changing the inspection plan retrospectively.

## Q04 — Supplier quality controls and sub-suppliers

Assess whether the controls needed for this order are actually being used. Look for current work instructions, identification, calibrated equipment, personnel qualifications, inspection records and control of nonconforming items. A quality certificate or polished manual is not proof that a specific traveler was followed. Sample actual jobs and preserve the limits of the sample.

Sub-supplier activities can carry important requirements, such as heat treatment, material testing or coating. Determine how the main supplier transfers requirements and verifies returned evidence. A subcontracted test report should identify the correct item, method, date and acceptance basis. The main order's traceability must survive the handoff. Do not assume a sub-supplier's generic certificate covers all delivered items.

Use findings to separate isolated record errors from potential common-cause issues. If several records use the same invalid tool, the affected population may be larger than the sample. Escalate through the approved quality process; the inspector must not invent a statistical sampling plan or infer lot acceptance from a convenient small sample.

**Worked case:** three of twelve reviewed travelers lack the required tool identity. The sample defect rate is 25%; this does not prove exactly 25% of all travelers are defective. **Changed case:** the same unidentified tool appears across another production lot. Identify the potentially affected scope and obtain an authorized review rather than limit the finding to the first twelve records.

## Q05 — Material receipt and traceability

Match physical items to their specified material and supporting records before fabrication obscures the identity. Check heat/lot marks, product form, dimensions and the required certificate fields. An authentic material report may still belong to the wrong plate. Record how identity transfers when an item is cut, machined or split into multiple parts. Unmarked remnants need controlled disposition, not guessed identity.

Separate material identity, mechanical properties, chemical composition and physical condition. One test may support one of these without proving all the others. Corrosion or damage found at receipt remains a condition issue even if the certificate is correct. If the order requires additional testing, confirm the sample traceability and authorized laboratory/method rather than accepting a result detached from the delivered item.

Use a receipt map tied to the bill of material. For each piece, record tag/heat, certificate, dimension check, visual condition and open issue. Do not multiply a certificate's coverage simply by attaching it to several unmatched pieces. Where markings cannot be read safely, record the limitation and arrange approved access or verification.

**Worked case:** six plates are required; four physical heat marks match reports, one is unreadable and one report belongs to another heat. Four of six have supported identity, 66.6667%; two are unresolved. **Changed case:** the unreadable mark is recovered but that plate has edge damage. Identity coverage improves; physical condition remains a separate decision.

## Q06 — Alloy verification and method limits

A material-verification plan defines which items, elements, locations and methods need checking, and how results are evaluated. The inspector verifies that the plan and records correspond to the actual equipment. A displayed alloy name is not a universal guarantee of all specified properties. Surface condition, contamination, geometry and the instrument's capability can affect what is established.

Handheld XRF cannot establish carbon content, so it cannot by itself resolve a grade distinction that depends on carbon. The manufacturer's public guidance identifies light-element limitations. [S03] Select the actual approved method through qualified personnel rather than inventing a workaround. Chemical identity also does not prove heat treatment, strength, toughness or weld quality.

For this course, use supplied synthetic reports and reference labels; do not operate X-ray or spark equipment. Review instrument identity, reference checks, item/location mapping, units, results and acceptance basis. An unexpected result requires segregation or the applicable hold and technical review. Never relabel a component to make it match a favored reading.

**Worked case:** an order requires a carbon-dependent distinction, but the packet contains only XRF grade matches. State the unverified property and request suitable evidence under the approved plan. **Changed case:** appropriate chemistry evidence arrives, while required heat-treatment records are missing. Close the chemistry issue only; the treatment requirement remains.

## Q07 — Manufacturing routes and inspection timing

Different manufacturing routes create different evidence needs. Casting, forging, rolling, forming, machining and welding do not leave the same features or records. Identify the route for each component and place inspection where relevant characteristics can be checked. A final dimensional check cannot establish every earlier treatment or hidden condition.

For a casting, examine the specified surface/volumetric examination and repair records rather than assuming a smooth exterior proves internal soundness. For a forging or formed plate, correlate the required material condition and treatment with the actual process record. For machining, verify critical dimensions and preservation of identification. This course supplies no universal acceptance limits for these processes; the actual specification controls.

Changes in process can affect qualification and inspection requirements. Ask whether the revised route has authorized approval and whether prior work is affected. Record the change before continuing the affected assessment. The inspector is not a process designer and should not prescribe heat-treatment cycles or repair methods from memory.

**Worked case:** an item specified as forged arrives with a casting route record. Hold the material/process discrepancy for authorized review. **Changed case:** a substitution is approved, but the approval requires additional examination not yet documented. The substitution decision alone does not complete its conditions.

## Q08 — Welding procedures and personnel evidence

Review the welding instruction, its supporting qualification basis and the welder/operator evidence as different records. TWI distinguishes procedure qualification from personnel qualification. [S04] A qualified person still needs the applicable approved production procedure; an approved procedure does not prove every person is qualified to use it. Match process, material, joint, thickness and other applicable variables through the governing requirements, without inventing qualification ranges.

Check actual production records against the approved instruction. Consumable identity/storage, preparation, preheat/interpass controls, sequence and any specified treatment should have appropriate evidence. If a required parameter was not recorded, do not fill it from the target value. A target is not proof of what happened. Raise the gap and preserve the affected weld IDs.

A weld map connects location, welder, procedure, examination and any repair. Keep it current when a weld is repaired or an item is renumbered. A successful examination does not automatically cure a qualification deficiency. The responsible technical authority determines disposition under the actual code and contract.

**Worked case:** WPS revision 4 is approved, but production used revision 2. Identify the changed variables and affected welds for authorized assessment; do not declare all welds good or bad without that basis. **Changed case:** the revisions are technically reconciled, but one welder's applicable qualification evidence is missing. That separate issue remains.

## Q09 — Fabrication fit-up and visual records

Before joining, inspect the specified preparation, alignment and identification using suitable tools and the approved drawing. Record actual gap, offset or dimensions at defined locations. After joining, record visible condition and any required dimensions under the applicable criteria. A visual observation must not be presented as volumetric examination or a complete fitness-for-service conclusion.

Use a repeatable reference: equipment tag, weld number, clock position or drawing datum. Photograph relevant observations when permitted, with scale and orientation where useful. Avoid ambiguous descriptions such as “small defect near end.” Describe the measurable feature and limitation. Do not classify a discontinuity as acceptable without the governing criterion and a valid method.

On inert training samples, practice gauge placement and reading without grinding, welding or entering operating equipment. If a measurement tool cannot access the feature, record that limitation and seek an approved method. Do not force the tool, alter the sample or choose only favorable locations. Retain repeat readings and their reason.

**Worked case:** a fictional maximum offset is 1.0 mm; valid readings are 0.8, 1.1 and 0.9. The 1.1 reading exceeds by 0.1, so averaging does not make the feature conforming. **Changed case:** corrected readings are all 0.9, but required surface examination remains pending. The dimensional correction does not close that examination.

## Q10 — NDT personnel and procedure review

Verify the examination method, written procedure, technique, personnel authorization and equipment evidence required by the order. A training certificate is not automatically an employer's method-specific qualification. ASNT's current public advisory emphasizes the employer's written practice and warns against independent training certificates presented as employer-based certification. [S05] Record the actual scope and validity of the evidence, not just a person's highest title.

The source inspector reviews and witnesses within their competence. Performing or interpreting specialized NDT requires the appropriate qualification and procedure. Do not sign as the examiner because the examiner is unavailable. If a report lacks an acceptance basis, coverage or item identity, request correction supported by actual records; do not supply guessed values.

Examination coverage matters. Ten reports may concern eight unique welds, and a partial scan may not cover the required volume. Reconcile unique locations and the prescribed extent. Where the technique cannot inspect a region, preserve the limitation for technical disposition. “No indication reported” does not necessarily mean the full required examination was completed.

**Worked case:** twenty welds require examination; eighteen unique welds have valid reports and two files are duplicates. Coverage is 90%, not 100%. **Changed case:** reports for the last two arrive, but one lacks applicable personnel authorization. The evidence gap remains until resolved through the proper process.

## Q11 — Examination methods and detection boundaries

Method selection depends on the material, geometry, expected discontinuity, access and governing requirements. Visual examination addresses accessible surfaces. Liquid penetrant relies on surface-connected openings in suitable materials; magnetic methods require appropriate magnetic behavior. Ultrasonic and radiographic methods have their own geometry, orientation and technique limitations. A source inspector must ask whether the prescribed method and coverage were used, not assume all NDT methods are interchangeable.

Review the actual report for technique, reference checks, sensitivity or other required setup evidence, coverage and interpretation. A simple pass stamp without supporting scope may be inadequate for the order. If a method substitution is proposed, obtain designated technical approval and the required qualified procedure. Do not replace a specified examination with a cheaper method on personal judgment.

This course teaches report and readiness review, not live radiography or independent specialist interpretation. Training uses synthetic result maps and safe visual specimens. Unresolved indications go to the qualified examiner and designated acceptance authority. Keep the distinction between detecting a feature, classifying it and deciding acceptance.

**Worked case:** a required volumetric examination is replaced by a surface-only report without approval. State the coverage/method gap and hold acceptance. **Changed case:** a substitution is formally approved, but the report does not show the approved coverage. Authorization of a method does not prove it was performed completely.

## Q12 — Heat-treatment evidence

A treatment record must relate to the actual item and approved cycle. Review identification, thermocouple/location mapping, equipment/reference status, time-temperature trace and required acceptance evidence. The source inspector compares against the supplied approved requirements; this course does not prescribe metallurgical cycles. A chart with an attractive curve is not adequate if it belongs to another batch.

Check the relevant intervals and all required locations. An average temperature can hide a location outside a specified band. A hold period needs the required conditions for the required duration, according to the actual procedure. Missing data, sensor failure or an interrupted cycle requires technical review; do not draw a line through a gap and call it measured.

Preserve original records when re-treatment occurs, and link the approved disposition and subsequent evidence. Re-treatment can have implications that require engineering/metallurgical judgment. The inspector must not order repeated heating simply to obtain a passing chart. Record the limits of what the available trace establishes.

**Worked case:** fictional hold band 600–620°C for 60 continuous minutes at every required channel. One channel remains at 598°C for ten minutes, then meets the band for fifty minutes. The required continuous hold is not established. **Changed case:** a new authorized cycle shows sixty-five continuous minutes in band, but one channel identity is unverified. The time comparison passes; traceability remains unresolved.
