# VUC018 — Confined Space Rescue Qualification

## Program definition and delivery

Audience: designated industrial or construction permit-space rescue personnel and their leaders. Purpose: prepare a team to assess, plan, protect, access, package and remove a casualty from its specified spaces, then demonstrate that capability. This is an original VNDRLY program, not an external accreditor's certificate or a universal technical-rescue license. It does not qualify a team for diving, structural collapse, trench collapse, fire attack, grain engulfment or unassessed high-angle systems.

Prerequisites: documented authorized-entrant proficiency, current first-aid/CPR training, and the employer's medical clearance, fit testing and training for the actual respiratory equipment used. At least one currently certified first-aid/CPR member must be available under the applicable rescue rule. Each learner must be able to demonstrate required tasks safely; a written score cannot waive medical restrictions or physical requirements. Entry-team instruction is embedded for review and remediation. Learners lacking entry proficiency first complete and demonstrate that foundation; these hours are additional.

Learning objectives R01–R12: select a suitable rescue capability; establish command; verify hazard controls; use respiratory protection; manage air/endurance; assemble a compatible retrieval system; control load movement; access and protect a casualty; package for the exit; respond to failures; hand over and restore readiness; demonstrate and maintain a bounded qualification. Every objective has worked instruction, two formative checks, three separate final questions and a practical application.

Planned delivery: 32 instructional hours, excluding breaks and prerequisites. Day one: capability/command 90 minutes, hazard-control review 90, respiratory demonstration/practice 180, air management 120. Day two: equipment/rigging 180, raising/lowering 180, casualty approach 120. Day three: packaging 180, contingencies 180, care/decontamination/readiness 120. Day four: representative integrated drills 300, individual knowledge examination 60, practical reassessment and debrief 120. These are VNDRLY planning allocations, not an OSHA prescribed course duration. Add time until every learner demonstrates each required duty; unused reassessment time becomes additional safe scenario practice, never automatic credit.

Lead instructor: documented current capability in the rescue systems and space configurations being taught, permit-space rules, respiratory program and adult practical instruction. Use a separate safety observer for integrated drills and enough qualified assistants to observe each active station. VNDRLY delivery limit: at most four learners per directly supervised technical station; one active entry team per dedicated safety observer. A larger class rotates through inactive tabletop work. An instructor cannot simultaneously be the only safety observer and a participant in the simulated rescue.

Training safety: use inert, secured training spaces with independently verified safe air and no uncontrolled energy/material. Simulate hazards with cards, never actual oxygen displacement, toxic gas, electricity or uncontrolled loads. Start patient movement with weighted dummies. No live-person suspension until the instructor has separately verified the complete system and a site-specific training safety plan authorizes it; dummy demonstrations suffice for this package's extraction tasks. Establish real emergency communications, first aid and stop signal. Stop immediately for distress, unplanned movement, unsafe equipment or lost oversight. Never disable an actual respiratory alarm to extend a drill.

## Assessment and credential rules

Formative checks are coached learning: answer, compare with explanation, correct, then explain a changed case. Final examination is protected and proctored: 36 constructed responses, 72 points, pass 58/72 and at least 4/6 in every R-domain. Score each response 2 for correct action plus essential reasoning, 1 for safe but incomplete, 0 for wrong/absent/unsafe. An endorsement of unprotected entry, unsupported load release, removal of respiratory protection in hazardous air or abandonment of necessary backup is a critical failure regardless of total.

Twelve practical stations follow. Score each of five observable criteria 0 (not performed/unsafe), 1 (safe only with prompt/incomplete), 2 (independent and correct). Pass each station at 8/10 minimum, with every starred critical criterion at 2. A safety intervention stops that attempt and is recorded as a failed critical criterion. Instructor demonstrations and coached practice precede independent assessment. Every learner must individually demonstrate each role claimed on the record; watching another person or relying on a strong team member earns no individual credit.

After failure, provide instruction and coached practice tied to the actual error. Use the protected alternate scenario for written reassessment and change geometry, fault or casualty location for practical reassessment while retaining the same safety limits. Preserve original and reassessment results. Two reassessment attempts are allowed by VNDRLY policy; additional failures require a new instructor-directed training plan and later assessment, not indefinite same-day guessing. Accommodation may change presentation, time or access, not the essential physical safety criterion.

Issue the VNDRLY skills credential only after prerequisites, all written domains and all relevant individual/team practical criteria pass. Record the specific equipment and representative configurations. An incomplete learner receives a training participation record with outstanding criteria, not rescue qualification. Employer designation and determination of actual workplace suitability remain separate. Annually review demonstrated capability and follow the applicable required rescue-practice schedule; retrain immediately for deficiencies, changed equipment/hazards/configuration or lost proficiency. Do not renew from attendance alone.

## A — Capability and preplan

Equipment: mock-space drawings, aperture measuring tool, hazard cards, staffing calendar, communications plan, stopwatch and preplan form below. Give the team a vertical open vessel and a horizontal vessel with a baffle; inject an unavailable off-site team member. Learner measures the access, traces a casualty route, evaluates the proposed service, chooses a feasible method and states when work cannot proceed. Worked resolution: fast travel is not enough if the stretcher cannot pass the baffle or required staff are unavailable; revise and demonstrate the plan before reliance.

Criteria: accurately documents geometry and hazards; includes all response stages; *rejects unavailable/inadequate capability; chooses an effective method with fallback; identifies a representative drill and acceptance limits. Repeat with a different opening and a changed response delay.

## B — Command and communications

Equipment: radios, entry board, role cards, scene boundary markers. Scenario: two entrants, one unresponsive, a coworker wants to enter immediately. Learner sends location/count/hazard report, assigns command, attendant, entry/backup and support roles, verifies communications and blocks spontaneous entry. Use explicit commands: operator name, movement, direction, acknowledgment, start; any STOP halts movement immediately. Silence is not agreement.

Criteria: accurate emergency report; *prevents unauthorized entry; preserves entry accountability; tests primary/backup communications; *assigns competent backup without stripping an essential role. Inject simultaneous conflicting movement calls; correct response is stop and resolve command, not choose the louder voice.

## C — Hazard control and monitoring

Equipment: isolated training vessel, labelled energy/inflow cards, locks/tags/blinds or safe replicas, suitable detector and manufacturer-approved functional-check equipment, sampling line, fan and clean-air duct. Learner traces sources, verifies controls, checks instrument coverage/function, samples the relevant locations with response delay, establishes monitoring and evaluates ventilation. Instructor presents a purge connection absent from the original permit and a detector lacking the relevant toxic sensor.

Criteria: *finds and controls the extra source before authorization; *rejects unsupported atmospheric assurance; correctly checks and samples; plans clean ventilation and failure response; records new rescue conditions rather than blindly reusing the work permit. No hazardous gas is released into the space for testing.

## D — Respiratory protection

Equipment: actual approved SCBA or supplied-air/auxiliary system, matching manual, inspection record, individually fitted facepiece, safe training area. Instructor demonstrates the model's inspection, pressure/connection/alarm checks, donning, seal check, breathing/communications checks and emergency exit method; learner repeats with direct feedback, then independently. Use the actual manufacturer's sequence and controls; do not transfer a valve sequence between models. Record manual/version and the steps observed.

Criteria: verifies prerequisites and correct system selection; *rejects defects or incompatible components; *dons and obtains required seal without interference; demonstrates model-specific checks/communications; *performs the trained simulated malfunction/withdrawal without removing protection. Never create an actual air failure. Written knowledge is not a substitute for this demonstration.

## E — Air and endurance control

Equipment: gauges or training cards, timer, board, representative safe access route. Give synthetic initial 4,500 psi, required exit reserve 1,500 psi and observed planning use 300 psi/minute. The available 3,000 psi represents ten minutes before that reserve; four minutes in plus four out leaves only two for work before any additional contingency. This simplified exercise is not an operating air rule. Learner establishes a more conservative actual plan using manufacturer/employer requirements and the limiting team member, records reports and orders withdrawal at the planned trigger.

Criteria: calculates the example correctly; budgets every phase and contingency; *does not use the low-air alarm as the planned turnaround; tracks both team members; *withdraws when the limiting condition is reached. Change consumption and exit duration on reassessment. Never run a learner's cylinder down to prove the calculation.

## F — Rigging and high point

Equipment: inspected approved personnel retrieval system, compatible connectors/harnesses, rated high point, controlled dummy load, manuals and inspection forms. Instructor demonstrates each assembly and the load path. Learner identifies components, rejects a damaged or unidentified item, assembles using its instructions, verifies height/direction/load compatibility and performs a protected functional check before loading. On a Sked-Evac tripod specifically, use secured adjustment pins, the prescribed leg spread and restraint chain, and the load rating for the actual height. Keep the load centered; do not assume a lateral pull or a convenient leg attachment is safe. Other tripods require their own instructions.

Criteria: traces complete load path; *rejects incompatible/unknown/damaged components; *correctly secures high point and connections; checks intended load/direction against actual ratings; obtains independent check and controlled test. Synthetic example: 95 kg casualty plus 12 kg package plus 8 kg equipment equals 115 kg; a 100 kg component is insufficient even before other system forces are evaluated.

## G — Raise, lower and transfer

Equipment: safe dummy, approved lifting/lowering system with appropriate backup, edge protection, communications. Instructor first demonstrates actual device operation, stopping, holding and the designed transition method unloaded, then with a controlled dummy. Learner directs a short lift, hold, lower and supported transfer. For transfer, establish and verify receiving support, gradually transfer under control, verify it carries the load, then release the old support only as the designed procedure allows. A rope freewheel position is not automatically a controlled lowering mode.

Criteria: *maintains protection and supported load throughout; correct named/acknowledged commands; controls speed/direction and holds; protects line/edge and stops on snag; *verifies receiving support before release. Fault: instructor calls a snag; learner stops, secures and investigates rather than increasing force. This is not a generic rope-technician qualification for arbitrary systems.

## H — Casualty access and initial care

Equipment: safe mock space, dummy, PPE/respiratory equipment, radio, first-aid trainer kit, casualty cards. Learner follows the planned route while managing lines and monitoring, contacts the casualty, reports condition, performs care within demonstrated first-aid competence and identifies required medical support. Inject a blocked route and an anxious conscious casualty. Correct response retains protection, reassesses access and gives clear reassurance; it never removes the rescuer's facepiece to be heard.

Criteria: *maintains personal protection and route; communicates condition/location; identifies immediate care priorities within competence; avoids added patient injury/contamination; *stops an unplanned unsafe route. No invasive procedure is taught or authorized by this station.

## I — Packaging and aperture

Equipment: actual rescue stretcher with its instructions and matched lifting attachments, dummy, measured mock hatch/baffle and restraint inspection sheet. Instructor demonstrates opening/preparation, patient positioning, restraint routing/fastening and configured lifting attachments according to the chosen model. Learner packages the dummy, checks airway/access and restraints, measures the assembled profile, obtains an independent check and moves through the whole route under control. A correct fit at the first hatch does not prove fit at the internal turn.

Criteria: selects intended orientation/device; *uses prescribed restraints and actual lifting points; protects airway/body position; *maintains support through the restriction; verifies all subsequent turns and rechecks after movement. If it will not pass, stop and revise the method with qualified guidance; removing a critical restraint is not an acceptable shortcut. Record model-specific observed steps, not just “packaged.”

## J — Contingency and backup deployment

Equipment: same safe mock rescue system, fault cards, backup team, communications board. Run separate simulated communications loss, abnormal air consumption and lifting-device fault. A visible instructor signal represents each fault; do not damage equipment or actually deprive a rescuer of air. Learner stops or withdraws as planned, maintains load/respiratory protection, alerts command, activates backup and preserves remaining rescue capability.

Criteria: promptly identifies trigger; *protects entrants and suspended load; uses alternate communication/withdrawal; *deploys only trained equipped backup with required notification and support; keeps accurate identities and count. A backup that enters impulsively and leaves no planned support fails even if the dummy is recovered.

## K — Medical handover and readiness

Equipment: dummy, exposure/SDS cards, clean/dirty zone markers, equipment tags, handover/restoration forms. Learner transfers casualty condition, known exposure, times, care and uncertainties; communicates contamination risk and selects qualified decontamination guidance. Then accounts for everyone, secures the space, quarantines suspect gear and verifies readiness items. Exercise: extraction succeeded but the only line is contaminated and cylinders need servicing.

Criteria: accurate prioritized handover; *does not expose clean responders to undisclosed contamination; complete personnel count; *quarantines suspect equipment and declares unavailable capability; assigns corrective work and verifies restoration rather than signing automatically. Do not invent exposure duration or treatment.

## L — Integrated representative rescue

Equipment: complete safe representative space, dummy, independent assessor and safety observer, all approved equipment, preplan and recording forms. Teams conduct both a vertical and an obstructed horizontal exercise when both are within their intended scope. Change casualty position from practice. Assessor records notification, ready-to-enter, contact, secured patient, exit and care-handover times. Compare to the documented hazard-based response needs and record individual duties; there is no invented universal stopwatch pass time.

Criteria: *all essential controls/respiratory/backup maintained; compatible controlled equipment operation; correct route/packaging and care; clear command/accountability; *meets planned capability without a critical safety violation. A geometry that cannot be safely demonstrated stays outside the credential. If the claimed workplace requires that geometry, qualification for that workplace remains pending. Remediate failures and repeat a changed complete scenario, not just a verbal debrief.

# VNDRLY/U Confined Spaces — Entry Team coursebook

Four reviewed owned foundation lessons are embedded, followed by twelve original technical lessons. Follow the applicable employer program; synthetic examples are not operating authorization.

## C01 — Classify the space before selecting the entry route

A confined space is large enough for bodily entry, has restricted entry or exit and is not designed for continuous occupancy. A permit space adds the relevant serious hazards, such as a hazardous atmosphere, engulfment, trapping configuration or another recognized serious safety or health hazard. A large open top does not by itself make a tank safe. Entry can begin when any part of the body breaks the opening plane; leaning through the hatch is not a harmless preliminary inspection.

Worked case: a mixing vessel has a low oxygen alarm history and an internal agitator. A supervisor proposes using ventilation alone because yesterday's readings were acceptable. Ventilation does not eliminate the agitator hazard. Determine the applicable program, isolate hazardous energy, assess engulfment and connected piping, and establish entry conditions. The special general-industry alternate procedure is not a blanket waiver for every ventilated space. Its eligibility and supporting evidence must be established under the standard. For general-industry reclassification, apply the hazard-elimination requirements; construction has its separately stated elimination/isolation provisions. Neither a respirator nor a watchful attendant proves the required reclassification conditions.

The host and contractor exchange known hazards, precautions and relevant experience. Identify who coordinates simultaneous activities: cleaning one vessel can affect another through connected piping. In construction, apply the construction confined-space framework rather than automatically copying the general-industry permit. The worker should be able to describe why the chosen entry route applies and identify who must resolve an uncertain classification before anyone crosses the opening.

## C02 — Make entrant, attendant and supervisor actions fit together

The entrant knows the hazards, uses assigned controls, maintains communication and exits when the conditions or instructions require. The attendant remains outside, tracks authorized entrants, recognizes conditions requiring evacuation and summons the designated rescue service. The entry supervisor verifies prerequisites, authorizes entry and terminates it when required. A person may hold more than one role only when trained and able to perform every assigned duty without conflict.

Before entry, walk through a communication failure. What does a missed response mean, how does the attendant order evacuation, and how is rescue summoned from this location? A radio that works at the gate may fail behind a steel vessel. Establish the actual communication method and backup. The attendant must not take a delivery call that diverts attention from entrants. Monitoring multiple spaces is acceptable only when the duties remain effectively performable under the employer's procedure; proximity alone is not proof.

Worked case: the attendant is asked to move a truck while two workers remain inside. The correct action is a controlled transfer to a trained, briefed replacement or withdrawal of the entrants before leaving the post. A clipboard on a chair does not transfer responsibility. The replacement needs entrant count, current conditions, alarms, communication arrangements and rescue contacts. On a change in acceptable conditions, the entrant exits and the supervisor addresses the cause; the team does not renegotiate alarm criteria while workers remain exposed.

## C03 — Rescue readiness and permit termination

A telephone number is not a rescue plan. Evaluate whether the designated service can respond in time for the identified hazards, reach the entry point, access the space and perform the needed rescue. An emergency service may be capable generally but unavailable or unequipped for a particular vessel. Share relevant hazards and permit access so the service can plan and practice. Where employee rescue duties are assigned, the required training, equipment, practice and first-aid provisions must be met.

Non-entry retrieval is planned where required and suitable; it is not simply attaching a line without considering entanglement, internal obstacles or the person's path through the opening. The team must know when retrieval is appropriate and who operates it. An attendant cannot abandon their protective duties and enter impulsively. Rescue entry requires its own trained personnel and safeguards.

Worked case: during cleaning, the atmosphere alarm activates and an entrant reports dizziness. Order immediate exit, summon the designated rescue service and provide location and hazard information. Perform only the trained actions that are safe from outside. If the team exits, do not restart just because ventilation lowers the reading. Cancel or suspend the permit as required by the applicable program, investigate the change and reestablish conditions through proper authorization.

Preserve the permit and abnormal conditions for program review. In the general-industry permit program, canceled permits are retained for at least one year to support review. A clean replacement permit must not conceal the earlier alarm or evacuation.

## C04 — A role-based entry-team evaluation

Set up a nonhazardous mock entry with a vessel drawing, an energy-isolation record, a sample permit and simulated readings. Each participant must demonstrate the assigned role. An entrant should identify the limits, inspect assigned equipment, communicate and withdraw on command. An attendant should keep an accurate count, recognize a prohibited condition, order evacuation and contact rescue. An entry supervisor should find missing prerequisites and refuse an incomplete permit.

Introduce two changes separately: an unexplained reading and a contractor opening a connected line. Ask the learner to explain how each change could invalidate the established conditions. Passing requires the correct stop, communication and authorization sequence, not merely naming a hazard. Do not use a real oxygen-deficient space as a testing venue. A mock retrieval may use a training dummy and suitable equipment under competent supervision; it must not expose a learner to a fall, suspended-load or entanglement hazard.

The rubric records classification reasoning, isolation interface, test interpretation, role discipline, communication and emergency action. Unsafe entry, abandoning the attendant post, inventing a gas result or treating a rescue phone number as proven capability is a critical failure. The evaluator stops the exercise, teaches the missed step and conducts another scenario. Preserve the initial failed result.

The employer retains the employee name, trainer identification and training date and makes the certification available as required. Record separately any rescue practice and site authorization. Reading or an online score establishes knowledge only; this package also requires independent physical role assessment before its skills credential. Materials authorship cannot assert actual performance or rescue-service availability.

# Confined-space entry: technical instruction and field decisions

## C05 — Identify the work, the space and the controlling framework

Start with what is being done, not the company name or the shape of the permit. General-industry operations use the applicable general-industry framework; construction activities require evaluation under Subpart AA. Agricultural and shipyard activities have different applicability. A contractor working at an operating plant can perform construction work. Obtain a documented determination from the employer's responsible person when the boundary is uncertain. A course certificate cannot resolve that uncertainty by itself.

Walk the work scope from outside: access opening, dimensions, intended occupancy, previous contents, adjacent operations and connected systems. Record the inventory identifier rather than relying on “the north tank.” A tank, vault or pit with a wide opening can still restrict escape. A room that permits ordinary continuous occupancy is not automatically a confined space merely because it has poor air; its hazards still require control. Classification is a structured decision, not a way to dismiss hazards that fail one definition.

Determine whether atmosphere, engulfment, trapping geometry or another serious hazard makes the confined space permit-required. Evaluate what the proposed work can introduce: coating solvent, welding fumes, inert purge, steam, water or unexpected rotation. An empty vessel may retain residues and connected inflow. Keep people outside while evaluating; putting a head through the hatch to smell the air is neither a valid test nor an exemption from entry.

Worked application: a contractor replaces an internal baffle while plant operators want to resume an adjacent transfer. The construction entry employer must evaluate the space and coordinate the interface; a plant-issued general permit alone does not discharge the contractor's obligations. List the work, governing procedure, people responsible and connected operations. If the drawing does not match the vessel, stop the authorization process and resolve the discrepancy from a safe location.

## C06 — Build an isolation boundary that can be verified

Trace all possible sources of energy and material: electricity, rotating equipment, gravity, hydraulic or pneumatic pressure, steam, product lines, drains, nitrogen and automatic control signals. Use current drawings and a physical walkdown by qualified personnel. A line labeled spare may still be connected. Record each isolation point with a unique identifier and the method required by the employer's procedure. The entry team verifies that the authorized isolation process has been completed; this course does not grant electrical or process-isolation authority.

A stopped motor is not necessarily isolated. An operating switch can be restarted remotely, stored energy can move an agitator and trapped pressure can drive material into the vessel. Proper isolation may require locks/tags, blanks, disconnected sections or an engineered double-block-and-bleed arrangement. A single closed valve is not assumed equivalent to a positive barrier. Drain, depressurize or otherwise dissipate stored energy using the approved process, then verify effectiveness by the specified safe method. Never loosen a flange as an improvised pressure check.

Document installation and verification separately. A signed list proves someone signed; it does not prove that the list covers the correct vessel. Match the permit, tags and field identifiers. Identify who controls changes and how group work and shift transfers preserve protection. Entrants may not remove another person's protection or accept an unrecorded change because the job is almost finished.

Worked example: the electrical disconnect for mixer M4 is locked, but a nitrogen purge and a gravity-fed wash line remain connected. Electrical isolation has addressed one hazard only. Reject entry until the material pathways are controlled and verified too. In the training exercise, mark each source on a schematic, trace it to a tagged mock isolation point and explain the remaining stored-energy test. No live energy or process material is used in the classroom exercise.

## C07 — Select and check an instrument for the actual hazards

A four-gas meter does not measure every chemical. Identify previous contents, introduced products and plausible contaminants before selecting sensors. An oxygen channel and combustible channel do not establish absence of benzene, carbon dioxide or every solvent. A photoionization reading also needs the correct response factors and interpretation; it is not a universal chemical identity. Escalate an unmeasured credible hazard to the technically qualified person instead of treating a blank channel as zero exposure.

Inspect the selected instrument, sensor list, battery, alarm outputs, pump, tubing, filters and calibration status. Perform the manufacturer's required functional/bump check with the appropriate test gas and calibration when required. A bump check demonstrates response and alarms; it is not a substitute for full calibration when that is due or the device fails. Test-gas identity, concentration and expiry matter. A clean-air zero must use known suitable clean air, not the potentially contaminated hatch atmosphere. A failed or overdue instrument is removed from the entry decision until corrected.

Read the manual for sensor limitations: oxygen dependence of some combustible sensors, poisoning, cross-sensitivity, temperature, humidity and sample-line losses. Verify pump blockage alarms and tubing integrity without damaging equipment. The person sampling must remain outside the hazard unless already authorized and protected under a valid entry arrangement. Record device identifier and checks so a later reading is traceable to the instrument actually used.

Worked application: a crew expects hydrogen sulfide but the meter has oxygen, combustible, carbon monoxide and sulfur dioxide sensors. Those labels are not interchangeable. Obtain appropriate detection and assessment. Practice matching three mock meter specifications to a product history, reject the mismatched unit and explain why a successful self-test does not install the missing sensor. Never expose learners to toxic gas to make this point.

## C08 — Sample, wait and interpret before moving

Hazard evaluation identifies what may exist and the necessary controls. Verification testing checks the actual permit conditions now. Test oxygen, combustible gases/vapors and then identified toxic hazards; a multi-channel instrument may display them together, but interpretation must account for oxygen-dependent sensor limitations. Follow the instrument's minimum response time and sample transport delay at each location. Do not take a reading immediately after moving a long sample hose and assume it represents the new location.

Sample the entry path, work area and relevant levels or pockets from a safe position. Stratification, residues and poor circulation can make hatch air different from the bottom. The atmospheric-testing appendix describes testing ahead and to the sides during descent and adjusting movement for sample response. The approved sampling plan must cover the actual configuration, not merely a universal top/middle/bottom checklist that misses a side compartment.

Record actual concentrations, units, time, location, tester and instrument alongside the permit's acceptance criteria. Percent oxygen is not percent LFL, and ppm is not percent by volume. For illustration, 1 percent by volume is 10,000 ppm; 10 percent LFL is a fraction of a material's lower flammable limit, not 10 percent gas in air. Never transfer a limit between units without competent conversion and the correct substance basis.

Worked calculation: a synthetic instrument manual specifies a 20-second hose transport interval and a 30-second sensor stabilization interval for this configuration. The planned minimum at a new sample point is 50 seconds, not 20. Four points therefore require at least 200 seconds of those intervals plus movement and recording. Actual equipment may require different times. A rising trend or an unexplained result is investigated; averaging a dangerous pocket with a clean sample does not create acceptable entry conditions.

## C09 — Interpret limits without making them universal permission

Oxygen deficiency and enrichment can incapacitate or increase fire risk. The general-industry definition uses less than 19.5 percent or more than 23.5 percent oxygen; the employer may set a more conservative acceptable range. Flammable concentrations above 10 percent LFL meet that hazardous-atmosphere definition, but being below that value does not authorize hot work or prove every other hazard controlled. A permit can require a much lower threshold. Toxic exposure criteria are substance- and time-specific; an eight-hour limit is not a universal instantaneous safe value or permission to tolerate an acute peak.

Inerting can reduce combustibility while creating a lethal oxygen-deficient atmosphere. Never interpret low combustible readings in a nitrogen-purged tank as breathable air. This entry-team course does not qualify learners to conduct IDLH entry; such work needs the separate program, equipment, protection and rescue capability appropriate to it. An air-purifying respirator does not supply oxygen.

Use the actual permit criteria and alarm action, not personal tolerance or smell. If the meter alarms, a prohibited condition occurs, symptoms appear or a required safeguard fails, exit and communicate. Do not remove a sensor, change its alarm or turn away from the source to obtain a favorable display. An entrant who feels well can still be exposed; an entrant who feels ill must not wait for an instrument to agree.

Worked decision: a synthetic permit accepts oxygen 20.0–22.0 percent and combustible readings below 1 percent LFL. Oxygen 19.8 and combustible 2 percent LFL are outside that permit even though someone quotes broader regulatory numbers. Refuse entry or withdraw. Another result of zero LFL with oxygen 12 percent is an emergency atmosphere, not a clean tank. State which criterion failed and what action follows without inventing a new limit.

## C10 — Ventilate effectively and preserve escape time

Ventilation must reach the occupied work area and remove or dilute the actual contaminant. Supply clean air from a location away from exhausts, process vents and other sources. Place and secure ducting so it does not obstruct entry, damage equipment or short-circuit fresh air straight back out. Use equipment suitable for the environmental and ignition hazards. Never use oxygen to freshen a space. An oxygen-enriched atmosphere can make ordinary materials burn much more readily.

Ventilation is not isolation. It cannot stop a rotating blade or a liquid surge. Establish the approved configuration before entry and verify its performance through the specified atmospheric measurements. Keep the required ventilation operating while people remain inside. Do not move ducting without assessing its effect on the breathing zone. A fan that is running can still deliver insufficient air through a disconnected or collapsed duct.

Plan the loss-of-ventilation response before work. The monitoring and alarm arrangement must provide enough time for safe exit under the actual hazard scenario. In construction, the employer must address this explicitly. A paper estimate is not a reason to continue after a required ventilation failure. Order withdrawal, account for everyone and reassess before authorized return.

Worked exercise: a synthetic clean-air estimate assumes a well-mixed 300-cubic-foot mock vessel and 100 cubic feet per minute of effective flow. One nominal volume takes three minutes; that arithmetic does not prove complete contaminant removal or account for pockets, generation and duct losses. The team must verify the actual conditions rather than pronounce the space safe after three minutes. During practice the instructor announces a disconnected duct; the correct action is withdrawal, not increasing a timer.

## C11 — Prepare access, tools and personal protection together

Protect the opening before removing covers and expose no one to pressure or other hazards during removal. Install barriers for pedestrians, vehicles and falling objects. A barricade protects others but must not obstruct the entrant's escape route or rescue access. Inspect ladders, walking surfaces and clearances. Route cables, hoses and retrieval lines so they do not create a trip, snag or entanglement hazard. Consider whether equipment or clothing that fits the work area can also pass through the opening during withdrawal.

Select lighting and tools for the actual atmosphere and electrical conditions. A consumer light is not automatically suitable for a potentially ignitable atmosphere. Grounding, electrical protection, hot-work control and explosion protection need their appropriate competent assessment. Keep compressed-gas cylinders and other sources outside where the applicable procedure requires, and control hoses and shutoffs. A hot-work permit supplements, rather than replaces, the entry permit.

PPE follows the evaluated hazard: eye/face, skin, head, hearing and other protection as appropriate. Respiratory protection requires its own selection, medical evaluation, fit testing where applicable, training and maintenance program. A course certificate does not satisfy those prerequisites. PPE must also be compatible with the retrieval configuration and the task. Do not remove required protection to fit through an opening; redesign the task or controls.

Worked case: a welding lead, air hose and retrieval line all cross a sharp hatch edge, while a pallet blocks the rescue team's approach. An acceptable gas reading does not resolve those defects. Protect/route lines and clear access before authorization. On the mock-up, the learner physically arranges the equipment, demonstrates unimpeded exit and identifies a lighting label that does not match the simulated classification. No live welding or hazardous atmosphere is created.

## C12 — Write a permit that controls a real operation

The permit identifies the exact space, purpose, authorized duration, entrants, attendants and entry supervisor. It records hazards, isolation/protection measures, acceptable conditions, actual tests with who/when, communication, rescue summons and required equipment, plus related permits and additional information necessary for safe entry. A generic “all good” tick cannot replace a missing numerical result or unresolved rescue arrangement. Use attachments where needed, retaining a clear connection to the same space and operation.

Before endorsing, the supervisor checks that the specified measures and equipment exist and the required tests were actually performed. Make the completed permit available to the team so entrants can confirm conditions, not just to an office archive. Authorized duration covers the necessary task; a shift-long blank permit is not indefinite permission for changed work. Confirm the proposed work, people and controls at the entry briefing.

During entry, keep the count and required readings current. If conditions invalidate the authorization, withdraw and apply the applicable termination/cancellation or construction suspension process. A changed task can introduce a new hazard even without an alarm. Preserve deviations and canceled permits for review rather than rewriting history. In general industry retain canceled entry permits at least one year; construction also requires retaining each canceled permit at least one year for program review.

Worked review: permit T-12 names two entrants and solvent cleaning but has yesterday's reading, no sample location and a rescue number with no capability confirmation. The supervisor rejects it. Learners correct the missing information using the supplied synthetic facts; they must mark unknown facts unresolved, never create convenient readings. The exercise assesses judgment and traceability, not handwriting or the number of checked boxes.

## C13 — Distinguish alternate procedures from reclassification

A full permit entry is the default when required hazards and roles demand it. General-industry alternate procedures require demonstrated eligibility, including that the only hazard is actual or potential hazardous atmosphere and continuous forced-air ventilation alone is sufficient to keep entry safe, with supporting monitoring/inspection data. Initial entry needed to obtain those data uses the appropriate permit process. Construction alternate procedures separately allow physical hazards eliminated or isolated through engineering controls and require a safe exit if ventilation stops. Do not copy that wording into a different jurisdiction and assume identical eligibility.

Alternate procedures still require preparation, atmospheric verification, ventilation, monitoring, safe access and documentation. They are not an invitation to send someone inside alone because the task is short. Follow the specific rule's monitoring requirement: construction calls for continuous atmospheric monitoring unless the employer demonstrates the stated exception; general-industry alternate procedures require periodic testing as necessary to ensure ventilation prevents accumulation. A less protective habit from an old job is not an assessment.

Reclassification is different: the applicable rule requires no actual or potential atmospheric hazard and elimination of the other hazards under general industry, or elimination/isolation under the specified construction provisions, with a certification documenting the basis. Control by continuous ventilation is not elimination of an atmospheric hazard. Where entry is needed to eliminate hazards, use the required permit protection first. If a hazard arises, people leave and the employer reevaluates classification; a certificate is not permanent immunity from change.

Worked comparison: vessel A has only a verified ventilatable atmosphere hazard; vessel B has an isolated agitator and potential solvent vapor; vessel C has all serious hazards eliminated and no potential atmosphere hazard. Determine the applicable industry and facts before selecting the route. Explain why A might meet an alternate route with evidence, B requires jurisdiction-specific evaluation and C may support documented reclassification. None is authorized by these brief descriptions alone.

## C14 — Coordinate employers and simultaneous operations

An entry can be affected by people who never approach the hatch. A transfer operator may reopen a line, a cleaner may discharge chemicals into a shared system and another contractor may block egress. Identify those interfaces during planning. Exchange known space hazards, previous experience and required precautions with the parties who can introduce or control them. Do not rely on a sign to carry all coordination information.

For construction, distinguish the host employer, controlling contractor and entry employer. The controlling contractor coordinates the exchange among entry employers and other employers whose activities could affect the space. Entry employers supply their program information and hazards they introduce, and relevant information is communicated after the operation. For general industry, follow the host/contractor exchange and coordination duties in that framework. The contract title does not remove an employer's own safety responsibilities.

Before simultaneous work begins, agree who may change each shared system, how changes are communicated, what requires withdrawal and who can reauthorize. Use names and confirmation, not “operations knows.” The briefing must reach the actual people doing the work and the next shift. If a conflicting activity is discovered during entry, protect entrants first; settle commercial scheduling afterward.

Worked exercise: a sewer-entry crew is below while another employer starts a pump upstream. The attendant receives a warning. Order exit under the planned response, communicate and invoke the coordinated controls; do not wait for water to reach the entrant. For continuous systems where isolation is infeasible, only the specifically assessed program, monitoring and early-warning provisions can address that condition. This course does not convert an uncontrolled surge into an acceptable routine entry.

## C15 — Plan retrieval without creating a second casualty

Non-entry retrieval is used where required unless it would increase overall risk or would not contribute to rescue; that exception requires real evaluation. The retrieval line, harness, anchorage, device, opening and casualty path must work as a system. Internal baffles, horizontal passages or snag points can prevent useful retrieval even when a winch is present. Never describe “rope attached” as a completed rescue evaluation.

The applicable rule specifies a chest or full-body harness with attachment chosen to support successful removal, with limited alternatives where justified. A mechanical device must be available for vertical-type permit spaces more than five feet deep. Device setup, inspection, rated components, anchorage and operation follow the actual manufacturer and competent plan; do not invent a universal winch ratio or anchor rating. A fall-arrest system and retrieval system may have different functions and compatibility requirements.

The attendant summons rescue immediately when assistance may be needed and performs only the non-entry actions assigned, trained and safe under the plan. If retrieval snags, do not increase force blindly or enter after the casualty. Communicate the obstruction to the capable rescue service. Construction requires an entry rescue service when non-entry rescue is not selected, and confirmed emergency assistance if selected non-entry retrieval fails. Entry rescue is a separate qualification with specific equipment, practice and medical/respiratory requirements. A fast unprotected entry can turn one casualty into two. Provide substance safety information to the treating medical facility after an exposure as required; transfer facts, not guessed diagnoses.

Worked drill: use an inert training dummy and a rated training arrangement inspected by the qualified instructor, at ground level or with engineered fall protection that introduces no learner exposure. Demonstrate attachment, clear path, controlled device operation and stop-on-obstruction. No person is suspended for this assessment. Evaluate rescue availability, access and response against the hazards before authorizing the real work; an emergency telephone number alone is not proof.

## C16 — Handover, abnormal events and authorization after training

A relief attendant receives the current entrant identities, permit limits, readings/trends, communication and alarm arrangements, changes, rescue availability and unresolved concerns before taking responsibility. Both people confirm the transfer while duties remain covered. A supervisor transfer also checks that conditions and the work still match authorization. If effective coverage cannot be maintained, withdraw entrants before leaving; do not treat a planned break as an exception.

After evacuation account for every entrant and prevent reentry. Record actual events, alarm values, times and actions, marking unknowns honestly. Investigate why the control failed and correct the cause. Construction permits may be suspended under the specified conditions and reentry evaluation; general-industry cancellation requirements must not be casually replaced with that route. Completion of the job also requires controlled permit closure and return to service under the employer's process, not unilateral removal of isolations by an entrant.

Training must establish proficiency for each assigned role. Changes in duties, hazards or procedures, or evidence of inadequate understanding, trigger further training under the applicable rule. VNDRLY records which roles were actually assessed and any restrictions. A knowledge pass alone is labeled knowledge only. Actual employer authorization, site familiarization, rescue arrangements and other prerequisites remain separate. A yearly internal review is a VNDRLY recommendation, not a fabricated universal OSHA annual card rule.

Worked handover: the incoming attendant signs the clipboard but does not know that one entrant changed work location behind a baffle. The outgoing attendant must not depart until the count, location and communications are reconciled or the crew is withdrawn. Practice the corrected exchange aloud, then record both the initial failure and the fresh reassessment. The purpose is reliable action under changing conditions, not a certificate obtained by concealing mistakes.

# VNDRLY/U Confined Space Rescue — Rescue-team instruction

This instruction builds on the embedded entry-team coursebook. It is for designated rescue personnel, not bystanders improvising an entry. Actual qualification is limited to the roles, space configurations and equipment independently demonstrated. No reading or written examination establishes physical rescue competence. Training uses representative safe mock spaces and dummies; hazardous atmospheres, engulfment, uncontrolled energy and unprotected suspension are never created for an exercise.

## R01 — Decide what rescue capability must exist before entry

A rescue plan begins with the employer's space inventory, not with a truck full of equipment. For each representative space identify the opening dimensions, vertical or horizontal access, internal restrictions, working location, plausible casualty position, atmosphere, energy sources and material pathways. Determine how an incapacitated person can leave wearing the intended protection. A person who walked through a hatch may not fit through it in a stretcher with breathing equipment and a rescuer beside them.

Match response capability to the harm that can occur. Include notification, mobilization, travel, setup and access to the casualty, rather than quoting only driving time. An atmosphere that can rapidly become IDLH may require the capable rescue team at the entry location. A written promise to call emergency services is not a demonstrated capability. Meet the service, share hazards, verify availability and observe a representative rescue exercise. Record deficiencies and correct them before relying on the arrangement.

Choose the least-exposing effective method. If the entrant can exit safely, direct self-rescue. If an appropriate non-entry system can remove the casualty without increasing overall risk, use it under the plan. If entry is necessary, qualified entry rescuers with protection, support and backup must conduct it. A line that cannot move a casualty around an internal obstruction is not a successful non-entry plan. Neither urgency nor the casualty's identity creates missing equipment or competence.

Worked case: a standby service can arrive in twelve minutes, but the assessed event can incapacitate an entrant within minutes. Do not call twelve minutes “prompt” without comparing it to the hazard. Establish a genuinely suitable response or change the planned work so it is not undertaken under that arrangement. In practice, learners produce a space-specific capability matrix and show which tasks and people were actually evaluated.

## R02 — Establish command, roles and a protected scene

On an alarm, obtain exact location, number of entrants, last known positions, likely hazards, existing controls and what has already happened. Activate the designated rescue and medical response immediately. Establish a command point in a safe location and prevent spontaneous entry. Retain the entry attendant's accountability function; do not strip that role to create a rescuer without competent relief. Assign rescue lead, safety oversight, entry personnel, outside backup, atmospheric monitoring, rigging operation, communications and medical support according to the plan and scale.

One person may perform compatible duties only when workload and simultaneous needs allow; a title chart does not prove adequate staffing. Outside backup must be able to act, not occupied operating every other essential function. Define the leader who authorizes movement and a stop signal anyone can use. Repeat critical commands and confirm receipt. Establish safe zones, keep access clear for emergency vehicles and prevent process operators from changing isolations or ventilation without coordination.

Develop an initial action plan: protect responders, establish controls and reliable information, select method, prepare patient care and exit route, then authorize the operation. Communicate abort criteria and backup actions before entry. If the scene exceeds available capability, summon appropriate resources and prevent further casualties while maintaining safe actions from outside. This is an operational decision, not abandonment of the casualty.

Worked drill: two coworkers rush toward a hatch after hearing a cry. The assigned responder stops them, summons the designated team and obtains the permit/entrant roster. The safety lead discovers traffic blocking the extraction area and resolves it while the technical team prepares. Assess whether these actions happen concurrently without leaving accountability uncovered. Do not place a learner in a dangerous opening to test courage.

## R03 — Stabilize energy, material and atmosphere before rescue entry

Rescue urgency does not make an agitator, inflow or electrical source harmless. Obtain the isolation boundary and verify it with qualified operators. Consider what failed: the original permit may be invalid, a line may have opened or the casualty may have crossed into an untested area. Record the actual status of each relevant control. Prevent unexpected restoration, gravity movement, pressure release and upstream flow. Where a hazard cannot be made acceptably controlled, choose a method and team specifically capable of it; do not pretend a generic certificate supplies that capability.

Sample from a safe position using instruments suitable for all identified hazards, following transport and response times. Continue monitoring at the rescuers' and casualty's actual locations as required. An oxygen/combustible meter cannot rule out every toxin. Unknown or inadequately characterized respiratory exposure must be treated according to IDLH requirements, not guessed from the absence of odor. Evaluate fire, chemical contact, heat and structural hazards in addition to breathing air.

Ventilate using a clean source with a plan that reaches the occupied area and does not introduce ignition or spread exposure to others. Never use oxygen as ventilation. Atmospheric improvement does not establish mechanical isolation or remove the need for the required protection. Record trends and communicate changes promptly. Rescue entry requires its own authorization and continuing control, not automatic reuse of a canceled work permit.

Worked case: the original entry record lists acceptable oxygen, but the rescue sample is falling and a purge valve status is unknown. The team does not rely on the earlier record. It verifies the purge isolation, applies the required respiratory/other protections and reassesses the rescue method. Practice identifying the unknowns and selecting safe controls, with simulated readings only.

## R04 — Select and use respiratory protection as a complete system

An air-purifying respirator does not supply oxygen and is not the response to an unknown or IDLH atmosphere. OSHA's IDLH selection includes a NIOSH-certified full-face pressure-demand SCBA with at least a thirty-minute rated service life, or a combination full-face pressure-demand supplied-air respirator with auxiliary self-contained supply. Rated duration is a test classification, not guaranteed work time. Selection must also address chemicals, temperature, access and escape. Escape-only equipment is not entry equipment.

Before assignment, verify the respiratory program's medical evaluation, appropriate current fit testing, training and equipment serviceability. Inspect the actual unit, cylinder pressure, facepiece, straps, seals, connections, hoses, regulator and alarms under its instructions. Don the unit in the manufacturer sequence, secure fit without twisted straps and perform the required user seal check. A seal check each donning does not replace fit testing. Facial hair or other equipment interfering with the seal is not corrected by pulling straps harder.

Conduct a buddy check of configuration, air supply, communications and snag hazards before entry. Maintain the protection while in the hazardous area; never remove the facepiece to talk or fit through a restriction. The team must practice the actual model's emergency procedures, supplied-air escape transition where used and communication while masked. An improvised regulator swap between incompatible units can be fatal. Approved breathing-air quality and maintained supply are part of the system, not an assumption about a nearby compressor.

Worked exercise: a rescuer presents a valid course certificate but no current fit-test record for the issued facepiece. Do not assign tight-fitting-respirator entry on that basis. Resolve the prerequisite or use another qualified person. In clean-air supervised practice, each learner demonstrates inspection, donning, seal check, communication, planned withdrawal and safe removal; medical clearance is verified before strenuous respiratory drills.

## R05 — Manage air, time and rescuer endurance

Plan air for access, work, removal, exit and contingency. The rescuer with the most limiting supply or condition can govern team withdrawal. Heavy exertion, stress, leaks and patient handling can sharply increase consumption. Low-air alarms are emergency warnings, not the planned moment to begin a long exit. Use the team's approved turn-around/reserve policy, actual pressure readings and observed consumption with conservative allowance for changing workload. Never promise a fixed number of rescue minutes from the cylinder label alone.

For a simple training estimate only, suppose a cylinder starts at 4,500 psi and a synthetic plan requires 1,500 psi left at exit. If measured pressure drops 300 psi per minute under representative work, 3,000 usable psi divided by 300 gives ten minutes for all planned entry, work and exit phases before that reserve. A four-minute approach and four-minute return leaves only two minutes for work, before further contingency. That may be unsuitable. Real compressed-air duration depends on the equipment and conditions; the calculation does not authorize an operation or replace the approved air-management plan.

Record entry time/pressure, agreed checkpoints, current pressure, location and exit decision for each member. Outside personnel monitor trends and communicate impending limits. Relieve teams before fatigue, heat or supply removes their ability to return safely. Maintain backup capability when replacing a team; do not consume all ready personnel in the initial entry. If a rescuer has a malfunction, entanglement, heat symptoms or unexpected restriction, execute the practiced emergency response immediately.

Worked case: an entry pair has similar start pressures, but one member consumes air much faster while moving the casualty. The plan adapts to the limiting member rather than averaging their gauges. Rehearse a clean-air drill with simulated pressure reports and identify the decision point that preserves exit and backup. Never deliberately exhaust a real cylinder with a learner inside a mock restriction.

## R06 — Inspect, select and assemble a compatible retrieval system

Treat anchorage, high point, connectors, rope/cable, lifting device, harness or stretcher, edge protection and backup as one system. Identify each component, intended function, working limits, inspection history and compatibility. The weakest applicable limit governs. A breaking strength is not a working load limit, a material-handling winch is not automatically rated for people, and a fall-arrest device is not automatically a raising/lowering device. Equipment with unknown history, damage, failed inspection or relevant recall is not used until properly resolved.

Survey the support surface and load direction. A tripod placed on unstable ground or subjected to an unapproved side pull can fail even when its catalog capacity appears sufficient. Set leg lengths, pins, feet/restraint and mounting exactly under the matching instructions. Check overhead clearance, load path and the rescue opening. Secure compatible connectors with gates closed/locked and orient them to avoid cross-loading or pressure on gates. Manage sharp edges and moving lines; padding that can shift away is not reliable protection.

Before a person could depend on the system, a second qualified rescuer checks the load path from anchor to casualty, operating/backup functions and control of every transition. Perform the instructed function/preload checks with a dummy or suitable test arrangement. Keep hands out of pinch points. Do not change gear ratios, bypass brakes or add unapproved power tools because the load is difficult. Unexpected resistance requires stopping and finding the cause, not greater effort.

Worked example: a synthetic planned load is 95 kg casualty, 12 kg packaging and 8 kg attached equipment: 115 kg before any rescuer load. Compare the full intended configuration with every applicable rated limit and its conditions. A 100 kg component fails this comparison; a stronger rope does not fix it. A two-person load needs its own compatible system evaluation. The arithmetic is not a universal design factor or proof that an anchor is suitable.

## R07 — Control raising, lowering and transitions

Establish who operates the device, who controls the backup, who watches the opening and who directs patient movement. Use brief agreed commands: ready, take tension, raise, lower, stop, with acknowledgments before movement. Anyone seeing a danger calls stop. One signal source directs routine motion; contradictory instructions are resolved while the load remains secured. Maintain patient/line observation where possible and reliable communication with entry personnel.

Operate the specific winch or rope-control device according to its inspected setup and current instructions. The operator demonstrates the correct direction, controlled speed, stop/hold function, line management and response to malfunction on a dummy first. Never assume a freewheel control is safe under a person's load. Never release a brake, transfer a connector or remove an anchor while that component is carrying an unsecured load. Before each transfer, establish and verify the receiving support, communicate, transfer gradually and confirm it holds before disconnecting the original support.

Mechanical advantage reduces ideal effort at the cost of travel and adds friction, component loads and reset complexity. A simple ideal four-part system supporting a 120 kg-equivalent load would require about 30 kg-equivalent effort and four metres of hauled rope for one metre of lift, ignoring friction. It does not make the anchor load disappear or authorize arbitrary rigging. Use a competent approved design, inspected hardware and the actual device diagram; this chapter is not a license to invent a rope system.

Worked drill: the casualty package reaches the lip but snags. Stop, secure the load, identify the snag and coordinate the planned edge transition; do not jerk or haul harder. A second run introduces a garbled lower command. The operator holds until the command is confirmed. Record both technical control and team communication, since a perfectly assembled system can still be misoperated.

## R08 — Access, communicate and protect the casualty

Entry rescuers follow the planned route, maintain protection and communication, and monitor conditions continuously as required. Account for line/air-hose routing so it remains compatible with movement and withdrawal. Do not disconnect protection to get through a restriction. If the expected route is blocked or the casualty is beyond the evaluated configuration, report and reassess rather than improvise an untrained passage. The outside team tracks locations and can act on lost communication.

At contact, assess responsiveness and breathing within the responder's medical competence while addressing immediate environmental threats. Tell a responsive casualty what is happening and provide simple directions that do not encourage unsafe self-movement. Coordinate urgent removal with medical priorities. Do not enter a hazard to deliver treatment that cannot be provided safely, and do not spend prolonged time on nonessential packaging in a rapidly deteriorating environment. Use appropriate respiratory support for the casualty only with equipment and training intended for that purpose; a rescuer's spare mask is not automatically a safe patient air system.

Control major bleeding and protect the airway as feasible under the plan and current first-aid/medical guidance. Suspected spinal injury influences movement but does not override immediate lifesaving needs. Chemical contamination affects both patient contact and the receiving team. Communicate condition, access restrictions and needed equipment before moving. No medical diagnosis or invasive procedure is conferred by this rescue credential.

Worked scenario: the casualty is conscious but panicked, and their clothing catches on a projection. The rescuer communicates, stabilizes the situation within the plan and informs the haul team to stop. They do not command an uncontrolled pull. Practice masked communication and casualty assessment on a dummy, with an instructor speaking the casualty responses. First-aid competence is a prerequisite, not simulated by a correct multiple-choice answer.

## R09 — Package for the actual exit path

Choose a harness, rescue strop or stretcher intended for the casualty condition, orientation and route. A device suitable for a horizontal drag may not be approved for vertical lifting. Check the current model instructions, lifting points, straps and required accessories. The packaging must restrain the casualty without restricting breathing and must remain secure during the actual tilt or transition. Do not attach lifting lines to convenient handles unless those handles are approved load attachments.

Prepare the device outside when practical, inspect it and arrange straps to avoid confusion in the restricted space. Position the dummy or casualty using the trained technique, support vulnerable areas and apply the prescribed restraints in sequence. Check fit, closure, circulation/comfort where appropriate, airway access and loose limbs or equipment. A second rescuer verifies correct attachment and orientation before load is applied. Recheck after movement, since straps can shift and a package can deform through an opening.

Measure the route against the packaged profile, not just the unencumbered person's shoulders. Account for bends, ladders, baffles and the space needed for a rescuer to guide. Use the planned tag/guide arrangement only if it does not entangle or expose another person. At an edge, secure receiving support and coordinate movement before changing orientation. Never remove essential restraints to squeeze a casualty through an unassessed opening.

Worked drill: a package fits a circular hatch horizontally on the floor but cannot make the turn through the vessel neck. The plan must be revised and demonstrated on a representative safe mock-up before reliance. The learner compares a vertical-lift and horizontal-movement device specification, selects the appropriate one, packages a dummy and demonstrates the full route. Passing requires secure movement through the representative geometry, not a neatly strapped stationary mannequin.

## R10 — Respond to failure without losing the backup team

Preplan loss of communications, deteriorating atmosphere, ventilation failure, respiratory malfunction, snagged line, disabled lifting device, rescuer injury and additional casualties. Define immediate actions, command words, backup personnel and limits. A plan that says “call for help” but identifies no capable available help is incomplete. Outside IDLH personnel must be trained and equipped for effective emergency rescue, maintain communication and follow the notification/assistance provisions before entering to rescue.

On a lost signal, use the agreed backup and withdrawal/emergency response promptly. Do not repeatedly try a dead radio while consuming the entrant's remaining air. On a device fault, secure the load through the trained system and reassess; do not disconnect it in hope another device will catch it. If an entry rescuer becomes impaired, the backup response protects the remaining team and preserves accountability. Rescue commands must clearly identify whether the original casualty, rescuer or both are being moved.

Additional hazards may require stopping the attempted method. Continuing because the team has already committed can convert a manageable problem into multiple casualties. Notify command of the changed facts, preserve safe protection and request appropriate capability. Staffing must remain sufficient for the revised operation; an exhausted team is not a ready backup simply because it is outside.

Worked case: while the first team packages a casualty, the outside monitor detects loss of a required control and the second rescuer reports abnormal air consumption. The correct decision follows the preplanned abort/rescuer-assistance response, not a vote to finish the package. A safe drill uses simulated faults and an instructor-controlled pause; no alarm is disabled and no learner is actually deprived of air.

## R11 — Transfer care, decontaminate and restore readiness

At removal, transfer to the designated medical team with the casualty's observed condition, exposure information, duration if known, treatment and changes. Provide relevant safety-data information. Do not delay urgent care for paperwork, but communicate contamination risks before exposing ambulance personnel or the clean treatment area. Establish decontamination appropriate to the substance with qualified guidance; do not improvise chemical neutralization or apply a single wash method to every hazard.

Account for all entrants and rescuers before declaring the operation closed. Keep the space secured and preserve the failed permit/control information for investigation. Quarantine equipment exposed to damaging chemicals, overload, shock or uncertainty. Follow manufacturer cleaning, inspection, retirement and service procedures. Replacing a used cylinder is not the entire readiness check: inspect facepieces, alarms, ropes/cables, packaging, communication and consumables too.

Provide responder medical/exposure follow-up and support after stressful incidents. Record actual actions and times, with unknowns marked honestly. Separate factual observations from interpretations. A review should identify control and system changes with responsible owners, not merely blame an individual or record that the team needs to be more careful. Verify restoration before the team is again relied upon for another entry.

Worked exercise: a successful extraction leaves the only retrieval line contaminated and the backup cylinders partly used. The team is not automatically ready for the next shift. Record the unavailable capability, communicate it to entry operations and restore verified readiness. No rescue service may be represented as available when its critical equipment or personnel are not ready.

## R12 — Demonstrate and maintain a real team qualification

Assess the team in representative spaces matching opening size, configuration and accessibility, while keeping the exercise environment safe. Measure notification, readiness, access, patient contact, packaging and removal stages, and compare performance with the hazards the team is expected to address. A stopwatch total alone does not show safe technique. Record missed communications, equipment faults, protective violations and patient-control problems even if the dummy exits quickly.

Each member demonstrates assigned tasks independently as well as in the team. Rotate roles only where qualification is sought and preserve restrictions for unassessed duties. Written knowledge, respiratory prerequisites, first-aid competence and physical rescue performance are separate evidence. A course cannot issue unlimited rescue authority for all industries, configurations, loads and equipment. An employer evaluates whether the demonstrated capability meets its actual spaces and expected emergencies.

General-industry employee rescue teams practice at least every twelve months using actual or representative spaces. Construction requires practice before actual rescue and at least annually, with the stated exception for a properly performed recent rescue in the same or a similar space. Do not casually transplant that construction exception into every other program. Relevant changes or observed deficits require further training; a calendar-valid certificate does not excuse a skill gap.

Worked debrief: the extraction time met the target, but the backup operator could not demonstrate a malfunction response and an entrant's pressure report was missed. Record the failed criteria, remediate and conduct a new scenario. The qualification remains pending for those duties until demonstrated. Materials completion supplies instruction and assessment tools; it is not a claim that a team has actually passed them.

# Rescue formative checks

Attempt first, then study the reason and correct the error. Explain a changed example before progressing. Safe equipment practice is separate.

## R01-L1

A service promises a twelve-minute drive. What else must the preplan evaluate?

**Answer:** Notification, mobilization, setup, access, hazard time scale, staffing and equipment capability for this space.

**Reason/remediation:** Arrival is not casualty access. Rework the plan with every stage and compare it with the expected harm.

## R01-L2

A retrieval line runs around a fixed baffle and will not move a dummy. Is a line alone adequate?

**Answer:** No. Demonstrate an effective safe method and suitable entry-rescue fallback when required.

**Reason/remediation:** A nominal system does not prove a viable path. Trace the whole extraction route.

## R02-L1

The attendant volunteers to enter because the victim is a friend. What must happen?

**Answer:** Prevent spontaneous entry, retain attendant coverage and activate the designated equipped rescue response.

**Reason/remediation:** Urgency does not create competence or replace accountability. Reassign only with qualified relief and a valid plan.

## R02-L2

Two people call opposite movement directions. What is the correct command response?

**Answer:** Stop, secure the load and resolve direction through the named movement leader/operator before resuming.

**Reason/remediation:** Ambiguous commands can injure patient and rescuers. Practice acknowledgment before movement.

## R03-L1

The original permit omitted a purge line. Can rescue use its original isolation approval?

**Answer:** No. Trace and verify the newly identified source and reassess rescue authorization and conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** A signature is not proof that changed conditions are safe. Repeat the source-path review.

## R03-L2

A four-gas detector has no sensor for the suspected solvent. What does a normal display establish?

**Answer:** Only results within the actual sensors and limitations; it does not establish absence of the solvent.

**Reason/remediation:** Select suitable evaluation and protection. Review detector coverage and unknown-atmosphere decisions.

## R04-L1

Why is a cartridge respirator unsuitable for oxygen deficiency?

**Answer:** It does not supply oxygen. Use the appropriate atmosphere-supplying system under the respiratory program.

**Reason/remediation:** Filtration cannot restore missing oxygen. Explain the difference between APR and atmosphere-supplying equipment.

## R04-L2

Does a successful seal check replace the required fit test?

**Answer:** No. Medical evaluation, fit testing, training and each-use seal checking are separate requirements.

**Reason/remediation:** A quick donning check cannot establish fit-test compliance. Identify each prerequisite and its record.

## R05-L1

Calculate available planning time for 4500 psi start, 1500 psi exit reserve and 300 psi/min consumption.

**Answer:** Ten minutes to that reserve; with four minutes in and four out, only two remain before added contingency.

**Reason/remediation:** Subtract the reserve before dividing. The synthetic result is not permission to use all ten minutes.

## R05-L2

One rescuer consumes air faster than the other. Whose remaining air governs the team plan?

**Answer:** The limiting member, together with other safety and exit constraints.

**Reason/remediation:** A team average conceals a person at risk. Recalculate withdrawal and contingency needs.

## R06-L1

A 95 kg casualty, 12 kg package and 8 kg equipment are proposed on a 100 kg rated component. Decide.

**Answer:** Reject: the load is already 115 kg before further system-force considerations.

**Reason/remediation:** Include the whole supported load, not just body weight; verify every component and configuration.

## R06-L2

Can an unidentified carabiner be used because it looks undamaged?

**Answer:** No. Establish its suitability, rating, history and compatibility or remove it from service.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance alone does not prove intended use. Trace the entire verified load path.

## R07-L1

What must happen before the original support is disconnected during a transfer?

**Answer:** The receiving support is established, checked and demonstrably carrying the load under the designed procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** A connection alone is not verified support. Practice the controlled transfer with a dummy.

## R07-L2

An ideal 4:1 system raises a load one metre. How much haul rope travels, ignoring real losses?

**Answer:** Four metres. Actual friction and system forces require separate evaluation.

**Reason/remediation:** Mechanical advantage changes force and travel; it does not establish component adequacy.

## R08-L1

The casualty cannot hear through your facepiece. What is the safe response?

**Answer:** Keep protection in place and use trained communication methods or relay while following the plan.

**Reason/remediation:** Removing protection can create another casualty. Practice speech/hand/radio methods while equipped.

## R08-L2

The route is blocked by equipment not on the drawing. What should the rescuer do?

**Answer:** Stop the unplanned advance, communicate and reassess an authorized safe route and rescue method.

**Reason/remediation:** An improvised squeeze may compromise PPE or trap the rescuer. Review route and withdrawal limits.

## R09-L1

The packaged dummy fits the hatch but not the internal turn. Did packaging pass?

**Answer:** No. The entire path must work while preserving essential protection and restraints.

**Reason/remediation:** A single aperture test misses the limiting geometry. Demonstrate the full route.

## R09-L2

Are carrying handles automatically acceptable vertical lifting points?

**Answer:** No. Use the device-specific prescribed attachments and orientation.

**Reason/remediation:** Convenience is not approval for lifting. Locate the actual model instructions and intended points.

## R10-L1

Radio contact fails during access. Is repeated calling the only action?

**Answer:** No. Implement the planned alternate communication and withdrawal/emergency response promptly.

**Reason/remediation:** Air and hazard exposure continue while waiting. Practice the defined lost-signal response.

## R10-L2

A lifting device faults with the dummy supported. What is the first principle?

**Answer:** Preserve support through the trained system, stop movement and reassess before any transfer or release.

**Reason/remediation:** Disconnecting an uncertain load can cause a fall. Rehearse the approved secured response.

## R11-L1

A casualty may carry a chemical contaminant. What must the receiving medical team know?

**Answer:** The suspected substance, exposure information, condition, care and contamination risk, with relevant safety-data information.

**Reason/remediation:** A clean-area transfer can spread harm. Give prioritized factual information and identify unknowns.

## R11-L2

After a successful rescue, all cylinders are depleted and the only line is contaminated. Is the service ready?

**Answer:** No. Declare the capability unavailable, quarantine/restore equipment and verify readiness before further reliance.

**Reason/remediation:** Success on one event does not replenish capacity. Complete the restoration form.

## R12-L1

The team is quick, but one member failed a backup task. Can the team score erase that failure?

**Answer:** No. Record and remediate the individual deficit, then independently reassess it and affected team performance.

**Reason/remediation:** A strong partner can conceal a critical skill gap. Qualification is role-specific.

## R12-L2

Does a course certificate authorize every confined-space rescue configuration?

**Answer:** No. It records demonstrated roles/equipment/configurations; the employer must evaluate actual suitability and designate personnel.

**Reason/remediation:** A paper credential is not universal rescue authority. State exclusions explicitly.

# VUC018 sources and objective coverage

Reviewed 2026-11-17. Scope is an original VNDRLY industrial/construction permit-space rescue program. No IADC, NFPA, OSHA or other organization's certification is claimed. State-plan, sector and employer obligations must be determined for the delivery site. Maritime, diving, collapse and other specialist rescue regimes are not silently included.

Primary references:

1. [29 CFR 1910.146](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.146), particularly (d)–(k): general-industry entry controls, roles, training and rescue capability/equipment/practice. Section(k) is the controlling regulatory text for its scope.
2. [29 CFR 1926.1211](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1211): construction rescue service evaluation, availability, proficiency, practice and retrieval. Read its express recent-rescue exception and pre-actual-rescue practice requirement; do not conflate those with a generic course-expiry rule.
3. [1910.146 Appendix F](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.146AppF): **nonmandatory evaluation guidance**, including initial and performance evaluation and representative configuration. The guidance's discussion of practice does not replace controlling regulation. Response evaluation considers more than travel and configuration evaluation includes the actual limiting route.
4. [29 CFR 1910.134](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.134): respiratory selection for unknown/IDLH atmospheres, medical evaluation, fit, use, inspection, air and outside rescue provisions. Its structural-fire two-in/two-out provision is not presented here as a universal confined-space staffing formula. The rescue plan still requires sufficient competent people for all simultaneous essential duties.
5. [1910.146 Appendix B](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.146AppB) and [1926.1204](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1204): atmospheric evaluation/verification, controls and construction monitoring. The embedded entry-team coursebook teaches the paired industry requirements rather than treating one as interchangeable with the other.
6. [Skedco official instruction index](https://skedco.com/skedco-instruction-manuals/) and [Sked-Evac tripod instructions](https://skedco.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Tripod-Instruction-Updated.pdf), public three-page document linked by the manufacturer at review: supports the limited named-tripod example in station F. Do not use another product's rating or generalize the setup to all high points. No numeric working-load claim is imported from an unseen label.

The actual detector, respiratory apparatus, retrieval/lowering device and stretcher manuals are mandatory equipment-specific teaching aids at delivery. Model/revision, compatible components and the actual observed sequence are recorded. This is normal equipment qualification, not a claim this document can authorize any device. A manufacturer link alone does not demonstrate a learner's operation. No paid textbook, proprietary question bank or unseen current technical standard is represented as reviewed. No generic knot/rope recipe is offered as authorization for arbitrary high-angle rigging.
