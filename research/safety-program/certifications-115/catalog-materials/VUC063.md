# VNDRLY/U Manual Ultrasonic Examination — Integrated Weld Assessment

VUC063 · Original instruction · 2026-11-18

## Scope and entry competence

This program integrates conventional manual UT setup, weld detection/characterization, length and applicable surface-connected crack-height measurement, and traceable reporting. It addresses the separately cataloged current manual-examination scope rather than repeating only the legacy detection course. Single- or dual-element techniques must be suitable for the actual task. Phased array, TOFD and other modes are not conferred by this award.

Require documented foundational UT competence, weld geometry, safe equipment handling and practical sizing prerequisites. The instructor verifies these before independent assessment. VNDRLY materials are original training, not API-approved instruction, employer certification or authority to accept production equipment. Current public API documents contain conflicting terminology and height-reporting sentences; the source register identifies those conflicts. The training criteria here are explicit original rules and do not silently resolve external exam rules on API's behalf. [S63-01]

## Q01 — Turn an assignment into an examination plan

Read the scope as a set of required outputs: identify the component, establish examination volume, find reportable responses, characterize them, locate endpoints, measure applicable height and document limits. Do not treat a successful detection scan as completion when sizing or records remain missing. Separate inspection findings from acceptance/disposition authority.

Create a task matrix linking each required output to technique, reference, evidence and responsible person. If a requested quantity is undefined, resolve its meaning before measuring. A complete report cannot be assembled by attaching unrelated readings after the fact.

**Worked:** plate and pipe examinations plus four crack bars are assigned. The learner scans plate and pipe but leaves the bars blank. Detection work may be valid, but the integrated task is incomplete. **Changed:** a bar is inaccessible. Record the limitation and seek a suitable authorized arrangement; do not enter zero as an assumed size. P01 uses an output/evidence matrix.

## Q02 — Readiness, identity and safe handling

Confirm material, dimensions, geometry, technique revision and equipment applicability. Check safe support, pipe restraint, cable/connector condition, sharp edges, couplant/SDS and spill management. Use only cool, depressurized training components. This course does not authorize hot or live-plant examination, electrical repair or hazardous access.

Record actual sample aliases and orientation before moving equipment. A correct result attached to the wrong component is an invalid report. If labels conflict, stop the identity-dependent task and resolve the discrepancy without guessing.

**Worked:** safe sample has two inconsistent identifiers on the worksheet and tag. Reconcile identity before scanning. **Changed:** identity is corrected but pipe can roll. Secure the arrangement first. P02 requires actual observations and a readiness record that does not substitute a signature for the checks.

Inventory instrument/software, probe, wedge, cable and reference block. Record configuration values used for each method and link every result to that setup identifier. A saved file is an aid, not evidence that its assumptions match the connected hardware or actual part. Demonstrate setup from documented requirements and verify independently.

Use distinct setup IDs for materially different configurations. When changing hardware or mode, determine which checks must be repeated. Never relabel an old setup as current without actual evidence. Maintain the original record to support later review.

Create the correct configuration record and perform required verification. **Changed:** only a display color changes with no measurement dependency. Record the change as appropriate without pretending it creates a new physical calibration. P03 distinguishes substantive configuration changes from cosmetic ones.

## Q04 — Single versus dual-element behavior

A dual probe has separate transmitting and receiving elements with a geometry-dependent path. It cannot be assumed to behave like a straight single-element pulse-echo probe over every thickness. Manufacturer guidance identifies possible thin-section nonlinearity and the need to verify the relevant range. [S63-02]

Set the correct operating mode and use the approved probe-specific measurement convention. Verify actual references across the intended range. A good thick-reference result does not prove accurate thin-section readings. Do not apply a generic manual correction unless the technique validates it.

**Worked:** known 4 and 12 mm references read 4.6 and 12.1 mm. Errors +0.6 and +0.1 show the thin point is problematic against fictional ±0.3 mm training checks. **Changed:** a supported correction/mode brings both within criterion. Record actual verification and the limited demonstrated range. P04 includes genuine probe/mode comparison on safe references.

## Q05 — Velocity, zero and path verification

Distance depends on timing, velocity and delay. For a simple material-only pulse-echo path, S=vt/2. Calibration establishes the relationship; reference verification independently tests it. Periodic instrument certification does not replace a setup-specific calibration. Manufacturer teaching distinguishes these activities. [S63-03]

Use identified references spanning the relevant range. Preserve raw readings and actual settings. If a reference fails, correct the cause and assess affected measurements instead of adjusting only the final reported value.

**Worked:** corrected 18 µs at 3.2 mm/µs gives path 28.8 mm. **Changed:** raw 21 µs includes 3 µs delay; using raw time gives 33.6 mm, 4.8 mm high. These calculations explain a simple case; they do not override dual-path or instrument-specific corrections.

## Q06 — Index, angle and physical coordinates

Establish beam index and refracted angle using the approved method. Define datum, positive direction and weld centerline separately from wedge position. For a flat first-leg path S at θ from normal, normal depth is S cosθ and surface distance S sinθ. The correct formula with a wrong index still gives a wrong location.

Record front-edge offsets only with their signed direction and actual measurement. Do not move plotted findings to make them look plausible. A mismatch is a reason to examine geometry or setup, not to invent coordinates.

**Worked:** path 36 mm at 60° gives depth 18 mm and offset 31.1769 mm. **Changed:** recorded lateral coordinate is known to be 2 mm too high from an index mistake. Subtract 2 from that coordinate traceably; do not alter the independent depth calculation. P06 requires actual index/angle verification and a relocated reference.

## Q07 — Legs, thickness and curved geometry

Identify the sound leg before calculating location. In a flat plate's second leg, normal depth is 2 t−S cosθ within that leg's range. For curved parts, use the applicable validated geometric correction/model and probe fit. A plate approximation is not automatically valid because the wall is thin.

Plan the examination against actual geometry, crown and access. Determine whether intended scan positions are physically reachable and whether the method can resolve the target feature. Record uncovered scope explicitly.

**Worked:** t=24 mm, S=72 mm at 60° gives unfolded travel 36 mm and second-leg depth 12 mm. **Changed:** the part is a small pipe with rocking wedge contact. The flat calculation alone cannot validate the examination. Establish proper contact/model and repeat required checks before use.

## Q08 — Reference, scanning and evaluation sensitivity

Reference sensitivity provides a reproducible response basis. Scanning sensitivity may differ from evaluation sensitivity under the procedure. Record both and return to the required evaluation basis before comparing an indication with a reporting criterion. More gain is not more physical flaw height.

Avoid clipping and distinguish peak validity from noise. Use applicable path-dependent compensation only as the technique permits. A response level without setup and reference context cannot be interpreted reliably.

**Worked:** valid 20% and 80% responses have ratio 0.25, or −12.0412 dB relative to the reference. **Changed:** evaluation performed at an unrecorded extra gain. Reestablish the required basis rather than comparing incompatible values. This is original amplitude arithmetic, not a claim that these percentages are universal reporting limits.

## Q09 — Complete directional and contact coverage

Translate the volume and likely orientations into an actual movement plan. Record sides, directions, scan limits, indexing, contact verification and interruptions. A single pass may not interrogate transverse features or regions shadowed by another response. Use the technique's required directions rather than convenience.

After lift-off, resume with a documented overlap or repeat the affected region. Do not count a clean display during loss of coupling as sound material. Record any unexamined interval even if the rest of the report is complete.

**Worked:** 240 mm assigned length, 180 mm demonstrably covered, leaves 60 mm and 25% unexamined. **Changed:** the remaining interval is scanned validly. Add its evidence and update scope without deleting the original interruption. P09 observes the actual scan rather than inferring coverage from elapsed time.

## Q10 — Discriminate geometry and reportable findings

Compare responses with known boundaries, root/crown, mismatch and beam behavior. Use approved movements and plotting to evaluate each candidate. A root echo can be geometry or coexist with a relevant feature; one explanation cannot erase all nearby responses. Report supported findings even when precise type remains unresolved.

False calls matter alongside misses. Calling every echo a crack is not cautious competence, and dismissing every awkward echo as geometry is not efficient examination. Preserve reasoning and uncertainty so follow-up is directed accurately.

**Worked:** one response tracks known root geometry; another persists at a distinct position. Evaluate separately. **Changed:** the second is shown to be another known boundary by corroboration. Revise the interpretation with its evidence; do not erase the earlier evaluation history. P10 includes sound and flawed teaching regions.

## Q11 — Characterize connection and feature type

Distinguish surface-connected from embedded conditions using actual location and corroborating evidence. Report the supported feature category, not merely the most familiar name. Planar, volumetric and laminar responses have different interpretation and coverage implications; waveform shape alone is not a universal classifier.

Where views conflict, revisit setup, geometry and interpretation. Do not average categorical disagreements or claim certainty because a dropdown requires a choice. Use the provided uncertainty/reporting path and technical review.

**Worked:** one permitted view supports an embedded planar response, another suggests surface connection. Investigate and retain both observations. **Changed:** verified evidence resolves a far-surface connection. Record the resulting classification and determine whether the assigned height method applies. P11 links characterization to the next required measurement.

## Q12 — Endpoints and length are traceable measurements

Use the approved endpoint criterion and a consistent datum/direction. Record start, stop, units, sensitivity and probe orientation. Response length is method-dependent; it is not automatically an exact physical boundary. Gross oversizing may wrongly implicate adjacent sound regions.

Check raw endpoints before entering the total. A typed length that disagrees with stop minus start needs resolution. Preserve the source observations and correct the report traceably.

**Worked:** start 48 mm, stop 83 mm: length 35 mm. **Changed:** valid revised stop 79 mm gives 31 mm, a 4 mm reduction. State why the measurement changed. P12 requires actual endpoint demonstration and relocation rather than a worksheet-only subtraction.

## Q13 — Define height, tip depth and ligament

For a flat far-surface-connected feature, height h=t−d where t is local thickness and d is normal depth to the established tip from the scanning surface. Near-surface remaining ligament is d. Surface-connected on the scanning side or embedded features require their applicable definition/method. Do not label a path length as depth or a ligament as height.

Use the actual local thickness where the feature is measured. Nominal drawing values may differ. Establish tip identity before subtraction; mathematics cannot prove the echo represents the crack boundary.

**Worked:** local 25 mm, valid far-surface tip depth 17 mm gives height 8 mm and ligament 17 mm. **Changed:** local thickness is actually 24 mm at that position: height 7 mm. Record the local basis and revise without changing the measured tip depth arbitrarily.

## Q14 — Find and corroborate a tip response

A tip can produce a weaker diffracted response distinct from a base/corner echo. Manufacturer explanation supports geometry-based location, but actual identification needs the validated technique and corroboration. [S63-04] Do not select an arbitrary weak echo or the brightest part of the display as the tip.

Follow permitted movements and verify consistency with the actual geometry. If the response cannot be uniquely identified, record sizing unresolved and obtain a suitable method rather than inventing a precise number. A detection procedure is not automatically a sizing procedure.

**Worked:** weak candidate is inconsistent across permitted positions. Height remains unsupported. **Changed:** repeated observations establish the same physical tip. Save those actual records and calculate using verified geometry. P14 uses known teaching samples before blind bars.

## Q15 — Multiple tips and maximum height

Select the greatest supported height under the defined method, not the mean or the smallest plausible value. A candidate proven geometric must be removed from interpretation with a traceable reason.

Distinguish maximum measured value from whole-feature maximum when coverage or interpretation remains unresolved. A confident result at one point does not fill a gap elsewhere.

**Worked:** in 25 mm local thickness, valid tip depths 18 and 15 mm yield heights 7 and 10 mm; maximum 10. **Changed:** 15 mm response is established as geometry. The 7 mm result remains supported only with valid remaining tip identity and full coverage. P15 requires an actual position/height table and interpretation history.

## Q18 — Drift, uncertainty and re-examination

Verify at required intervals and after relevant configuration changes. Determine which results depend on a failed check. Preserve failures, correct the cause and repeat affected work under the procedure. Later success cannot automatically validate earlier records.

Use independent reference truth and a prior uncertainty/decision rule. Consistent readings may share bias. Do not invent an uncertainty allowance or round a failed physical result into a pass. Escalate an unresolved basis and document pending work.

A learner cannot offset poor sizing by detecting everything or offset false calls with a high written score. Protected specimen truth must be independently supported and suitable for the announced tolerances.

## Q20 — Handover, remediation and credential boundary

Record actual demonstrated scope and any method/geometry limits. External certification requires its own authorized process.
