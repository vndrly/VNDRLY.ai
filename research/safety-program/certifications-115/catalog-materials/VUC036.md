# VNDRLY/U Asset Integrity — Piping Inspection

VUC036 • Original learner coursebook • Materials version 1.0

## Program definition

This program teaches inspection planning, evidence evaluation and documented integrity decisions for in-service metallic process piping. It is intended for inspection trainees, maintenance integrity personnel and engineers who coordinate piping examinations. Learners need basic algebra, unit conversion, drawing literacy and supervised industrial inspection experience. Before laboratory work they must demonstrate safe equipment handling and explain the difference between an observed condition and an authorized engineering disposition.

The VNDRLY credential records completion of this instruction, examinations and observed training tasks. It does not confer API 570 certification, NDE personnel certification, welding qualification, engineering licensure or employer authorization to release equipment. Practical exercises use isolated, depressurized training articles and synthetic records. Actual field inspection requires the site's authorization, competent personnel, applicable controlled standards and safe-work arrangements.

On completion, the learner will be able to: (1) establish inspection boundaries and traceable records; (2) connect service conditions to credible damage and an effective examination plan; (3) evaluate thickness data and calculate defensible screening results; (4) select and apply the correct reference basis for joints, materials and component ratings; (5) review NDE and welding evidence; (6) evaluate repair and test packages within their authority; and (7) communicate restrictions, unresolved findings and required follow-up without issuing an unsupported release.

The cases are fictional. Exercise-supplied factors, intervals and acceptance limits are training inputs, not universal field limits. A correct calculation is only one part of an integrity decision. Each chapter includes instruction, a worked decision and a changed condition. Attempt the changed condition before reading its explanation.

## Q01 — Establish the boundary before inspecting

An inspection starts by identifying what system the evidence describes. A line number is useful only when its physical boundaries, service and revisions are known. Trace the line from a positively identified origin to its destination. Mark branches, isolation points, equipment connections, reducers and changes of material. Compare the field route with the process drawing and isometric; record discrepancies rather than quietly correcting a sketch from memory. A drawing can show intended construction while the field contains a later tie-in.

Separate three questions: who owns the inspection program, who performs the examination, and who can approve an integrity disposition? An examiner reports results within an approved technique. An inspector evaluates those results within assigned responsibilities. An engineer may need to establish required thickness, assess damage or approve a repair. One person's several qualifications do not eliminate the need to record which authority they exercised.

Open an inspection record with asset ID, boundary sketch, current service, operating and design conditions, construction basis, inspection history, responsible persons and known gaps. A missing record is a gap, not evidence that an item is compliant. Resolve conflicting tags using independent information and the owner; do not choose the convenient identity because its history looks better.

**Worked case.** Line P-402 on an isometric terminates at Vessel A. The physical line continues to a new blending skid, and the skid branch has no inspection history. The existing results can remain associated with their verified locations, but they do not establish coverage of the added branch. Mark the boundary discrepancy, identify the change record and obtain the branch's design and baseline information before accepting a whole-line conclusion.

**Changed condition.** The owner confirms the branch was installed last month. Does its age remove the gap? No. Newness does not establish material, construction examination, testing or baseline condition. Seek those records and establish an inspection plan appropriate to its service.

## Q02 — Read piping components as a connected system

A pipe schedule describes a dimensional series; a pressure class describes a component rating family. Neither alone states the allowable service pressure of an assembled system. Identify material, actual dimensions, manufacturing route, temperature and the relevant component standard. Nominal pipe size is an identifier, not a substitute for measured outside diameter in a calculation. A pipe, elbow, valve, flange, gasket and bolting set may each introduce a different limitation.

Read an isometric by following flow and elevation, then locating welds, supports and branches. Sketch orientation so someone can find the same inspection location later. For a horizontal pipe, establish what clock position means and the direction from which it is viewed. For a vertical line, identify a fixed reference and elevation. Record whether a measurement belongs to pipe, fitting, weld or adjacent material.

Examine component function as well as appearance. A gate valve used to isolate a line, a check valve intended to prevent reverse flow and a relief device serving overpressure protection are not interchangeable. A clean valve body does not prove that its internals operate. An actuator's position indication does not prove isolation. A pipe support transfers load; removing it to improve inspection access may create a new hazard.

**Worked case.** A replacement flange fits the bolt pattern and is stamped with the expected class. Its material marking differs from the specification. Stop acceptance and compare its material-temperature rating and compatibility. Bolt alignment proves fit, not suitability.

**Changed condition.** The material is correct but the gasket projects into the bore. The assembly still needs correction: gasket dimensions and seating affect function, and a compatible alloy does not cure incorrect assembly. Record the mismatch before the joint is hidden.

## Q03 — Build corrosion circuits and choose locations deliberately

A useful circuit groups piping expected to behave similarly, while preserving local exceptions. Begin with fluid composition, water content, temperature, pressure, velocity, metallurgy and operating modes. Then identify places where those assumptions change. A cold branch, an injection point or a stagnant drain may need separate treatment even though the line number remains the same. Do not group by paint color or proximity alone.

A condition monitoring location must be reproducible. Record its position, component, extent and measurement technique. Distinguish the location from the individual examination points within it. A minimum thickness at one point is not automatically the minimum for the entire elbow. Select locations to test the damage hypothesis: flow impingement, low points, transitions, mixing regions and contact areas deserve attention when the service supports those mechanisms.

Plan both routine coverage and discovery. Routine locations support trends. Broader scans or other examinations can discover damage between them. If unexpected damage is found, revisit the circuit model and extend coverage using a documented basis. Merely adding the new minimum to a spreadsheet leaves the sampling weakness unresolved.

**Worked case.** Circuit C-12 has six accessible straight-pipe locations with stable readings. A downstream elbow at a two-phase flow change has never been scanned. The stable trend says something about those six locations; it does not establish the elbow's condition. Add a suitable elbow examination and review similar geometries.

**Changed condition.** A scan finds localized loss only at the elbow outlet. Do not average it into the stable straight-pipe readings. Map its extent, identify the mechanism and obtain the appropriate local assessment. Reconsider other outlets under comparable flow conditions.

## Q04 — Deadlegs, injection and mixing points

Stagnant regions can behave differently from flowing pipe. A closed branch may collect water, concentrate contaminants or remain at a different temperature. A drain that is used occasionally is still capable of stagnant periods. Identify the actual configuration and operating history: orientation, low points, valves, connected equipment, flushing and whether the supposed isolation leaks.

At an injection point, the local environment is governed by mixing and impingement as well as the bulk fluid. Examine the injection hardware's identity and condition, operating history, chemical concentration and flow distribution. The susceptible area is not necessarily limited to the fitting where the chemical enters. Define inspection coverage using the applicable guidance and the corrosion specialist's assessment. Record the physical upstream and downstream boundaries so the next inspection can reproduce them.

The practical sequence is to verify the configuration, establish credible damage, select an effective method, examine the defined region and compare findings with assumptions. If a quill is missing or chemical concentration changed, the previous plan may no longer describe the current exposure.

**Worked case.** An inhibitor injection line has regular parent-pipe measurements but no examination of the mixing region. Operations reports that the quill was shortened during maintenance. The inspector requests the change record, verifies the installed geometry and expands the assessment to the altered mixing conditions. Historical parent-pipe stability cannot validate the modified arrangement.

**Changed condition.** The chemical pump is currently off. Is the region no longer susceptible? Current shutdown does not erase prior exposure, stagnant fluid or future restart. Document the operating period and condition, then assess both accumulated damage and the planned return to service.

## Q05 — External damage, insulation and buried transitions

External inspection is more than looking for rust. Look for water entry, damaged weather barriers, coating breakdown, contact points, displaced supports, deformation and evidence of leakage. Insulation can conceal damage; its outer appearance does not reliably establish the metal beneath it. Choose access and screening methods around the credible damage and their detection limitations, not simply the easiest place to open the jacket.

At a soil-to-air transition, several environments meet. Identify the coating termination, water accumulation, soil contact and accessible metal. Buried piping also requires attention to coating, protective systems where fitted, soil conditions and operating history. A favorable reading on the exposed riser cannot be assigned to buried pipe that was not examined.

Plan removal and restoration. Access work may involve hot surfaces, hazardous insulation, work at height or excavation. Obtain the proper controls before disturbing anything. After inspection, document surface condition and repair requirements, then verify that insulation and weather protection are restored. An inspection opening left unsealed can create the next damage location.

**Worked case.** A low point has a cracked jacket seam and rust staining. A neighboring inspection port shows sound metal. Keep both observations: the port result does not resolve the suspect seam. Arrange appropriate exposure or a qualified screening-and-follow-up plan for the actual wet area.

**Changed condition.** A screening method reports no significant indication, but its stated blind zone includes the pipe support contact. The contact remains unexamined. Document that limitation and arrange another method or approved access; do not convert “not detected” into “not present.”

## Q06 — Damage mechanisms: establish a hypothesis and test it

Damage recognition connects material, environment and stress. Uniform wall loss, localized thinning and cracking require different examination strategies. Start with the process history and mechanism review; use appearance to refine the hypothesis rather than diagnosing solely from a photograph. Ask what would make the proposed mechanism plausible and what evidence would distinguish alternatives.

For wet acidic service, investigate fluid chemistry, water distribution and affected metallurgy. For deposit-associated attack, investigate the deposit and the local environment beneath it. For dissimilar-metal contact, consider the electrical connection and shared electrolyte. For erosion-corrosion, relate the damage pattern to flow direction and geometry. For vibration fatigue, inspect the loading source and restraints as well as the crack-prone connection. A remaining-life calculation for uniform thinning cannot predict crack growth reliably.

The response must address the cause as well as today's condition. Replacing a cracked small-bore connection with an identical unsupported assembly can recreate the failure. Increasing inspection frequency without changing a severe vibration source may leave little opportunity to detect a developing crack.

**Worked case.** A pump discharge branch vibrates visibly. Its last wall reading is almost nominal. The relevant concern includes cyclic loading, which the thickness result does not resolve. Notify the responsible operating and engineering personnel, evaluate the loading and use an appropriate examination for cracking. Do not touch or brace the live line by hand.

**Changed condition.** The pump stops and the vibration disappears. That identifies an operating relationship, not proof of an undamaged branch. Assess accumulated damage and the restart condition before closing the finding.

## Q07 — Obtain trustworthy thickness data

An ultrasonic thickness number depends on the sound path, velocity assumption, instrument setup and identification of the correct echo. Use a suitable procedure, probe, couplant and reference article. Check instrument response over the thickness range involved. Surface condition, curvature, coatings, temperature and material differences can alter the result. Retain enough information to explain how the measurement was obtained and why it is usable.

Repeat questionable readings after examining the signal and setup. Do not erase a low value merely because it disrupts a trend. Investigate whether it represents a pit, geometry effect, incorrect location or measurement error. Conversely, a high reading can be wrong: coating, echo selection or scale error can conceal metal loss. Correlate unexpected findings with another suitable measurement or examination.

Use two reference values to distinguish a constant offset from a scaling error. In the training setup, blocks of 4 and 12 mm read 4.5 and 12.5 mm: both are high by 0.5 mm. If they read 4.4 and 13.2 mm, both are high by 10%. These patterns guide troubleshooting; neither licenses an improvised subtraction from field records instead of proper calibration and remeasurement.

**Worked case.** A coating remains on a line while the historical measurements were taken on bare metal. The new readings appear thicker. Record the technique difference and establish comparability before calculating a negative corrosion rate.

**Changed condition.** A post-use reference check fails. Identify the affected measurement interval, quarantine the questionable results and repeat the necessary work after correcting the setup. Preserve the original record and correction trail rather than silently replacing it.

## Q08 — Corrosion rates, remaining life and uncertainty

For a comparable point, loss divided by elapsed time gives a rate. Use consistent units and actual dates. Compare long-term and recent behavior; a process change can make an old average misleading. Before selecting a rate, examine measurement quality, location identity, localized damage and process history. A negative calculated rate is not metal regeneration. A zero apparent loss may reflect resolution or uncertainty.

The simple thinning model predicts remaining time from the current thickness above the established required thickness divided by the selected rate. It assumes the chosen rate describes future loss and that the required thickness is appropriate. Cracks, localized defects, structural loads or changing service can invalidate that simplification. A model output is not an operating authorization.

**Worked case.** The same point measured 8.0 mm six years ago, 7.4 mm two years ago and 7.0 mm today. Long-term rate is (8.0−7.0)/6 = 0.167 mm/year; recent rate is (7.4−7.0)/2 = 0.20 mm/year. With an exercise-approved required thickness of 5.0 mm and selected rate of 0.20, the screening life is 10 years. Record why the selected rate is defensible and what could change it.

If the current reading's justified conservative lower bound is 6.8 mm, the same exercise gives 9 years. This sensitivity is useful: a precise spreadsheet display should not conceal uncertain inputs.

**Changed condition.** A neighboring pit is 4.7 mm. The ten-year result cannot dispose of it. Map and assess the local damage, communicate the below-required finding and obtain an authorized disposition. Never average the pit away.

## Q09 — Turn an assessment into an inspection plan

A due date needs a basis: damage mechanism, condition, consequence, examination effectiveness, applicable limits and authorized judgment. Keep thickness monitoring, external inspection and special examinations distinct. A thickness due date does not automatically satisfy an external inspection requirement. Risk-based inspection is a structured assessment with defined inputs and approvals, not a label used to extend an inconvenient date.

For non-RBI screening, API 570 relates the interval to remaining life and service-class limits, with specific provisions and exceptions. Retrieve the applicable controlled provision before making a field recommendation. In this course, each numerical case supplies its governing exercise rule so the learner can demonstrate reasoning without inventing a class or silently adopting a maximum.

**Worked case.** The exercise rule is the lesser of half the calculated remaining life and a five-year cap. Life is eight years, so the initial candidate interval is four years. A process review identifies a likely chemistry change in six months. The inspector documents the four-year calculation but recommends an earlier review tied to that change. An upper limit is not a promise that conditions will stay acceptable until then.

A deferral must expose the unresolved risk, not conceal an overdue task. Identify what was due, why it was missed, current evidence, credible deterioration, compensating measures, authorized decision, revised date and conditions that invalidate the decision. Preserve the original due date and approval trail. Scheduling software must not manufacture a new compliant history by resetting the clock.

**Changed condition.** The same line has eleven years of screening life but a two-year special-examination limit in the exercise. Half-life is 5.5 years; neither that nor the five-year general cap overrides the two-year special requirement. Use two years or earlier justified action, and identify which examination that date covers.

## Q10 — Calculate straight-pipe thickness without losing the assumptions

For the applicable straight-pipe internal-pressure model, B31.3 uses t = PD/[2(SEW + PY)]. Symbols must be matched to the code: outside diameter D, pressure P, allowable stress S, quality factor E, weld strength reduction W and coefficient Y. Required allowances are added separately. Check the equation's range and other loads; a straight-pipe equation does not qualify a branch, elbow, crack or unsupported span.

**Worked case.** Supplied exercise inputs are P = 300 psi, D = 6.625 in, S = 20,000 psi, E = 0.85, W = 1 and Y = 0.4. The numerator is 1,987.5 psi·in; denominator is 34,240 psi. Pressure thickness is 0.05805 in. With 0.060 in allowance, the required minimum becomes 0.11805 in. If selecting new nominal stock with a supplied 12.5% minus tolerance, nominal thickness must be at least 0.11805/0.875 = 0.13491 in. Choose a documented available size meeting the requirement, then verify all other design conditions.

For existing pipe, use validated actual metal dimensions and the correct in-service evaluation basis. Do not subtract a manufacturing tolerance a second time from measured thickness as though it were merely an ordered nominal value. Do not retain future corrosion allowance in two different places without explaining the convention.

**Changed condition.** Use SI inputs P = 2 MPa, D = 168.3 mm, S = 138 MPa, E = 0.85, W = 1, Y = 0.4. The pressure thickness is approximately 1.425 mm. Adding a 1.5 mm allowance gives 2.925 mm. Mixing 168.3 mm with a stress in psi would destroy dimensional consistency even if the calculator produced a plausible-looking number.

## Q11 — Pressure capacity, joint factors and rerating

Rearranging the same applicable pipe equation gives P = 2SEWt/(D−2Yt). The t used in a future-condition calculation must represent metal available for the pressure function at that future date. Start from validated current thickness and subtract the explicitly selected future loss and other applicable deductions. Identify the limiting component of the whole system; a satisfactory straight spool cannot raise a valve or flange rating.

Quality factors are evidence-dependent. Find the material specification, manufacturing method, longitudinal seam details and applicable examination records. Do not use a girth-weld examination as proof of a longitudinal seam factor. Casting quality and weld joint quality are separate concepts. A higher factor needs its actual qualifying evidence and applicable reference basis.

**Worked case.** A synthetic pipe has D = 6.625 in, S = 20,000 psi, E = 0.85, W = 1 and Y = 0.4. Current usable thickness is 0.220 in. A four-year forecast at 0.010 in/year leaves 0.180 in. The model gives approximately 944.3 psi. A connected component's verified allowable pressure is 700 psi at the proposed temperature. The system cannot be accepted at 900 psi on the pipe calculation alone.

Rerating is a controlled engineering change. Gather the new conditions, component and material basis, assessment, examination/test requirements, relief implications, documentation and approvals. A spreadsheet result is supporting evidence, not the rerating itself.

**Changed condition.** The pressure stays at 650 psi but temperature increases. Re-evaluate stress values, component ratings, materials, expansion and relief implications. “Below the old pressure” does not demonstrate acceptability at the new temperature.

## Q12 — Permanent blanks, flanges and bolted joints

A permanent blank needs a design basis; an unmarked plate that fits between flanges is not automatically a rated isolation device. For the applicable B31.3 blank model, tm = dg√[3P/(16SEW)] + c. The gasket geometry determines dg. Confirm the configuration and assumptions before using the equation. Installation and isolation planning remain separate from this dimensional calculation.

**Worked blank.** Given dg = 6 in, P = 300 psi, S = 20,000 psi, E = W = 1 and c = 0.060 in, pressure thickness is 0.3182 in and required total is 0.3782 in. A plate with verified minimum thickness 0.375 in fails this exercise despite being close. Do not round the requirement downward to make stock fit.

A flange rating must be read for its material group, class and temperature. Verify markings against records, then inspect dimensions, facing, bolting and gasket suitability. The class number is not a universal psi rating. A pressure-temperature table is also not a repair procedure for damaged sealing surfaces.

**Worked rating.** The fictional approved component sheet allows 20 bar at 100°C and 16 bar at 200°C, with linear interpolation expressly permitted. At 150°C the allowed pressure is 18 bar. A requested 19 bar fails. A different material's table cannot be substituted because it gives a larger number.

**Changed condition.** A proposed flange has the correct rating but its measured remaining section is below the approved minimum after resurfacing. The rating lookup does not resolve dimensional loss. Hold the item for authorized repair evaluation or replacement. Record both the rating and the dimensional finding so one is not mistaken for the other.

## Q13 — Thermal movement, supports and structural limits

Heated pipe changes length. Restraints convert movement into forces and moments, so pressure thickness is not the only integrity concern. Record cold and hot positions, support condition, guides, anchors and spring indicators where installed. Do not move a support or unlock a spring without the approved procedure. A displaced support may be a symptom of thermal movement, settlement or incorrect installation.

For a simple exercise with a constant supplied coefficient, free expansion is αLΔT. Actual design may use temperature-dependent expansion data and flexibility analysis. The simplified result estimates movement; it does not calculate allowable stress or prove that a nozzle can carry the reaction.

**Worked case.** A 30 m steel line uses an exercise coefficient of 12×10⁻⁶/°C and heats by 150°C. Free expansion is 0.054 m, or 54 mm. The sketch allows only 20 mm before a shoe reaches an obstruction. Record the discrepancy and refer the arrangement for engineering review. Do not cut the obstruction or relocate the anchor as an inspector's field improvisation.

Structural minimum thickness can govern even when a pressure equation gives a small number. Span, weight, wind, vibration, attached loads and handling can matter. Any tabulated screening minimum has applicability limits. Check them before treating the value as a universal acceptance threshold.

**Changed condition.** The same line now carries a heavier fluid during testing. Reassess temporary loading and supports. A system adequate for gas service can be overloaded by test liquid even when the pressure test value is correct.

## Q14 — Toughness, preheat and heat treatment

Strength, toughness and hardness answer different questions. A strong material may still be vulnerable to brittle failure under the wrong temperature and restraint. Determine the material identity, thickness, design/test temperatures and applicable exemption or test requirement. A Charpy report must correspond to the actual material and required test condition; a passing number from an unrelated heat is not transferable evidence.

Preheat and postweld heat treatment are controlled operations. Match the procedure to material, thickness, joint and applicable construction or repair requirements. Verify temperature measurement locations, instruments, heating/cooling controls, hold records and interruptions. An operator's statement that the weld “felt hot enough” is not a temperature record.

**Worked report.** The fictional acceptance sheet requires an average of 30 J, no individual below 22 J, at −20°C. Results are 35, 33 and 21 J at −20°C. Average is 29.67 J, and one specimen is below 22 J: both criteria fail. Results of 36, 34 and 24 J would satisfy those numerical criteria, but still require identity and test-condition verification.

**Worked heat-treatment review.** A procedure requires a documented hold within a stated temperature band. The chart shows one monitored zone below the lower limit for part of that hold. Do not average it with a hotter zone. Identify the duration and location, obtain the authorized disposition and retain the original chart.

**Changed condition.** The impact specimens pass numerically but were tested at +20°C. They do not demonstrate the specified −20°C condition. Ask for appropriate evidence or an authorized technical disposition; do not alter the report's temperature.

## Q15 — Welding evidence: WPS, PQR and personnel

A welding procedure specification tells the welder how to make the weld. A procedure qualification record records the conditions and results of the qualification test. A performance qualification establishes the welder's demonstrated range under the applicable rules. Review these as linked but distinct records. A signed procedure does not prove a welder's qualification, and a welder's qualification does not validate an unsupported procedure.

Build a comparison sheet: production material and thickness; process; filler; joint and backing; position; electrical and technique variables; thermal controls; qualified ranges; required tests; personnel continuity. Locate applicable essential-variable rules in the controlled edition. If toughness is required, account for its additional qualification implications rather than assuming an ordinary qualification covers everything.

**Worked case.** A production package specifies GTAW. The attached welder qualification covers SMAW only. The procedure may be valid for GTAW, but the personnel evidence does not cover the proposed work. Hold welding by that person until the correct qualification is established. A supervisor's confidence in the welder is not the missing record.

**Changed-range exercise.** A supplied qualification sheet permits deposited weld metal from 3 to 12 mm for this exercise. The production requirement is 14 mm. It falls outside the supplied range. Changing the WPS maximum to 14 without supporting qualification would change text, not competence evidence.

Before welding, verify joint preparation, fit-up, cleanliness and consumable controls. During welding, record required parameters and thermal controls. Afterwards, verify examination, repairs and final traceability. This sequence prevents a final appearance check from becoming the sole basis for accepting work whose process was uncontrolled.

## Q16 — NDE planning, visual examination and acceptance

Select an examination method for the defect sought, the material and the access. Visual examination can establish many surface and assembly conditions but cannot establish an unseen internal volume's condition. PT reveals suitable surface-breaking discontinuities; MT requires an appropriate magnetic material and field arrangement. Radiography and ultrasonic examination have different sensitivities and geometric limitations. No method is simply “best” without a defined purpose.

Separate technique validity from acceptance. First ask whether qualified personnel, an appropriate procedure, calibration and coverage produced usable evidence. Then apply the governing acceptance criteria to that evidence. An image of inadequate quality is not an acceptable weld. An acceptable image quality indicator does not mean every visible discontinuity is acceptable.

**Worked case.** A weld report says “acceptable,” but the technique coverage omits a segment hidden by a support. Ask whether the approved requirement permits that coverage. If not, the missing segment remains unresolved even though the examined portion is acceptable. Record the extent precisely rather than converting a partial result into whole-weld acceptance.

For visual work, establish lighting, access, viewing arrangement and measuring tools. Map indications with scale and orientation. Distinguish a geometric mark from a relevant discontinuity by examination, not by wishful classification. Apply the specified service and joint criteria; do not import a vessel acceptance table into a piping assessment without a valid basis.

**Changed condition.** A random sample contains a rejected weld. The repair of that weld does not automatically close the sampling issue. Follow the applicable expansion rules for the defined lot and responsible welding personnel, document additional results, and separately verify the repair.

## Q17 — Review radiographic, surface and leak-test evidence

Radiographic records need traceable identification, demonstrated coverage and image quality appropriate to the technique. Check the relationship among the weld map, exposure positions, image identifiers and report. Verify the applicable image-quality indicator, placement and visibility requirements using the approved procedure. Density or digital-image controls, backscatter and geometric limitations affect interpretation. Never enter a radiography exclusion area to obtain a better view; exposure work belongs to authorized personnel under radiation controls.

For PT, preparation must leave discontinuities accessible to the penetrant. Follow the qualified sequence for application, dwell, excess removal, development and observation. Over-cleaning can remove useful evidence; inadequate cleaning can create background that masks it. Check product compatibility, temperature range and viewing conditions. For MT, establish suitability of the material, field direction and equipment performance; one field direction may not reveal all orientations. Prod contact introduces additional surface-damage risks and must follow the authorized technique.

A bubble test needs a controlled pressure source, a suitable leak-detection liquid and defined observation coverage. A pressure gauge that does not visibly drop is not a substitute for examining the required joints: leakage may be too small relative to the system volume to register promptly. Conversely, temperature changes can affect pressure without a leak.

**Worked evidence review.** A report identifies weld W-18, while every image is marked W-81. The technician believes it is a typing error. Resolve the identity using traceable records before accepting the result. Guessing the intended identity can leave one weld unexamined and another incorrectly credited.

**Changed condition.** A PT report states acceptable results but records a part temperature outside the procedure's qualified range. The report is not validated by the favorable indication result. Obtain an authorized technique review and, where necessary, repeat the examination under valid conditions.

## Q18 — Positive material identification and traceability

Material verification asks a specific question. Alloy sorting, chemical composition verification, carbon discrimination and mechanical-property confirmation are different tasks. Choose a method capable of answering the required question and verify its calibration, surface preparation, reference checks and interpretation limits. A device's automatic alloy name is a classification result, not a complete material certificate.

Maintain identity from receipt through cutting, fabrication and installation. A correctly identified parent length can produce unmarked offcuts. Reconcile material reports, heat numbers, transferred marks and installed locations. When a discrepancy is discovered, define the potentially affected population; do not assume the visibly wrong item is the only one. Segregate questionable material and document its disposition.

**Worked case.** A repair requires distinguishing a low-carbon grade. The supplied instrument procedure states that carbon is not measured by that setup. The displayed alloy family cannot resolve the low-carbon requirement. Use an authorized method or traceable documentation that actually establishes the required characteristic; do not select the more favorable grade name manually.

**Changed condition.** A measured element lies just below an upper limit, but the measurement uncertainty crosses it. Apply the approved acceptance decision rule. Without that rule, the inspector should not silently treat an uncertain result as conclusive compliance. Record the result, uncertainty and required disposition.

The credential does not qualify a learner to operate an X-ray source or spark-producing analyzer in a hazardous area. The exercise uses reports and inert samples; field methods require appropriate personnel, equipment and permits.

## Q19 — Repair selection: restore function and manage the remaining life

A repair decision begins with damage characterization, remaining geometry, material and service. Define whether the proposal contains leakage, restores structural capacity, replaces damaged material or changes the design. A device that stops a leak may not carry the load of a failed pipe. A temporary designation needs an expiry or replacement trigger, monitoring and an owner; it is not permission to forget the item.

For a full-encirclement sleeve or clamp, review load paths, fit, end conditions, sealing, temperature, pressure, corrosion beneath the device and future inspection. For a welded patch or buildup, review the remaining substrate, weldability, qualified procedure, finished dimensions and examination. For an insert or spool replacement, verify material, joint preparation, alignment, support and the new inspection baseline. Reference the repair article appropriate to the actual configuration rather than accepting a generic vendor brochure.

**Worked case.** A leak-sealing clamp is proposed over a deeply thinned line. The supplier's document covers sealing pressure but excludes axial load capacity. The proposal does not demonstrate that the pipe can resist separation. Request the missing structural assessment and a safe execution plan; do not interpret leak cessation as restoration of all functions.

**Changed condition.** The clamp is approved for a limited interval, but the line's operating temperature later rises. Re-evaluate the repair against the changed condition and update the monitoring/expiry decision. Its original approval did not cover every future service.

No live welding, hot tapping, grinding of pressurized pipe or pressure-containing repair is performed in this course. Learners assess packages and isolated mockups. Qualified design, execution controls and owner authorization are separate requirements.

## Q20 — Repair execution, excavation and restoration records

The repair package must translate design intent into verifiable hold points. Define the defect extent, removal method, allowable remaining geometry, welding and thermal controls, examination stages and final acceptance. Excavation can reveal a larger flaw than expected. Stop at the specified hold point when the remaining thickness or flaw extent no longer matches the approved plan; do not continue removing material simply to obtain a clean surface.

Examine the prepared area with a method suitable for the defect. Record whether indications remain and whether excavation geometry allows the proposed weld. After repair, evaluate the finished condition, not merely the deposited amount. Machining or grinding can remove part of an overlay's qualified thickness. A flange face can be smooth yet dimensionally unacceptable after excessive machining.

**Worked case.** The synthetic repair requires at least 3.0 mm finished overlay. The welder deposits 4.0 mm, but final machining leaves 2.6 mm at one area. The deposited amount does not satisfy the finished requirement. Record the 0.4 mm shortfall and obtain a qualified correction/disposition before acceptance.

**Changed condition.** A local excavation leaves 7.5 mm where the approved plan requires 8.0 mm. The technician proposes filling it immediately so it will no longer be visible. Stop and record the changed condition. Welding cannot retroactively authorize a substrate outside the plan's limits.

Closeout should link the original defect, approved design, actual material, weld identity, personnel, thermal records, examination results, test or approved alternative, dimensional results, operating restrictions and new baseline. Keep unresolved items visible. Replacing a damaged spool changes the history at that location; preserve the removed component's findings and identify when the new component entered service.

## Q21 — Hydrostatic and pneumatic test planning

Pressure testing creates a new hazard while evaluating containment. Define the test boundary, medium, pressure, temperature, elevation, weakest component, temporary supports, relief, instruments, barriers and depressurization. Verify isolation and control trapped volumes. A correct pressure calculation does not make a poorly supported closure or uncontrolled exclusion zone safe. Use the approved test procedure; learners review documents and inert mockups, never pressurize equipment.

For the applicable B31.3 hydrostatic case, PT = 1.5P(ST/S). Pneumatic testing has different limits and precautions. The code's initial pneumatic leak-check stage is the lesser of half the test pressure or 25 psi; do not import a vessel test sequence into piping work.

**Worked hydrostatic case.** Given P = 200 psi, ST = 20,000 psi and S = 16,000 psi, the calculated value is 375 psi at the specified reference point. A point 40 ft lower in water has about 17.3 psi additional static head, using the supplied 0.433 psi/ft. Its pressure would be about 392.3 psi. If a component there has an approved test limit of 385 psi, the proposed arrangement fails that limit even though the reference gauge reads 375.

**Worked pneumatic case.** A synthetic procedure proposes 220 psi for a 200 psi design basis and supplies an independently verified upper limit of 240 psi. The target lies within those supplied bounds. The preliminary check stage is 25 psi, not 110 psi. Subsequent steps, barriers, relief and examination conditions still require the authorized procedure.

**Changed condition.** An additional vessel is connected to the test boundary. Re-evaluate both its allowable test conditions and stored energy. The old boundary sketch and hazard assessment no longer describe the test.

## Q22 — Relief devices and critical valves

An overpressure protection system includes more than the device's set-pressure certificate. Establish the protected equipment, credible scenarios, inlet and outlet arrangements, isolation status, backpressure, service compatibility and maintenance history. A bench result evaluates specified device behavior under the test conditions; installed restrictions or changed service may still compromise protection.

Inspect accessible external condition and records without defeating the protection. Look for unintended isolation, damaged discharge supports, plugging evidence, leakage and unauthorized changes. Route concerns to the responsible authority. Do not lift a lever, change a set pressure or remove a device as an informal diagnostic action.

Critical check valves may protect against reverse flow under a specific scenario. Identify what function is required and how the maintenance/testing plan demonstrates it. A valve that is present on a drawing may be stuck, installed incorrectly or inappropriate for current flow conditions. External appearance alone cannot establish internal closure performance.

**Worked case.** A relief device has a current bench certificate. An upstream isolation valve is found closed with no approved alternate protection established. The certificate does not resolve the unavailable flow path. Follow the site's immediate escalation and safe operating response; do not declare the protected system acceptable because the device itself passed.

**Changed condition.** The isolation is correct, but a process change increases the relieving requirement. Obtain a protection-system review. Previous capacity selection and testing do not demonstrate adequacy for a different scenario.

Document the distinction between observed condition, functional test evidence and engineering adequacy. This prevents the inspection report from promising more than was actually examined.

## Q23 — Safe access and abnormal findings

Plan exposure before approaching piping. Identify pressure, temperature, toxicity, flammability, stored energy, traffic, height and access hazards. Coordinate with operations and follow the site's permit, isolation and atmospheric-testing arrangements. Do not loosen insulation, scrape an actively leaking area or manipulate a valve to make inspection easier without the appropriate authorization and controls.

If a leak, unexpected deformation, severe vibration or suspected imminent failure is discovered, move to a safe position and initiate the site's response. Communicate asset identity, location, observed condition, exposure concerns and what has not been established. Do not delay urgent reporting to finish a complete dimensional survey. A safe incomplete report is preferable to obtaining a final measurement inside an uncontrolled hazard.

**Worked case.** During an external walkdown, an inspector sees mist near a hot hydrocarbon line. The wind carries it toward an access route. The inspector withdraws from the potential release, warns through the site's designated channel and reports the location and observation. They do not approach to smell the fluid or remove insulation to find the source.

**Changed condition.** Operations says the mist is probably steam. That hypothesis should be evaluated through the site's safe response process. It does not require the inspector to personally test the theory by entering the release area. Record what was observed without asserting a substance identity that was not established.

For planned intrusive work, a zero gauge alone is not proof of isolation. Apply the approved isolation and verification process, including trapped energy and the possibility of blocked sensing paths. This course's mockup must be physically incapable of receiving pressure during learner handling.

## Q24 — Issue a decision others can safely use

A useful report makes the reasoning traceable. State the asset and boundary, service basis, methods, coverage, results, limitations, calculations and dispositions. Separate measured facts from inferred mechanisms and engineering decisions. Include references and revisions used, photographs tied to locations, uncertainty where material, responsible reviewers and dates.

Use precise status language: examined and acceptable within the stated criteria; partially examined with unresolved coverage; repair required; engineering assessment required; or not evaluated. Avoid a global “passed” when important components or failure modes were outside scope. The person receiving the report should know what they can rely on and what still requires action.

**Worked closeout.** A repaired spool has satisfactory final examinations and test evidence, but the support change remains unapproved. The pressure-boundary repair can be documented as completed while overall return-to-service approval remains withheld pending the support disposition. Closing one item does not close the system.

**Changed condition.** The support approval arrives after the report is issued. Add a controlled revision linking the approval and any updated restrictions; preserve the original status and revision history. Do not rewrite the historical report as though the approval existed earlier.

Build follow-up around specific triggers: due dates, operating-window excursions, process changes, repair expiry and new indications. Give each action an accountable owner. A course credential records assessed learning; continuing competent practice requires supervision, current references, experience and reassessment when tasks or conditions change.

# VUC036 application casebook

All records below are fictional and supplied for training. These are worked learning cases, not final-examination questions. For each case, first write (1) the decision, (2) evidence and reasoning, and (3) the next controlled action. Score each 0–2: pass 5/6 with no unsafe action, fabricated evidence or unsupported release. Read the explanation after attempting the case. Reassess a missed case using its changed condition and retain both attempts.

## M01 — Amine cracking and the wrong inspection question

An amine-service carbon-steel branch has stable thickness history. A surface examination near a highly restrained weld reveals a linear indication. The planner proposes another thickness reading to clear the indication.

**Instruction and worked response:** distinguish cracking from wall loss. The amine environment and stressed weld make environmental cracking a hypothesis for specialist evaluation. Thickness repetition may confirm wall dimensions but does not characterize a crack. Recover service and heat-treatment history, map the indication, select qualified crack-sensitive examination and refer the finding for disposition. Do not assert a mechanism solely from the fluid name.

**Changed condition:** the indication proves to be a surface scratch. Resolve that indication with evidence, but retain the mechanism review and appropriate future coverage; a disproved individual crack does not prove permanent immunity.

## M02 — Atmospheric attack and a hidden contact

A painted outdoor line is sound at accessible spots. Water collects where a shoe contacts a corroded support. The support cannot be seen from below.

**Worked response:** inspect both the pressure boundary and support function. The visible pipe result cannot establish the hidden interface or load path. Plan safe access and suitable examination, recording the unexamined region. Restore coating only after documenting the condition and addressing deterioration. Do not jack the live line to obtain access without an approved lifting/support plan.

**Changed condition:** the pipe is sound but a support weld is cracked. Route the load-path defect for engineering disposition rather than closing the record as acceptable thickness.

## M03 — Condensate history versus today's sample

A condensate return has pits at low points after repeated shutdowns. Today's treatment sample is within the site's limits.

**Worked response:** today's sample does not characterize the complete exposure period. Review oxygen ingress, treatment interruptions, deposits and layup conditions. Measure pit depth and distribution with an appropriate technique. Establish whether the data support general or local evaluation. Treatment correction may reduce future attack but does not restore existing wall.

**Changed condition:** the lowest point was omitted because it is difficult to reach. Document that coverage gap and arrange suitable examination; do not average accessible readings and call the low point represented.

## M04 — Caustic concentration and metal loss

A caustic line shows local thinning near a heated zone. Bulk concentration records are normal, but heat tracing was recently modified.

**Worked response:** investigate local concentration and temperature rather than relying only on bulk samples. Map the affected area and review tracing configuration, operating periods and materials. Use actual metal-loss data for assessment and refer the process cause for correction. This case concerns wall loss; do not assume it rules out cracking elsewhere.

**Changed condition:** a crack-like indication appears near a weld. Open a separate crack characterization and stress/environment review instead of treating it as another thickness point.

## M05 — Caustic cracking and repair recurrence

A caustic-service welded connection has been repaired twice at the same location. The latest proposal repeats the previous weld without reviewing the environment or residual stress.

**Worked response:** investigate the recurrence before accepting another identical repair. Recover prior examination, weld and heat-treatment records, characterize the current indication and review material/environment/stress conditions with the responsible specialist. A repair that removes a visible crack may leave the conditions that caused it.

**Changed condition:** the repair passes surface examination immediately afterwards. That is relevant closeout evidence, but it does not establish that the recurrence mechanism has been controlled. Record both findings separately.

## M06 — Chloride cracking beneath insulation

An insulated austenitic stainless line has wet jacketing and branching surface indications near a weld. The team says stainless cannot corrode.

**Worked response:** stainless is not immune to all environments. Investigate chloride exposure, temperature and tensile stress, using suitable crack-sensitive examination and specialist interpretation. Map wetting and affected locations; do not substitute a wall-loss calculation for crack assessment. Evaluate insulation/weather-barrier restoration as part of future prevention.

**Changed condition:** a neighboring carbon-steel line has broad external thinning. The same wet environment can produce a different damage form. Select methods and assessments for each material and observed condition rather than assigning one mechanism to the entire rack.

## M07 — CUI sampling bias

Three windows were opened where insulation was easiest to remove; all are sound. Damaged weather seals near two branches remain unexamined.

**Worked response:** the sample is biased away from the suspect locations. Document that limitation and target the water-entry and retention areas. A screening method can help define follow-up, but its detection limits and blind zones must remain visible in the report.

**Changed condition:** one window is thin and two are sound. Preserve the thin result and map its extent. An arithmetic average cannot turn a localized integrity concern into an acceptable circuit.

## M08 — Erosion-corrosion at a change of direction

A two-phase stream has stable straight-pipe readings but a downstream elbow outlet is locally thin. Flow increased after a production change.

**Worked response:** relate the damage location to flow and geometry; examine comparable susceptible locations and recover the change history. Do not extrapolate the straight-pipe trend into the elbow. Assess local geometry and remaining section using an appropriate engineering method, and address the changed operating conditions.

**Changed condition:** a proposed replacement has a thicker wall but identical geometry and service. Additional metal may change the time to damage but does not by itself eliminate the mechanism. Establish its design and inspection basis rather than claiming a permanent cure.

## M09 — Galvanic contact and the electrolyte

A dissimilar-metal connection remains wet under a leaking roof. Local attack is concentrated on one side of the connection.

**Worked response:** investigate the metal pairing, electrical contact and electrolyte along with competing explanations. Map the affected material and section. Correcting the water source may help control exposure, but existing loss still needs assessment. Do not install an improvised insulating joint without reviewing pressure, electrical bonding and process requirements.

**Changed condition:** the roof is repaired. Close the water-source action after verification, but keep the integrity finding open until the metal condition and disposition are resolved.

## M10 — HCl and a cold local region

A hot stream is described as dry. Corrosion appears near a cool branch where condensation is plausible.

**Worked response:** distinguish bulk conditions from the local environment. Review temperature profiles, water and chloride sources, startup/shutdown and deposit history. Examine the vulnerable region and establish material loss. A dry bulk-stream description cannot dismiss a locally wet acidic condition.

**Changed condition:** the branch is warmed, and current samples improve. Verify the control but retain assessment of accumulated damage and monitor whether the correction remains effective during transient operation.

## M11 — Vibration fatigue at small-bore piping

A heavy instrument assembly is attached to a small branch near a reciprocating machine. A crack appears at its weld, although thickness is nominal.

**Worked response:** assess cyclic loading and the attachment arrangement as well as the crack. Notify the appropriate operating/engineering authority and avoid touching the moving assembly. The remedy must address the loading or susceptibility, not merely restore nominal thickness. Select suitable crack examination and document similar locations for review.

**Changed condition:** an improvised brace stops visible motion. It may introduce other loads or transfer the problem. Obtain an engineered support disposition and evidence of the repaired condition before closing the finding.

## M12 — MIC is a hypothesis, not a photograph diagnosis

A rarely used water branch has deposits and deep pits. A photograph is labeled “MIC confirmed,” but no supporting investigation exists.

**Worked response:** microbial influence is plausible, not established by pit appearance alone. Review stagnation, treatment and sampling history; preserve useful samples through the qualified investigation process; map loss and compare competing mechanisms. The structural assessment must proceed on the actual damage while the cause is investigated.

**Changed condition:** a microbial sample is negative after cleaning. That sample does not reconstruct the earlier condition. Record its timing and limits; do not erase the observed damage or prior uncertainty.

## M13 — Soil corrosion and the transition gap

A buried line's exposed riser is well coated. Corrosion is found immediately below grade at a damaged coating termination.

**Worked response:** examine the transition and the extent of affected buried pipe under an approved excavation/access plan. Review coating and protective-system information where applicable. Good exposed coating is not evidence for an unexamined underground surface. Preserve location, depth, dimensions and photographs before restoration.

**Changed condition:** the protective-system readings are within their specified range. Those data support the system review but do not remove existing metal loss. Evaluate the actual defect and repair/restoration needs.

## M14 — Acidic sour water and changing chemistry

A carbon-steel sour-water line experiences intermittent chemistry changes and localized thinning at a low point. An old rate is used to forecast a long life.

**Worked response:** recover the chemistry and operating history, identify the affected material and local environment, and obtain comparable measurements. **Changed condition:** a new sample has a different pH and contaminant profile. Update the mechanism review rather than assuming the old classification still applies. A course label is not a substitute for process-specific corrosion analysis.

## M15 — Sulfidation and unrepresentative components

An inspection program relies on readings from fittings while straight spools from different material heats have little coverage. The service is susceptible to high-temperature sulfur-related attack.

**Worked response:** review component-level metallurgy and coverage. Establish a defensible examination/material-verification plan and consider a more resistant design through the engineering process. CSB's Chevron investigation illustrates why representative material knowledge matters; this fictional case is not a reconstruction of that incident.

**Changed condition:** one susceptible spool is replaced. Determine whether other components share the vulnerability. Closing the replacement work order does not close the system-wide mechanism issue.

## W01 — Scope, classification and an inspection interval

Identify the actual basis for a circuit's class rather than choosing the class with the longest interval. Record the owner/inspector responsibilities and conditions attached to an exception before applying it.

**Worked synthetic decision:** the supplied exercise policy uses half-life with a 4 year cap. Life is 10 years, giving 4 years. The external inspection is independently due in 18months. Scheduling thickness monitoring at 4 years cannot close the external task. A proposed deferral needs the applicable assessment and authorization, with the original due date preserved.

**Actual lookup task:** use D01, including its LPG service, occupied-area consequence, omitted bypass and dated records. **Changed exercise and explanation:** a valid risk-based assessment establishes a different plan. Verify its scope, current inputs and approval; do not mix selected pieces of the RBI and non-RBI paths merely to obtain the longest date. Retain the complete adopted basis.

## W02 — Straight-pipe factors and material identity

## W03 — Flange rating, dimensions and blank geometry

## W04 — Toughness, preheat and heat-treatment applicability

Audience: inspection trainees and personnel responsible for coordinating metallic process-piping integrity work. Use qualified NDE, welding, corrosion or engineering specialists for work outside the instructor's competence. # VUC036 formative checks

## Q01-L1

A field tie-in is absent from the current drawing. What must change in the inspection record?

**Explained answer:** Record the boundary discrepancy and obtain the tie-in design, construction and baseline evidence.

**Why and remediation:** A verified old boundary does not cover an undocumented addition. Revisit Q01 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q01-L2

Can an examiner independently approve a rerating because their measurements are accurate?

**Explained answer:** Only with the separately established authority and required engineering process; measurements alone do not grant it.

**Why and remediation:** Examination evidence and disposition authority are distinct. Revisit Q01 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q02-L1

Does a matching flange bolt pattern establish its rating?

**Explained answer:** No; verify material, class, temperature rating, dimensions and assembly compatibility.

**Why and remediation:** Fit is not pressure-temperature suitability. Revisit Q02 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q02-L2

Why record the viewing direction for clock-position measurements?

**Explained answer:** So another examiner can reproduce the location.

**Why and remediation:** An ambiguous orientation can corrupt a thickness trend. Revisit Q02 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q03-L1

What makes a corrosion circuit useful?

**Explained answer:** A defensible common service/material/damage basis with local exceptions identified.

**Why and remediation:** Geographic closeness alone does not establish comparable deterioration. Revisit Q03 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q03-L2

Can six sound straight-pipe points clear an unexamined elbow?

**Explained answer:** No; examine the elbow using a mechanism-appropriate plan.

**Why and remediation:** Coverage must address the susceptible geometry. Revisit Q03 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q04-L1

Does an intermittently used branch necessarily avoid deadleg behavior?

**Explained answer:** No; review stagnant periods and actual configuration.

**Why and remediation:** Occasional flow does not eliminate accumulated local exposure. Revisit Q04 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q04-L2

What changes when an injection quill is shortened?

**Explained answer:** Review mixing/impingement conditions and the inspection boundary.

**Why and remediation:** The earlier plan may no longer represent the modified exposure. Revisit Q04 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q05-L1

What does a sound insulation inspection window prove?

**Why and remediation:** Unexamined water-entry/contact locations may differ. Revisit Q05 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q05-L2

Why verify insulation restoration after examination?

**Explained answer:** To prevent new water entry and preserve protection.

**Why and remediation:** Inspection access can create a future damage location. Revisit Q05 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q06-L1

Why does a wall-loss forecast not clear a crack?

**Why and remediation:** The model does not represent the relevant failure mode. Revisit Q06 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q06-L2

Does disappearance of vibration after shutdown prove the branch is undamaged?

**Explained answer:** No; evaluate accumulated damage and restart loading.

**Why and remediation:** Stopping excitation is not a crack examination. Revisit Q06 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

Two 4 and 12 mm blocks read 4.5 and 12.5. What pattern is present?

## Q07-L2

**Why and remediation:** Questionable data cannot support an unqualified integrity decision. Revisit Q07 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q08-L1

For 8.0 to 7.0 mm over six years, what is the long-term rate?

**Why and remediation:** Loss is divided by actual elapsed time at a comparable point. Revisit Q08 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

**Why and remediation:** The calculation is not a release authorization. Revisit Q08 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q09-L1

An exercise uses half-life capped at five years; life is eight. What interval results?

**Explained answer:** Four years before considering more restrictive conditions.

**Why and remediation:** A maximum is not an automatic recommendation. Revisit Q09 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q09-L2

Does resetting an overdue date create a valid deferral?

**Explained answer:** No; retain the original date and documented authorized assessment.

**Why and remediation:** A schedule edit is not a risk decision. Revisit Q09 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q10-L1

Where is manufacturing minus tolerance applied when choosing new nominal pipe?

**Explained answer:** To ensure the ordered nominal produces at least the required minimum.

**Why and remediation:** Do not apply a nominal tolerance twice to verified actual in-service thickness. Revisit Q10 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q10-L2

Can the straight-pipe equation qualify a branch reinforcement?

**Explained answer:** Not by itself; the configuration needs its applicable design basis.

**Why and remediation:** Equation validity is geometry- and load-dependent. Revisit Q10 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q11-L1

Does RT of a girth weld establish a longitudinal seam quality factor?

**Explained answer:** No; obtain evidence applicable to the longitudinal seam and its manufacturing/qualification basis.

**Why and remediation:** Different joints have different evidentiary roles. Revisit Q11 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q11-L2

Why assess connected components during rerating?

**Explained answer:** The system may be limited by a component other than the calculated straight pipe.

**Why and remediation:** A local calculation cannot raise another component rating. Revisit Q11 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q12-L1

Is class 150 a universal 150 psi rating?

**Explained answer:** No; use material and temperature-specific rating information.

**Why and remediation:** Class is a designation, not a constant pressure. Revisit Q12 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q12-L2

Can a smooth resurfaced flange be accepted without dimensions?

**Explained answer:** No; verify remaining dimensions and approved repair/assembly criteria.

**Why and remediation:** Surface appearance does not establish structural adequacy. Revisit Q12 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q13-L1

What does αLΔT estimate in the simple exercise?

**Explained answer:** Unrestrained thermal length change under the supplied constant-coefficient assumption.

**Why and remediation:** It is not a flexibility or nozzle-load acceptance calculation. Revisit Q13 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q13-L2

Why review supports for a water test of a gas line?

**Explained answer:** The added fluid weight may change support loads.

**Why and remediation:** Pressure adequacy does not establish load-path adequacy. Revisit Q13 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q14-L1

An average impact result passes but one specimen is below the supplied individual minimum. Accept?

**Explained answer:** No; both criteria apply.

**Why and remediation:** A mean can conceal an unacceptable individual result. Revisit Q14 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q14-L2

Does a warm-temperature impact test prove a colder specified requirement?

**Explained answer:** Not by itself.

**Why and remediation:** The test condition must match the applicable requirement or authorized technical basis. Revisit Q14 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q15-L1

Which record documents actual procedure-qualification test conditions?

**Explained answer:** The PQR.

**Why and remediation:** The WPS provides production instructions; it is not the same record. Revisit Q15 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q15-L2

Does a process-qualified WPS establish every welder is qualified?

**Explained answer:** No; review personnel qualification and continuity for the production work.

**Why and remediation:** Procedure and performance qualification are distinct. Revisit Q15 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q16-L1

What must be established before applying an acceptance criterion to NDE results?

**Explained answer:** That the technique, personnel, calibration and coverage produced valid evidence.

**Why and remediation:** Invalid examination cannot support a favorable acceptance result. Revisit Q16 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q16-L2

Why does repair of one sampled defect not necessarily close the lot?

**Explained answer:** The applicable sampling expansion remains a separate requirement.

**Why and remediation:** Repair and population confidence are different issues. Revisit Q16 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q17-L1

Does an IQI meeting requirements mean the weld has no rejectable flaw?

**Explained answer:** No; it supports image quality, while discontinuities require separate interpretation.

**Why and remediation:** Technique validity and acceptance are distinct. Revisit Q17 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q17-L2

Can a stable pressure gauge replace required bubble examination?

**Explained answer:** No.

**Why and remediation:** Small leakage may not produce a visible pressure change over the observation period. Revisit Q17 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q18-L1

Can an alloy-family display establish an unmeasured carbon limit?

**Explained answer:** No.

**Why and remediation:** The method must measure or otherwise validly establish the required characteristic. Revisit Q18 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q18-L2

What happens to identity when a marked parent pipe is cut?

**Explained answer:** Maintain traceability of each relevant piece through controlled marking/records.

**Why and remediation:** Unmarked offcuts cannot inherit identity by assumption. Revisit Q18 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q19-L1

Does a clamp that stops leakage necessarily restore axial capacity?

**Explained answer:** No; establish the design load path and capability.

**Why and remediation:** Leak sealing and structural restraint are different functions. Revisit Q19 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q19-L2

What makes a temporary repair manageable?

**Explained answer:** An approved envelope, monitoring, accountable owner and expiry/replacement trigger.

**Why and remediation:** Temporary must not mean forgotten. Revisit Q19 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q20-L1

An overlay passes deposited thickness but fails finished thickness. Which governs the stated final requirement?

**Explained answer:** The finished condition.

**Why and remediation:** Machining can remove required qualified material. Revisit Q20 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q20-L2

Why record excavation dimensions before welding?

**Explained answer:** They establish the actual substrate and whether the approved repair still applies.

**Why and remediation:** Filling the area cannot erase a changed-condition hold. Revisit Q20 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q21-L1

Why account for elevation in hydrostatic testing?

**Explained answer:** Local pressure varies with liquid head.

**Why and remediation:** A reference gauge can conceal excessive pressure lower in the boundary. Revisit Q21 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q21-L2

Why is adding gas volume a test-plan change?

**Explained answer:** It changes the boundary and stored-energy hazard.

**Why and remediation:** Unchanged pressure does not mean unchanged risk. Revisit Q21 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q22-L1

Does a recent bench certificate prove installed relief protection is available?

**Explained answer:** No; verify the installed path and applicable system conditions.

**Why and remediation:** Isolation or restrictions can defeat a tested device. Revisit Q22 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q22-L2

Can exterior appearance prove a critical check valve closes correctly?

**Explained answer:** No; appropriate functional/maintenance evidence is needed.

**Why and remediation:** Its safety function is internal and scenario-dependent. Revisit Q22 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q23-L1

Should an inspector approach a suspected release to identify it by smell?

**Explained answer:** No; withdraw and use the site response process.

**Why and remediation:** Uncontrolled exposure is not a diagnostic technique. Revisit Q23 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q23-L2

Does a zero gauge prove no trapped pressure?

**Explained answer:** No; use the approved isolation and verification process.

**Why and remediation:** Blocked sensing paths or trapped volumes can invalidate the inference. Revisit Q23 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q24-L1

How should an unexamined region appear in the final report?

**Explained answer:** Explicitly identified with limitations and required follow-up.

**Why and remediation:** A global pass would overstate the evidence. Revisit Q24 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.

## Q24-L2

How should a late approval be added?

**Explained answer:** Through a controlled revision retaining original history.

**Why and remediation:** Do not backdate the earlier status. Revisit Q24 and explain the corrected assumption in your own words.
