# VUC070 — Digger Derrick Operation

## Scope and learning outcome

This original VNDRLY/U program develops safe setup, lifting, augering, pole handling and securing decisions for a digger derrick. The operating credential requires actual equipment performance, including digging and pole handling, on the recorded machine and tasks. It is not CCO certification, qualified electrical-worker status, a driving license, a platform endorsement or authority to design a pole foundation or anchor. Knowledge-only completion cannot be represented as actual operation.

Use actual manufacturer instructions, configuration charts, engineering/job specifications and applicable current requirements. D70 examples are fictional classroom data, not actual crane ratings, soil approvals, electrical clearances, pole-setting depths or anchor design. The instructor must validate real equipment and site conditions before delivery. Follow chapters with their explained checks and changed applications; stop when essential information is absent instead of filling it with an assumption.

## Q01 — Task scope and responsible roles

A digger derrick may be used in utility work, telecommunications or other construction. Applicable requirements depend on the work, not the machine's name. The construction-crane scope has a conditional digger-derrick exclusion for specified utility/telecommunication activities, tied to compliance with the applicable alternative requirements. It is not a blanket exemption from safety or operator competence. Using the same machine to lift unrelated building components can change the applicable scope.

Clarify operator authority, site control, electrical qualification, rigging and signaling roles, excavation/utility locating responsibility and who approves the actual lift/digging plan. An operator training record does not make a person qualified to work on energized conductors. Review equipment familiarization and readiness before each assignment. Stop unsafe work and communicate the specific unresolved condition rather than relying on a supervisor's urgency.

Worked case: a utility crew's digger derrick is asked to set a steel building beam. Do not assume its utility-work treatment applies to that new task. Establish applicable requirements and qualifications before use. Changed: the work returns to a pole carrying electric lines. The task facts change, but applicable utility protections and competent operation still must be established.

## Q02 — Site access, job briefing and public protection

The job brief connects actual work to hazards and controls: site entry/exit, traffic, pedestrians, overhead obstacles, underground utilities, support conditions, load/auger path, weather and emergency response. Include the deployed boom, suspended load, pole length and rotating auger in the envelope. A truck that fits down a road may not have room to deploy safely. Keep unauthorized people outside the operating and fall zones.

Plan separate safe positions for the operator, signaler and ground crew. Do not use a worker as a barrier or allow traffic beneath a suspended pole. Traffic control must follow the applicable approved arrangement, not a few cones placed without a plan. Identify escape/access routes and how to stop work if a member of the public enters. Review changes in conditions at the actual site rather than treating yesterday's briefing as permanent approval.

Worked: a planned pole swing crosses an open pedestrian route. Hold until the route or lift is properly controlled. Changed: the sidewalk is closed but the pole tail extends into vehicle traffic. Reassess the whole load envelope, not just the pole tip or hook.

## Q03 — Ground, outriggers and level

Obtain the required support information, including known voids, tanks, utilities, fill, slopes and excavation edges. Surface appearance is not a bearing-capacity test. Use the actual stabilizer/outrigger configuration and ratings, suitable supporting materials and specified level tolerance. Do not divide truck weight equally among legs to estimate maximum reaction; boom position and load change reactions substantially.

Deploy legs with required visibility/exclusion so no one is trapped. Verify permissible partial/no-outrigger conditions only from actual governing instructions and requirements; inability to extend a leg does not authorize full-rating operation. Mats must provide suitable effective support and structural strength. Rain, thaw, vibration or new digging can change conditions after setup. If support settles, stop safely and obtain an appropriate recovery/support review, not an improvised leveling maneuver under load.

Worked D70 paper example: supplied maximum leg reaction 180 kN and allowable pressure 300 kPa require ideal area 0.60 m². Nominal 0.8 × 0.9 m gives 0.72 m² and 250 kPa average. That comparison does not prove real soil or mat adequacy. Changed: effective area is only 0.45 m²; pressure becomes 400 kPa and fails the supplied basis.

## Q04 — Underground facilities and excavation readiness

Before augering, establish the excavation authorization, current utility-location information and actual work boundaries through the applicable owner/locate process. Compare drawings, markings and site evidence. No mark is proof of no utility, and a locate request is not the same as completed positive verification. As work approaches an estimated installation, use the required safe acceptable means to establish its exact location. Do not invent a universal tolerance zone or waiting period across jurisdictions.

Confirm approved hole location, diameter/depth and support implications, and protect existing installations and the open hole. The operator does not design the pole foundation from a remembered depth formula. Keep people out of an augered hole; this course does not authorize excavation entry. Establish spoil placement and barriers so material cannot roll back or people fall in. Unexpected material, utility evidence, obstruction or changing ground conditions requires a stop and review.

Worked: paint marks conflict with a drawing and a service pedestal suggests a crossing at the proposed hole. Hold for reconciliation and proper locating/exposure, not a test auger. Changed: marks are reconciled but the requested hole is moved one metre. Confirm that the new location is covered and safe before digging; authorization for the old point is not automatically transferable.

## Q05 — Equipment identity and pre-operation inspection

Identify the actual model, boom sections, turret, winch/load line, auger drive/stowage, pole guide, outriggers, controls, PTO, attachments and any platform/insulated components. Match manuals, placards and chart revision. Inspect critical structure/retention, cylinders/hoses, line/hooks/rigging, controls, safety devices, aids, warnings and transport locks using the actual procedure. Report inaccessible checks rather than climbing or reaching into hazardous areas.

A shift inspection, periodic inspection and required post-repair or comprehensive inspection serve different purposes. A pre-use check cannot replace missing required inspection evidence. Faults such as unintended rotation, drift, damaged structural components or hydraulic leakage require appropriate removal from operation and qualified correction. Do not hand-check a pressure leak or repair a safety circuit by improvisation. Suspected pressure-injection injury requires immediate professional medical response, not waiting for visible swelling.

Worked: a boom retaining component is absent while the control system appears normal. The missing structural retention is independently unsafe. Hold/report and obtain qualified correction/release. Changed: the part is replaced but a required functional/inspection release is not documented. Replacement alone does not establish readiness.

## Q06 — Load charts, range and work areas

Choose the actual chart for boom length/configuration, outrigger state, work area, attachment and reeving. The range diagram shows geometry, not capacity by itself. Match actual radius and height without substituting boom length for radius. Read notes identifying structural versus stability limits and what capacity includes. A crane can be structurally limited even when it appears stable; visible lack of tipping does not prove a lift is permissible.

D70 fictional full-support rear-sector gross chart at radii 3/5/7/9 m: 5, 000/3, 600/2, 400/1, 600 kg. Fictional restricted-side values are 3, 500/2, 500/1, 600/1, 000 kg. Use the next larger listed radius for intermediate values and no extrapolation. Gross includes all listed suspended components; an example may separately supply an additional attachment deduction. These values are paper data only, not a Terex or other manufacturer's chart.

Worked: payload 1, 800 kg, rigging 150 and block/other listed accessories 100 total 2, 050. At 6 m use 7 m: rear margin 350, restricted-side deficiency 450. A path that crosses into the restricted side fails even though pickup passes. Changed: rear radius reaches 8 m, selecting 1, 600 capacity and failing by 450 as well. Replan rather than interpolate a favorable rating.

## Q07 — Load weight, rigging and center of gravity

Determine actual load weight, retained contents, hardware and required deductions. Pole weight depends on actual material, length, treatment, moisture and attachments; do not apply a generic weight to every pole of similar appearance. Use approved lifting points, compatible rated rigging and edge protection. Position the hook to control the center of gravity and prevent unexpected tipping or rotation as a long load leaves its support.

All components must be suitable in their actual configuration. A lifting beam rating cannot compensate for an unidentified sling. For an ideal symmetric two-leg classroom lift, leg tension equals W/(2 sin theta), theta above horizontal; real unequal geometry requires qualified analysis. Keep people clear of tensioning, tail swing and landing pinch zones. Do not ask someone to stabilize a heavy shifting pole by bodily force.

Worked D70: an ideal 2, 000 kg load at 30 degrees produces 2, 000 kg-force equivalent per leg. A half-load estimate of 1, 000 omits angle. Changed: a pole has an attached transformer near one end. The center of gravity and actual component weights must be established before reusing the original sling location.

## Q08 — Load line, winch and reeving

Digger derricks may use wire or synthetic load line. Identify the exact approved construction, capacity, terminations, care and removal criteria. Synthetic line is not simply wire rope with different color; heat, cuts, contamination and abrasion require its own inspection and handling. Follow the actual rope's instructions for lubrication or prohibition of inappropriate products. Do not substitute a same-diameter line without approved suitability.

Confirm safe line pull, parts of line, drum/spooling condition, hook/block capacity and permitted travel. Boom extension can change line geometry and two-block clearance. Avoid slack, crossed wraps and twisting. Required prevention devices are checked safely under the instructions, never bypassed to reach farther. Rope replacement, reeving changes and service adjustments require the appropriate competent procedure and verification.

Worked D70 comparison: permitted ideal line pull 1, 200 kg with two parts yields 2, 400 kg, but the applicable chart permits 2, 000. The chart remains the lower ceiling before other restrictions. Changed: one part is used; the line comparison becomes 1, 200, so the original plan is invalid. Neither multiplication establishes actual equipment efficiency or permission without the manufacturer data.

## Q09 — Signals and controlled lifting

Agree and test standard hand/voice signals, crane identity and directing responsibility. Ensure signal personnel have the required competence/qualification for their role. A special signal must be understood before use. Stop on conflicting, unclear or lost required communication; respond to emergency stop from anyone. Blind work needs reliable observation of load and landing through the approved signaling arrangement.

Perform the prescribed initial lift/check, then coordinate hoist, boom, telescope and swing smoothly. Avoid side loading, dragging, abrupt reversal and impact. Maintain the whole load and boom envelope and protect people in the fall/swing zone. Do not rely on a horn to make a person beneath a load safe. If a load twists or swings, follow trained safe recovery and land when appropriate; do not spin or jerk it experimentally.

Worked: the hook is clear but the long pole tail moves toward a worker outside the operator's view. Stop and restore the full-path protection/signaling. Changed: the worker leaves but the signal radio fails. That separate stop condition remains; do not finish the last command from memory.

## Q10 — Auger deployment, digging and spoil

Verify approved auger type/diameter, teeth/pilot condition, retention, drive and stowage against the actual job and machine. Deploy only with a protected area and the manufacturer's sequence. A rotating auger creates entanglement, crushing and thrown-material hazards. Keep people, clothing, tag lines and tools out of its rotating/working zone. Never have a helper hand-guide or clean a moving auger.

Use the approved drilling alignment, feed and rotational controls; excess force or a jam is not solved by uncontrolled boom movement. Stop and use the authorized isolation/recovery process before clearing an obstruction. Withdraw and handle spoil under the prescribed procedure with people clear. Measure hole condition/depth by a safe method without entry or reaching beside moving equipment. Stop on unexpected obstruction, facility evidence, unstable ground or an unauthorized location/depth change.

Worked: the auger binds and a helper reaches for debris while the drive is enabled. Stop and isolate under the actual safe clearing procedure; no hand clearing of rotating/energized equipment. Changed: the auger is stopped but hydraulic energy remains available. A stopped rotation alone does not establish isolation.

## Q11 — Pole pickup and setting

Establish actual pole weight/condition, rigging point, center of gravity and permitted load path. Inspect for damage or attachments that change behavior. Control both ends during pickup with the qualified rigging/signaling plan and exclusion zone. Keep personnel away from the butt, tail swing and pinch zones. Raise and position smoothly while maintaining actual capacity and clearances throughout the changing orientation.

Pole guides assist alignment and control; they are not substitutes for load-supporting rigging. Do not clamp them to carry pole weight or use them as a grapple. Keep projections from snagging the guide and apply the manufacturer's adjustment method. A hole is not ready merely because it exists: verify the approved location, geometry, conditions and setting/support plan. Release load support only after the authorized stabilization/backfill/support requirements are satisfied by the responsible crew.

Worked: a pole rests in the guide but the load line goes slack before the pole is securely supported. That transfers unintended weight to the guide. Stop safely and restore the approved support/control arrangement. Changed: the line supports the pole but a protruding bolt catches the guide. Resolve the interference through the approved method without forcing motion.

## Q12 — Pole extraction and stuck loads

An embedded pole presents unknown breakout forces in addition to its free weight. Do not rock it loose with the boom or use the load line to forcibly extract it. Use the appropriate approved pole-puller or extraction method to break it free, with the pole controlled and personnel protected. The crane's role and load condition must be established before lifting the released pole.

Attachments, deterioration, buried obstructions and nearby conductors can complicate removal. The pole can release suddenly or fail structurally. Plan restraint, exclusion, rigging and safe support before applying the authorized extraction system. A pole guide is not an extraction clamp. Other stuck or submerged loads likewise require qualified planning; a low nominal dry weight does not establish release force or suitability.

Worked: an operator sees no movement at a high indicated load and proposes additional boom force. Stop; the line is being used to discover unknown resistance. Changed: the pole has been released by an approved puller but may be rotten. Verify condition and safe handling before lifting; successful release does not establish structural integrity.

## Q13 — Screw anchors and accessory limits

Screw-anchor installation requires an approved anchor system, adaptor, torque/drive capability and engineered job requirements. The operator follows the specified installation process and records actual values; they do not invent an anchor capacity from turns, pressure or depth. Hydraulic pressure is not automatically installation torque without the valid equipment relationship and instrumentation. Torque is not automatically soil holding capacity without the applicable approved design basis.

Verify compatibility and retention of adaptor/anchor components and keep people clear of rotating equipment and reaction hazards. Stop on unexpected resistance, misalignment, damaged components or utility uncertainty. Do not use improvised extensions, drive couplings or body force to guide the rotating assembly. This package provides original planning/simulator exercises for anchor decisions; an actual anchor-installation endorsement requires separately validated actual equipment and a dedicated observed task, not a generic digging pass.

Worked D70: a fictional specification requires recorded installation torque 6–8 kN·m, and measured value is 5.5. It does not meet that assigned requirement. The operator reports and seeks the authorized disposition; they do not declare a capacity. Changed: reading is 7, but the instrument calibration is invalid. The apparent passing number is not valid acceptance evidence.

## Q14 — Electrical qualification and dielectric limits

Distinguish insulated and non-insulated parts and verify the actual equipment's declared insulation/test/maintenance condition. A fiberglass-looking component is not proof that the whole truck, attachment, pole or load line is electrically safe. Contamination, damage and conductive paths can defeat assumptions. This course does not qualify a learner for energized work or select their minimum approach distance.

For the applicable utility work, the employer establishes required approach distances and protection through qualified electrical personnel and applicable rules. Grounding, bonding, insulating cover and barricading have different purposes and limitations; grounding alone does not make touching the truck safe. Control touch/step exposure and ensure required observation/clearance measures. Nonutility construction has its applicable electrical requirements; do not interchange methods casually. All course electrical work is tabletop or genuinely de-energized training equipment under verified conditions.

Worked: a truck is grounded and a worker assumes they can hold its metal body while a pole approaches an energized line. Grounding does not remove possible touch/step potential. Stop and obtain the qualified electrical work plan. Changed: a platform liner is present but its condition/test status is unknown. Do not infer dielectric protection or energized-work permission.

## Q15 — Platforms, phase lifting and multiple equipment

Personnel-platform work requires an approved system, capacity/configuration, access, fall protection, emergency lowering/rescue and qualified operation under applicable requirements. A platform fitted to a boom is not permission to lift people during a normal material-handling exercise. Combined use can change load limits and restrictions. Never mix a platform and material lift without the manufacturer's permitted arrangement and the applicable plan.

Phase-lifting equipment is specialized electrical work with dedicated devices, insulation and procedures. Recognition training is not authorization to move energized conductors. Multiple-digger lifts also require qualified coordinated planning: actual load share, changing geometry, rigging, supporting structures, control and a responsible leader. Equal sharing cannot be assumed from equal-looking booms. Use tabletop/simulator cases here unless a separately approved specialty training plan and qualified personnel establish actual work.

Worked D70: a 4, 000 kg load assumed to split equally between two 2, 500 kg systems actually transfers 70% to one side. That side receives 2, 800 kg before accessories and exceeds its stated rating by 300. Changed: load sharing is corrected but platform compatibility is absent. Resolving one specialty issue does not authorize another.

## Q16 — Devices, weather and changing stability

Know the required safety devices, operational aids and warning functions on the actual machine. Level indication, hydraulic holding devices, horns, load/angle indication, anti-two-block and outrigger monitoring do not have interchangeable failure rules. Do not invent a workaround; any permitted temporary alternative must come from the applicable requirement and authorized implementation. A failed safety requirement is not cured by an experienced operator watching more carefully.

Weather can affect stability, control and electrical exposure. Use actual limits and monitoring for wind, gusts, ice, snow, visibility and temperature. Ground may soften after setup. Stop for loss of control or support even below a nominal wind limit. Do not bypass overload protection, falsify stabilizer state or use a pressure gauge as an unverified load scale. On a fault, follow the actual permitted recovery procedure and obtain qualified help.

Worked: the outrigger monitor indicates full deployment although one leg is partial. Hold and resolve configuration before relying on any displayed capacity. Changed: configuration is correct but the mat sinks after rain. Actual support must be re-established; a correct display does not measure every ground hazard.

## Q17 — Emergency response, maintenance and records

Plan response for overhead/underground utility contact, loss of stability, control malfunction, twisting/two-block hazard, person entering the fall zone and fire. Protect the area and activate the applicable site/emergency plan. Do not approach potentially energized equipment or an unstable boom to attempt an unplanned rescue. If an underground facility is damaged, stop, withdraw/control exposure as the emergency plan directs and contact the responsible emergency/utility parties; do not bury or conceal damage.

Maintenance requires authorized isolation, support and verification of stored hydraulic, electrical and gravity energy. Operators report accurate symptoms rather than adjusting a valve or brake under load. Keep records of inspections, defects, repair and separate return-to-service checks. An alarm that disappears after restart remains unexplained. Preserve restrictions in handover and do not mark equipment normal until the required authorized evidence exists.

Worked: the auger contacts an unexpected cable and a worker proposes pulling it out to see what it is. Stop and treat the facility as hazardous under the response plan; do not manipulate it. Changed: no visible spark appears. Absence of a spark does not establish safe voltage, pressure or service status.

## Q18 — Stowing, transit and integrated completion

Land/support loads and secure the auger, boom, hooks/lines, attachments, outriggers, tools and materials in the actual prescribed sequence. Verify locks, pins and restraints, correct PTO/control state and permitted travel dimensions. Do not rely on hydraulic pressure to hold a transport position. Pre-travel vehicle/road requirements and loading/unloading a transport trailer need their own appropriate competence and plan; this operating course does not confer road-driving authorization.

Worked D70 integrated case: total suspended load 2, 200 kg at 6 m rear selects 2, 400 capacity, leaving 200 margin. The lift still cannot proceed if utility-location clearance for adjacent augering is incomplete or the line is damaged. Changed: after those issues are resolved, the route shifts to restricted side with 1, 600 capacity, failing by 600. Readiness is reassessed for the actual whole job.

The independent practical requires actual inspection, setup, controlled transfers, auger operation in a verified safe training area, pole handling and stowing. Special electrical, platform, phase-lifting and anchor-installation work remains explicitly outside the ordinary actual-performance award unless separately assessed under a validated specialty program. Do not report a learner or equipment pass merely because this complete material package has been written.

# VUC070 — Instructor, application and assessment workbook

## Preparation and resources

Plan 72 hours: 14 taught instruction/checks, 8 application labs, 28 supervised operating stations, 14 integrated practice/assessment, 4 written final/review and 4 remediation/records. Allow additional learning time as needed and record actual hours. Instructor must be competent/authorized for the digger derrick and observed tasks, with qualified rigging, site/support, excavation and electrical specialists providing their required functions. Do not infer electrical qualification from crane certification or this training.

Provide a suitable inspected digger derrick, actual manuals/charts, approved auger and pole-handling equipment, inspected rated load line/rigging, verified stable loads and nonenergized training poles, validated support/mats/chocks, approved safe digging area with facility clearance and soil conditions, safe supported pole-setting receptacle or engineered practice arrangement, protected landing supports, radios/signals references, barriers, emergency equipment/plans and forms. Actual digging must occur in a verified authorized training medium/site, not beside unknown utilities. Nobody enters an augered hole. Platforms/phase-lifting/energized work and actual anchor installation are not part of the ordinary practical.

Before delivery validate support and maximum reactions, setup/level, chart/reeving, load paths, auger location/diameter/depth, hole/spoil protection, pole condition and approved support/setting plan. Use generous capacity margin and nonhazardous test loads. Preserve actual equipment protections; faults remain photographs/tabletop/simulator cases. Do not create live two-block, hydraulic failure, utility contact or unstable support. The instructor must not claim this authored plan substitutes for actual engineering/site verification.

Diagnostic: units, chart radius/sector, basic load arithmetic, stop signals, hazard identification and differentiation of pole guide/load rigging. Demonstrate a complete known safe task, practice a changed task with feedback, then assess independently. Learner-facing packets contain all necessary load and configuration data; protected keys contain scoring, not undisclosed information essential to safe decisions.

## I70 original independent practical

Create distinct teaching T and assessment A/B sets. Each set includes two independently verified stable lifting loads, a suitable nonenergized free training pole with known weight/CG and approved rigging, and an authorized drilling point in known safe material. The responsible instructor/site team specifies actual allowed hole diameter, depth and tolerance from the safe training plan, records the auger and instrument IDs, and validates the support/setting arrangement. Hole dimensions are exercise targets, never foundation-design instructions. No excavation entry, live utility, hot work or personnel hoisting occurs.

Validate the machine/manual/chart/configuration, load line/reeving, all actual weights/ratings, support/level, worst path radii/sectors, weather, signs/barriers, signals, emergency plan and hole closure/protection. Use lightweight route markers and landing rectangles at least 0.5 m clear of the test-load footprint on every side, or more if the actual plan requires. Keep the operator/crew outside rotating, fall, pinch and pole-tail zones. No person is an obstacle. Schedule about three hours plus familiarization; scoring does not reward speed.

Independent sequence: identify scope/authority and documents; inspect; survey/brief; verify locating and support; deploy/setup; calculate the entire lift; perform unloaded familiarization and signals; complete two controlled load transfers and a return transfer; conduct one actual approved auger cycle; pick up and place the free pole in the validated support/receptacle; establish stable support and release under the approved sequence; stow boom/auger/line/legs/PTO and complete handover.

All three load transfers require appropriate initial check, correct response to signals, controlled envelope, no side pull/collision/unplanned ground contact or person exposed beneath load, and stable placement inside the designated landing area. The auger cycle requires correct actual location/diameter/depth within the predeclared safe plan, no reach/entanglement exposure, controlled withdrawal/spoil and protected hole; an out-of-tolerance result must be safely corrected and reassessed without hiding the first result. The pole task requires verified rigging/CG, controlled tail/butt, guide used only for its intended purpose and stable supported release. All elements must pass.

Assessor intervention to prevent danger fails independent performance. Safe voluntary pauses are allowed. Equipment/weather interruptions are recorded separately from learner error; restore safe conditions and repeat the complete affected task. A different machine requires fresh configuration/familiarization and a complete integrated attempt. Set B changes loads/path/pole or authorized drilling point, with equivalent safe conditions and renewed plan/calculations. Retain all failed and passed observations; do not award from a diagram alone.

## Written examination and credential

Private Form A/B each contains eighteen original three-decision cases, 108 points. Allow 180 minutes, calculator/coursebook but no key or assistance. Pass at least 87 total and at least 4/6 per domain with no unresolved critical error. This is not the external CCO 75-question examination. Changed reassessment includes original context and new facts; protect both answer keys from learner distribution.

Require all corrected checks, eight labs, all stations, I70 and written final before awarding the ordinary VNDRLY digger-derrick operating training credential. If actual digging, pole handling or other required performance is missing, issue only a knowledge/limited record explicitly stating the unearned elements. The ordinary award excludes energized electrical, actual phase lifting, actual platform work, engineered anchor acceptance and unrestricted multi-equipment lifting. Those require their separate qualifications, authorized programs and observed evidence. Do not interpret material completeness as an awarded learner credential.

Remediation identifies the failed knowledge/action, reteaches with worked examples, requires changed practice and uses Form B/physical set B. Preserve critical-error history and evidence of correction. Accommodations may support access/communication but cannot erase essential actual performance. VNDRLY's one-year training review reminder is separate from external certification terms; reassess earlier for material equipment/task change, incident or unsafe performance.

## Record forms

**Learner/readiness:** identity, diagnostic, prior scope, accommodations, instructor qualifications, actual hours, selected equipment/tasks and restrictions.

**Site/equipment validation:** machine/control/manual/chart, support/reactions/level, locate authorization/current markings/exact training point, hole target/tolerance/protection, auger/pole/load IDs, weights/rigging/line, full radius/sector path, weather/signals/exclusion, emergency response and validator/date.

**Observation:** set/station, actual actions/results, five rubric scores, critical condition, stop/intervention, hole measurement method/results, pole support/release evidence, assessor/date, remediation/retest.

**Defect/release/transit:** actual symptom/time/location, safe state/restriction, informed party, repair action, separate required verification/release, actual stowed locks/legs/PTO/attachments and incoming acknowledgment.

**Credential:** checks/labs/stations/I70/final, critical-error closure, actual operating scope, explicit exclusions, evidence/version/hash, reviewer/date and review trigger. Do not prefill learner results or actual equipment approval.

# VUC070 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Does the name digger derrick establish a blanket crane-rule exemption?

**Answer:** No. Actual work and the conditions of the applicable exclusion determine scope.

**Reason/remediation:** A change from utility work to unrelated construction can change requirements. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

Does this operating course qualify a worker for energized electrical work?

**Answer:** No. Electrical qualification and task authorization are separate.

**Reason/remediation:** Operating familiarity does not establish electrical competence. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

Why include pole tail swing in a job briefing?

**Answer:** The full pole envelope can reach people or traffic beyond the hook path.

**Reason/remediation:** Protecting only the hook leaves a foreseeable strike hazard. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L2

Is yesterday's traffic plan automatically valid today?

**Answer:** No. Reassess current traffic, work area and load path.

**Reason/remediation:** Changed conditions can invalidate prior controls. Revisit Q02 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L1

May maximum outrigger reaction be estimated as truck weight divided by four?

**Answer:** No. Use appropriate actual reaction and support information.

**Reason/remediation:** Load and boom position can distribute reaction very unevenly. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q03-L2

Does nominal mat area always equal effective bearing area?

**Answer:** No. Contact and structural support must be established.

**Reason/remediation:** Unsupported portions do not automatically spread reaction into adequate soil. Revisit Q03 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L1

Does an unanswered locate request mean the hole is clear?

**Answer:** No. Obtain the required completed location verification.

**Reason/remediation:** Requesting information is not receiving safe clearance. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q04-L2

Who establishes the actual pole hole dimensions?

**Answer:** The approved job/design and safe work plan, not an operator's generic formula.

**Reason/remediation:** Ground, foundation and equipment conditions differ. Revisit Q04 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L1

Is a missing boom retention pin acceptable if hydraulics hold?

**Answer:** No. Stop and obtain qualified correction and release.

**Reason/remediation:** Hydraulic force is not a substitute for required structural retention. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q05-L2

How should a suspected pressure-injection injury be handled?

**Answer:** Seek immediate professional medical response under the emergency plan.

**Reason/remediation:** A small visible wound can conceal severe internal injury. Revisit Q05 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L1

Does a range diagram alone establish lifting capacity?

**Answer:** No. It establishes geometry; the correct load chart and restrictions are also required.

**Reason/remediation:** Reach and permissible load are different properties. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q06-L2

May a stable-looking machine exceed a structural chart limit?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Lack of visible tipping is not proof against structural failure. Revisit Q06 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L1

Can similar-looking poles be assigned the same weight without evidence?

**Answer:** No. Establish actual material, condition, attachments and weight.

**Reason/remediation:** Moisture and attached hardware can change mass and center of gravity. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q07-L2

Does an identified lifting beam make an unidentified sling acceptable?

**Answer:** No. Every load-bearing component needs suitable verified capacity.

**Reason/remediation:** The system is limited by each component in its actual configuration. Revisit Q07 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L1

Can generic wire-rope lubricant be used on any synthetic load line?

**Answer:** No. Follow the specific approved line instructions.

**Reason/remediation:** Incompatible products can damage or impair the line. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q08-L2

Does a two-part multiplication replace the manufacturer's reeving limits?

**Answer:** No. It is only a simplified comparison when explicitly supplied.

**Reason/remediation:** Actual efficiency, terminations and machine limits still govern. Revisit Q08 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L1

Who may give an emergency stop?

**Answer:** Anyone who identifies the need.

**Reason/remediation:** The operator must respond even if the person is not the designated directing signaler. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q09-L2

What happens when required radio communication fails?

**Answer:** Stop safely and restore an approved communication arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** Continuing from a remembered command risks uncontrolled exposure. Revisit Q09 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L1

May a helper brush soil from a rotating auger?

**Answer:** No. Stop and use the approved isolated clearing procedure.

**Reason/remediation:** Hands, clothing and tools can be drawn into rotation. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q10-L2

Does stopped rotation prove hydraulic isolation?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Stored or available energy can restart movement unexpectedly. Revisit Q10 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L1

What is the pole guide's intended role?

**Answer:** Alignment and control within its approved use, not supporting pole weight.

**Reason/remediation:** The line and approved rigging carry the lifting load. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q11-L2

When may load-supporting rigging be released from a set pole?

**Answer:** Only after the authorized stable support/setting requirements are verified.

**Reason/remediation:** Being upright or inside a hole does not prove stability. Revisit Q11 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L1

Does known free pole weight establish breakout force?

**Answer:** No. Embedded resistance is additional and can be unknown.

**Reason/remediation:** The line must not be used as an experimental extraction force gauge. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q12-L2

May the boom rock an embedded pole loose?

**Answer:** No. Use the appropriate approved extraction method.

**Reason/remediation:** Side force and sudden release can overload or destabilize equipment. Revisit Q12 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L1

Does a hydraulic pressure reading automatically equal anchor torque?

**Answer:** No. A valid equipment relationship and suitable instrumentation are required.

**Reason/remediation:** Pressure alone does not establish installed anchor performance. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q13-L2

Does an operating course authorize anchor capacity design?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Engineering acceptance and actual anchor-installation qualification remain separate. Revisit Q13 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L1

Does truck grounding make it safe to touch near energized conductors?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Ground potential rise and touch/step exposure can remain. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q14-L2

Does a fiberglass-looking boom establish dielectric protection?

**Answer:** No. Verify the actual declared system and required condition/test evidence.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance does not establish insulation or qualification. Revisit Q14 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L1

May two equal-looking booms be assumed to share equally?

**Answer:** No. Establish actual changing load share through qualified planning.

**Reason/remediation:** Geometry and rigidity can transfer unequal forces. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q15-L2

Does fitting a platform establish permission for combined personnel/material lifting?

**Answer:** No. The system, configuration and applicable plan must permit it.

**Reason/remediation:** Combined use can introduce separate load and rescue/fall-protection requirements. Revisit Q15 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L1

Does an outrigger monitor prove soil support is adequate?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Position sensing does not establish bearing capacity or absence of voids. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q16-L2

Can an experienced observer replace any failed device?

**Answer:** No. Only an applicable authorized alternative may be used where actually permitted.

**Reason/remediation:** Different safety devices and aids have different requirements. Revisit Q16 and perform its changed application.

## Q17-L1

Does absence of sparks establish an exposed cable is safe?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Hazardous voltage or other facility conditions may have no visible sign. Revisit Q17 and perform its changed application.

## Q17-L2

Is a repair invoice alone a return-to-service check?

**Answer:** No. Required qualified inspection/function release must also be established.

**Reason/remediation:** Repair and verified readiness are different records. Revisit Q17 and perform its changed application.

## Q18-L1

May hydraulic pressure substitute for a required auger transit lock?

**Answer:** No.

**Reason/remediation:** Travel restraint must follow the actual positive securing requirements. Revisit Q18 and perform its changed application.

## Q18-L2

Does completing this material package mean a learner passed the practical?

**Answer:** No. Actual observed evidence is required.

**Reason/remediation:** Authored instruction is not evidence of a person's performance. Revisit Q18 and perform its changed application.
