# VNDRLY/U Helicopter Landing Officer — Workplace Competence

VUC095. Original available-source normal-operations package. Actual operator procedures and supervised workplace evidence govern delivery. No external accreditation, aircraft command, unrestricted emergency-team or refuelling qualification is conferred.

## Q95-1 — Role, preparation and assessment boundaries

The HLO coordinates the helideck team and the installation side of helicopter operations within the approved helideck operations manual. This is not pilot command, aircraft performance approval or unrestricted authority over every aviation task. Establish the site, aircraft interfaces, assigned duties, competent team and actual procedures before assessing performance. Initial helideck instruction followed by supervised workplace development is the expected preparation; a workplace assessment is not a shortcut around learning the role.

Confirm fitness for the planned practical activities and appropriate PPE. Identify additional qualifications needed for specialist functions such as refuelling or emergency-team leadership. This package assesses normal HLO operations and safe recognition/escalation of abnormal conditions; it does not substitute for a full specialist emergency qualification. Evidence must identify what the candidate personally did, independently, in the actual context.

Worked and changed applications: A candidate has directed practice on a mock deck but no authenticated workplace cycle. Credit practice and arrange actual observation before a workplace outcome. Changed case: the HLO is asked to perform a fuel-system repair outside authorization. Preserve the defect, obtain the authorized specialist and do not extend competence by job title.

## Q95-2 — Operating information and local limitations

Read current installation and aircraft-operator information before planning a movement. Identify applicable deck dimensions and load rating, markings, obstacle environment, lighting, access routes, equipment status, weather information, and any restrictions. A familiar aircraft name does not prove compatibility with today’s deck condition. Use controlled records and the operator’s acceptance process rather than memorized generic limits.

Check revisions and communicate material changes. A restriction entered after the morning briefing can invalidate earlier readiness. The HLO must know where to obtain authoritative information and who can resolve uncertainty. Do not convert a missing value into a favorable assumption. In assessment, explain the relationship between local readiness and the flight crew’s separate decisions; a helideck report is not a flight clearance.

Worked and changed applications: A training card says fictional deck mass limit 12,000 kg and proposed aircraft operating mass 12,200 kg. Flag the 200 kg exceedance and refer; do not use aircraft empty mass instead. Changed case: mass is below the card value but a compatibility field is missing. The numerical comparison alone cannot establish readiness.

## Q95-3 — Deck inspection and foreign-object control

Inspect the deck and access arrangements using the approved checklist before operations and after relevant changes. Look for loose objects, contamination, damaged surfaces, unsecured equipment, obstructed routes and defects in required markings, lighting or perimeter protection. Inspect only when access is safe and authorized. A checklist tick must reflect an actual observation, not memory of the last flight.

Foreign objects can move under rotor wash and harm people or aircraft. Secure or remove them through the authorized safe process; never chase a loose item toward a running aircraft. Report defects and verify correction before changing status. Maintain clear emergency and passenger routes. The assessor should see a systematic inspection, accurate findings and a controlled reinspection after correction.

Worked and changed applications: A strap is found on the marked mock landing area. Hold readiness, arrange safe removal and recheck; do not step into a live rotor area to retrieve it. Changed case: the object is removed but a slippery patch remains. Keep the separate defect open until the approved resolution is verified.

## Q95-4 — Weather, environmental effects and motion information

Obtain and report current required weather and, for moving decks, motion information from authorized systems or trained sources. State time, units and any sensor limitations. Wind, visibility, precipitation, turbulence, exhaust effects and vessel motion can affect operations; do not substitute a casual visual estimate for required data. The aircraft operator determines applicable flight limits and acceptance.

Check freshness and consistency. If a sensor is unavailable, use only an approved alternative with its conditions and notifications; do not invent a replacement reading. A report that was acceptable earlier may no longer describe current conditions. Recognize installation activities that change airflow or exhaust exposure and communicate them. Assessment should use synthetic values to test reporting and hold decisions without teaching fabricated universal limits.

Worked and changed applications: The exercise card requires reports no older than ten minutes. At 14:12, a 13:58 observation is fourteen minutes old and fails that fictional freshness rule. Obtain a current approved report. Changed case: a fresh report has no units or motion-source status; resolve those gaps rather than treating its timestamp as sufficient.

## Q95-5 — Concurrent work and obstacle coordination

Coordinate cranes, lifts, work parties, nearby structures and other activities that could conflict with helicopter operations. Use the approved communication and work-control arrangements to establish safe status before arrival or departure. A crane operator’s acknowledgment that a message was heard is not confirmation that the crane is in the required position. Verify the actual required condition and communicate it clearly.

Consider changing or unusual obstacles and conflicting operations near more than one landing area. Do not infer that simultaneous operations are acceptable because they occurred previously. Refer to the operator’s agreed arrangements and responsible installation authority. If a conflict develops, hold or suspend the affected operation and notify the right parties. Record who confirmed the condition and when.

Worked and changed applications: A mock crane response says request received, while the status display still shows movement. The HLO must obtain verified safe status rather than announce readiness. Changed case: crane is secured but a maintenance team remains in an access route. Resolve that independent conflict before release.

## Q95-6 — Team organization, briefing and communications

Assign competent personnel to required roles, confirm equipment and communication readiness, and brief the movement plan, hazards, signals, passenger route, special arrangements and stop criteria. Identify the HLO clearly. Check understanding with concise readback or task confirmation. Staffing is determined by the approved operation and emergency arrangements, not by an invented minimum from this course.

Use the approved radio and signaling procedures. A missed or ambiguous message requires clarification; do not proceed on a guess. When communications degrade, apply the approved alternative only if all conditions are satisfied, otherwise hold. Keep a shared picture of deck status and passenger movement. The assessor should observe leadership that invites safety concerns and does not penalize a team member for reporting a defect.

Worked and changed applications: An HDA cannot repeat the assigned passenger route. Rebrief before movement rather than assuming experience will compensate. Changed case: radio failure occurs after briefing. Establish the approved fallback and required coordination, or hold; hand signals are not a blanket substitute for every missing communication.

## Q95-7 — Arrival readiness and controlled access

Before reporting readiness, confirm deck condition, concurrent-work status, required equipment and team, current information, and controlled access. Communicate restrictions accurately to the responsible aviation and installation parties. The pilot retains aircraft decisions; the HLO does not guarantee landing safety merely by completing a local checklist. Maintain exclusion and observation throughout arrival.

People must remain outside unsafe areas until the approved aircraft-specific process authorizes access. Rotor, tail-rotor, exhaust and visibility hazards vary with aircraft and conditions. Do not teach a universal approach direction from a generic diagram. Use the operator’s current safe routes, crew indications and HLO control. A new hazard after readiness requires an immediate status update and appropriate hold.

Worked and changed applications: The deck was reported ready but loose material is then observed. Update the status promptly and prevent personnel movement; do not hide the change to avoid disruption. Changed case: someone tries to approach because the aircraft has landed. Landing alone is not permission to enter the controlled area.

## Q95-8 — Passenger disembarkation and embarkation

Coordinate with flight crew before passenger movement. Confirm the authorized route, team positions, required briefing and special assistance arrangements. Maintain positive control so passengers do not wander toward hazardous areas or retrieve dropped objects. Keep baggage and other tasks from creating a competing movement path. Do not improvise aircraft emergency instructions or assistance methods.

Reconcile passenger identities and counts with the current manifest through the approved administrative interface. A matching headcount can conceal an unmanifested substitute. Resolve discrepancies before dependent movement and ensure the crew has current information. Assess calm communication and safe pacing rather than speed. The HLO must stop a developing unsafe movement, not rely on a last-second correction.

Worked and changed applications: Manifest lists six people and six appear, but one ID differs. Hold, verify and revise through the authorized process; do not accept the number alone. Changed case: a passenger drops a bag near a marked hazard boundary. Stop and arrange controlled recovery when safe rather than allowing pursuit.

## Q95-9 — Baggage, freight and dangerous-goods interface

Control the safe movement of bags and freight according to the operator’s loading and route instructions. Match item identity, destination, count and recorded mass; refer loading suitability to authorized personnel. Heavy or unusual items may require specific handling arrangements. Changes after weighing or manifest preparation must be reconciled before acceptance.

Recognize suspect or undeclared dangerous goods and stay within assigned training. Hold and refer uncertain items without improvised opening, packaging or classification. Keep personnel routes clear during item handling. The HLO coordinates the interface but cannot authorize a prohibited item by relabeling it as baggage. Assess the candidate’s ability to preserve a hold under time pressure and communicate the revised status.

Worked and changed applications: A toolbox is added after manifest approval. Stop the addition, obtain verified data and authorized loading/DG review, and update records. Changed case: a damaged battery is discovered among bags. Activate the applicable safety route and specialist response; do not simply move it to another bag.

## Q95-10 — Departure checks and post-flight restoration

Before departure, complete the approved crew coordination and confirm people, equipment and items are clear of required areas. Verify actual status rather than relying solely on a checklist completed before loading. Reconcile unresolved discrepancies and communicate any change. The HLO’s local readiness report is not an aircraft dispatch clearance.

After the movement, record actual times and relevant events, inspect when safe, restore equipment and identify defects or replenishment needs. Do not mark the deck fully ready for another flight if a required resource remains unavailable. Preserve reports, manifest references and handover information. An accurate post-flight record supports both the next movement and later investigation.

Worked and changed applications: A handling tool is missing from the count before departure. Hold and resolve its location through the safe process; do not assume it is in storage. Changed case: the aircraft departs but a required response item is defective. Record and notify the resulting readiness restriction rather than carrying forward the previous ready status.

## Q95-11 — Equipment defects, refuelling boundaries and abnormal response

Know the required operational and emergency equipment and the approved process for unserviceability. A minimum-equipment provision is conditional; it is not permission to ignore a defect without meeting its conditions, authority and notification requirements. If a required capability is not established, report the restriction and hold affected operations. Do not repair safety-critical systems beyond authorization.

Refuelling requires separately competent personnel, aircraft-specific procedures and the approved HLO/crew interface. This package does not train hands-on fuel delivery. Maintain the separation and controls required by the actual procedure; do not combine passenger movement and fuelling as a time-saving improvisation. For a fire, spill or other emergency, raise the alarm, protect people and initiate the established response within trained role; do not turn an assessment into uncontrolled live rescue.

Worked and changed applications: A required response resource is unavailable and no approved alternative is established. Notify responsible parties and withhold readiness. Changed case: a radio defect has a documented conditional alternative, but the alternate link has not been tested. The conditions are not yet met; a document alone is not restored capability.

## Q95-12 — Workplace evidence, remediation and maintained competence

Assessment must cover normal preparation, arrival, passenger/item control, departure and restoration through independent workplace observations. Use declared mock exercises for defects and unusual conditions that cannot safely be introduced into operations. Record the candidate’s actions and any prompts, supporting work products and authenticated witnesses. Simulation is valuable evidence of decisions but cannot alone prove actual workplace performance.

For this original package, require two observed routine cycles in the defined context, all stations and changed-condition exercises, and the separate final. A critical safety or integrity failure prevents a pass regardless of total score. Remediate the specific gap and assess a changed independent task. Record employer/operator scope and limitations; no OPITO credential or universal HLO appointment follows from the authored materials.

Worked and changed applications: A candidate passes written questions but directs a person into a prohibited mock area. Stop, record the critical error, re-teach and assess changed movement control before any award. Changed case: a competent HLO moves to a different deck or operator. Review local differences and obtain the necessary preparation and evidence rather than assuming every procedure transfers unchanged.

## I95 integrated assessment

Run both A and B as complete changed mock cycles. Controller releases injects at the relevant stage, records the candidate's decisions and does not coach. All independent holds must be tracked through resolution. Require two actual routine workplace cycles W1 and W2 with date, site, operator, candidate contribution, observer and secure evidence references. A competent reviewer checks every domain and any restrictions. Simulation success is not proof that actual workplace observation occurred.

Twelve dimensions correspond to P95-1 through P95-12. Score 0 absent/unsafe, 1 major coaching, 2 safe but incomplete/prompted, 3 independently acceptable, 4 accurate and independently explained. Each integrated decision requires at least 39/48, every dimension at least 3, all stations and required cycles passed, and no critical error. Critical: knowingly false readiness, unauthorized unsafe movement, ignoring a missing person/item or active restriction, unsupported DG/fuelling authority, invented operational data or fabricated evidence. Stop the attempt, preserve the result, provide targeted teaching/practice, then assess a changed independent task. Correct safety holds are credited even if the mock flight is delayed.

## Trace and maintenance

Each Q95 chapter has two explained formative checks, a corresponding P95 station, one protected three-decision final case and a changed retake. Six worked labs connect the domains to supplied mock datasets. I95 includes two changed mock cycles and evidence forms for two actual independent workplace cycles. Six stations use safe physical mock-area tasks; six are observed review/roleplay. No actual aircraft, emergency or learner performance is claimed by package creation.

Before delivery verify current local manual, aircraft interfaces, equipment requirements, weather/motion sources, communications, movement routes, concurrent-work arrangements, specialist boundaries and emergency contacts. Record applicable revisions. Synthetic comparison values and report-age rules are explicitly not live criteria. Preserve failed evidence and reassess material changes.
