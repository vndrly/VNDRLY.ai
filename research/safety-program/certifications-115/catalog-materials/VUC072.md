# VUC072 — Concrete Pump Operation

## Purpose and scope

This original VNDRLY/U program develops safe truck-mounted concrete boom-pump preparation, setup, controlled placement, cleanout and securing. The operating award requires actual machine and controlled pumping performance on the recorded equipment. It is not CCO or ACPA certification, a commercial driving license, engineering approval for supports or authority to operate every line pump, separate placing boom or high-rise system. Record actual equipment and unassessed limits.

Use current actual manufacturer instructions, applicable requirements and the validated site/pour plan. P72 numbers are fictional learning data, not real equipment ratings or engineering designs. The instructor validates equipment, ground, pipeline, concrete and training conditions before actual work. Learners must stop for missing essential information instead of guessing. Independent performance follows taught examples, checks and supervised changed practice.

## Q01 — Responsibilities and pre-trip planning

Confirm the job's location, pour type, reach, volume, mix, planned rate, access, expected duration and cleanup arrangements. Coordinate with the pour supervisor, controlling entity and placing crew before dispatch. Identify who provides verified ground information, traffic control, pipeline support, electrical clearance and authorized mix decisions. An operator can refuse an unsafe setup; schedule pressure does not establish missing conditions.

Select actual compatible hoses, clamps, gaskets, safety pins, pads/dunnage, spill materials, tools, approved spare parts, manuals and required paperwork. Prepare appropriate PPE, weather clothing, drinking water and charged communication/remote batteries. A spare part that looks similar is not necessarily rated or approved. Plan contingencies for a delayed truck, changing weather or an unavailable washout location.

Worked: a pump arrives with enough reach but no approved location for primeout or washout. Do not improvise discharge into a storm drain; resolve containment before pumping. Changed: containment is arranged, but the specified hose and gasket sizes do not match. Fix compatibility rather than assume the clamp can compensate.

## Q02 — Truck inspection, road readiness and route

Inspect the vehicle and review required defect/repair information before driving. Confirm brakes, tires, steering, lights, mirrors, horn, emergency equipment and secure loads as applicable. Check actual travel height, width, mass, axle/route limits and permits. Driving qualifications, medical/duty-status and other applicable carrier requirements remain separate from pump operation. Do not assume a special-purpose vehicle is exempt from every road requirement.

Plan access and exit for the pump's actual dimensions, including bridges, overhead lines, weight restrictions and turning room. Avoid distractions and drive for the vehicle's stability and stopping distance. A route that worked with a different machine may not fit this one. Stop safely rather than continue under an uncertain clearance. Public-road assessment requires a separately qualified driver/assessor; this course's controlled-site maneuvers do not grant a license.

Worked P72: travel height is 4.00 m and a route has a verified 3.85 m clearance. The vehicle exceeds it by 0.15 m; choose a verified lawful alternate. Changed: alternate clearance is adequate but the bridge's permitted weight is unknown. Height alone does not approve the route. Preserve actual inspection and restriction records instead of marking ready from appearance.

## Q03 — Pump, boom and safety-system inspection

Match actual equipment identity, manual revision, boom configuration and installed delivery system. Inspect boom sections, pins/retention, hydraulics, pipe/elbows, clamps/gaskets, hose condition, outriggers, locks, hopper guards, interlocks, controls and required emergency functions. Follow actual inspection intervals and qualified wear-measurement procedures; a pre-use glance does not replace periodic examination or a required thickness check.

Keep hands away from moving or pressure-exposed components. A worn elbow, missing clamp safety pin, faulty guard/interlock or unintended boom movement is not cured by slower pumping. Record precise symptoms and remove affected operation from service for qualified correction and required release. Do not operate a failed protection just to see whether it really matters. Water box, lubrication and fluid checks are performed under safe manufacturer procedures.

Worked: an elbow shows unusual external wear and its inspection record is overdue. Hold until suitability is established rather than assume no leak means adequate wall thickness. Changed: the elbow is replaced, but a boom retention pin is missing. Resolve the separate structural defect before operation.

## Q04 — Site review, ground information and pour coordination

Meet the responsible pour personnel and verify information rather than accepting a vague all clear. Inspect underground features, excavations, slabs, fill, soft ground, overhead obstructions, traffic, public exposure and truck staging. Include the full deployed boom, hose and delivery-truck envelope. A paved surface can conceal a vault or unsupported slab. Identify a safe operator position with sight of the work and suitable communication where visibility is limited.

Agree pour sequence, startup exclusion, hand signals, designated signaler, stopping process, concrete delivery, primeout/restart discharge area, cleanup and emergency access. Keep people out of backing and outrigger pinch zones. Reassess if another trade enters or the work moves to a different part of the structure. The setup point must serve the entire planned pour safely, not merely the first discharge location.

Worked: the initial pour is reachable, but the far end requires the boom to pass under an overhead service. Hold and revise the complete plan. Changed: the boom route is safe, but mixer trucks now need to reverse across a public walkway. Traffic/backing controls must be established separately.

## Q05 — Outriggers, support and level

Use actual maximum outrigger reactions and appropriately assessed ground/support information. Do not divide vehicle mass equally among legs. Pads and dunnage must have adequate structural capacity, effective bearing and stable placement. Excavation edges, buried services and slopes require appropriate assessment; a simple one-to-one sketch or pocket penetrometer reading alone cannot approve every ground condition. Follow the actual permissible setup distance and engineering requirements.

Deploy outriggers in the prescribed sequence with people clear and verify required extension, support and level. Partial deployment is permitted only where the actual manufacturer system and work-sector limitations authorize it; never invent a reduced-capacity rule or falsify the sensed state. Monitor support through the pour, especially after rain or vibration. Stop safely for settlement and obtain the proper recovery/support response.

P72 worked paper comparison: maximum reaction 240 kN and supplied allowable pressure 300 kPa require ideal area 0.80 m². Effective 0.60 m² gives 400 kPa and fails. Changed: effective 1.0 m² gives 240 kPa, but an unverified underground vault still prevents acceptance. These computations do not establish real soil or pad design. Use actual level tolerances, not an external test's generic number.

## Q06 — Electrical hazards and emergency boundaries

Survey electrical hazards before positioning, unfolding, pouring, cleaning and folding. The boom, line, concrete and hose can form hazardous paths; a rubber-looking hose is not proof of insulation. Obtain the applicable clearance/protection arrangement for actual voltage and circumstances through the responsible parties. Do not use a single remembered distance for every voltage or configuration, and do not treat a spotter as permission to enter a prohibited area.

Plan the whole motion envelope, including load/boom deflection and operator distraction. Where required, establish effective observation and clear limits. Keep unauthorized people away and treat unidentified overhead lines as hazardous until appropriately verified. For contact or suspected energization, activate the site/electrical emergency plan and keep others away from the truck, hose and surrounding exposure. Do not approach to perform an unplanned rescue.

Worked: the boom clears a line at setup, but the final placing point reduces that separation beyond the approved boundary. Stop before the move and revise the plan. Changed: the utility establishes an authorized de-energized condition for specified lines, but another nearby circuit is outside that confirmation. Do not extend the confirmation to that circuit by assumption.

## Q07 — Boom unfolding, reach and permitted attachments

Identify actual boom sections, folding pattern, control directions, limits and permitted setup. Follow the prescribed unfolding sequence and clearance envelope; do not force a section past its stop or use another machine to extend reach. A concrete placing boom is not a crane. Do not lift roof trusses, equipment or other unrelated loads from it or use a crane to improvise additional boom extension.

Check approved end-hose length, filled weight, reducers and attachment limits. Include concrete weight and all specified fittings; do not compare only empty hose mass. Approved pump attachments still need correct installation, gaskets, clamps, safety pins and required restraints. Match the complete actual configuration; arithmetic cannot authorize an unapproved attachment.

P72 worked: a supplied attachment ceiling is 180 kg. Hose 55 kg, contained concrete 100 and approved fittings 15 total 170, leaving 10. Changed: an extra reducer adds 20, making 190 and exceeding by 10. This is fictional arithmetic, not an actual tip-load rating. The operator must also verify whether that reducer/configuration is permitted at all.

## Q08 — Delivery pipes, hoses, couplings and supports

Use components rated and compatible for the actual concrete pressure, wear condition, diameter, end style and application. The lowest suitable component rating limits the assembled system. Pipe and clamp appearance alone do not establish remaining strength. Inspect, identify and verify components through the appropriate procedure. Correct gaskets, full clamp engagement and retained safety pins matter; do not substitute wire or a mismatched coupling.

Provide designed supports, anchors and routing for actual line weight, pressure effects and movement. The applicable OSHA discharge-pipe support requirement includes design for 100 percent overload; this is not permission to increase operating pressure or improvise support calculations. Avoid trip, crush and vehicle-damage exposure along the line. Use the intended single-ended hose at discharge rather than a double-ended delivery hose with metal mass at its free end.

Worked P72: pump maximum 85 bar, pipe 100, hose 80 and clamp 75 give a supplied comparison ceiling no higher than 75. Planned 82 exceeds it by 7. Changed: clamp is replaced with a compatible 100-bar unit; the hose now limits the comparison to 80, so 82 still fails by 2. Actual suitability and permitted pressure-setting methods remain required.

## Q09 — Concrete properties, batch information and pumping plan

Review the actual mix designation, aggregate/pumpability information, batch time, delivery condition and applicable acceptance requirements with the responsible pour personnel. Temperature and delays can change behavior. Do not add water or admixture on personal judgment to overcome resistance; only the authorized quality process can approve a change and record it. Apparent flowability alone does not prove the concrete meets the job specification.

Match delivery and placement rates to the pump and crew without compromising support, forms or hose control. Confirm hopper feed and avoid conditions that draw air into the system. Unexpected resistance, segregation or interruption requires communication and appropriate disposition, not ever-higher pressure. Keep batch/volume/delay records accurate so the quality team can evaluate the actual placement.

Worked P72: planned volume 12 m³ at an assumed steady 24 m³/h gives 0.5 h or 30 minutes of ideal pumping, excluding startup and interruptions. Changed: deliveries support only 18 m³/h, making ideal time 40 minutes. This is scheduling arithmetic, not permission to rush a crew or accept delayed concrete without review.

## Q10 — Truck backing, hopper and personnel protection

Establish a safe mixer approach, backing signaler and clear exclusion from pinch points between vehicles and equipment. The driver stops when required sight or communication is lost. Do not stand behind the mixer or between moving vehicles to guide by body position. Stabilize and secure equipment under the actual procedure before discharge. Coordinate truck changes so the operator can maintain controlled feed without distraction.

Keep hopper grates and required interlocks functioning. Never reach into the hopper, agitator, valve or moving mechanism to clear material while hazardous energy can cause motion. Use the actual lockout/tagout, verification and supported maintenance procedure for access; emergency stop alone is not full isolation. Approved tools do not make reaching around a guard safe. Protect against wet-concrete contact, slips and pinch zones around chutes.

Worked: a large object rests on the hopper grate and a worker proposes lifting the grate while the agitator runs. Stop and use the authorized clearing/isolation process. Changed: the agitator appears stopped, but remote starting remains possible. Verify full required hazardous-energy control before access.

## Q11 — Signals, remote control and positioning

Confirm remote identity, battery condition, control directions, emergency stop and communication before operation. Secure the remote against unintended input while handling other tasks. Do not operate by a display alone when the actual boom/hose and people are not adequately observed. Use the agreed signal arrangement for blind placement and stop if directions are unclear or conflicting.

Coordinate boom sections smoothly to keep the hose controlled and avoid kinking, dragging, impact or entanglement with structure. Do not pull a hose loose with the boom. The placing crew needs a safe standing/working platform and clear escape from the hose zone. Maintain the startup/restart exclusion until flow is confirmed safe under the procedure; a signal to approach is deliberate, not inferred from silence.

Worked: the signaler disappears behind a wall and a radio message is incomplete. Stop instead of continuing the previous direction. Changed: radio is restored, but the operator cannot establish which of two crews is directing the pump. Resolve directing responsibility before motion or flow resumes.

## Q12 — Priming and initial flow

Plan the approved priming method and safe discharge destination before starting. Ensure the assembled system, supports, hose type, exclusion area and concrete supply meet the actual procedure. Priming material and initial discharge need controlled containment and authorized quality disposition; they are not automatically acceptable in the structure. Keep all people a prudent safe distance from the discharge hazard as established by the actual plan.

Air in the line can compress and release suddenly, causing violent hose movement. The first stroke and restart are therefore not routine opportunities for a worker to hold the hose steady. Start and establish flow using the actual manufacturer procedure, observing the discharge from a protected position. Do not increase output blindly when discharge is absent. Only permit the placing crew to approach after the specified safe flow condition and communication are established.

Worked: the pump starts but no material appears and the hose twitches. Stop and apply the approved pressure/blockage response; do not ask a worker to grip the hose harder. Changed: steady flow is established but the primeout discharge is contaminating a drain. Stop and correct containment; successful flow does not resolve environmental exposure.

## Q13 — Placement, monitoring and changing conditions

During placement monitor the boom/line, hopper feed, pressure behavior, crew position, ground support, weather and interacting equipment. The operator's job is continuous control, not setting a rate and leaving. Keep the hose within permitted length/configuration and avoid kinks or unapproved rigid end fittings. Coordinate any move with the crew before motion; do not let someone ride or hang on the hose.

Stop for unexpected leakage, pipe/coupling movement, loss of support, control problems or unsafe personnel exposure. Do not service or grease a moving/pressurized hazard unless the actual procedure explicitly provides a safe authorized method. The approved pour plan also considers placing rate and structural/formwork capacity; an operator cannot increase rate solely to recover schedule time.

Worked P72: fictional plan permits 20 m³/h at the receiving work area, while pump capability is 40. A request for 30 exceeds the plan by 10 and requires authorized review, not automatic compliance. Changed: a revised plan permits 30, but a pad begins settling. The separate stability problem still requires a safe stop.

## Q14 — Interruptions, air entry and restart

After a pause, changed boom position, low feed or any condition that may admit air, reassess restart hazards. Concrete draining from a downward line, aging mix or restricted flow can worsen the risk. Do not assume a pause is safe because it lasted only a few minutes. Establish the proper discharge exclusion or move the boom to an approved safe restart area when the work arrangement permits and the plan requires it.

Restart under the actual manufacturer's sequence with direct observation and controlled output. If flow is absent or stops unexpectedly, stop and use the approved pressure-relief/blockage process. ACPA guidance emphasizes separation from the discharge and confirmation of steady flow; it is not a substitute for machine-specific procedure. Never rely on a person holding a hose to prevent whip or allow early approach because a supervisor is impatient.

Worked: after a delivery delay, a downward hose has drained and a finisher waits beside it. Hold restart until the required separation and procedure are in place. Changed: the finisher moves away but an unseen crew is below the discharge level. Verify the complete hazard area before pumping, not just the people visible from the cab.

## Q15 — Blockages, residual pressure and safe recovery

Recognize abnormal pressure/flow, stopped discharge, hose deformation and other signs through the actual instructions. Stop instead of forcing pressure upward. Inform and clear affected personnel and apply the authorized isolation/pressure-relief sequence. Reverse strokes or a zero indication may be part of a procedure but do not automatically prove every isolated segment is depressurized. Concrete plugs can trap pressure separately.

Do not crack a clamp to test for pressure, stand in a potential discharge path or strike a loaded line. Qualified recovery must establish a safe state, supports and verified pressure control before disassembly. Inspect and reassemble correctly after correction, then treat resumed flow as a controlled restart. Preserve the cause/repair record; do not hide an incident because production eventually resumes.

Worked: the pump gauge reads zero but a plug separates the far line section. The distant section may retain pressure. Hold opening until the authorized method verifies safe conditions for that section. Changed: pressure is safely relieved, but the removed clamp is damaged. Replace/verify suitable components before restart rather than reuse the defect.

## Q16 — Cleanout, washout and environmental control

Select the actual approved cleanout method and location before the pour. Provide containment, safe supports, exclusion and appropriate tools/PPE. Different pump/line arrangements require different procedures; do not copy a high-rise or compressed-air method to a boom system. Compressed air can store substantial energy and eject plugs, cleaning devices or material. It requires its own authorized equipment, capture/retention and trained procedure; it is not a shortcut in this ordinary practical.

For maintenance/access, isolate and verify all hazardous energy before opening guards or reaching into pump components. Safely remove attachments only after support and pressure conditions permit. Washout water and cementitious waste need authorized containment/disposal; keep drains and public routes protected. For offsite cleanout, use the actual approved transport/containment plan, considering remaining material and time, not an improvised uncovered hopper trip.

Worked: the designated washout is full and the operator suggests blowing material into an open field with compressed air. Stop and obtain an approved alternative; no unplanned projectile or discharge operation. Changed: a valid containment area is found but the crew lacks the specified cleanout capture equipment. Location alone does not authorize the method.

## Q17 — Emergency response and occupational exposure

Brief the response to power contact, support loss, hose whip/line failure, hopper entrapment, hydraulic injury, fire and wet-concrete exposure. Stop and control the area without entering a secondary hazard. Untrained entry into an energized area or a moving mechanism can create more casualties. Emergency stop is an immediate control, followed by the applicable isolation, rescue and medical response.

Use suitable protection and hygiene against cement burns and eye exposure, noise, heat and other actual hazards. Promptly follow the product SDS and emergency washing/medical instructions; do not wait for pain to prove harm. Do not hand-search a hydraulic leak. Suspected injection injury needs immediate professional medical attention even with a tiny puncture. Record the actual event and preserve equipment restrictions for investigation.

Worked: grout enters a worker's boot while the pour continues. Arrange safe relief/stop and prompt decontamination/medical response as appropriate rather than waiting for shift end. Changed: exposure is addressed but the hose failure that caused it is unexplained. Keep the affected equipment restricted until qualified correction and release.

## Q18 — Folding, travel and end-of-day handover

Follow the actual shutdown/folding sequence with the complete movement envelope clear. Verify attachments removed or stowed as required, boom seated and retained, outriggers fully retracted/secured, pads/tools restrained, PTO/control state correct and residual material safely contained. Walk around the vehicle before movement; an indicator alone does not verify every pin or protruding item.

Complete the required vehicle/pump defect reports, repair needs and job records. Prepare fuel, water, lubrication and batteries for the next job through authorized safe procedures. Record actual batch/volume/interruptions and unresolved restrictions; do not sign a safe condition that was not checked. Road readiness must be reassessed for the actual return route and driver status. One good pour does not prove a safe trip home.

Worked P72: projected travel height after incorrect stow is 4.20 m, while the verified route allows 4.10; it exceeds by 0.10. Correct actual stow and remeasure rather than choose a favorable label. Changed: height is corrected but an outrigger lock is not engaged. That separate restraint defect still prevents travel. The independent practical combines all phases, including actual controlled pumping and safe cleanout, before the operating award.

# VUC072 — Instructor and practical workbook

## Preparation and teaching sequence

Plan 72 hours: 16 instruction/checks, 8 application labs, 28 supervised stations, 12 integrated practice/assessment, 4 final/review and 4 remediation/records. Extend learning as needed and retain actual hours. Instructor/assessor must be competent for the actual boom pump, concrete system, cleaning method and observed tasks. Road driving requires its separate legal qualifications and assessment; public-road operation is not created as a course exercise.

Provide inspected pump truck and current actual manuals, approved inspected delivery system and end hose, suitable pads/supports, validated stable site, known safe boom envelope, protected lightweight targets and landing areas, actual approved pumpable concrete, controlled delivery/receiving crew, approved primeout/restart/cleanout containment, PPE/SDS/washing facilities, communication and emergency supplies. Pumping uses a planned nonstructural training placement or a separately approved supervised placement with all actual job controls; do not improvise a pour to fill an assessment checkbox.

Validate ground reactions/support, setup/level/sector, electrical and traffic boundaries, pipeline ratings/wear/supports, attachment filled mass, hopper guards/interlocks, controls/emergency stops, concrete batch/pumpability acceptance and waste disposition. No fault is physically introduced into a live pressurized system. Simulate air/plug/contact/settlement problems verbally or with photographs; do not deliberately cause hose whip, blockages, entrapment or instability. All normal protections remain functioning.

Diagnostic: units, pressure versus capacity, hose filled mass, pad-area comparison, signal recognition, startup separation and distinction between stop and verified isolation. Teach a complete safe sequence, practice a changed case and assess independently. Learners receive all actual limits and targets before assessment; private keys do not contain hidden operating data.

## Application labs

### W01 — Dispatch and route

Packet: actual-size equipment assignment, missing cleanup location and fictional 4.10 m travel height versus 4.00 m route clearance. Worked: clearance fails by 0.10 m and cleanup is independently unresolved. Changed: verified alternative route clears the vehicle but the driver qualification/duty basis is missing. Learner submits dispatch/route checklist and accurate readiness holds; no course certificate is treated as a driving license.

### W02 — Site and support

Paper reaction 210 kN, allowable pressure 250 kPa and effective area 0.70 m². Worked ideal area 0.84 m²; pressure 300 kPa fails. Changed effective area 1.0 m² gives 210, but the point lies over an unverified slab void. Learner submits calculation and a site-specific validated setup checklist. Actual lab uses safe accepted supports only. A paper pass does not approve the void or pad strength.

### W03 — Inspection and attachment

Packet: missing clamp pin, overdue elbow examination, faulty hopper interlock; fictional attachment mass hose 60 + concrete 95 + fittings 20 = 175 kg versus 190 allowable. Worked mass margin 15 does not clear any inspection fault. Changed extra fitting 25 yields 200, exceeding by 10. Actual lab safely inspects and assembles approved depressurized/supported attachments, checking compatibility, gaskets, clamps, pins and restraints.

### W04 — Pipe pressure and routing

Paper ratings pump 100 bar, pipe 110, hose 85, clamp 90; planned 92. Worked ceiling 85 and exceedance 7. Changed rated hose 100 makes clamp 90 the lowest, so 92 still fails by 2. Learner maps actual line supports, exposure and end-hose selection and verifies actual ratings. No real overpressure is applied. Required support design and actual allowed pressure are obtained through qualified procedures, not created from this arithmetic.

Learner reviews batch/pumpability information and writes a delay/quality referral without unauthorized water addition. ### W06 — Boom control and startup

Packet: approved setup/envelope and primeout area, safe target positions, an absent-discharge verbal inject and a person depicted in the hose zone. Worked: establish controlled startup separation and stop on abnormal discharge; do not hold the hose to suppress whip. Changed: flow is steady but containment is breached. Actual lab performs safe boom placement and normal manufacturer-directed priming/flow with the approved material and containment. Fault cases are simulated without inducing them.

### W07 — Interruptions and pressure

Packet: delayed delivery, downward line that may have drained, crew near discharge, later zero pump gauge with a hypothetical isolated plug. Worked: use the approved restart exclusion/sequence and do not infer all line sections are depressurized. Changed: verified pressure relief but damaged clamp. Hold until suitable repair/assembly/restart. Actual lab rehearses safe communication, stop and normal planned restart; blocked-line opening is a tabletop/isolated training assembly exercise, not a live induced plug.

### W08 — Cleanup and return

Packet: full washout, missing capture equipment, a proposed compressed-air shortcut and fictional 4.25 m stowed height versus 4.15 route clearance. Worked: resolve method/location and correct excess height 0.10. Changed height corrected but outrigger restraint absent. Learner demonstrates actual approved normal cleanout and stowing, with no improvised compressed-air cleanout, and completes defect/return records. No discharge to a drain or false ready status is accepted.

## I72 independent practical

Prepare separate teaching T and assessment A/B plans. Validate actual equipment, operator/crew roles, site/support/level/sector, electrical clearances, full boom envelope, attachments and filled masses, delivery ratings/supports, batch acceptance, pressure/rate limits, PPE, controls, receiving and washout arrangements. Use an appropriately small planned concrete volume and safe placement area validated by the responsible instructor/pour team; record actual material and volume. There is no universal minimum volume that substitutes for demonstrating all required functions.

Set three safe boom/hose target areas with clear marked boundaries and a route requiring coordinated section movement and an approved change in height. Targets and all clearances are established from the actual machine/site plan, with people outside the motion/hose zone. Do not copy external test dimensions or attach unapproved weights/chains. Learner packet specifies all target boundaries, safe elevations and permitted contact/support conditions before work. Schedule about four hours plus familiarization; no speed score rewards rushed movement.

Independent sequence: review dispatch/vehicle/pump documents; perform inspection; survey/brief; verify site/support and electrical/traffic controls; set up and unfold; safely assemble permitted attachments; demonstrate remote and signals; move hose to all three targets without collision, dragging, kinking or unplanned contact; verify actual concrete/batch and crew readiness; perform approved priming and confirm steady flow with the hazard zone clear; conduct controlled placement at two approved receiving points; perform one normal planned stop/restart with renewed separation and observed safe flow; stop and clean through the approved ordinary method; remove/support attachments as prescribed; fold/stow/secure and complete travel/handover checks.

Pass requires every critical limit and sequence, correct response to signals, all target placements inside the predeclared safe boundaries, protected people, proper supported/retained attachments, effective guards, no pressure opening without verified safe state, controlled concrete discharge and containment, actual planned restart and safe completed cleanout/stow. All restrictions and actual observations must be recorded. A verbal fault inject during a safe pause tests recognition without creating the condition. Do not deliberately admit air, create a blockage, bypass an interlock or approach a live power line.

Assessor intervention to prevent danger fails independent performance. Safe voluntary pauses are permitted. Weather/equipment interruption is recorded separately; restore safe conditions and repeat the complete affected task. Set B changes approved target sequence, placing/containment point or compatible attachment within a newly validated plan. A different machine requires fresh familiarization and complete integrated attempt. Retain first results and remediation evidence.

## Examination and credential decision

Private Form A/B each has eighteen original cases, three scored decisions each, 108 points. Allow 180 minutes with coursebook/calculator, no keys or assistance. Pass at least 87/108 and at least 4/6 per domain with no unresolved critical error. The changed form retains original situation context. This is not the external CCO 80-question test or an ACPA assessment; keys stay outside the learner packet.

Require corrected formative checks, eight labs, eighteen stations, I72 and controlled final before awarding the actual recorded boom-pump operating scope. State equipment class/model/configuration and cleanup method observed. Exclude public-road licensing, unassessed line/separate placing-boom/high-rise systems, compressed-air cleanout, crane use and engineering authority. Missing actual pumping means limited status. Do not report a real learner pass because materials are finished.

Remediation names the specific error, reteaches using an example, includes changed supervised practice and ends with changed independent assessment. Preserve critical-error history. Accommodations support access/communication without removing essential performance. VNDRLY's one-year review reminder is distinct from external certification duration; reassess earlier for equipment/task change, incident or unsafe performance.

## Record forms

**Learner:** identity, diagnostic, prior scope, requested equipment scope, accommodations, actual hours, instructor/assessor qualification and version.

**Validation:** machine/manual/configuration, actual support/reaction basis, setup/level, electrical and traffic plan, line/attachment IDs/ratings/filled mass, concrete/batch/volume, receiving/primeout/restart/washout plan, targets and safe clearances, emergency response, validator/date.

**Observation:** station/set, actual action/result, five scores, critical issue/intervention, signals, startup/restart separation and flow, pressure/containment state, cleanout/stow verification, assessor/date and changed retest.

**Job/defect record:** actual volume/batches, interruptions and authorized mix decisions, fault symptoms, isolated/restricted state, repair and separate release, waste disposition, driver/vehicle readiness and receiving acknowledgment.

**Award:** checks/labs/stations/I72/final, unresolved failures, exact earned scope/exclusions, evidence/version/hash, reviewer/date and review triggers. No prefilled equipment approval or learner pass.

# VUC072 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q01-L1

Can primeout/cleanup be planned after pumping starts?

**Answer:** Resolve approved method and location before starting.

**Reason/remediation:** Missing containment can force an unsafe interruption or discharge. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q01-L2

Does a similar-looking spare coupling prove compatibility?

**Answer:** No. Verify actual size, end style, rating and approved use.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance does not establish safe pressure-system assembly. Revisit Q01 and perform its changed application.

## Q02-L1

Does a pump operating certificate replace a commercial driving license?

**Answer:** No.

## Q03-L2

May a faulty hopper interlock be bypassed for a short pour?

Does pour-supervisor reassurance replace observing site hazards?
