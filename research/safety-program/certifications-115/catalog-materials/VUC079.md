# VNDRLY/U Well Control Awareness

Program VUC079 · Original materials · 18 November 2026

For new entrants, ancillary workers and others who need to recognize well-control concerns. No prior well-control qualification is required. Basic reading and arithmetic support are provided. This is a VNDRLY awareness award, not an IWCF certificate or operator/supervisor authorization.

Outcomes: explain the well life cycle; trace a simplified circulation path; interpret a static pressure example; recognize an unexplained warning; identify containment and intervention boundaries; communicate and follow the site emergency plan. Ten teaching chapters support these outcomes. All numbers, well identifiers and operational cards below are fictional.

## Q79-01 — The well, the industry and your role

Oil and gas occur in pore spaces and fractures within rock, not necessarily in an underground cavern. Exploration estimates whether a resource is present; appraisal reduces uncertainty; development provides wells and facilities; production brings fluids to surface for processing and transport. Water can be produced with hydrocarbons. An unexpected fluid movement matters even when nobody sees flames or smells gas. Well control protects people, the environment and the installation by keeping subsurface fluids within intended boundaries.

A well connects pressure sources at depth to equipment and people at surface. A production target, a reservoir estimate and a safe operating envelope are different information. A new employee may know the name of the reservoir without knowing its pressure. Missing information is a reason to ask the responsible person, not to assume a familiar well is harmless.

Your awareness role is to recognize a condition that may threaten control, communicate what you observed, and follow the emergency instructions for your assigned role. It is not to decide a kill method, close an unfamiliar valve, adjust mud density or issue a restart. Stop your own unsafe activity and raise the alarm through the site procedure. Do not wait for mathematical certainty before reporting an obvious warning. Know the well identifier, supervisor, alarm method and muster location before entering the work area.

Worked situation: a delivery driver sees liquid escaping from a line near a wellhead. The driver should stay clear, notify the designated contact with location and observation, and follow instructions from a safe position. Calling it water because it looks clear is an unsupported diagnosis. Driving through the release to avoid delaying the job creates another exposure.

Changed application: the release stops before the supervisor arrives. Report the time and location anyway; an intermittent symptom is still evidence. Record what was seen rather than claiming the well is safe. A useful report distinguishes observation, uncertainty and requested response.

## Q79-02 — Well life cycle and changing barriers

A typical well passes through planning, construction, completion, production, intervention, suspension and permanent abandonment. The order can include repeated interventions and changed uses. Construction creates the borehole and installs casing and cement. Completion equips the well for its intended flow path. Production operates that path within a managed envelope. Intervention investigates or changes an existing well. Suspension leaves a well for later work; abandonment establishes permanent isolation under the applicable design and regulatory requirements.

A barrier is a means of preventing unintended flow along a particular path. A fluid column, casing, cement, packer, plug and valve may contribute to a barrier system when suitable and verified for the actual condition. Merely naming two items does not demonstrate two independent barriers. They may share a failure path, be in the wrong location or lack evidence of integrity. Awareness learners identify the intended path and ask for the current verified barrier status; competent personnel determine the design and acceptance criteria.

A barrier drawing must match the current operation. Removing tubing, opening an access point or changing fluid can alter what contains pressure. A signature from the previous shift does not prove the configuration remains unchanged. Handover should identify the well, current operation, barrier status, unresolved issues, prohibited actions and the person responsible for the next decision.

Worked situation: a suspended well has a locked surface valve and a drawing showing a downhole plug. The plug verification field is blank. Do not translate the drawing into a claim that both barriers were tested. Ask the responsible supervisor to resolve the evidence before the planned access work. A lock can prevent casual operation but cannot establish the integrity of every flow path.

Changed application: the plug record is found, but it names a different well. The evidence mismatch remains. Correct the identity and configuration before relying on it. Long inactivity, low production and a clean exterior are not proof of zero stored energy.

## Q79-03 — Reading a rig and its circulation path

Land rigs, jack-ups, fixed offshore installations and floating units place equipment in different environments. A floating installation adds movement and marine interfaces; it is not simply a land rig placed on water. At awareness level, identify the work areas, controlled access, alarm arrangements and escape routes rather than assuming equipment locations from a familiar site.

In a simplified conventional drilling circuit, fluid travels from the active tanks through pumps and the drillstring, exits near the bit, and returns through the annulus to surface processing and tanks. The annulus is the space outside the pipe and inside the surrounding hole or casing. Cuttings are carried upward with the returning fluid. Transfers between tanks and additions to the system affect the measured volume. These activities must be communicated so that an unexplained change is not hidden by an assumed transfer.

Separate the hoisting, rotating, circulating and pressure-control functions. A derrick or mast supports lifting operations; the rotary system turns the string; pumps circulate fluid; pressure-control equipment supports containment when the approved operation requires it. Knowing a component's name is not permission to enter its danger zone. Suspended loads, moving pipe, high-pressure lines and rotating equipment create hazards independent of the well's pressure balance.

Worked situation: a simplified drawing labels A active tank, B pump, C drillpipe, D bit, E annulus, F solids processing. Trace A-B-C-D-E-F-A. A learner who traces the normal return through the drillpipe has reversed the conventional classroom model. Actual reverse-circulation and specialist operations require different approved diagrams; do not apply this diagram universally.

Changed application: the drawing is for a subsea installation, but the induction map shows only a land muster point. Request the correct installation instructions before access. The pressure-control location and emergency routes cannot be inferred from a generic training sketch.

## Q79-04 — Pressure, depth and the limits of a simple model

Pressure is force per unit area. Hydrostatic pressure is produced by the weight of a fluid column. In this course's uniform, static liquid model, pressure in psi equals 0.052 multiplied by density in pounds per gallon multiplied by vertical fluid height in feet. The factor is specific to these units. Use true vertical height, not the longer distance measured along a deviated well. State units and assumptions before calculating.

Worked model: a full 4,000 ft vertical column of 10 ppg liquid gives 0.052 × 10 × 4,000 = 2,080 psi. A 5,000 ft measured pipe path to the same vertical depth does not give 2,600 psi of hydrostatic head. If the liquid level drops by 500 vertical ft, the remaining 3,500 ft column gives 1,820 psi. The lost head is 260 psi. These are teaching calculations, not a fluid program or a safe work limit.

Compare pressure at the same depth and on the same reference basis. A gauge at surface is not automatically the pressure at the reservoir. Gas, mixed fluids, temperature, flow friction, surface pressure and transients can make the simple model inadequate. A correct calculation from incomplete inputs can still support a wrong decision. Report the limitation rather than concealing it behind extra decimal places.

Too little wellbore pressure relative to a communicating formation can permit an influx. Excessive pressure can cause losses or other damage. The answer is not always to make the fluid heavier. Qualified personnel evaluate the operating window and the actual condition. The awareness learner does not prescribe a density or pressure setting.

Changed model: 11 ppg and 3,000 ft produce 1,716 psi. At 2,800 ft the result is 1,601.6 psi, a reduction of 114.4 psi. Explain the reduction, then identify why this alone cannot establish whether a real well is safe. No formation pressure, dynamic condition or approved envelope has been supplied.

## Q79-05 — How an influx can develop

An influx is unintended entry of formation fluid into the well. It may involve gas, oil or water. A kick is not synonymous with a visible blowout: an early indication may exist while the system still contains the fluids. A blowout involves loss of control. Prevention and early recognition matter because waiting for an obvious release sacrifices time and control options.

Pressure can become inadequate because the fluid density is unsuitable, the well is not kept full, fluid is lost, formation pressure differs from the forecast, or movement creates a temporary reduction in pressure. Pulling pipe can create a swabbing effect; running pipe can create a pressure increase known as surge. These are reasons to follow the approved movement and monitoring procedure, not instructions to improvise a tripping speed. An apparently adequate static calculation does not eliminate transient effects.

Gas can expand as pressure falls. A small amount at depth can occupy more space nearer surface. Do not use this general principle to forecast a real kick's size without the appropriate model. The practical awareness consequence is that a small early warning deserves attention; it is not safe to wait for a large tank gain or visible gas cloud.

Worked situation: the crew believes the fluid density is correct, but returns are being lost and the fluid level may fall. Correct density alone cannot preserve the head of a column that is no longer full. The learner reports the losses and uncertainty immediately to the responsible operator and follows the assigned response. Adding fluid or changing circulation is the operator's controlled task.

Changed application: no loss is reported, but flow continues unexpectedly after the planned pump stop. Do not dismiss it because the last density sample was correct. Record the observed behavior and alert the responsible person; do not conduct an unauthorized flow test to prove the point.

## Q79-06 — Recognizing signals without explaining them away

Monitoring combines trends, current operations and equipment condition. Unexpected active-volume gain, a return-flow change or flow when it is not expected can be important warning signs. A sensor reading may also be affected by transfers, equipment problems or other operations. An alternative explanation is a hypothesis to check, not permission to suppress a warning. Awareness personnel must report promptly and leave operational diagnosis to the designated competent person.

Use a small balance to understand why communication matters. Start with 80 bbl in the classroom active tank. A documented addition is 3 bbl and there are no other planned transfers. Expected volume is 83 bbl. The display reads 87 bbl, leaving 4 bbl unexplained. Report the actual reading, expected addition and difference. Do not call the unexplained amount a confirmed influx solely from this arithmetic. Equally, do not call it harmless because some addition occurred.

When making a report, include identity, well/location, time, observed change, ongoing operation and any known transfer. Ask for acknowledgment. If the primary contact does not respond, use the site's escalation route. A radio message that was transmitted but not received is not a completed handover. Do not enter a hazardous area to read a gauge more closely.

Worked message: 'This is the tank observer at training well W79. At 10:04 the active display is 87 bbl. We started at 80 and the recorded addition is 3. I have 4 bbl unexplained. Please acknowledge.' This states evidence without pretending to know the cause.

Changed application: maintenance says the display may be faulty, but there is also an unexpected flow observation. Keep both observations in the report. A suspected sensor fault does not invalidate an independent indication. Avoid repeated polling that delays the required alarm or protective action.

## Q79-07 — Containment equipment and evidence of readiness

Pressure-control equipment is a system with components, connections, controls, energy supply, procedures and trained people. A blowout preventer can support containment under its designed conditions. An annular element and different ram arrangements have different capabilities. A device suitable for one pipe condition is not automatically suitable for another. A shear capability, for example, cannot be assumed for every item passing through the stack.

A choke provides controlled restriction in an appropriate flow path; an accumulator stores energy for hydraulic operation. Do not assume a choke will automatically solve a kick or that a closed-command indicator proves the barrier achieved its intended state. Function, position and integrity are different kinds of evidence. A pressure test, function test and visual inspection answer different questions, and each requires defined acceptance criteria.

At awareness level, ask whether the required equipment and current verification are confirmed by the responsible person. Do not perform a test, alter a valve alignment or operate an emergency control outside your authorization. If you are assigned an emergency action, it must be covered by site-specific instruction and competence, not by the fact that this course mentioned the component.

Worked situation: the control panel shows 'close command sent' while the response indication is unavailable. Report the discrepancy. Do not write 'barrier closed and verified' on the handover. The supervisor must use the approved response to establish the actual condition.

Changed application: a pressure-test certificate is valid for a similar stack with another equipment identifier. That is not proof for this assembly. Match the identification, configuration, test conditions and current acceptance record. The course does not supply universal test pressures, intervals or equipment ratings; these must come from the applicable program and competent technical authority.

## Q79-08 — Intervention and workover awareness

Existing wells need investigation, maintenance and repair as well as drilling. An intervention may convey instruments or tools into a well, restore access, remove restrictions or support changes to production. A workover may involve more extensive changes to the completion. The boundary between terms varies with the job and organization; the actual equipment and pressure exposure matter more than the label.

Recognize wireline as a way to convey tools on a line, coiled tubing as continuous small-diameter tubing used for conveyance and/or circulation, and jointed tubing as connected pipe sections. Some operations intentionally retain well pressure while moving equipment through pressure-control arrangements. That requires a designed system and qualified crew. 'No drilling' and 'routine service' do not mean no well-control risk.

Before an intervention, the responsible team establishes the current well condition, intended flow paths, barriers, equipment compatibility and emergency responsibilities. An ancillary worker should understand the exclusion zones and communicate a change that could affect the operation. A service company's familiar equipment does not replace the well owner's current information. Simultaneous activities can affect access, ignition control, communication and response.

Worked situation: a wireline crew is ready to start, but the site briefing describes yesterday's pumping operation. A newcomer should request the current briefing and confirm the responsible contact. Entering because the crew looks experienced transfers an unresolved interface into the work area.

Changed application: a tubing replacement job is described as minor maintenance. Ask what pressure boundary will be opened and which competent person has confirmed the controls. Do not classify the job as low risk solely by its duration. The awareness learner remains outside live pressure-control manipulation and tool deployment.

## Q79-09 — Pressure boundaries during intervention

An intervention pressure-control arrangement must contain pressure while allowing the intended operation. Equipment may include a lubricator to contain a tool assembly, sealing elements around a moving line or tubing, valves and emergency closure provisions. Exact arrangements depend on the conveyance, well conditions and approved procedure. A drawing used for awareness is not an installation specification.

Opening a pressure-containing connection is a distinct exposure. A gauge reading zero is not complete proof that every trapped section is depressurized or isolated. A blocked sensing path, isolated pocket or different pressure source may remain. Only the competent crew using the approved isolation and verification procedure establishes that it is safe to open. Awareness personnel should never loosen a fitting to 'see whether it is still pressured.'

Equipment ratings must be appropriate for the actual service, including pressure, temperature, fluid and configuration. Matching a nominal pressure number alone is inadequate. Seals, line sizes, tool dimensions and emergency closure compatibility are technical matters for the authorized team. A missing equipment record or changed component is information to resolve before relying on the system.

Worked situation: a line-conveyance assembly has been changed after the briefing. The new component has an unreadable identification plate. Report the change and missing traceability. A confident verbal claim that it is the same size does not establish suitability or approved configuration.

Changed application: a gauge reads zero after shutdown, and someone asks a new worker to loosen a union. The safe response is to refuse the unverified opening, keep clear and refer the task to the competent isolation team. The worker may assist only within a specifically authorized role after the controls are established. A completed awareness certificate is not that authorization.

## Q79-10 — Emergency communication, muster and safe handover

An emergency response begins with the site's alarm and role instructions. Recognize the signal, stop your own unsafe exposure, notify through the designated channel and move or remain where the plan directs. Do not approach a release, search for a leak by smell or attempt rescue into a hazardous atmosphere without the required specialist team and equipment. Gas may present flammability, toxicity or oxygen-displacement hazards; absence of odor cannot establish safety.

Muster is an accountability process, not simply standing somewhere away from equipment. Use the assigned route and location unless the emergency instructions require an alternative. Report your identity and any known missing person information. Do not leave the site, return for belongings or self-dispatch to look for a colleague without authorization. A blocked route must be reported and the designated alternative used; do not improvise a path through the hazard.

After a concern, do not assume that silence means all-clear. Restart and re-entry require the responsible authority under the site procedure. Handover must preserve unresolved facts, actions taken, acknowledgments and restrictions. Say 'cause not established' where appropriate. A clean narrative that deletes uncertainty deprives the next team of information.

Worked integrated example: a volume discrepancy is reported during an operation change. The supervisor acknowledges, and an alarm then requires muster. The learner follows the muster route and reports at the register. They do not stay by the tank to collect a better trend. Later the handover records the discrepancy, time, known addition, acknowledgment and muster action; it does not state that an influx was confirmed unless authorized investigation established it.

Changed application: a colleague says the alarm is probably a drill and asks you to keep working. Follow the alarm instruction until an authorized announcement says otherwise. The same disciplined response protects people in a real event and makes a drill meaningful. The final outcome of this course is a person who recognizes, reports and acts within role—not a person authorized to control a well.

## I79 — Integrated awareness handover

Purpose: demonstrate that a learner can connect an observation to safe communication and accountability. Instructor controls all cards; learner is an ancillary observer, never the well operator. Use a paper location board, mock radio, observation form and muster register. The technical supervisor role is played by the instructor. No valve action, kill calculation or real emergency call is requested.

Practice sequence: (1) W79 is changing from pumping to intervention; learner asks for current briefing. (2) Active volume is 80 bbl with 3 added and display 87; learner reports 4 unexplained. (3) Supervisor says the addition explains it; learner respectfully repeats that the addition is already accounted for. (4) Independent unexpected flow is reported. Learner adds it without declaring a proven cause. (5) Alarm requires muster; North is blocked and plan designates West to B. Learner follows the alternate and reports identity. (6) One colleague is absent; learner reports last known facts without self-dispatching a search. (7) No all-clear exists; learner rejects a request to return for records. (8) Learner completes a handover with unresolved cause and authority boundary.

Practice debrief: good performance is not a diagnosis of an actual kick. It is accurate evidence, prompt acknowledged escalation, protective behavior and truthful handover. Do not let arithmetic delay an immediate alarm if the scenario gives an obvious hazard first.

Integrated rubric: six dimensions (current briefing/role, evidence balance, acknowledged escalation, independent-cue treatment, safe muster/accountability, truthful handover), each 0 unsafe/missing, 1 substantially prompted, 2 safe but incomplete, 3 independently correct, 4 correct and clearly explains limitations. Require 20/24 with every dimension at least 3 and no critical error. Dangerous approach/opening, suppressing an unresolved warning, unauthorized equipment manipulation, disregarding the alarm, unauthorized rescue or fabricated verification is a critical failure. ## Final, remediation and award

Final Form A contains ten cases with three scored decisions each: 60 points, 60 minutes, pass 48/60 and at least 4/6 in every domain with no unresolved critical error. Each decision scores 2 correct with sound reason, 1 safe but incomplete, 0 incorrect/missing/unsafe. Coursebook/calculator allowed; keys, other people and outside assistance prohibited. A changed Form B is reserved for reassessment and includes the original context plus the changed task. Assess only the changed task's three decisions. Reasoning and safe limits matter more than matching exact words. Do not round a failed criterion upward.

For an error, identify the exact chapter and behavior, explain the misconception, require a corrected worked example and a changed application. Reassess only after the instructor establishes readiness; repeat all failed stations and the integrated exercise when a critical behavior affected it. Final reassessment uses the complete changed form to preserve the breadth of evidence. If further attempts are needed, an instructor prepares and reviews a genuinely new equivalent form; do not repeatedly expose the same answer key.

Issue 'VNDRLY/U Well Control Awareness' only after all chapters and 20 checks are corrected, ten stations pass, integrated exercise passes and final passes. Retain identity, version/hash, attendance, adjustments, scored papers, individual observations, remediation and assessor/date. A written-only learner receives a record of learning, not this complete award. Certificate lists awareness scope and expressly excludes IWCF accreditation and operational authorization. No external exam or certificate is awarded by this package.

VNDRLY administrative review interval: two years, or earlier after a significant role/procedure change, incident or demonstrated gap. This is an internal training rule, not a claim about IWCF certificate expiry or a regulatory interval. Site induction and task-specific competence remain necessary. Learner may appeal a result to a second competent assessor using retained evidence; protect exam keys and personal records. Never amend an original score without preserving the correction and reason.

# VUC079 formative checks

Attempt before reading the explanation. Correct errors and revisit the matching chapter/station. These are not the controlled final.

## Q79-01-L1

A driver sees a clear liquid escaping near a wellhead. What should the driver report?

**Answer:** The location, time and observed escape, with the fluid identity unknown; remain clear and notify the designated contact.

**Reason/remediation:** Appearance is not reliable identification and investigation must not create exposure. Revisit Q79-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-01-L2

The escape stops. Is reporting still useful?

**Answer:** Yes. Preserve the observation and timing even when the symptom is intermittent.

**Reason/remediation:** Disappearance of a symptom is not proof that its cause was removed. Revisit Q79-01 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-02-L1

A suspended well drawing shows a plug, but verification is blank. Can the plug be counted as verified?

**Answer:** No. Resolve the missing evidence with the responsible supervisor before relying on it.

**Reason/remediation:** A planned component and a verified barrier are different. Revisit Q79-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-02-L2

What must a handover identify beyond the well name?

**Answer:** Current operation and configuration, barrier status, unresolved issues, restrictions and responsible contact.

**Reason/remediation:** The receiving team needs the present condition, not merely historical equipment names. Revisit Q79-02 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-03-L1

Trace the classroom conventional circulation circuit A tank, B pump, C drillpipe, D bit, E annulus, F solids processing.

**Answer:** A-B-C-D-E-F-A.

**Reason/remediation:** Returns rise through the annulus in this particular conventional model. Revisit Q79-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-03-L2

Does a land-rig induction establish offshore muster arrangements?

**Answer:** No. Obtain the actual installation briefing and emergency routes.

**Reason/remediation:** Different environments and configurations require their own instructions. Revisit Q79-03 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-04-L1

Calculate the static pressure for 10 ppg and 4,000 ft vertical height.

**Answer:** 2,080 psi: 0.052 × 10 × 4,000.

**Reason/remediation:** The units and vertical head are part of the model; this is not a field operating limit. Revisit Q79-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-04-L2

The same column loses 500 ft of liquid height. Find the remaining head and reduction.

**Answer:** 1,820 psi remaining and 260 psi reduction.

**Reason/remediation:** Use 3,500 ft remaining height; a suitable density does not compensate for an unfilled column. Revisit Q79-04 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-05-L1

Why can a correct density sample fail to prove safety while fluid is being lost?

**Answer:** The fluid column may no longer be full and pressure can decrease.

**Reason/remediation:** Density is only one input; head and changing conditions also matter. Revisit Q79-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-05-L2

Why report unexpected flow even when the last static calculation looked adequate?

**Answer:** A real observation may indicate conditions outside that simplified calculation.

**Reason/remediation:** Dynamic effects and uncertain inputs can invalidate reassurance based only on static arithmetic. Revisit Q79-05 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-06-L1

Start 80 bbl, documented addition 3 bbl, display 87 bbl. Calculate the unexplained difference and response.

**Answer:** Expected 83 bbl; 4 bbl unexplained. Report immediately with the known addition and actual observation.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not hide the discrepancy or claim its cause is proven by arithmetic alone. Revisit Q79-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-06-L2

A suspect display and independent unexpected flow are reported. May the flow be dismissed?

**Answer:** No. Report both and follow the designated response.

**Reason/remediation:** One potentially faulty source does not invalidate an independent observation. Revisit Q79-06 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-07-L1

Is close command sent the same as barrier closure verified?

**Answer:** No. State the command and the missing achieved-state evidence separately.

**Reason/remediation:** A command, a position indication and integrity confirmation answer different questions. Revisit Q79-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-07-L2

A test record names a different assembly. Is that enough?

**Answer:** No. Resolve identity/configuration and verification for this assembly.

**Reason/remediation:** Similar equipment is not the same traceable equipment. Revisit Q79-07 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-08-L1

Why can a routine wireline job need pressure-control planning?

**Answer:** It accesses an existing well that may retain pressure and uses a specific containment arrangement.

**Reason/remediation:** The absence of drilling does not remove the pressure source. Revisit Q79-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-08-L2

Yesterday’s pumping briefing is offered for today’s tubing job. What is missing?

**Answer:** The current operation, pressure boundaries, controls, exclusion zones and responsibilities.

**Reason/remediation:** An outdated briefing can omit the actual interfaces and hazards. Revisit Q79-08 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-09-L1

A zero gauge is offered as the sole reason to loosen a union. What should the learner do?

**Answer:** Do not open it; keep clear and refer to the competent isolation and verification process.

**Reason/remediation:** Other trapped pressure or a sensing problem may remain. Revisit Q79-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-09-L2

A replacement component has no readable identity. How should an awareness worker respond?

**Answer:** Report the change and unresolved suitability/traceability; do not approve it.

**Reason/remediation:** Nominal size or appearance cannot establish its rating, service compatibility or authorization. Revisit Q79-09 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-10-L1

An alarm sounds and a colleague calls it probably a drill. What governs?

**Answer:** Follow the site alarm and muster instructions until an authorized all-clear or other direction.

**Reason/remediation:** Speculation must not replace the response plan. Revisit Q79-10 and perform its changed application.

## Q79-10-L2

Can the learner return to the tank to improve the event log after muster starts?

**Answer:** No. Follow muster/accountability; report the known observations from safety.

**Reason/remediation:** Data collection must not override emergency protection. Revisit Q79-10 and perform its changed application.
