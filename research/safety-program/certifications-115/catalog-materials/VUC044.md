# VNDRLY/U Fabrication — Welder Qualification Learning Program

VUC044 · Original available-source instruction · 2026-11-18

## Purpose and credential boundary

This program teaches welders to prepare, perform and document welding within a defined approved procedure and performance-test scope. It covers SMAW, GMAW, FCAW and GTAW fundamentals and the qualification process; a learner's practical scope is only the process/joint/position actually demonstrated and assessed. Written knowledge is not proof of deposited-weld skill. Employer authorization and external certifications remain separate.

AWS's Certified Welder program requires its own hands-on performance test through an AWS Accredited Test Facility.[S01] VNDRLY cannot issue that credential. This original program provides complete instruction, exercises and assessment tasks without claiming external-code equivalence. Live training/testing requires a competent supervised welding facility using an actually approved applicable WPS and test basis; synthetic record exercises are not operating procedures. No live welding has occurred merely because these materials are written.

## Q01 — Qualification is bounded performance

A welder performance qualification demonstrates a person's ability under defined conditions. It is different from procedure qualification, which supports the welding method, and from inspection of an actual production weld. Passing one coupon does not automatically qualify every material, thickness, process or position. The applicable program determines scope and maintenance; record actual variables rather than relying on the word“certified.”

Before work, compare the assigned production task with the actual supported scope. If an important variable is outside it or unknown, ask the responsible welding authority before proceeding. Do not change an old record to match new work. A knowledge-course completion can support readiness but must not be presented as a weld-performance result.

**Worked case.** A learner passes a flat fillet demonstration with SMAW. No automatic GTAW pipe or overhead qualification follows. **Changed case:** an employer authorizes another task; authorization still needs a valid competence/qualification basis, not merely a new job title.

## Q02 — Prepare a controlled work area

Welding combines radiant energy, hot metal, electricity, fumes and fire hazards. Establish the actual work authorization, shielding of nearby people, suitable PPE, stable position, ventilation and safe materials before energizing. OSHA's welding provisions address relevant controls; the applicable sector and task matter.[S02] An instructor demonstration does not waive them.

Keep combustibles, trip hazards and other people clear under the facility rules. Know emergency stop, fire response and reporting arrangements. Do not weld unknown containers, closed volumes, contaminated parts or live equipment as a learning exercise. The training supervisor must establish a safe coupon task and intervene when controls fail.

**Worked case.** A clean practice coupon is moved beside flammable storage. The coupon's cleanliness does not make the new location suitable. **Changed case:** storage is removed but nearby people lack arc screening; resolve both independently.

## Q03 — Electrical circuit and equipment condition

The welding circuit includes the power source, leads, electrode/gun/torch and work return. The work-return connection is not a substitute for protective grounding of equipment. Use the actual equipment instructions and facility inspection process. Damaged insulation, wet conditions, loose connections or unsuitable setup require correction by the authorized competent person before exposure.

Do not touch energized parts or improvise repairs. Isolate equipment before adjustments that require isolation and before servicing. Inspect holders, cables and connections within training authority; do not open a power source. Cable routing must avoid mechanical damage and trip hazards. Stable dry gloves and clothing are not a reason to ignore defective equipment.

**Worked case.** A cable has exposed conductor near the holder. Stop use and report it; wrapping an unknown repair around it is not proof of safe condition. **Changed case:** cable replaced but the work-return path remains inappropriate; verify the whole setup.

## Q04 — Fire, hot metal and stored contamination

Sparks and heat can reach beyond the visible arc area. Review both sides of the work and nearby openings under the hot-work system. Required fire watch and post-work observation follow the actual task/facility rules. Mark and handle hot coupons with suitable tools; appearance is not a reliable temperature indication.

Never assume an apparently empty drum or pipe is safe to weld. Residual substances, coatings or enclosed volumes can create serious hazards. Such work is outside the supplied beginner coupon activities and requires a separate competent procedure. A learner should recognize and decline the unsupported task rather than experiment.

**Worked case.** A sealed scrap section is offered for practice because it“only held water.” Its history and trapped volume are unverified. Use the approved clean open coupon instead. **Changed case:** an end is cut off but contamination remains unknown; opening alone does not establish suitability.

## Q05 — Fumes, ventilation and exposure

Fume composition depends on process, consumable, base metal, coatings and conditions. Visible smoke amount is not a complete exposure measure. Use specified local exhaust/ventilation and exposure controls; respiratory protection, where required, needs the actual program, selection and fit requirements. Do not place your face in the plume to see better.

Unknown coatings or residues should be identified and handled under the approved preparation process. Grinding can create its own exposure and projectile hazards. If extraction fails, stop the affected work and restore required controls rather than increase fan improvisation or rely on a dust mask. Confined-space welding is not authorized by this course.

**Worked case.** Extraction stops during a practice bead. Follow the facility's stop/isolation/report process; the last few centimeters are not an exception. **Changed case:** extraction works but shielding gas flow is disturbed by a new draft; safety and weld quality both need competent review.

## Q06 — Compressed gases and shielding systems

Gas systems require correct cylinder identity, compatible regulators/connections, secure storage/handling and inspection under manufacturer/facility instructions. Do not identify gas by cylinder color alone or use improvised adapters. Protect cylinders from heat/damage and keep required valve protection during movement. Never use oxygen for ventilation or cleaning.

Shielding gas choice and flow are procedure variables; more flow is not automatically better. Leaks, drafts or wrong mixtures can undermine the weld and create exposure/asphyxiation hazards. Perform checks only by the approved method; do not use a flame. Qualified personnel resolve damaged regulators or uncertain fittings.

**Worked case.** Correct wire is paired with an unidentified gas cylinder. Wire identity does not establish shielding suitability. **Changed case:** gas identity confirmed but regulator connection leaks; identification does not close the equipment defect.

## Q07 — Read the WPS before setting the machine

A welding procedure specification gives the applicable production instructions: process, joint/material, consumable, polarity, parameter ranges, shielding, thermal and technique requirements as relevant. Read the full current document, including notes and referenced details. A machine setting chart is not necessarily a qualified WPS, and a procedure qualification record records supporting facts rather than automatically directing every production setting.

Compare the coupon or workpiece with the WPS scope. Verify actual material thickness and joint preparation instead of assuming a familiar task. If a required field is missing or contradictory, stop dependent setup and obtain the authorized clarification. Do not choose values merely because they worked on another material.

**Worked case.** Synthetic WPS supports6–10 mm plate; actual plate12 mm. Changing current does not extend procedure scope. **Changed case:** plate changed to8 mm but joint type differs; thickness compliance alone is insufficient.

## Q08 — Base material and surface preparation

Identity and condition determine whether material is suitable for the procedure. Keep grade/heat linkage and separate unverified scrap from approved training or production coupons. Clean surfaces by the specified method to remove contaminants that interfere with welding, without grinding away required dimensions or introducing another hazard.

Different alloys and coatings can demand different preparation, shielding and thermal controls. Similar appearance does not establish equivalence. Cutting and beveling must preserve geometry and safe edges; inspect the actual result before fit-up. Use stable fixtures and appropriate guarding for any authorized preparation task.

**Worked case.** Two visually similar plates have different grades and only one matches the WPS. Use verified identity, not color or weight. **Changed case:** correct grade has oil residue; identity does not replace preparation.

## Q09 — Consumable identity and condition

Electrode/wire/filler classification, size and actual product condition must match the procedure and manufacturer guidance. Keep package/lot identification where required. Storage and exposure controls vary by consumable; do not invent universal oven temperatures or reconditioning cycles. A low-hydrogen label does not prove the electrode's handling history.

Prevent mixing consumables at the bench and identify returned material. Damaged coating, rust or uncertain condition requires disposition. The filler classification's strength designation is not a complete selection rule; chemistry, process and service requirements also matter. Do not substitute a convenient size or product without the applicable approval.

**Worked case.** Correct classification sits in an unlabeled open container with unknown exposure. Obtain valid condition evidence or approved replacement; a certificate alone is insufficient. **Changed case:** controlled storage is verified, but diameter differs from the WPS; resolve that independent variable.

## Q10 — Joint preparation, fit-up and tacks

Root opening, bevel, land, alignment, restraint and backing influence access and fusion. Measure the actual joint against the supplied drawing/WPS before welding. Tacks can become part of the weld and must meet applicable requirements; cracked or unsuitable tacks are not made acceptable merely by covering them.

Use approved fixtures and sequence rather than excessive force to hide poor fit. Distortion control must not introduce unsafe loading or unapproved thermal treatment. Record or demonstrate required fit-up holds before the joint is obscured. A learner should be able to explain why a gap or mismatch matters, not just reproduce a number.

**Worked case.** Supplied gap2–3 mm; actual3.4 mm at one end. The local0.4 mm excess needs correction/disposition, not averaging with a2.2 mm opposite end. **Changed case:** geometry corrected but tack cracked; both conditions must be addressed.

## Q11 — Set and verify actual parameters

Select process mode, polarity, consumable and settings according to the approved procedure and equipment instructions. Setpoints are not always actual arc measurements. Keep the distinction between current, voltage, wire-feed speed, travel speed and heat input; units and measurement conventions matter.

Before a live demonstration, the instructor verifies setup and safe work. Make approved adjustments only within the applicable range and record them where required. Do not bypass interlocks or exceed equipment duty limits to finish a coupon. If operation is unstable, identify cause with the instructor rather than indiscriminately raise energy.

**Worked case.** Synthetic allowed current100–130 A; actual recorded135 A is5 high even if the dial was set125. **Changed case:** measurement instrument is invalid; resolve actual evidence before claiming either conformity or a precise deviation.

## Q12 — SMAW technique and control

SMAW uses a consumable coated electrode; maintaining the arc and controlling the molten pool require coordinated electrode advance and travel. Current, arc length, electrode orientation, manipulation and speed influence bead quality.[S03] Use the instructor's approved procedure-specific demonstration rather than treat a single angle or motion as universal.

Watch the pool and joint edges while maintaining safe posture and visibility. An electrode that sticks or an unstable arc calls for a controlled stop and instructor diagnosis under equipment rules, not bare-hand contact or unsafe pulling. Clean slag between passes by the approved safe method and inspect starts/stops and tie-ins. Do not bury a known discontinuity under another pass.

**Worked case.** A learner travels faster to compensate for poor pool visibility, leaving uneven edge tie-in. Improve safe view/technique within procedure before continuing. **Changed case:** bead appearance improves but interpass cleaning is omitted; surface shape does not establish internal quality.

## Q13 — GMAW process variables

GMAW continuously feeds wire and uses external shielding. Wire, gas, transfer mode, polarity, voltage/feed relationship and technique must suit the applicable procedure. A machine's synergic preset is not evidence that every material/position is qualified. Maintain the specified contact-tip-to-work relationship and shielding arrangement without inventing universal distances.

Inspect wire feed, contact tip and gas path under safe isolation instructions when troubleshooting. Porosity or erratic operation may involve contamination, shielding, settings or equipment; diagnose one supported cause at a time with the instructor. Do not assume a smooth bead demonstrates adequate fusion.

**Worked case.** Correct wire diameter is used with the wrong gas mixture. Diameter match does not establish process conformity. **Changed case:** gas corrected but transfer mode outside procedure; another independent variable remains.

## Q14 — FCAW and slag-bearing processes

FCAW uses a tubular consumable whose formulation and shielding requirements matter. Self-shielded and gas-shielded products are not automatically interchangeable. Match actual electrode, polarity, gas where required and procedure. Slag management and access between passes affect quality; a high deposition rate is not a reason to omit cleaning or inspection.

Control technique within the approved product/procedure guidance, especially around toes, starts and stops. Avoid trapping slag by depositing over an unresolved irregularity. Correct equipment or technique problems through the instructor rather than change shielding assumptions. Fume and work-area controls remain required regardless of whether external gas is used.

**Worked case.** A gas-shielded product is used without external gas because another FCAW wire did not need it. Product-family name does not establish shielding equivalence. **Changed case:** correct gas added, but slag from the prior pass remains; both process and cleaning evidence matter.

## Q15 — GTAW cleanliness and coordination

GTAW uses a nonconsumable tungsten electrode and may add separate filler. Electrode preparation, shielding, polarity/mode, material cleanliness and filler identity follow the actual procedure. Touching the tungsten into the pool or contaminating filler can require a controlled stop and approved correction. Do not conceal contamination by simply continuing.

Coordinate torch movement and filler addition under instructor demonstration while maintaining shielding and safe hand position. Base-material differences can change shielding and thermal needs; carbon-steel practice does not automatically qualify reactive alloys. Avoid excessive or unapproved adjustments to chase bead appearance.

**Worked case.** Filler rod touches a contaminated surface before entering the pool. Treat its condition under the approved procedure rather than assume the arc sterilizes it. **Changed case:** filler replaced, but tungsten contamination remains; address each source.

## Q16 — Position, access and progression

Position changes how gravity, pool control, visibility and access affect technique. A learner should first demonstrate the approved basic task, then progress to additional positions through supervised training and the actual qualification route. Do not infer scope from a casual description such as“all position”on a consumable package.

Secure the coupon in the assigned position and do not rotate it during a controlled performance test unless the test rules permit it. Moving it to make the weld easier changes what was demonstrated. Record progression and actual position; distinguish practice assistance from independent test performance.

**Worked case.** A vertical test coupon is rotated flat for the final pass. The record cannot claim an unchanged vertical demonstration. **Changed case:** instructor permits rotation during practice; that practice accommodation is not automatically permitted in the qualification test.

## Q17 — Pass sequence, starts and stops

Follow the approved sequence and inspect each stage that can be concealed. Root, fill and cap have different access and geometry concerns. Clean between passes, handle starts/stops and maintain required bead placement without inventing a universal weave pattern. Repair or grinding between test passes must follow the test rules and be recorded.

Avoid depositing over visible cracks, slag or unresolved fusion concerns. Ask the instructor for diagnosis in practice; during controlled testing, follow the proctor's allowed communication rules. Distinguish learning feedback from coaching that would invalidate independent performance evidence.

**Worked case.** A root discontinuity is visible but learner wants to hide it with fill. Stop and obtain allowed disposition. **Changed case:** root accepted but cap exceeds supplied profile limit; earlier acceptance does not clear later geometry.

## Q18 — Preheat, interpass and heat input

Thermal controls can affect cooling, microstructure, hydrogen cracking, distortion and properties. More heat is not universally safer. Measure at required locations/times using suitable equipment and follow minimum/maximum limits. A remote surface temperature does not automatically represent the joint.

For steady supplied values, arc energy per length can be calculated asV×A×60/(1000×travel mm/min), in kJ/mm. A net heat-input convention may include an efficiency factor; do not compare unlike quantities. This teaching equation does not establish a qualified parameter window or validate pulsed averages.

**Worked case.**24 V,150 A,180 mm/min gives1.2 kJ/mm arc energy. At supplied efficiency0.8 the illustrative net value is0.96. **Changed case:** actual criterion is arc-energy maximum1.1; using0.96 to claim pass would be wrong.

## Q19 — Recognize and report discontinuities

Porosity, slag inclusions, lack of fusion, cracks, undercut, overlap and profile problems have different causes and detection limits. A welder should recognize visible signs and seek diagnosis, not issue unsupported internal-quality claims. The applicable test/production criteria determine acceptance. A discontinuity name alone does not supply an allowable size.

Use records of preparation, consumables, parameters and technique to investigate causes. Correct one variable at a time under approved guidance and verify the result. Do not grind away identification or alter a test specimen beyond permitted preparation. Preserve the original observation and repair history.

**Worked case.** A smooth cap covers uncertain root fusion. Surface appearance cannot establish the hidden root. **Changed case:** qualified examination clears the root but finds another indication; evaluate each under the actual basis.

## Q20 — Plan the performance test scope

Before testing identify the governing program/WPS, process, material, joint, dimensions, position, backing, variables, examination/testing and permitted assistance. The actual authorized test plan establishes what a pass can support. The learner must understand identity and independence requirements and when a pause or restart is allowed.

Separate instructor practice from qualification testing. External programs may require particular facilities/personnel and independence; use their current rules when seeking that credential.[S01] VNDRLY's own learning assessment must also retain a truthful record of assistance. Never transform a coached practice coupon into an independent qualification result by changing its label.

**Worked case.** A coupon passes after the instructor completed the root. It is a joint training product, not the learner's independent full-weld test. **Changed case:** learner repeats the full coupon independently under the approved plan; record it as a separate attempt, not overwrite the first.

## Q21 — Coupon identity and specimen preparation

Mark and preserve coupon identity under the test rules so the finished weld, samples and laboratory reports remain linked. Record actual dimensions, material, process/position and permitted interventions. Cutting test specimens can remove identifiers; transfer them through the controlled process before that link is lost.

Specimen locations, preparation, conditioning and test methods follow the actual plan. Grinding or machining can alter evidence if done improperly; learners must not prepare or substitute samples to remove a known defect outside the permitted method. Only qualified personnel operate destructive test equipment.

**Worked case.** Two coupons are cut together and labels mixed. A favorable report cannot be assigned to the preferred learner. **Changed case:** photos recover one sample's identity; the other remains unresolved until valid evidence is found.

## Q22 — Interpret visual and test results

Visual, bend, fracture, tensile and NDT results address different aspects of performance. Apply the specified method and criteria, including location and specimen validity. A passing tensile number does not automatically cancel a failed bend criterion or wrong sample identity. Test limitations should appear in the record.

Basic arithmetic helps detect errors: tensile stress is load divided by original specified area under the stated test calculation. Actual test standards define additional details; classroom numbers are not a substitute. The authorized examiner determines conformity within their role, and the learner should understand why a result passes, fails or remains invalid.

**Worked case.** Supplied load45,000 N/area90 mm² gives500 MPa. Whether it passes depends on the supplied criterion and valid specimen. **Changed case:** numerical criterion met but identity wrong; no learner qualification follows from that result.

## Q23 — Remediation, repair and honest retest

A failed attempt should lead to diagnosis and targeted practice, not concealed record changes. Identify whether the issue was preparation, parameters, technique, process control, examination validity or understanding. Follow the actual retest rules; do not assume one quick repair always rescues a qualification coupon.

During training, instructor feedback can be detailed. During a controlled independent test, allowed intervention is bounded by the test plan. Preserve first result, coaching, changed practice and new test outcome. A second person's weld or selected favorable samples cannot substitute for the candidate's demonstrated performance.

**Worked case.** Learner fails a bend and repeats after targeted root-control practice. Keep both attempts and valid new specimen records. **Changed case:** only the easy region is sampled without authorization; that is not a valid retest under the original plan.

## Q24 — Scope records, continuity and production assignment

The final record should identify what was actually demonstrated, the governing basis, supported scope, examiner, date, results and maintenance conditions. Continuity rules belong to the actual qualification program and process; a payroll entry or job title does not necessarily prove relevant process use. AWS describes its own maintenance system; VNDRLY must not invent compliance with it.[S01]

Before production assignment, compare current supported scope with the actual task and confirm any employer requirements. A lapse, new process or changed variable may need review/requalification under the applicable system. Record unknowns and obtain the proper decision instead of backdating activity. The course's materials completion does not mean any learner has welded or qualified.

**Worked case.** Record supports one process but continuity evidence describes another. Do not treat them as interchangeable. **Changed case:** correct process use is documented but a new joint/position exceeds supported scope; continuity does not expand the qualification.

# VUC044 coached process clinics

These clinics connect the fundamentals to observed practice. They do not provide substitute WPS limits. Instructor selects the approved training task and demonstrates it on actual equipment before the learner rehearses. Observe one defined skill at a time, explain its effect, then integrate skills. Record guidance during practice separately from the independent performance attempt.

## C01 — SMAW: seeing and controlling the pool

Arrange safe access and a supported stance that permits a clear protected view of the joint. Identify the approved electrode, polarity and current range before striking. The electrode is consumed as travel proceeds: advancing along the joint and feeding toward the work are different motions that must remain coordinated. Losing that distinction can lengthen the arc as the electrode shortens. Watch the actual pool/joint relationship rather than merely copying a hand speed. Miller identifies current, arc length, angle, manipulation and travel as interacting technique elements.[S03]

Practice sequence: first, de-energized dry travel along the intended joint to check access and cable freedom; second, instructor demonstrates a short approved practice bead while describing observations; third, learner repeats with feedback; fourth, compare the cooled bead and actual logged variables with task criteria. If the electrode sticks, use the equipment's approved safe release/isolation method; do not grab exposed energized parts. Clean and inspect between passes as required. A narrow bead, wide bead or irregular ripple is an observation that prompts investigation, not a universal proof of one cause.

**Worked diagnosis:** the initial arc is stable, then grows long as the electrode burns down. The learner kept longitudinal travel but failed to feed the shortening electrode. Practice the combined motions within approved variables; do not automatically increase current to mask the control issue. **Changed diagnosis:** arc length remains stable but travel becomes erratic when the cable snags. Correct safe layout/access before more practice. **Teach-back:** explain how electrode consumption changes the required hand motion, what must be observed at the joint and why appearance alone is not qualification.

## C02 — GMAW: separating setup from technique

GMAW feeds a continuous electrode through a gun. The feed system, power settings, shielding and torch manipulation form one controlled setup. Verify wire identity/diameter, compatible consumable parts, correct gas and actual mode using the approved equipment procedure. Isolate as required before setup; wire-feed mechanisms and exposed wire introduce their own contact/pinch hazards. Keep the gun/cable arrangement suitable for stable feed. Voltage, wire-feed speed and travel affect the process together; changing one value while ignoring the others is not a sound diagnostic method.[S09]

Practice dry motion first. Observe whether access allows the approved gun orientation and consistent relationship to the joint. During supervised welding, maintain the required technique and monitor actual process behavior. After safe cooling, compare measured bead geometry and required examination findings with the task basis. Excess spatter, unstable arc or irregular geometry can arise from several setup or technique problems. Inspect the system and approved settings before guessing. Do not change transfer mode merely to improve appearance during a controlled test.

**Worked diagnosis:** the learner reports erratic feed and compensates with frequent uncontrolled speed changes. Pause and have competent personnel check the actual feed path/setup; then repeat controlled practice with stable verified conditions. **Changed diagnosis:** feed is stable but shielding is disrupted by airflow. Resolve protection/ventilation compatibility through the supervisor; turning off required extraction to obtain a prettier bead is unacceptable. **Teach-back:** identify what can be corrected within authorized practice, what requires procedure review and what must be preserved in the record.

## C03 — FCAW: identify the system before applying technique

Flux-cored wire is not a single interchangeable system. Some consumables use external shielding gas and others do not; their instructions and approved procedures differ.[S10] The core and resulting slag influence welding and interpass preparation. Identify the exact consumable before selecting equipment, polarity, gas or technique. A spool from another job is not acceptable merely because it fits the feeder.

In supervised practice, establish the actual approved setup and safe access, then deposit the planned pass while observing the joint/pool. Allow safe handling conditions and remove slag through the approved method before the next pass. Examine the cleaned area and resolve indications before covering them. Never assume flux makes surface preparation unnecessary. Grinding/chipping methods require their own eye/face, noise, dust and equipment controls. The welder records genuine interruptions and corrections rather than presenting a smooth final surface as evidence that all steps were followed.

**Worked diagnosis:** a learner copies a self-shielded setup for a wire requiring external gas. The problem starts with consumable identification, not hand technique. Stop, verify the correct system and obtain disposition of affected work. **Changed diagnosis:** correct gas is present, but unremoved slag remains at a pass boundary. Resolve cleaning and examination under the approved task rules before proceeding. **Teach-back:** explain why process name alone does not establish gas requirements and why the next pass must not conceal unresolved conditions.

## C04 — GTAW: coordinate heat source, filler and shielding

GTAW uses a tungsten electrode to establish the arc; it is not intended to be consumed as filler. Added filler, when required, is separately controlled. Clean appropriate material, suitable tungsten preparation and correct shielding are foundational.[S04] Prepare and maintain the actual equipment in accordance with manufacturer and WPS instructions. Use the required safe preparation tools and control any preparation dust. Do not substitute a gas mixture from another process because a cylinder is convenient.

Begin with isolated dry coordination: torch path, filler approach and any approved current control must be reachable without unstable posture. Instructor demonstrates how the actual approved task establishes and maintains the pool, introduces filler where required and ends the weld under its specified controls. Learner practices coordinated movement rather than chasing the bead with unrelated motions. Shielding coverage depends on setup and conditions; excessive gas flow can create turbulence rather than guarantee protection.[S05] If contamination or shielding failure is observed, stop and resolve it through the approved method; do not keep depositing over an unresolved condition.

**Worked diagnosis:** filler contacts the tungsten and an abnormal event is observed. Identify the interruption, stop and obtain authorized electrode/weld correction before continuing. **Changed diagnosis:** no contact occurs, but a draft affects coverage. Investigate environmental/setup conditions without defeating required ventilation. **Teach-back:** distinguish tungsten, filler and shielding functions, explain the stop decision and identify the evidence needed for a valid resumed attempt.

Training bay packet: clean known coupons; current approved facility WPS; screened adjacent walkway; one lead with exposed conductor; ventilation fan fault; correct labeled filler in sealed package; cylinder unsecured; emergency stop identified. Learner proposes welding because “only ten minutes.” Produce a stop/correct/recheck sheet identifying each independent control.

For this exercise only, efficiency factor is0.8 and the specified maximum is **arc energy1.1kJ/mm**, not efficiency-adjusted heat input. Calculate both quantities and decide the specified comparison. T2 records22V,120A,180mm/min with the same factor and limit.

These calculations do not alone authorize a procedure.

## W05 — Technique observation and causal reasoning

Four supervised practice records: A/SMAW: frequent long-arc excursions and inconsistent travel; B/GMAW: an unapproved transfer mode selected; C/FCAW: interpass slag left in place; D/GTAW: tungsten contacted the pool and welding continued. For each identify the observable departure, a safe training response and evidence needed before another assessed attempt. Avoid diagnosing every discontinuity from appearance alone.

**Model:** A requires coordinated arc/travel practice with the approved procedure; B requires mode/procedure review, not simply reducing visible spatter; C needs approved cleaning and examination before covering a pass; D needs stopping and authorized contamination correction, with affected weld disposition. Keep actual records. **Changed:** attractive surfaces are produced for all four. Appearance does not erase process deviations or prove internal soundness. **Instructor probe:** identify where coaching is permitted during practice and where it would invalidate a claimed independent assessment.

## W06 — Coupon and specimen chain

Candidate Noor welds coupon N08. Witness signs the original permanent mark. Cutting plan calls for specimens N08-A and N08-B. Laboratory receives bags marked N80-A and N08-B; courier record gives N08-A/B but no photograph or witnessed correction. Laboratory proposes issuing both as N08 because handwriting “looks similar.” Explain what may and may not be accepted and prepare a discrepancy record.

**Model:** hold disputed identity; preserve actual labels, courier record and original witness chain. A plausible typo is not established traceability. Obtain authorized documented reconciliation with evidence or replace/retest under the test plan; do not relabel secretly. # VUC044 delivery and actual practical evaluation

## Entry, delivery and limits

Instructor: demonstrably competent in the selected welding process, equipment, local safety controls and approved performance-test basis. Assessor must be competent to judge the specified task and preserve independent candidate work. External AWS testing requires AWS's own authorized route. This course cannot appoint an AWS examiner.

Entry check: learner interprets units, reads a short procedure and explains a stop instruction; provide supported teaching/access accommodations where needed without changing technical or safety criteria. Confirm the learner can safely use the selected equipment under the facility's requirements. No prior qualification is assumed. Use plain language, diagrams, demonstration, guided rehearsal, teach-back and an independent attempt. Offer translated instructional support while preserving controlled procedure meaning and independent assessment.

Planned supervised learning:60hours—12 teaching/demonstration,12 paper workbook and parameter practice,24 supervised process practice/stations,4 integrated dossier,4 remediation and4 written final. This is a planning allocation, not a claim that60hours guarantees welding proficiency. Extend supervised practice when necessary; do not shorten evidence requirements to fit the timetable. Every learner studies all four process fundamentals; the awarded practical scope includes only processes actually independently demonstrated. A learner pursuing multiple processes needs separate practical records and enough additional training.

Sequence: Q01–06 with P01–06 before any energized work; Q07–11 and W01–04 with setup checks; Q12–19 with instructor demonstration, repeated practice and corresponding process observations; Q20–24 with traceability/results work; W05–08 and I44; independent practical and controlled written final. Stop and reteach at unsafe or misunderstood steps. Revisit the changed case after correction.

## Equipment and controlled task packet

Use a properly supervised welding training facility: suitable inspected power source and correct process accessories, screened welding station, validated task ventilation, task-specific PPE, safe gas supply where applicable, approved identified coupons/consumables, fixtures, suitable dimensional/temperature/parameter instruments, marked cooling area and emergency equipment. Specimen cutting/testing is performed by appropriately competent facility personnel with their own controlled equipment; learners do not improvise destructive tests.

Before a live attempt the responsible facility authority signs an actual packet: WPS and revision; applicable test/acceptance basis; coupon material, dimensions, joint, position and progression; permitted process/consumables/parameters; required measurements; coupon identification and cut map; required visual/mechanical/NDT examinations and acceptance criteria; examiner authority; permitted assistance; retest rules. **Do not substitute workbook S44/R44 for that packet.** The supplied task sequence, candidate record and scoring below are complete course evaluation instructions; actual welding limits must come from the valid procedure for the selected equipment/material, not an invented universal training WPS. Missing approved task inputs mean the live assessment is not ready, not that a quiz can replace it.

## Station scoring and performance gate

Each P station: four scored elements,0–2 each.2=correct independent performance with explanation/evidence;1=safe but incomplete requiring a prompt;0=incorrect, missing or unsafe. Require at least6/8 at every station and correction of every technical gap. Safety-critical acts, invented evidence, unauthorized variable change or substituted candidate work fail the attempt regardless of total. Assessor records actual observations, not a prefilled pass. Guided learning and independent assessment are separate records.

For knowledge-only process stations, use actual isolated equipment, approved illustrations or supplied records; do not falsely record live welding. For the **selected practical process**, the corresponding P12/P13/P14/P15 must be observed live, along with P02/P03/P07/P08/P09/P10/P11/P16/P17/P18/P19/P20/P21/P22/P23/P24 as applicable to the approved task. P23 can use a no-failure scenario to demonstrate the correct retest decision; it does not require deliberately failing a live coupon. P04–06 controls must be verified before live work. The independent coupon must satisfy every actual approved acceptance requirement. Station averages and written scores cannot override a failed required coupon test.

Score classification, evidence boundary, assignment comparison, accurate wording. Fail any claim of unlimited or AWS qualification from the VNDRLY worksheet.

## P02 — Bay readiness

Use the actual de-energized bay and W02 defect cards. Candidate walks the area, identifies controls, stops at deficiencies and records rechecks. Score people/fire protection, ventilation/PPE, defect response, documented release. No actual dangerous defect is introduced for testing.

## P03 — Electrical readiness

With isolated equipment and the equipment manual, candidate identifies welding circuit components, work return, equipment grounding and damage-reporting boundaries. Score identification, inspection within authority, isolation/repair boundary, correct return setup under supervision. Do not energize until supervisor accepts readiness.

## P04 — Fire and hot-metal controls

Provide photograph cards of a clean coupon, closed drum and contaminated part; use a safe mock layout. Candidate segregates prohibited/unreviewed work and plans safe cooling/marking. Score hazard recognition, authorization, nearby exposure controls, residual-hot-metal communication.

## P05 — Ventilation response

Demonstrate the facility's approved ventilation check without disabling protection during welding. Present fan-failure card. Score readiness check, prompt stop, exposure reporting, valid restart evidence. Respirator use must follow the applicable employer program; this station does not qualify respiratory users.

## P06 — Gas and filler match

Use restrained cylinders or label cards and the actual WPS. Candidate matches gas identity/regulator compatibility and filler to the process, identifying unsafe substitutions. Score identity, condition/restraint, compatibility verification, discrepancy action. Never test a leak with flame or introduce incompatible gas.

## P07 — Read the approved WPS

Score completeness, actual applicability, revision control, unresolved-variable stop. Instructor must not supply answers during the independent attempt.

## P08 — Prepare identified material

Candidate verifies coupon identity and dimensions, prepares surfaces by the approved method and preserves markings. Score traceability, dimensional checks, safe preparation, clean suitable condition. Unknown coatings or contaminated parts are held for review, not ground/welded experimentally.

## P09 — Consumable control

## P10 — Fit-up measurement

Use a de-energized fixture, coupon and suitable gauge. Candidate measures required stations, records individual readings and compares each with the approved limits. Score gauge use, locations/units, local comparison, authorized correction/recheck. A mean cannot conceal a local failure.

## P11 — Actual parameter observation

Supervisor provides safe instrument access. Candidate distinguishes settings from measured values and records the approved parameter observations; use W03 for a mismatch exercise. Score instrument/basis, units, actual recording, deviation response. No probing energized internals.

## P12 — SMAW coordination

Instructor demonstrates under the actual WPS; candidate practices starts, steady arc/travel, required position and interpass cleaning. For a selected SMAW independent attempt, candidate performs the required coupon without technique coaching. Score controlled starts/travel, approved variables, cleaning/pass management, honest record. Visual neatness alone is not final acceptance.

## P13 — GMAW coordination

On isolated equipment first, identify wire feed, contact tip, shielding and required mode. Practice gun control under the actual WPS. If selected for qualification, independently perform the required coupon. Score correct setup/mode, coordinated travel, shielding and interruption management, actual record. Do not substitute an appealing machine preset for procedure applicability.

## P14 — FCAW coordination

Identify whether the actual consumable requires external shielding, then practice the specified technique and interpass cleaning. If selected, independently weld the required coupon. Score correct wire/shielding system, approved variables, slag removal before covering, evidence. Do not assume all flux-cored wires are self-shielded.

## P15 — GTAW coordination

## P16 — Position and progression

Candidate verifies fixture orientation against the approved packet and maintains permitted progression. Score initial verification, stable safe access, adherence, recorded departures. Rotating the coupon to an easier position without permission changes the claimed demonstration.

## P17 — Pass and interruption management

Candidate records pass sequence, cleaning, stop/restart and inspections on the supplied pass log. Score sequence, preparation for next pass, approved restart, traceability. During assessment, unauthorized instructor repair or completion of the weld invalidates independent performance.

## P18 — Thermal measurement

Using task-suitable instruments and approved method, candidate measures at required locations/times and records interpass/preheat requirements. Use W04 for separate arithmetic. Score method/location, actual reading, comparison to correct quantity, stop/correction boundary. No bare-hand temperature estimate or fabricated reading.

## P19 — Examine and report

After safe cooling, candidate examines the coupon under the applicable method and records indications without unauthorized grinding or concealment. Score coverage, accurate description/location, comparison to supplied criteria, disposition/referral. Candidate self-check does not replace required examiner acceptance.

## P20 — Independent attempt control

Assessor verifies identity, approved task, permitted assistance and start authorization; candidate explains rules and completes the selected live task. Score identity, task comprehension, independence, truthful record. Log safety interventions; do not convert a coached attempt to an independent pass by omitting them.

## P21 — Specimen chain

Candidate maps coupon marks to the actual required specimens and witnesses/records transfer as assigned. Use W06 for mismatch exercise. Score cut-map reading, marking preservation, chain record, discrepancy hold. Cutting is done only by authorized trained personnel.

## P22 — Result interpretation

Candidate assembles valid visual/test reports and completes W07. Score specimen identity, each-required-result accounting, correct calculations/comparisons, overall disposition. Missing or invalid required result prevents completion even if every available result passes.

## P23 — Remediation and changed attempt

Candidate analyzes an actual failed practice result or W08, identifies a targeted practice need and describes authorized independent retest with preserved history. Score cause evidence, teaching plan, retest compliance, honest history. Actual failed required performance is remedied only through the required new demonstration, not the paper explanation.

## P24 — Scope record and handoff

Candidate completes the supplied learning record using verified results and actual maintenance basis. Score demonstrated scope, supporting evidence, limits/maintenance, employer handoff. Assessor checks every credential statement. Do not issue an AWS credential or imply production authorization.
