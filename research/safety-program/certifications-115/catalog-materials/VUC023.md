# VUC023 program and instructor guide

Audience: employees assigned to operate mobile elevating work platforms and the designated ground responders needed for their work. Purpose: safe selection, inspection, setup, operation and recovery within an explicitly demonstrated machine/type/workplace scope. Prerequisites: employer eligibility/authorization to train; ability to understand controls, signals and safety information with appropriate accommodation; access to safe actual equipment, manuals, fall-protection equipment and a qualified instructor. This is not an energized-electrical, technical rope-rescue, maintenance, crane or transport qualification.

The twelve objectives are the actions taught in M01–M12. Learners complete instruction, worked applications and two explained checks per chapter, a separate controlled final and independent practical demonstrations. Instructor explains the hazard and control, demonstrates the safe sequence, provides coached practice and then observes without prompts. Ground-level theory and simulations precede elevation. The evaluator must be experienced and competent in the machines/tasks assessed, including auxiliary lowering, and able to stop the exercise safely. Use actual operator instructions and load plates; this material does not replace model-specific controls.

Suggested initial delivery is sixteen instructional hours, excluding breaks. Day one: selection/roles/manuals M01–M02 60 minutes; inspection/support/load M03–M05 120; fall protection/operation/entrapment M06–M08 120; electricity/environment M09–M10 60; rescue/full-cycle M11–M12 60; guided cases/checks 60. Day two: model demonstrations and rescue familiarization 90; coached practice 180; independent stations/work-cycle evaluation 150; final/feedback 60. Each day 480 minutes. Add time for every learner's actual performance and each additional type. This is a teaching plan, not a claimed regulatory minimum. A large group cannot count one person's demonstration as everyone's practice.

Scope covers vertical/scissor, boom-supported and stationary/outrigger-supported concepts. Actual qualification names only demonstrated types/models and conditions. To claim the full taught multi-type operating scope, demonstrate the applicable stations on a vertical/scissor and a boom-supported machine and the supported configuration where assigned; otherwise record a restricted actual credential rather than an all-types claim. Required but unavailable tasks remain pending for the learner. A completed material package contains all the tasks and scoring; it does not assert real performances occurred.

Final:36 constructed-response items, three per domain,72 points. Score 2 correct decision plus reason,1 safe incomplete,0 wrong/absent/unsafe. Pass 58/72 and 4/6 per domain, with no critical unsafe endorsement. Critical examples: bypassing interlocks, exceeding limits, entering an unverified electrical approach, climbing rails, using an unapproved anchor, raising on unsafe support or attempting unplanned dangerous rescue. Time 60 minutes with accessibility accommodations; no answer key or coaching. After failed domains are retaught/practiced, administer the separate changed-domain reassessment for failed domains plusM11/M12 as applicable. Preserve attempts and practical failures.

Each practical has five criteria scored 0–2:2 independent correct,1 safe but prompted/incomplete,0 incorrect/omitted. Pass at least 8/10 with all **critical** criteria 2. Stop on danger; do not average a critical failure away. Repeat failed tasks independently after correction with changed conditions. Actual control operation, safe movement and auxiliary lowering cannot be replaced by a quiz. Emergency contact, entrapment and dangerous weather are simulated without actual exposure.

Credential rules: issue VNDRLY/U operator training and practical-evaluation record only after knowledge and required actual demonstrations pass, with named scope and restrictions. Employer authorization is separate and must reflect the site. Ground-responder familiarization is specifically documented; the operator card does not establish someone else can rescue. Retrain for unsafe performance, accident, changed equipment/hazards or lost proficiency. Annual observed review is VNDRLY's recommended quality practice; apply any stricter governing requirement and do not falsely label a forklift renewal rule as MEWP law. No IPAF, ANSI accreditation or manufacturer approval is claimed.

# Practical assessment packet

Equipment: safely maintained actual machine(s), serial-matched manuals/plates, suitable level practice area and approved support, barriers/cones, safe mock task target, known-weight tools/materials, proper fall protection for the machine, communication equipment, authorized ground responder and emergency access. Never place people in an entrapment geometry, touch a live line, exceed a limit, stage a tipover or disable a safety system. Low-height rescue practice must remain within the actual machine's safe method.

## A — Select equipment and reject unsuitable work

Provide task cards: indoor overhead inspection; outward reach over fixed piping; outdoor sign installation near a trench. Learner chooses appropriate type and lists needed facts before approval. Assessor supplies actual manual/plate and asks about indoor-only/wind and electrical constraints. Score: task envelope; people/load; **critical support/environment suitability**; **critical no unsupported authorization**; scope/rescue requirements. Reassessment changes sign to a large panel with low weight but high wind area. Expected: capacity weight alone is insufficient.

## B — Inspection and function-test readiness

Learner inspects the actual stowed machine and its records, then performs prescribed safe function tests. Assessor provides photographs of cracked structure, a failed gate latch and damaged hose for disposition, not live defects. Score: structure/access/labels; power/hydraulic/wheel checks; **critical safety/control tests**; **critical defect removal from service**; accurate record. Reassessment changes defect to failed auxiliary lowering or unreadable control labels. Required response is hold, not use normal controls only.

## C — Support, setup and exclusion

Learner surveys the actual safe route/setup area, identifies potential edge/floor issues, verifies support evidence and sets brakes/outriggers as applicable. Document case gives reaction 6000 lb and areas 2/4 ft²; learner calculates 3000/1500 lb/ft² but states this does not design a real mat. Score: survey; **critical support/leveling evidence**; correct prescribed setup; exclusion/traffic plan; **critical refusal of unknown void/edge support**. Reassessment gives 8000 lb over 4 ft²=2000 lb/ft² with unknown soil: still hold. Do not test sinking or instability physically.

## D — Load and fall-protection preparation

Use the actual plate and actual occupant/tool weights. Learner calculates total/zone loads, selects and inspects suitable protection, identifies approved anchor where required, dons/connects correctly and closes access. Score: total and local capacities; **critical protection/anchor compatibility**; equipment inspection/fit; gate/floor behavior; **critical no rail climbing or improvised reach**. Paper case 190+210+65+55=520 lb versus 500 rejects the trip. Reassessment changes a compliant total but overloads the extension deck; learner must still refuse.

## E — Control familiarization and low-level operation

On each assessed model, learner identifies selector, normal/emergency controls, orientation markings, enable/alarms and safe stopping. Then operates smooth low-level movements in a segregated area. Score: manual/control identification; **critical correct orientation**; controlled movement/stopping; **critical no defeated protection**; communication. Reassessment changes turntable orientation on a suitable boom under safe conditions. Do not assume joystick direction from the previous position.

## F — Work positioning and envelope awareness

Learner raises/positions to a safe mock target after complete checks, maintains comfortable reach and inspects the entire moving envelope and descent path. Assessor uses a distant marked overhead exclusion rather than a real crush point. Score: pre-elevation brief; **critical clear envelope/people**; smooth appropriate movements; **critical floor stance/limits**; controlled descent. Reassessment changes target position so safe repositioning is required. No climbing or basket-to-structure transfer is performed.

## G — Travel and changing workplace conditions

Learner travels the designated route in the actual permitted configuration, uses a safe spotter where required and stops for an agreed signal/lost communication. Assessor supplies document-only cases of holes, excessive gusts, indoor-only equipment outdoors and unknown electrical voltage. Score: **critical permitted travel/configuration**; route/view/spotter; traffic separation; **critical correct stop for changed hazard**; safe recovery/replanning. Reassessment includes a manufacturer prohibition on elevated travel; learner must lower/stow rather than rely on the drive control's presence.

## H — Ground response and auxiliary lowering

Instructor first demonstrates exact normal/auxiliary controls. Learner and designated ground responder then independently demonstrate the prescribed safe low-height recovery, with a healthy consenting occupant, clear area and all protection. No actual fault is induced unless the manual's test procedure explicitly provides a safe simulation. Score: **critical correct controls/sequence**; communication/permission except simulated emergency; **critical clear safe movement path**; completed controlled lowering; fault isolation/report. Reassessment changes normal-power-unavailable scenario to incapacitated-operator communication simulation. Document scenarios of live-line contact and entrapment require withholding unsafe automatic lowering and activating the appropriate rescue plan.

## I — Emergency decision tabletop

Cases: platform against a beam trapping an operator; suspected electrical contact; a fall-arrest casualty; normal power failure with healthy occupants. Learner identifies immediate stop, communications, qualified assistance and whether machine movement can be safe. Score: **critical no action worsening entrapment**; **critical no ground contact with energized machine**; prompt appropriate rescue/medical escalation; capability boundaries; usable location/information. Reassessment changes the accessible descent path to an obstruction. Expected: reassess rather than blindly execute the earlier lowering instruction. No real suspended person or entrapment is staged.

## J — Independent complete task and shutdown

Observe the learner plan, inspect, establish support/load/protection/rescue, operate a representative safe task, descend and secure the actual machine. Repeat for additional claimed types/configurations. Score: **critical independent go/no-go judgment**; **critical continuous hazard control**; actual-model skill; communication/records; secure stow/shutdown. Reassessment uses a different safe task position after remediation. Record detailed observed behavior, model and conditions; no all-types pass from one machine.

# VNDRLY/U Mobile Elevating Work Platform Operator

Original instruction, version 1.0, 17 November 2026. A MEWP positions people at height; it does not eliminate fall, overturn, entrapment, electrical or falling-object hazards. This US-focused program covers selection and operation of vertical/scissor, boom-supported and supported/stationary configurations, with actual model-specific evaluation before authorization. It is not an IPAF PAL card, manufacturer license, electrical qualification or technical-rescue qualification.

## M01 — Roles, machine choice and operating scope

Choose access equipment from the task, not from what happens to be available. Identify work height and horizontal reach, people/tools/materials, ground and overhead conditions, required travel, indoor/outdoor exposure and emergency access. A vertical platform can place a worker directly above its base; a boom can reach outward but introduces an extended movement envelope and ejection risk. Vehicle-mounted or outrigger-supported units require their own setup. Some machines can travel elevated within specified limits; others must be fully lowered or stowed before movement. Appearance does not establish capability.

The employer selects suitable equipment and authorizes trained, evaluated operators. The operator inspects, surveys, operates within limits and stops when conditions are unsafe. Occupants must know platform behavior, access, fall protection, load limits, emergency signals and actions; being a passenger is not permission to operate controls. Ground personnel must know the assigned emergency controls and rescue plan. A supervisor must provide resources and resolve hazards rather than instruct the operator to defeat a safety system.

General training teaches principles and decisions. Familiarization establishes how the particular machine works. Neither proves practical skill by itself. Demonstrate inspection, setup, controls, safe travel/elevation, emergency lowering and a complete work cycle on the applicable type. A change of type, unfamiliar controls or different operating conditions requires appropriate instruction and evaluation before use.

**Worked decision.** A scissor-lift-trained worker is offered a boom to reach over piping. The old training is useful background, but the new movement envelope, restraint requirements and recovery controls must be taught and demonstrated. Do not simply issue the key. No online completion authorizes every MEWP.

## M02 — Read the manual, plate and control layout

Locate the operator manual on the machine and match it to the model/serial range. Read load capacity, maximum occupants, indoor/outdoor rating, wind limit, slope limits, height/reach restrictions, tire/outrigger requirements and attachment permissions. Some machines have different capacities for different platform extensions or operating envelopes. A nominal model height is not proof that a loaded basket can reach every point at that height.

With the machine secured, identify platform/ground control selection, emergency stops, enable/foot switches, drive/steer, lift/lower, boom functions, platform rotation, leveling, auxiliary power/lowering and warning indicators. Explain what direction each command produces when the boom or turntable is rotated. Confirm the orientation markings before driving; a familiar-looking joystick can produce an unexpected chassis movement. Do not experiment while near a person, structure or edge.

Safety functions such as tilt alarms, load sensing, pothole protection, outriggers and secondary guarding reduce specific risks but are not a substitute for a safe site. Learn their actual limitations and response instructions. Never bridge switches, tape down an enable control, add weight to defeat a sensor or restart repeatedly to clear a genuine warning. A control fault requires removal from service and authorized repair. Modifications must have the required written manufacturer/equivalent approval; an operator cannot alter rails, anchors, tires or counterweight to suit a task.

**Worked decision.** The platform shows a tilt warning while the ground appears level. Stop the unsafe action, follow the actual safe recovery procedure and investigate from a safe state. Do not use the alarm as a leveling instrument or assume the switch is faulty because the job is urgent.

## M03 — Pre-use inspection and function tests

Inspect before each work shift and test controls daily before use, following the actual manual. Start with the machine safely stowed and secured. Check structure, welds visibly accessible to the operator, pins, fasteners, platform floor, rails, gate, anchor points, tires, wheels, hoses, cables, fluid leaks, batteries, guards, decals and manual. Inspect outriggers/pads and extension mechanisms when fitted. Do not reach into scissor arms or beneath an unsupported raised platform; maintenance supports and service isolation belong to trained maintenance procedures.

Confirm required periodic inspections/maintenance are current through the employer's records. A pre-use check does not replace them. Report cracks, missing fasteners, damaged guards, fluid leaks, unreadable controls or uncertain safety status. Remove defective equipment from service and prevent use until authorized personnel restore it. Never diagnose a high-pressure hydraulic leak with a hand, or use a machine with a known failed emergency function because normal controls still work.

In a clear exclusion zone, perform the manual's ground and platform function tests, emergency stops, enable functions, brakes/steering, alarms and auxiliary lowering checks. Test only in the prescribed safe configuration. Do not deliberately drive to an actual edge to test pothole protection or tilt the machine toward overturn to test an alarm. Observe unusual noise, drift or inconsistent response and stop if unsafe.

**Worked decision.** The access gate does not latch. A harness does not replace the missing barrier. Tag the machine out and obtain repair or suitable replacement. Record the defect; do not hold the gate with a rope and call it an equivalent repair.

## M04 — Ground support, floors and setup

Walk the route and setup area before moving the machine there. Identify holes, trenches, recently filled soil, vaults, drains, floor openings, slab edges, soft shoulders, slopes, slippery material and suspended floors. Obtain evidence of load-bearing capacity where uncertain. The total machine mass is not distributed uniformly; wheels and outriggers can impose high localized loads, especially with the boom positioned outward. A surface that supports pedestrians may not support a wheel or outrigger.

Use manufacturer wheel/outrigger reactions and an appropriate ground/floor assessment. A simple pressure calculation illustrates why support area matters: a synthetic 6,000 lb reaction over two square feet produces 3,000 lb/ft²; over four square feet it is 1,500 lb/ft². This does not select a real mat. Mat strength, stiffness, actual load distribution, soil, edge distance and hidden voids must also be suitable. An operator cannot approve a slab by dividing machine weight by platform area.

Set brakes and deploy stabilizers/outriggers as instructed, on approved pads or solid support. Establish required leveling before elevation; gradeability for travel does not equal permission to elevate on that grade. Do not place improvised blocks under wheels to defeat a leveling limit. If an incline is permitted for the specific operation, apply the required chocking safely and follow all other controls. Establish an exclusion zone against vehicle impact and unauthorized entry.

**Worked decision.** One outrigger is near a trench covered with steel plate but the support rating is unknown. Stop setup until the support/edge condition is assessed and corrected. Moving the pad a few inches by guesswork does not establish bearing capacity.

## M05 — Capacity, occupants, tools and material forces

Count the combined load of all occupants, clothing/PPE, tools, materials and approved accessories. Check both total capacity and any extension-deck, restricted-zone or occupant limit. Distribute loads as instructed. Secure tools and small materials against falling without creating snag or trip hazards. Do not rest large panels on guardrails or attach unapproved pipe racks. An allowable weight does not prove an allowable side load or wind area.

**Worked calculation.** A synthetic platform rating is 500 lb. Two occupants at 190 and 210 lb, tools at 65 and material at 55 total 520 lb, so the proposed load is unacceptable. Removing 30 lb produces 490 lb but still does not resolve an extension-deck limit, maximum occupants or a large panel catching wind. Check every applicable limit independently. Do not use the load alarm as a scale or lift until it trips to discover capacity.

Do not use a personnel platform as a crane, jack, brace or pushing tool unless specifically designed/approved for that exact use. Pulling a stuck pipe or drilling with high side force can destabilize the machine even with little material aboard. Snagged hoses/cables can impose hidden forces during movement. Keep the platform clear of fixed structures; do not tie it to a building to make it feel stable. Never use a forklift or another machine to boost a scissor platform's height.

**Common mistake:** calculating only material weight and omitting the crew. The load sheet must reflect the actual people and equipment for that trip. If the task exceeds capacity or force limits, choose a different machine or work method rather than split an indivisible operation into misleading paperwork.

## M06 — Fall prevention, restraint and safe access

Enter and leave through the intended access point with the platform at the proper access position. Maintain secure contact and keep the floor free of trip hazards. Close the gate or chain as designed. Stand firmly on the floor; do not climb rails, sit on the edge or add a ladder, box or plank to gain reach. If work is beyond a comfortable safe reach, reposition safely or select suitable equipment.

Boom-supported aerial lifts require the applicable personal fall-protection system attached to the designated boom/basket anchorage. Select equipment compatible with the machine, worker, task and manufacturer instructions. A full-body harness is used for fall arrest; the old regulatory body-belt wording does not permit a belt as fall arrest. A correctly configured travel-restraint system prevents reaching an ejection/fall position. A fall-arrest setup must account for clearance, forces, swing and rescue; a long lanyard can allow impact before arrest in a low platform. Do not assume any ring or rail is a rated anchor. Never tie off to an adjacent pole or building while working in the lift.

Scissor platforms rely on a complete effective guardrail system under the applicable scaffold rules. Do not automatically transfer boom-lift rules to all scissor lifts or claim a harness can replace rails. Additional personal protection may be required by the manufacturer, task assessment or employer; use only approved anchorage and configuration. Inspect and fit the actual personal equipment before use. Damaged or impact-loaded items are removed from service under their instructions.

**Worked decision.** A worker proposes stepping from an elevated basket onto a roof. This course's ordinary operating scope does not authorize elevated transfers. Stop and require a specifically planned, permitted manufacturer-compatible transfer method with continuous protection and competent review, or use another access method. Reaching the roof does not establish safe access.

## M07 — Driving, positioning and controlled elevation

Plan the route and the entire movement envelope: wheels, outriggers, turntable counterweight, boom knuckles, platform and the occupants' bodies/tools. Use barriers, traffic coordination and a trained spotter where needed. The spotter must stay outside the crush path and use agreed signals. Stop if communication or visibility is lost. Warning beacons and horns do not establish a clear path.

Travel only in a configuration the machine permits. Stow/lower first unless elevated travel is specifically allowed for that model and conditions. Observe speed, slope, load and surface limitations; do not drive across holes or obstacles that can launch a basket occupant or upset the machine. Keep appropriate clearance from edges and overhead objects. For supported/stationary units, stow the boom and outriggers correctly before relocation, with occupants in the permitted position. A drive control being present does not imply unrestricted elevated travel.

Before elevating, inspect above and around the platform, brief occupants and ground personnel, confirm support/limits, then make smooth controlled movements. Keep hands and limbs inside the protection envelope and watch the boom's rear/swing path as well as the platform. Avoid simultaneous movements near hazards until specifically trained and safe. Do not push a platform against an overhead beam or use controls to force through a snag. Reposition the chassis from a safe configuration if the task cannot be reached safely.

**Worked decision.** A worker can see the basket clear a pipe but the boom knuckle would strike another line behind it. Stop and choose a different position/sequence. The platform is only one part of the moving machine. At the end, inspect the descent path before lowering; ground conditions can change while the crew is aloft.

## M08 — Entrapment and overhead hazards

Crushing often happens when a platform or operator moves toward a beam, ceiling, pipe rack or door frame. The body can be trapped between the control console and the obstruction, potentially keeping a control activated. Identify these gaps before elevation and select a position that leaves escape/clearance. Reduce speed and simplify movements near structures. Do not lean over controls to look underneath an obstacle while driving toward it.

Secondary guarding or anti-entrapment devices have specific activation and reset behavior. Familiarize on the actual system and test as instructed. They do not guarantee prevention of every crush event. Do not remove or defeat them to reach closer to a structure. Maintain communication with a ground person who can recognize distress and access emergency controls promptly.

If an entrapment occurs, stop movement and initiate the rescue plan. The ground operator must understand which movement releases pressure; simply lowering may worsen the crush if the platform is pinned under a structure. Use the actual emergency controls only with a safe direction and trained decision. Call emergency assistance as required and avoid moving an injured person unnecessarily except to remove immediate danger. Never enter beneath unstable equipment or climb the boom to improvise a rescue.

**Worked scenario.** A platform is under a beam and an operator is pressed against the console. The response is not “always push down.” Stop, communicate/assess the geometry, use the planned trained emergency action to relieve the entrapment and summon medical assistance. Practice this on a ground-level mock-up or document scenario, never by placing a person in a crush position.

## M09 — Electricity, insulated devices and safe separation

Treat overhead conductors and unknown cables as energized until the responsible utility/qualified authority establishes otherwise. Electricity can arc without direct contact. Account for the closest possible part of the machine, person, conductive tool or material, including boom movement and line sway. A ten-foot rule of thumb is not a universal clearance for all voltages. Under the general-industry unqualified-person rule, up to 50 kV to ground requires ten feet; above 50 kV the specified distance increases by four inches for every additional 10 kV. The employer must establish the applicable rule, voltage and separation for the actual work. Unknown voltage is a reason to obtain information and stop, not assume the lowest distance.

Arrange de-energization/guarding through the responsible utility and qualified personnel where appropriate, and verify the work plan before approach. This course does not qualify anyone for energized electrical work or the specialized approach distances used by qualified electrical workers. A fiberglass bucket or rubber tire does not make an ordinary lift electrically insulated. Insulated equipment has design, inspection/testing and use restrictions; another conductive path can defeat isolation. Do not drill or modify insulating components.

If the machine contacts a line, warn everyone to stay away, call emergency/utility assistance and follow the planned electrical emergency procedure. Ground personnel must not touch the machine or approach to operate controls while it may be energized. Avoid creating a second path to ground. Occupants should not casually step out onto a structure or climb down. An immediate life-threatening escape situation requires the trained emergency response, not an improvised routine instruction.

**Worked decision.** A metal conduit carried on the platform projects toward a line although the basket itself is ten feet away. The conductive object's closest approach counts. Stop and correct the plan; basket-center distance is irrelevant to the exposed end.

## M10 — Wind, weather and other environmental limits

Check the machine's indoor/outdoor designation and actual wind rating before work. Some machines or configurations have no outdoor wind allowance. Do not adopt a generic 28 mph limit for every lift from a handout. Gusts at platform height may exceed a ground reading; exposed corners, buildings and passing storms can change conditions quickly. Large sheets, banners or covers increase sail area and may be prohibited even below the ordinary wind limit. Never attach a tarp to rails to make a windbreak without approval.

Establish who monitors conditions and the trigger for safely lowering/stowing before limits are reached. Consider lightning, rain, ice, poor visibility, heat/cold effects, unstable soil after rain and changing traction. A tilt or wind alarm is not the planned signal to begin thinking about evacuation. Stop when the safe operating conditions cannot be maintained, and do not elevate again until the problem is resolved.

Indoor engines can create harmful exhaust; adequate ventilation must be established, not assumed from an open door. Hazardous atmospheres require suitable approved equipment and a controlled task plan. Battery charging/fueling occurs in designated areas using the actual instructions, with ignition, spill, ventilation and electrical controls. An operator must not repair hydraulic/electrical systems or change safety settings without the required maintenance competence and authorization.

**Worked decision.** A synthetic model permits wind only up to 20 mph; a gust forecast reaches 24. Another model's 28 mph rating is irrelevant. Choose suitable timing/equipment or postpone. Even a forecast below the rating does not authorize a large unapproved panel that changes wind loading.

## M11 — Ground controls, emergency lowering and rescue

Before elevation, identify a trained available ground responder, communication method, keys/access and the exact normal/auxiliary lowering procedure. Check that the descent path and emergency-control access will remain clear. Review credible failures: loss of normal power, incapacitated occupant, entrapment, stuck platform, electrical contact and a suspended fall-arrest casualty. A plan that only says “call 911” does not establish how an accessible stranded platform will be brought down or how responders will reach it promptly.

Demonstrate normal and auxiliary lowering using the actual manual in a safe low-height configuration. Identify selector position, emergency-stop state, auxiliary pump or manual valve sequence and limitations. Do not write a universal “open this valve” instruction across machines: the wrong valve or sequence can cause unintended motion. Lower controls ordinarily require permission from the occupants, except in an emergency. Ground personnel must assess whether movement is safe; electrical contact, structural failure or entrapment may make routine lowering hazardous.

Use normal/auxiliary controls for recovery only when the procedure and conditions permit. A person-to-person transfer, rope rescue or another MEWP requires its own planned equipment, competence and assessment. Do not improvise climbing down scissors or a boom. If a worker is hanging in fall arrest, initiate prompt rescue and medical response under the preplanned system; do not leave them suspended while inventing a plan.

**Worked scenario.** Normal platform power fails with two healthy occupants. They stop attempts, communicate location and condition, keep protection in place and wait while the designated ground responder checks the area and operates the tested authorized auxiliary procedure. After recovery, remove the faulted machine from service. Resetting power and resuming work without diagnosis is not the end of the event.

## M12 — Complete work cycle, shutdown and continuing competence

A safe cycle includes task planning, machine/record checks, inspection/function tests, route/support survey, load calculation, protection/rescue briefing, controlled operation, safe descent, unloading and secured shutdown. Leaving out rescue because the job is short or omitting inspection because a prior shift used the machine creates a gap in that cycle. Record hazards and corrections as they occur; do not sign all boxes from memory at the end.

Stow the platform/boom and accessories using the actual instructions, secure loose equipment, park in a suitable location, apply brakes and isolate unauthorized operation. Charge/refuel only within assigned competence and report faults. Leave emergency access, walkways and fire equipment clear. For transport/towing/loading onto a carrier, use the manufacturer's separate approved procedure and qualified personnel; ordinary operation training is not a rigging or transport qualification.

The employer monitors continued safe performance and provides retraining when unsafe operation, an accident, unfamiliar hazards/equipment or loss of proficiency shows a need. Model familiarization is renewed when controls/features differ, and the rescue personnel need the same relevant update. Do not invent a universal federal three-year MEWP interval by borrowing the forklift rule. Follow applicable manufacturer, employer, jurisdiction and consensus-program requirements; VNDRLY's annual observed review is a quality recommendation, not a claim of a statutory renewal period.

**Capstone.** A crew wants to use an indoor scissor lift outdoors near a trench and power line to install a large sign. Reject the proposed plan before anyone elevates. Reassess machine suitability, wind/sail area, bearing/edge conditions, electrical separation, load/occupants, protection and rescue. A correct response can be selecting another safe access method rather than finding a way to make the available machine fit. Qualification means making that decision independently as well as moving controls smoothly.

# Formative learning checks

Attempt independently, then review the answer and explanation. Correct mistakes using the chapter and explain a changed example before practical assessment.

## M01-L1

Does scissor-lift experience automatically authorize a boom lift?

**Answer:** No; appropriate type training, actual-model familiarization and observed competence are required.

**Reason/remediation:** Reach, envelope, ejection protection and recovery differ.

## M01-L2

What does an occupant need to know even without operating controls?

**Answer:** Access, protection, limits, signals and emergency actions.

**Reason/remediation:** Passenger status does not remove platform hazards.

## M02-L1

Why check control orientation after rotating a boom?

**Answer:** Drive direction relative to the platform can change; use actual markings/instructions.

**Reason/remediation:** Joystick familiarity alone can produce an unexpected movement.

## M02-L2

May an operator tape an enable switch to simplify work?

**Answer:** No; never defeat safety functions.

**Reason/remediation:** Revisit model controls and remove defective equipment from service.

## M03-L1

Normal movement works but emergency lowering fails. Use the lift?

**Answer:** No; remove from service pending authorized repair.

**Reason/remediation:** Normal function does not establish recovery capability.

## M03-L2

Can a pre-use check replace periodic maintenance/inspection records?

**Answer:** No; both applicable requirements must be met.

**Reason/remediation:** Different checks detect different problems.

## M04-L1

Synthetic 6000 lb reaction over 2 ft² gives what pressure?

**Answer:** 3000 lb/ft², which does not by itself design a real mat.

**Reason/remediation:** Bearing area is only one component of support adequacy.

## M04-L2

Is travel gradeability permission to elevate on the same slope?

**Answer:** No; elevation/leveling limits are separate.

**Reason/remediation:** Read the actual operating configuration limits.

## M05-L1

People 190+210, tools 65, material 55 total what against 500?

**Answer:** 520 lb; over capacity.

**Reason/remediation:** All platform loads count.

## M05-L2

A large light panel is below weight capacity. Is that sufficient?

**Answer:** No; wind area, side forces, dimensions and approved carrying method also matter.

**Reason/remediation:** Weight alone does not establish suitability.

## M06-L1

Can a body belt be used for fall arrest because older text says belt?

**Answer:** No; body belts are not fall-arrest systems.

**Reason/remediation:** Use the applicable compatible system and actual designated anchorage.

## M06-L2

Can a harness compensate for a broken scissor guardrail?

**Answer:** No; restore the required barrier and safe configuration.

**Reason/remediation:** Personal protection is not an improvised repair.

## M07-L1

Does a drive joystick prove elevated travel is allowed?

**Answer:** No; verify model and condition-specific permission.

**Reason/remediation:** A control can exist for stowed travel only or restricted use.

## M07-L2

What must be checked besides basket clearance?

**Answer:** The whole machine envelope, including boom knuckles, tail swing, people/tools and descent path.

**Reason/remediation:** Another part may strike while the basket clears.

## M08-L1

Should ground personnel always lower immediately during entrapment?

**Answer:** No; assess geometry and use the trained planned movement that does not worsen crushing.

**Reason/remediation:** Downward movement can increase entrapment.

## M08-L2

Does secondary guarding eliminate the need for overhead clearance?

**Answer:** No; its protection has limitations.

**Reason/remediation:** Keep safe positioning and do not defeat the device.

## M09-L1

Is ten feet a universal safe distance for all voltages?

**Answer:** No; applicable distances depend on voltage/task/rule and may be greater.

**Reason/remediation:** Unknown voltage requires resolution, not a lowest-distance assumption.

## M09-L2

Can a ground responder touch controls after suspected line contact?

**Answer:** Not while equipment may be energized; keep people away and call emergency/utility assistance.

**Reason/remediation:** Touching can create a path through the rescuer.

## M10-L1

May an indoor-only machine be used outdoors because wind seems calm?

**Answer:** Not without the actual rating/instructions permitting the conditions.

**Reason/remediation:** Do not substitute a subjective weather judgment for design limits.

## M10-L2

A model limit is 20 mph and gusts reach 24. Use another model's 28 mph limit?

**Answer:** No; the actual machine limit governs.

**Reason/remediation:** Ratings cannot be transferred between models.

## M11-L1

Does a plan saying only call 911 establish complete rescue readiness?

**Answer:** No; plan available personnel, communications, controls, access and credible recovery scenarios.

**Reason/remediation:** Emergency services still need access and time; ordinary recovery must be prepared.

## M11-L2

May lower controls be used routinely without occupant permission?

**Answer:** No; permission is required except in emergencies.

**Reason/remediation:** Unexpected platform movement endangers occupants.

## M12-L1

Is the forklift three-year evaluation rule automatically the MEWP rule?

**Answer:** No; apply actual governing requirements and event-triggered retraining.

**Reason/remediation:** Different equipment has different regulatory provisions.

## M12-L2

Does operator training authorize loading a lift onto a transport trailer?

**Answer:** Not automatically; transport/loading requires its specific procedure and competence.

**Reason/remediation:** Scope must name demonstrated tasks.
