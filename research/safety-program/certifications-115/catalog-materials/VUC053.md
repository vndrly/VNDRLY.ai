# VNDRLY/U Refractory Inspection and Assessment

VUC053 · Original available-source materials · 2026-11-18

## Purpose and route

This program builds inspection and assessment judgment for refractory linings in service and during outages. Retained Q01–20 teach installation quality, materials, sampling, curing and dryout. Q21–32 add service history, damage interpretation, inspection selection, measurement, conditional life calculations and repair assessment. Read the foundation and complete W01–08 before the specialist W09–14 and integrated I53. The audience is inspection and maintenance personnel with industrial safety, drawings, measurement and arithmetic readiness; provide prerequisite instruction where needed.

The external API982 subject concerns refractory inspection and assessment. Its public program requires its own experience and active API936 history; this course does not award or substitute for either API credential.[R01] Public topic/effectivity documents were reviewed, not the full controlled standards.[R02–03] All R53 numerical limits and life projections are fictional classroom data, not operating recommendations or API acceptance tables. Employer appointment, specialist methods and actual engineering disposition remain separate.

## Q21 — Service history and inspection objectives

A lining's present condition must be interpreted in the context of its job. Collect equipment identity, original lining design, layer map, installation and repair history, operating temperatures, cycling, process changes, feed or fuel changes and abnormal events. Identify what information is measured, estimated or missing. A recent surface photograph cannot establish the full history of a multilayer lining, and an apparently sound hot face does not prove intact backup or anchors.

Define the inspection question before selecting a method. The question might concern local wear, suspected separation, an external hot region or readiness after a repair. These are different questions and may need different access and evidence. Plan locations around the actual flow, geometry, prior findings and suspected mechanisms; do not inspect only the easiest wall and call it representative. Record inaccessible areas and why they matter.

**Worked case:** R53 heater H has a shell-temperature anomaly near a burner after a fuel change. Request comparable operating conditions, burner/flow history and lining maps; do not declare lining thinning solely from temperature. **Changed case:** an outage photograph shows a surface crack but no depth evidence. Preserve both observations and seek the required assessment. The inspector's role is to form and test hypotheses with appropriate specialists, not to label every anomaly with a familiar cause.

## Q22 — Design functions and thermal reasoning

Dense hot-face material, insulating backup, joints and anchors have different functions. Changes in thickness, conductivity, continuity or support can change performance. A higher temperature rating does not automatically make a substitute suitable for abrasion, chemical exposure or thermal cycling. Match actual material selection to service requirements through the authorized design process. In an FCC system, for example, different zones can face different combinations of particle wear, temperature and chemistry; one generic product description cannot establish every zone's suitability.

For a simplified classroom plane wall with constant conductivity and steady one-dimensional conduction, thermal resistance per area is thickness divided by conductivity. Layer resistances add; heat flux equals temperature difference divided by total resistance. This model omits joints, anchors, radiation, convection, temperature-dependent properties and geometry. Use it to understand sensitivity, never to approve a heater or infer a precise defect from shell temperature.

**Worked case:** R53 layers are 0.10 m at 2W/(m·K) and 0.05 m at 0.25W/(m·K). Resistances0.05 and 0.20 total 0.25 m²K/W. With supplied layer-boundary difference 800K, flux is 3200W/m². **Changed case:** backup thickness halves to 0.025 m; total resistance 0.15 and flux 5333.33W/m². The66.67% increase illustrates the importance of backup; it is not a field heat-loss prediction or safe-shell-temperature rule.

## Q23 — Damage mechanisms and competing explanations

Describe morphology before assigning cause: location, extent, orientation, depth, loose or missing material, deposits and relation to flow or supports. Cracking can reflect several processes; some cracks are stable under a design basis while others accompany loss of integrity. Spalling describes loss of pieces, not a single proven cause. Erosion, chemical attack, overheating, gas flow behind a lining and carbon-related damage can coexist. Compare service history, material, deposits and change over time before selecting an explanation.

Build a hypothesis table with supporting evidence, contradictory evidence and the next discriminating observation. A smooth worn surface near a high-velocity path suggests a different investigation from widespread softened material after a chemistry change, but neither appearance alone proves causation. Preserve representative samples through an approved plan and maintain identity. Sampling itself can alter the lining and expose hazards, so obtain authorization before removal.

Use the following original reasoning guide to choose evidence, not to diagnose from one photograph:

- **Thermal or mechanical cracking:** map orientation, width where measurable, depth evidence and relation to joints, supports and thermal events. A crack intersecting loose or displaced material raises a different question from an isolated stable surface feature. There is no universal harmless crack width in this course.
- **Spalling and separation:** record missing pieces and suspected separation planes. Review installation interfaces, thermal history and actual remaining support. A lamination-like plane can require examination beyond the visible surface; do not assume its origin is proven by the word used to describe it.
- **Erosion:** compare location and directional wear with flow/particle exposure and geometry. Use matched thickness maps, not only bulk average loss. A change in flow can make an earlier linear wear rate misleading.
- **Chemical attack or infiltration:** preserve deposit and material identities, compare service chemistry and obtain appropriate laboratory interpretation. Discoloration alone does not prove chemical failure, and an unrepresentative sample cannot define the whole lining.
- **Carbon-related damage:** investigate deposit/infiltration history and thermal or process changes with specialists. Removing visible deposits does not necessarily establish that underlying material and interfaces remain sound.
- **Overheating or gas bypass:** correlate operating and temperature records with joints, penetrations, gaps and affected layers. An external hot region can have several explanations; check the measurement and actual flow/support evidence before attributing it to a specific missing layer.

Each hypothesis needs a possible disconfirming observation. For example, apparent directional wear may instead reflect an original construction profile if no baseline exists. State that limitation and obtain the baseline or appropriate technical interpretation. Do not choose a repair until the required system and mechanism questions have been addressed.

**Worked case:** R53 zoneA has directional loss at an inlet; zoneB has discolored material and a new deposit. Record separate damage descriptions and request flow and chemistry review rather than one universal erosion label. **Changed case:** a laboratory result supports chemical interaction atB but sampleA was not tested. Do not transfer the conclusion toA. Repairing both with the same material requires independent compatibility assessment; visual resemblance is not design approval.

## Q24 — Brick, fiber and special lining systems

Brick systems depend on layout, joints, support and accommodation of movement as well as material properties. Displaced units, joint changes or deformation need assessment against the actual design and service history. Time-dependent deformation under load differs from an immediate impact or an installation misfit. Do not hammer a brick back into place or fill an intended movement joint to improve appearance without approved repair instructions.

Fiber systems and multilayer arrangements require their own attachment, compression, orientation and exposure controls. A fluffy-looking surface is not proof of remaining thickness or attachment integrity. Special shapes such as ferrules and tiles have individual identities and interfaces; a cracked component may affect the adjacent region even when the surrounding lining looks sound. Refer suspected fiber/dust hazards through the actual product and exposure controls; this course uses inert surrogates, not loose refractory fiber.

For brick, compare repeated geometry under known conditions when evaluating suspected creep or structural movement; a single shifted unit does not distinguish installation error, impact and time-dependent deformation. For fiber, document shrinkage/gaps, attachment and exposed backing against the actual design, without assigning a generic allowable gap. For a multilayer system, identify which layer was visible or measured and which was concealed. For ferrules or tiles, record the exact component and neighboring interfaces so a replacement plan addresses fit, support and material compatibility together. Separate the condition of the refractory from any pressure-boundary or metallic-support concern and route each to its qualified authority.

**Worked case:** an inert model shows three displaced brick units beside a support and a neighboring fiber module with a visible gap. Create separate findings for movement/support and module configuration. **Changed case:** replacement bricks match dimensions but not documented material identity. Keep material compatibility open. A joint that has narrowed from 8 mm to 3 mm changed by 5 mm; that arithmetic records movement, not an acceptance limit or proof of creep. The engineering basis determines the significance.

## Q25 — Anchors and concealed support assessment

Anchors and attachments may degrade while much of the visible hot face remains present. Map exposed and concealed support information by region. Separate observed broken or distorted anchors, suspected hidden damage and unexamined locations. Consider service temperature, cycling, corrosion, attachment condition and mechanical loading in the hypothesis set. A sound-looking exposed anchor does not establish the condition of its neighbors or of a weld hidden by lining.

Plan selective exposure or other examination with the responsible technical and safety personnel. Removing material may destabilize adjacent lining or create falling-object and dust hazards. The inspection course does not authorize demolition, weld repair or entry beneath unsupported refractory. Define the evidence needed, preserve the actual support pattern and document the limits of the sample.

**Worked case:** a40-anchor region has 12 exposed positions. Three show damage, nine do not show damage by the stated method; 28 remain unexamined. Report25% observed damage in the exposed sample, not 25% proven damage in the entire region. **Changed case:** another eight are examined and two damaged. Total observed damage is 5/20=25%, with 20 still unexamined. The unchanged sample percentage does not establish that examination can stop or that concealed supports are acceptable.

## Q26 — Visual mapping and remote inspection

A useful visual record connects each observation to a reproducible location, orientation and scale. Establish the coordinate convention and retain a zone map. Record lighting, cleanliness, access, viewing angle and obstructions. Distinguish “not observed” from “absent.” A remote camera can reduce access exposure, but deposits, perspective and lack of a scale may limit size or depth interpretation. Keep original images and the annotation history so later comparison is possible.

Use consistent categories for missing material, cracks, displaced units, exposed anchors and unexamined surfaces. Trace the inspected extent on the map rather than relying on the number of photographs. Two views of the same area do not double coverage. Avoid reaching into an unsafe space to improve a picture; obtain an approved alternative method or state the limitation.

**Worked case:** ten equal grid cells are planned. Seven have valid views; two of those seven also have duplicate photographs. Coverage is 70%, not 90%. **Changed case:** one valid view is obscured by deposits when reviewed; supported coverage becomes 60%, with four cells needing follow-up. If a remote image lacks scale, record the observed feature without inventing millimetres. P26 requires actual mapping of a benign model and a changed viewpoint to demonstrate traceability.

## Q27 — Sounding, mechanical methods and selective exposure

Different inspection methods reveal different aspects of a lining. Sounding can indicate a change in response but depends on geometry, material, support, method and assessor interpretation. It is not a universal numerical bond-strength test. Mechanical tests require their specified apparatus, preparation and method; a casual scratch or improvised impact is not an equivalent certified property test. Selective tear-out can expose layers and supports but is destructive and may not represent every region.

Choose methods against the inspection question and their limitations. Radiographic work requires separately authorized qualified personnel and radiation controls; this program teaches review of its scope, not operation. Document method identity, coverage, surface condition, reference, personnel and limitations. Where results conflict, retain both and obtain a technically justified follow-up rather than selecting the more convenient result.

**Worked case:** a mapped area has a changed sounding response, but a visual view shows no surface loss. Record a suspected subsurface condition and request the authorized confirmation plan; neither result cancels the other. **Changed case:** limited exposure finds a void at one location. Map its actual extent and do not label the entire lining delaminated without evidence. Classroom practice uses prepared inert layered models and supplied response records, with no demolition, dust or overhead tapping.

## Q28 — Thermography and comparable trends

Infrared measurements are influenced by surface emissivity, reflected surroundings, viewing conditions and atmospheric factors; the camera's displayed value is not independent of those inputs.[R04] Record the instrument and settings, location, distance, angle and operating state. Compare like conditions when trending. A shiny patch or a changed surrounding heat source can alter apparent temperature without an equivalent change in the lining. A hot region warrants the actual response process, not an improvised field touch test.

Separate observation, plausible explanations and confirmation. An infrared image may identify a region for further investigation, but it does not by itself establish refractory thickness, anchor integrity or remaining life. Use the qualified thermographer's method and equipment limits. Classroom work uses supplied image records or a safe room-temperature reference object; no exposure to a hot operating shell is required.

**Worked case:** comparable synthetic records show 120°C then 155°C at the same patch: increase 35°C. Do not express that as a physical percentage-temperature increase using Celsius values; Celsius has an offset zero. A fictional escalation trigger is 20°C change, so notify the designated authority; it is not a universal shutdown threshold. **Changed case:** the second record used different emissivity and an altered view of a nearby hot surface. The arithmetic remains 35, but comparability is not established. Escalate the uncertainty and arrange proper verification rather than declaring measured deterioration.

## Q29 — Thickness, wear rate and conditional life

Compare thickness at matched locations using a method appropriate to the lining and access. Record original and current measurements, dates, uncertainty and whether the same layer or reference surface was measured. A local minimum must not disappear into an average. If the damage mechanism or operating conditions changed, a historical rate may not describe future loss. A remaining-life calculation is conditional information for the responsible technical authority, not a permission to operate until the calculated date.

For supplied linear wear data, rate=(earlier thickness−later thickness)/elapsed time. Conditional time to a supplied limit=(current thickness−limit)/rate, only when rate is positive and the assumptions are justified. Zero or negative apparent loss does not prove infinite life; examine resolution, location and actual mechanism. Keep units consistent and retain conservative uncertainty scenarios without calling them statistical confidence bounds.

**Worked case:**120 mm to 108 mm in 2 years gives 6 mm/year. Supplied minimum 90 mm yields 3 years conditional time. With±1 mm uncertainty on each measurement, plausible rate bounds are 5–7 mm/year; using current 107–109 gives time bounds 17/7=2.43 to 19/5=3.8 years. **Changed case:** current 104 mm gives 8 mm/year and 1.75 years by the same nominal model. Neither result resolves a new anchor failure or changed service. In USC units, 4.5 in to 4.2 in in 1.5 years gives 0.2 in/year; supplied 3.6 in limit yields 3 years, subject to the same restrictions.

## Q30 — Repair extent, area and quantities

A repair scope starts with the damaged system and the authorized extent, including interfaces with apparently sound material. Record the proposed boundary, removal limits, anchor treatment, joints, material, method and verification points. Do not enlarge or shrink the scope solely to match a convenient bag quantity. Adjacent concealed damage and the stability of remaining lining may require technical review before removal. The inspector verifies the approved scope and records changes; they do not invent a structural repair.

Calculate areas from a dimensioned map and avoid double-counting overlap. Volume equals area times specified depth only for the simplified uniform-depth exercise. Material quantity additionally needs the approved yield/waste basis; procurement allowance is not acceptance evidence. Curved surfaces require appropriate geometry, not an unqualified flat projection. State the geometry assumptions in the record.

**Worked case:** two rectangular repair regions 1.2×0.8 m and 0.6×0.5 m overlap by 0.1 m². Net area 1.16 m². At0.10 m depth volume 0.116 m³; with 10% ordering allowance 0.1276 m³. **Changed case:** overlap is 0.2 m² and depth 0.12 m: net 1.06 m², volume 0.1272 m³, order 0.13992 m³. In a separate USC exercise, 3×2 ft at 0.25 ft depth is 1.5 ft³ before allowance. These calculations do not establish repair suitability or installed quality.

## Q31 — Repair quality, cure and return-to-service evidence

Repair inspection must connect material and substrate preparation, anchors/supports, application, sampling, cure, dryout and final acceptance to the approved system. Cast, gunned, brick and fiber repairs have different workmanship and verification needs. A contractor's familiarity with one method is not authorization to substitute another. Preserve the original defect, approved disposition, execution record and verification result. A new surface finish cannot erase an unresolved concealed support issue.

Curing and dryout are distinct stages, and the material/system-specific procedure governs them.[R05] The foundation explains how to review supplied charts without operating a live furnace. For a repair, establish whether retained material, moisture, geometry or instrumentation changes affect the approved plan. A recorded heating interval does not prove every region met the required condition. An interruption or missing sensor requires the stated technical disposition rather than a guessed extra hold.

**Worked case:** R53 fictional hold requires all three named channels at the specified condition for 2 continuoushours. A was valid 2.4 h, B2.1 h and C has a30 minute gap. The data do not prove a common two-hour interval. **Changed case:** a competent review resolves C's gap but imposes a follow-up condition still open. Do not close the repair solely because the reviewer signed. The owner retains return-to-service authorization after all applicable evidence and restrictions are addressed.

## Q32 — Assessment report, uncertainty and follow-up

The final report should allow another competent reviewer to reconstruct the assessment. Include equipment/service basis, lining system, inspection map, methods, actual findings, unexamined areas, measurements, calculations and assumptions, hypotheses and supporting evidence, repair dispositions, open restrictions and responsible owners. Identify which conclusions are observations, which are interpretations and which are decisions by others. Preserve original data and earlier failed records with the correction trail.

Prioritize findings by their actual consequence and uncertainty under the approved process, not merely by affected area. A small support failure can matter more than a larger superficial feature. Recommend further examination or technical review with specific questions; avoid vague “monitor” instructions without method, location, trigger and owner. An inspection interval and an operating decision require the relevant authority and applicable requirements, not just a classroom wear-rate quotient.

**Worked case:** twelve findings include nine verified closures, two pending support dispositions and one unexamined high-consequence region. Closure is 75%, but the three open issues still prevent the proposed unconditional acceptance. **Changed case:** one support issue closes and one earlier closure reopens after new evidence. The percentage remains 75%, while the issue list changes. Issue a revised traceable assessment, maintain the restriction and communicate the exact remaining decisions. This is the standard for a defensible course completion report.

## Q01 — What a refractory lining must accomplish

A refractory system protects equipment against its specified thermal, chemical and mechanical environment. Some layers primarily insulate; others resist hot-face abrasion or chemical attack. Selection therefore involves more than a maximum temperature printed on a bag. Temperature cycling, process chemistry, particle impact, gas flow, support and shell limitations influence design. The inspector verifies specified work and reports deviations; an attractive finish is not evidence that the system meets its design duty.

Map the lining system from shell to hot face, including backup layers, anchors, joints and penetrations. Identify which layer and property each inspection concerns. Do not assume a strong dense material is a suitable replacement for insulating backup, or that matching bulk chemistry proves identical installation behavior.

**Worked case.** The drawing calls for an insulating backup and abrasion-resistant hot face. A contractor proposes one dense layer because its temperature rating is higher. This changes thermal and mechanical behavior and requires authorized design review. **Changed case:** thickness remains identical; equal dimensions still do not establish equivalent performance.

## Q02 — Material families and installation compatibility

Monolithic materials include castables, gunning mixes, ramming/plastic materials and other formulated systems. Binders and installation methods influence handling, setting, curing and dryout. A product intended for one application method is not automatically suitable for another. Manufacturer guidance distinguishes water requirements and application types.[S02] Treat product identity, method and revision as linked controls.

Read the approved product sheet for supplied form, mixing liquid, dosage, equipment, temperature, working time, placement and subsequent treatment. The label “refractory” is not a universal recipe. For an unfamiliar binder or additive, obtain its actual instructions rather than borrowing cement-castable practices. Keep separate materials and accessories identifiable so an unapproved substitution cannot disappear into a common stockpile.

**Worked case.** Two bags have similar alumina content but one is a casting product and the other a gunning formulation. Matching chemistry does not authorize substitution. **Changed case:** manufacturer confirms another method is possible, but the job procedure has not been revised; reconcile both technical and project approval before dependent work.

## Q03 — Establish an inspection and test plan

An effective plan connects the specified lining to inspectable stages: receipt, storage, substrate, anchors, preparation, qualification/mockup where required, mixing/application, sampling, cure, dryout, repair and release. Identify acceptance basis, records, notification responsibilities and hold/witness points. A hold point means work cannot proceed past that stage without the required release; a witness arrangement follows the actual project rules, not an inspector's assumption.

Obtain approved drawings, product instructions, procedure revisions and responsibilities before installation. Resolve conflicting documents through the authorized technical route. A missed inspection must be recorded and technically dispositioned; signing later cannot recreate evidence of a covered anchor or inaccessible layer.

**Worked case.** Installation covers anchors before their required hold release. Record the missed hold and affected area; seek authorized verification/disposition rather than backdate approval. **Changed case:** photographs exist but lack location and scale. They may help investigation but do not automatically satisfy the specified examination.

## Q04 — Receipt, storage and traceability

Verify product, batch, quantity, packaging condition, shelf-life information and documentation against the approved order. Storage conditions must preserve the material as specified; moisture-damaged bags, unexplained hard lumps or expired stock require disposition. A batch certificate identifies supplied material, not proof that every installed section came from that batch.

Create a chain linking bags/batches, mixing records, placement location and test samples. Preserve split batches and material changes at identifiable boundaries. Record rejected or quarantined material so it cannot re-enter production by convenience. Use the manufacturer's actual storage and shelf-life rules, not an invented universal duration.

**Worked case.** Batch B is documented, but the installed panel's log says only “castable.” The certificate does not establish panel identity. **Changed case:** the bag count reconciles, but some bags were unmarked; arithmetic reconciliation alone cannot prove their formulation.

## Q05 — Safe inspection access and exposure control

Refractory work can involve dust, hot surfaces, falling material, moving mixing equipment, stored energy, confined spaces and combustion during heating. Review the actual job hazards before seeking access. OSHA's silica rules require applicable exposure-control measures; whether construction or general-industry provisions apply depends on the work.[S03] A disposable mask is not an automatic substitute for an exposure assessment and proper respiratory program.

Use established isolation, access and permit systems. Do not enter a vessel merely because installation has stopped, stand below unsecured lining, or inspect through a firing opening. Sampling and demolition can create hazards absent during normal service. The inspector should request safe evidence and access; production urgency does not create entry authorization.

**Worked case.** A vessel is cool but contains residual atmosphere hazards and an unsecured overhead lining. Cool temperature alone does not make entry safe. **Changed case:** a gas reading is acceptable once; required isolation, ongoing controls and physical hazards remain separate considerations.

## Q06 — Substrate and existing-condition preparation

The substrate and repair boundary must match the approved system. Examine cleanliness, soundness, geometry, contamination, moisture conditions and access as specified. Old lining that appears intact may be poorly bonded or deteriorated below the surface. Removal extent and examination method require the approved technical basis; do not prescribe aggressive hammering or removal on operating equipment.

Record shell findings separately from lining findings. Corrosion, distortion or damaged attachment areas may need the equipment owner or engineer; covering them with new refractory does not repair the pressure boundary. Confirm whether surface preparation and interfaces are ready before they become inaccessible.

**Worked case.** A repair cavity has loose edges and shell corrosion. The installer wants to fill it immediately. Establish sound approved boundaries and route shell condition for separate disposition. **Changed case:** the shell is accepted, but loose edges remain; one approval does not clear the other defect.

## Q07 — Anchors, supports and attachment evidence

Anchors support the lining while accommodating the specified service and movement. Their material, geometry, orientation, spacing, attachment and required cover are design-controlled. Improvised changes can alter restraint, load transfer or hot-face exposure. Verify the actual approved pattern and records before concealment; do not choose anchor spacing from memory.

Attachment welding has its own qualification and acceptance requirements. Refractory inspection competence does not make a person a qualified welding examiner or designer. Report missing/damaged anchors and mismatched materials by location. Required expansion provisions or coatings must follow the design rather than be omitted because they are hard to see after casting.

**Worked case.** One anchor position is skipped because it interferes with formwork. The drawing still requires it; obtain an authorized solution before covering. **Changed case:** a replacement is welded in, but material identity and attachment inspection are missing. Physical presence alone does not close those records.

## Q08 — Forms, thickness and joints

Forms establish geometry and must tolerate placement loads without movement or leakage that undermines the lining. Check dimensions, clearances, release materials, access and removal provisions against the procedure. The final hot face, layer thickness and joints are separate requirements. An acceptable average thickness cannot cure a local minimum violation where a local minimum is specified.

Joints and interfaces can accommodate movement or define installation sections. Their type, location and treatment follow the design; filling an intended joint for appearance can defeat its purpose. Record reference surfaces and measurement locations so dimensions can be reconstructed after forms are removed.

**Worked case.** Supplied training minimum is95 mm; readings are100,94,102 mm. The94 mm location fails by1 mm even though the mean is98.67 mm. **Changed case:** that location is built up without an approved interface method; dimensional correction alone does not establish repair integrity.

## Q09 — Equipment, water and batch control

Use the specified mixer, measuring devices, mixing liquid and sequence. Check equipment cleanliness and functioning before the first batch, and record calibration/verification appropriate to the job. Product-specific liquid dosage affects workability and properties; do not change it merely to make placement easier.[S02] Count all additions, including water added after an initial measurement.

For a specified mass percentage, multiply dry material mass by the fraction. Do not confuse percentage of dry solids with percentage of total wet mix. Where volume is used, apply a stated density and adequate measurement method. Bag count is not enough when partial bags or wet material invalidate the nominal dry mass.

**Worked case.** Synthetic water target6% of200 kg dry product requires12 kg water. Adding2 kg later makes14/200=7%, not6%. **Changed case:** batch size halves; the target halves only when the same dry-mass basis and product instructions apply.

## Q10 — Mixing, working time and temperature

Record actual start, mixing sequence, duration, temperature, water/additives and placement end against the applicable procedure. Working time depends on the formulation and conditions; it is not reset by remixing or adding liquid unless the approved instructions specifically allow such a practice. Material that becomes difficult to place requires disposition, not an undocumented adjustment.

Temperature control includes materials, liquid, equipment and installation environment where specified. A comfortable air reading does not prove cold stored bags have equilibrated. Plan batch size and labor so material can be placed within its applicable window. Document interruptions and identify affected locations if nonconforming material was already used.

**Worked case.** A synthetic30-minute placement window starts09:00; last placement09:38 is8 minutes late. Good appearance does not erase that deviation. **Changed case:** another batch is within time but outside its specified mix-temperature range; each criterion remains independent.

## Q11 — Placement and consolidation

Placement must distribute material around supports and into the intended geometry without segregation, trapped voids or unsupported layers. Use the product-specific method and sequence. Consolidation energy that is too little or inappropriate can leave voids; uncontrolled intervention can also segregate or damage the mix. Inspect the actual operation rather than rely solely on finished surface appearance.

Document interruptions, cold interfaces, leakage, movement of forms and unexpected loss of workability. Do not add surface water or a cosmetic slurry to conceal defects unless the system's approved instructions explicitly require that treatment. Preserve the link from batch to placed section for any later testing or repair.

**Worked case.** A smooth surface hides a documented placement interruption and an unreviewed interface. Seek technical disposition of that interface. **Changed case:** the interruption was within a documented qualified procedure; verify actual compliance rather than assume every interruption is automatically a reject.

## Q12 — Gunning and hand application

Gunning introduces additional process variables, including equipment/feed, liquid control where applicable, nozzle operation, access, rebound and layer development. The installation procedure and any required demonstration establish acceptable practice; visual familiarity alone does not qualify a nozzle operator. Rebound and waste must be handled under the approved process, not silently returned as fresh material.

Hand-packed or rammed systems also require their specified material consistency and compaction. A rule appropriate to a castable does not automatically transfer to a plastic/ramming mix. Inspect access behind supports and transitions, because difficult geometry can conceal poorly consolidated material. Document observed technique changes and interruptions.

**Worked case.** Rebound is swept into the next lift to save material without authorization. Its altered condition and uncontrolled reuse require disposition. **Changed case:** waste is removed correctly, but a support blocks nozzle access; correct waste handling does not demonstrate consolidation behind it.

## Q13 — Sampling and specimen identity

Sampling should follow the approved purpose, frequency, preparation, curing/conditioning and test method. A sample must be linked to the actual product batch, installation process, location and time it represents. A laboratory-prepared specimen may characterize supplied material but not necessarily replicate field installation. Label before identity can be lost and record custody transfers.

Test validity depends on preparation and condition as well as the instrument result. Wrong dimensions, conditioning, orientation or method can make results incomparable. A favorable result with uncertain identity cannot validate a panel simply because it was tested the same week. Retain invalid or suspect reports with their disposition rather than delete them to improve the average.

**Worked case.** Sample S7 passes, but its batch field is blank and two products were installed. It cannot be assigned to whichever panel needs a pass. **Changed case:** batch identity is recovered but curing history remains unknown; one traceability repair does not establish full test validity.

## Q14 — Density, porosity and dimensional change

Interpret each property according to the test method and specimen condition. Bulk density is mass divided by bulk volume under the stated measurement basis; it is not true material density. Apparent porosity concerns connected pores accessible under the test method, not necessarily all voids. Dimensional change requires initial/final measurements and stated treatment history. Comparing values from different drying/firing conditions can be misleading.

Simple arithmetic can flag a transcription error but does not reproduce a standardized laboratory method. For supplied training dimensions100×100×100 mm, volume is0.001 m³. A dry mass2.2 kg yields2,200 kg/m³ under that geometric illustration. Actual test procedures may use another volume determination and require controlled specimen preparation.

**Worked case.** A100 mm reference length becomes99.2 mm after the specified training treatment: change−0.8%. **Changed case:** another sample is measured after a different treatment; the numerical comparison is not automatically a valid acceptance comparison.

## Q15 — Strength and abrasion test interpretation

Cold crushing strength and modulus of rupture describe different loading responses. ASTM's public scope identifies tests on dried or fired refractory shapes; cold results do not automatically establish hot-service performance.[S04] Ensure the report's method, conditioning and specimen basis match the specified acceptance requirement. Load divided by area is useful arithmetic for a supplied compressive example, not a complete test procedure.

Abrasion tests evaluate loss under a defined apparatus and exposure. Larger loss generally represents poorer resistance within a comparable method; cross-method rankings require caution. ASTM lists a specific room-temperature refractory abrasion method.[S05] Never invent its apparatus settings or acceptance limit from a product brochure.

**Worked case.** Synthetic load50,000 N on2,500 mm² yields20 N/mm²=20 MPa. This does not establish an entire lining's strength. **Changed case:** required minimum is22 MPa under the supplied same method; the result fails by2 MPa and needs authorized disposition, not averaging with unrelated tests.

## Q16 — Curing is not dryout

Curing develops the installed material's bond under its prescribed conditions; dryout removes water through controlled heating. They are related but not interchangeable. Product binder and conditions determine applicable treatment. HWI and Resco emphasize following the correct product/application guidance rather than using one schedule for every lining.[S06–S07]

Record cure start/end, relevant temperatures and protection from adverse conditions as required. A surface that feels hard does not prove the required cure history. Do not shorten curing because a later heatup is planned. If an excursion occurs, retain actual conditions and obtain manufacturer/technical disposition for the affected material.

**Worked case.** Supplied project cure requires24 hours within its stated conditions; dryout begins after18. Six hours are missing. **Changed case:**24 hours elapsed but part was outside the required conditions; elapsed time alone does not demonstrate compliant curing.

## Q17 — Review a dryout plan before heating

Dryout needs an approved product/system-specific procedure covering heating equipment, circulation/exhaust, measurement locations, ramp/hold controls, abnormal conditions and responsible authority. Thick, layered or constrained systems can differ from a simple lining. The hazards include retained moisture and pressure generation; do not conduct experimental heating to prove a classroom concept.[S06]

Inspect readiness records and monitoring arrangements. Know which temperature the schedule controls: furnace gas, hot face or another specified point cannot be silently substituted. Pass154/192 and at least 4/6 per domain, no unresolved critical error. Each decision scores 2 correct with reasoning, 1 safe but incomplete, 0 wrong/missing/unsafe. Correct failed domains through teaching before changed reassessment; reobserve skills when the failure concerns technique. After two unsuccessful changed attempts, provide individualized instruction and a fresh equivalent supervised assessment. An appeal records the disputed criterion/evidence and uses another competent assessor where feasible.

Award requires all 32 chapters/checks, 14 labs, 32 stations, both integrated cases and final, including eight actual demonstrations. Record learner identity, dates/hours, version hash, artifacts, scores, equipment/reference, assessor, accommodations, remediation and limitations. Protect keys and personal records under actual provider controls. Award “VNDRLY/U Refractory Inspection and Assessment — original training and assessment completion,” not API982 or 936. Use a24-month VNDRLY review interval, with earlier review after changed service/tasks, material guidance or demonstrated gaps; this is not an external recertification rule. Actual field appointment and specialist qualification are separate.
