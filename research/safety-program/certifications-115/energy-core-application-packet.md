# Energy Safety Core — application exercises and observation record

Revision: 2026-11-17. Companion to the ten G99 teaching chapters and the delivery guide.

## Delivery instructions

These are safe tabletop demonstrations of orientation decisions. They are not equipment qualifications, live emergency drills, respirator fit tests, or rescue training. Use fictional documents and inert props only. Never introduce hazardous chemicals, compressed gas, energized equipment, moving vehicles, suspended loads, or unexpected emergency alarms.

The facilitator must understand the ten chapters and the worked solutions. An employer supplying a real site procedure must confirm its current version and applicability. Do not substitute an instructor's general familiarity for a qualified assessor when an actual task requires one. Offer spoken or written responses to these exercises; assess the decision, not handwriting or accent. Do not coach the learner through a scored response and then record the coached response as independent performance.

Use **demonstrated**, **needs coaching**, or **not observed** for each criterion. Record the learner's actual response. A dangerous intervention fails that attempt even if other criteria are met. Stop, explain the missed exposure or boundary, allow instruction, and repeat with changed details. Preserve both observations. Successful tabletop work does not authorize the corresponding live task.

## Station 1 — a usable stop-work message

**Equipment:** a paper site sketch with a pump skid, marked unloading boundary, pedestrian route, and safe waiting area; a mock radio or verbal role-play. No operating radio channel is used.

**Setup:** Place a delivery driver inside the marked unloading boundary on the sketch. Tell the learner that the lift has not started, but the crew is ready. A person playing the supervisor replies, “We are late; just tell him to watch out.”

**Learner task:** Make a specific message, identify where you will stand, respond to the pressure, and describe the conditions needed before the activity resumes.

**Observable criteria:**

- Identifies the unloading location and the driver inside the boundary.
- Requests a hold before lifting, without ordering an unsafe abrupt movement of equipment.
- Stays outside the potential load and vehicle path rather than entering to deliver the message.
- Obtains acknowledgment from the person controlling the activity.
- States that restart needs the boundary restored, people clear, and the responsible person to confirm controls.

**Critical error:** walks into the marked load path, directs lifting with the driver still exposed, or treats schedule pressure as permission to proceed.

**Debrief:** “Be careful” leaves both the action and recipient unclear. A useful message identifies the exposure and the safe hold. A worker raising a concern is not automatically the person authorized to restart the lift.

**Retry variation:** the pedestrian is now outside the landing area but inside the equipment swing area. The learner must recognize that the hazard boundary is not limited to the final load footprint.

## Station 2 — job analysis after a change

**Equipment:** a blank three-column worksheet headed Task step / Exposure / Control and owner, the site sketch, and a card saying “Adjacent crew begins hot work.”

**Setup:** The original task is delivery of a pump to a separated waiting area. After the learner writes the steps, introduce the adjacent-work card and a second card saying “Normal pedestrian route blocked.”

**Learner task:** Identify who could be harmed, update the analysis, propose a stronger control where possible, and identify who resolves the conflicting work.

**Observable criteria:**

- Separates arrival, waiting, access, and unloading rather than using one vague “deliver pump” step.
- Describes vehicle contact, blocked escape/access, and the introduced ignition/interface concern.
- Proposes separation or rescheduling before relying only on warnings or PPE.
- Refers the interacting work to the site coordinator and responsible permit authorities.
- Explains that changed conditions require review before exposure, not a signature afterward.

**Critical error:** authorizes hot work from this worksheet or instructs workers to walk through the vehicle area without a controlled alternative.

**Debrief:** A JSA is useful because it connects a specific step to an exposure and a verified control. Copying yesterday's form without reassessment preserves paperwork, not protection.

**Retry variation:** the work itself is unchanged, but night conditions reduce visibility. The learner must identify that environment changes can invalidate a control.

## Station 3 — chemical information without guessing

**Equipment:** an inert, empty, clearly marked fictional container; a mock workplace label; this fictional training extract. Do not present the extract as a complete or legally compliant SDS.

**Fictional extract, Product TRAINING-CLEANER:** Section 1 identifies supplier contact and intended use. Section 2 identifies eye irritation and combustible vapors. Section 4 directs removal from exposure and use of the product's first-aid instructions. Section 6 distinguishes a small incidental release handled under the employer procedure from a release requiring emergency response. Section 7 identifies storage/ignition precautions. Section 8 says exposure evaluation and task-specific protection are required. No exposure limit, glove material, respirator, or extinguishing agent is specified in this exercise extract.

**Learner task:** Explain what information you can obtain, what remains missing, and what you do when the container label and the provided document have different product names.

**Observable criteria:**

- Checks that document and container identify the same product before relying on the document.
- Locates hazard identification and the first-aid and handling sections for their distinct purposes.
- Recognizes that exposure and protection decisions cannot be completed from an unspecified extract.
- Requests the correct current SDS and employer procedure rather than selecting a respirator or glove by guesswork.
- Keeps the container closed and does not sample, sniff, mix, or conduct a spill demonstration.

**Critical error:** recommends odor as a safety check, selects a specific respirator from insufficient information, or handles an unidentified substance to identify it.

**Debrief:** An SDS supports a task assessment; it does not replace one. Product identity comes first. Reading only the first-aid section does not identify how to prevent exposure.

**Retry variation:** the product name matches but the task changes from wiping to spraying. The learner must recognize the changed exposure route and seek a new task assessment.

## Station 4 — affected-worker energy boundary

**Equipment:** a paper pump diagram with electrical supply, a pressure line, and a raised component; a work-pack tag marked P-204 and a diagram tag marked P-240.

**Setup:** Tell the learner that they are an affected worker, not the authorized person performing isolation. Another person says, “The switch is off, so open it.”

**Learner task:** Identify the mismatch, the kinds of energy shown, the limit of your role, and the next safe communication.

**Observable criteria:**

- Recognizes that the equipment identities differ.
- Identifies electrical, pressure, and gravity/stored-energy concerns.
- Does not treat an off switch as verified isolation.
- Refers identification and the applicable isolation procedure to the authorized person.
- Maintains the boundary and requests a corrected briefing before affected work proceeds.

**Critical error:** removes a lock, tests a live circuit, opens a pressure connection, or claims authority to verify isolation based on this orientation exercise.

**Debrief:** Recognition of energy sources is the affected worker's useful contribution. It does not transfer the authorized worker's responsibilities. The governing procedure depends on the actual work and jurisdiction; do not apply one generic isolation sequence to every site.

**Retry variation:** tags match, but the raised component has no identified restraint in the plan. Matching labels alone do not control stored energy.

## Station 5 — alarm, evacuation, and accountability

**Equipment:** paper map showing an alarm point, primary and alternate routes, assembly point, and a list of four fictional workers. Read the alarm scenario aloud; do not activate a real alarm.

**Setup:** The site instructions direct evacuation on the alarm. Introduce an obstruction on the primary route. One worker was last seen near the skid but is not at assembly.

**Learner task:** Describe your route decision, emergency report, accountability entry, and response when another worker volunteers to go back alone.

**Observable criteria:**

- Follows the supplied site instructions and chooses the designated safe alternative when the primary route is unusable.
- Gives emergency responders the location, observed condition, and callback/contact information from safety.
- Reports the missing person's last known location without recording an unconfirmed safe arrival.
- Discourages untrained reentry and gives the information to the responsible response personnel.
- Waits for the authorized all-clear and separate task restart arrangements.

**Critical error:** returns to search alone, crosses a marked hazardous area, or marks an absent person accounted for based on assumption.

**Debrief:** Accurate accountability helps responders. An optimistic guess can redirect or delay a search. Protecting someone missing does not mean creating a second victim.

**Retry variation:** everyone is accounted for, but an evacuation route has changed. Confirm that the change is handed over before the next crew enters.

## Station 6 — factual handover and corrective action

**Equipment:** blank handover form and the previous station records.

**Setup:** The delivery is deferred. The pedestrian-route problem is resolved, the equipment-tag mismatch remains under review, and the next shift starts in thirty minutes.

**Learner task:** Write a handover that identifies completed corrections, open restrictions, responsible contacts, and what the next shift must confirm.

**Observable criteria:**

- Separates observed facts from unverified reports.
- Identifies the route correction and who confirmed it.
- Keeps the unresolved equipment identity and work restriction visible.
- Names a responsible contact or states that the assignment must be obtained before work proceeds.
- Uses closed-loop acknowledgment rather than assuming a sent message was read.

**Critical error:** records “all clear” while the equipment identity is unresolved, or describes the next shift as authorized merely because a handover was sent.

**Debrief:** A good handover makes the next decision easier. It does not bury an unresolved restriction in a narrative about completed work.

**Retry variation:** the tag mismatch is resolved, but the receiving shift has not acknowledged the revised unloading restriction. The learner must retain that restriction and obtain acknowledgment rather than marking the handover complete because the original defect was corrected.

## Actual-task evaluation referral

When the learner will perform a specialist task, identify: task and site; governing employer procedure/version; required prior instruction; equipment model; evaluator competence basis; applicable practical method and acceptance criteria; restrictions; record owner; and reassessment trigger. Supply that task's dedicated program and actual assessment materials. Do not use this six-station orientation packet as a replacement.
