# Shared mechanical maintenance — new specialist content under development

This is one proposed course shared by Field Mechanic and Pump Mechanic, not
two copies. It is outside the application catalog. It does not reteach the
existing VUC019 Energy Control courses or the existing safety, lifting and
chemical courses. Those retain their IDs and supply the prerequisite skills.

Audience: mechanics conducting documented inspection, diagnosis and mechanical
service within an employer-approved equipment scope. Entry requires the existing
hazardous-energy training, relevant equipment experience and safe use of ordinary
hand/measuring tools. Electrical work, pressure-boundary welding, structural
repair and engineered modifications remain separate authorized specialties.

Outcomes M01–M10 are taught and checked below. All numerical acceptance values
are fictional training-card limits; actual tolerances and procedures must come
from the correct current equipment documentation. An original teaching example
is not a service bulletin.

## M01 — Define the symptom and authorized repair scope

A work request such as "pump weak" or "machine won't move" is not a diagnosis.
Record the asset identity, configuration, reported symptom, operating conditions,
when it began, recent changes, relevant alarms and what the operator actually
observed. Preserve the distinction between "flow reduced" and "impeller worn."
The first is a measurement; the second is one possible explanation requiring
evidence. Ask whether the symptom is repeatable and under which conditions, but
do not run unsafe equipment just to reproduce an incident.

Check the equipment's current operating and maintenance documents, service
history and applicable bulletins. Identify the repair authority and any limits
on the field mechanic's scope. Select a diagnostic sequence that tests competing
explanations with the least additional exposure. Stop at a missing safety basis,
unqualified task or unavailable valid procedure rather than improvising a repair.

**Worked case:** a unit loses output only after a recent hose replacement. Compare
replacement identity, routing and configuration against the specified assembly
before condemning the main pump. This is a hypothesis, not permission to open
the hose while energized. **Changed case:** output was poor before replacement;
the history changes the hypothesis priority but does not prove the pump failed.

**Check:** What information is lost by writing only "bad pump"? **Answer:** The
conditions, measured symptom, alternatives and evidence needed to support a
repair decision. **Remediation:** rewrite a complaint as observation, conditions,
unknowns and next safe diagnostic test.

## M02 — Read the mechanical and fluid paths

Trace the path of power, load, lubricant and process fluid using the actual
diagram and equipment. Identify the driver, coupling or transmission, supported
shaft/load, bearings, seals, control devices, service points and protective
features. For a hydraulic system distinguish supply, return, case drain and
control lines; matching hose diameter does not make their functions interchangeable.
For a mechanically driven unit distinguish the drive train from the guarded
interface through which a person might contact motion.

Compare the diagram with the as-found state. Mark a difference for review instead
of editing the diagram to make the machine appear compliant. The VUC019 energy
control course supplies the isolation procedure competence. Apply it to every
relevant energy path and load, including those that remain after the driver stops.
Use approved support/blocking arrangements; never treat hydraulic pressure alone
as a mechanical support for a person working below a raised component.

**Worked case:** a disconnected driver is isolated, but a suspended attachment
can descend. The maintenance boundary is not safe until the approved load
support and verification are established. **Changed case:** the attachment is
lowered but an accumulator remains charged. The stored fluid energy is a separate
boundary requiring its own prescribed control.

**Check:** Does locating an electrical disconnect finish the energy-path review?
**Answer:** No; mechanical loads, fluid pressure and other stored energy must be
addressed. **Remediation:** annotate all relevant paths on a sample schematic
and identify the existing authorized procedure for each.

## M03 — Establish an as-found inspection record

Inspect without erasing the evidence. Record leaks, contamination, looseness,
abnormal wear, heat discoloration, guard condition, mounting condition and
instrument readings before cleaning or dismantling where it is safe to do so.
Use photographs and labeled observations to preserve location and orientation.
Do not reach into machinery or touch a suspected high-pressure leak for a
better observation. Obtain a safe state through the existing procedures first.

Separate symptoms from acceptance criteria. A shiny contact mark can indicate
unintended rubbing but does not by itself identify which part is out of position.
A loose fastener may be cause, consequence or a maintenance error. Inspect
related surfaces and obtain the appropriate dimensional or condition evidence.
Where the repair requires specialist examination, retain the part and request
qualified disposition rather than grinding away the indication.

**Worked case:** a seal leaks and the nearby bearing housing is unusually hot.
Record both, operating conditions and available trends. Replacing only the seal
without examining the supporting system may leave the cause intact. **Changed
case:** the same leak appears immediately after reassembly. Verify installed
parts, orientation, setting and startup conditions before assuming normal wear.

**Check:** Why retain an as-found photograph before cleaning? **Answer:** Cleaning
can remove the location/pattern of a leak or contamination needed for diagnosis.
**Remediation:** prepare an evidence record that distinguishes observations,
hypotheses and disposition.

## M04 — Measure against the right tolerance

Select an instrument with suitable range, resolution and verification status.
Clean the measurement surfaces as the procedure requires; account for temperature,
orientation and the feature being measured. Record units, equipment identity,
location and the actual result. A number without its feature definition cannot
be compared reliably with a tolerance. Do not use a broad-range ruler to establish
a precision fit simply because its display is easy to read.

Fictional shaft card M-S permits24.98–25.00mm at the specified measurement
locations. Readings24.99,24.97 and24.99mm include one result outside that range.
An average of24.983mm does not rescue the nonconforming location. Confirm the
measurement method and instrument, then record the actual condition for approved
repair/replacement disposition. Do not change units or round selectively to
make a part pass.

Fictional runout card M-R has maximum total indicated movement0.05mm under its
specified setup. Observed0.08mm exceeds it by0.03mm. Confirm fixture and method
before attributing all movement to the shaft; the setup itself can introduce
error. This is a teaching tolerance, not a universal pump or engine limit.

**Check:** Can a fit be accepted from one favorable measurement when the procedure
requires several? **Answer:** No. Complete the prescribed locations and evaluate
each relevant condition. **Remediation:** repeat a changed sample with full
location/unit/instrument records and explain the disposition.

## M05 — Dismantle and handle components without creating new damage

Plan the sequence from the current procedure. Establish isolation and support,
drain/decontaminate as applicable, identify lifting points and weights, and prepare
a clean organized work area. Assign a safe place for removed parts and containment
for fluids. The existing rigging/lifting course covers lifting competence;
this course does not authorize an unqualified mechanic to design a lift.

Mark permitted orientation references and retain traceable component identity.
Protect openings against dirt and moisture. Use the specified pullers, fixtures
and support methods rather than transmitting uncontrolled force through a
bearing, sealing surface or fragile housing. Never substitute a hammering
sequence for an unknown disassembly procedure. Record parts damaged during
removal rather than hiding the damage as pre-existing wear.

Inspect contact surfaces and fit features after removal. Preserve samples or
failed parts needed for analysis. Separate reusable, replace, and hold-for-review
items physically so that a rejected component is not accidentally reinstalled.
Use the actual procedure's reuse criteria; "it came out in one piece" is not
a condition standard.

**Worked case:** a seal face has a scratch after an improvised tool was used.
Segregate it and obtain disposition, even if the original failure involved a
different component. **Changed case:** the orientation mark is missing. Recover
the correct assembly basis from documentation rather than guessing.

**Check:** Why label an apparently identical spacer? **Answer:** Its location,
dimensions or matched assembly may matter; appearance does not establish
interchangeability. **Remediation:** rebuild a mock parts trace without relying
on memory alone.

## M06 — Control replacement parts, fluids and fastening

Match replacement parts to the actual model, serial/configuration and service
requirements. Confirm dimensions, material, seals and applicable ratings through
approved sources. A part that fits can still be unsuitable for the fluid,
temperature, pressure, speed or loading. Check packaging and identity before
opening or installing it, and retain the traceability required by the repair.

Use the specified lubricant and quantity/method. Mixing products or overfilling
to be safe can create a new failure. Establish cleanliness at fill points and
protect containers from contamination. Where fluid sampling is required, use
the correct sample location, method and label so the result reflects the system.
Do not diagnose a machine from oil color alone.

For fasteners, use the approved grade, size, condition, sequence and tightening
method. Torque depends on the defined assembly conditions; do not copy a value
from a different joint or assume lubrication does not matter. Use a suitable
verified tool and record critical steps required by the procedure. An unknown
torque specification is a hold, not an invitation to tighten by feel.

**Worked case:** a replacement elastomer is dimensionally identical but its
material is unverified for the service. Hold installation. **Changed case:**
material is correct but the torque procedure assumes dry threads and the joint
has lubricant. Resolve the method before tightening.

**Check:** Is a correct part number sufficient if its condition is damaged?
**Answer:** No; identity and acceptable condition are separate checks.
**Remediation:** complete a parts/fluids/fastening verification sheet for a
changed assembly, identifying every unresolved item.

## M07 — Alignment, supports and guarded rotating assemblies

Alignment concerns the relationship between rotating axes under specified
conditions, not simply whether a coupling can be bolted together. Check the
mounting, support, fasteners, foundation and applicable soft-foot condition
before correcting alignment. Follow the actual instrument and equipment method.
A flexible coupling is not permission to ignore misalignment or external strain.

Do not force connected piping into place using the pump's flanges. Record and
resolve external loads or poor support through the responsible installation
process. Alignment established before piping changes may require rechecking
after those changes and under the procedure's specified final conditions.
Use the manufacturer's tolerances, thermal targets and sequence rather than
a universal value from this course.

Fictional card M-A allows offset0.04mm and angular deviation0.03mm/100mm.
The measured values are0.06mm and0.02mm/100mm. Angular deviation passes that
card, offset does not. Both conditions must be satisfied; a good result in
one dimension cannot compensate for the other. Correct the cause using the
approved procedure and remeasure, recording before and after.

**Worked case:** alignment changes when a connected pipe is released in a
controlled isolated setup. Escalate the evidence of external strain; do not
simply pull the pump back with its bolts. **Check:** Can a guard remain off
during a normal proving run for easier viewing? **Answer:** No. Use the approved
guarded test arrangement and safe measurement methods. **Remediation:** prepare
a proving-run readiness sheet that includes guards and personnel clearance.

## M08 — Diagnose hydraulic and engine-support faults within scope

Use the actual schematic and troubleshooting procedure to distinguish supply,
control and load problems. Establish safe external observations first: correct
fluid level by the prescribed method, condition indicators, configuration,
documented codes and maintenance history. A slow actuator may reflect a load,
control setting, supply restriction, leak or other condition; replacing the
pump without testing the hypothesis can waste time and leave the hazard.

Pressure measurement requires approved rated instruments, correct test points,
safe connection and controlled operation by qualified personnel. Do not loosen
a fitting to see whether pressure is present. Do not search for a pinhole leak
with a hand. A suspected fluid-injection injury requires immediate emergency
medical escalation and information on the fluid, even if the skin mark is small.

For engine-driven equipment, preserve codes and symptom conditions before
clearing anything. Follow the authorized diagnostic process for fuel, cooling,
air, lubrication and starting systems. High-pressure fuel, hot pressurized
coolant and rotating drive components need their specific controls. Electronic
control changes, electrical tests and emissions-system interventions outside
the mechanic's authorization go to the qualified specialist.

**Worked case:** a temperature alarm appears after a cooling-system service.
Stop/secure according to the operating procedure and investigate the service
history; do not remove a hot pressurized cap. **Check:** Does a fault code prove
the named component should be replaced? **Answer:** No; follow the diagnostic
tree and verify the condition. **Remediation:** draw at least two plausible
causes and a safe authorized test distinguishing them.

## M09 — Reassemble, test and return equipment deliberately

Before reassembly, confirm that each defect has an approved disposition and
that replacement components, clearances, fastening and lubrication satisfy the
actual procedure. Check tools, loose materials and temporary supports. Restore
guards and protective functions. Record any work deliberately left incomplete
and its effect on readiness; do not conceal it in a general "service done" note.

The return-to-service sequence belongs to the equipment-specific procedure and
existing energy-control program. Coordinate with affected operators, clear
personnel, remove controls through the authorized process and use an approved
test plan with limits and abort criteria. A proving run is not an uncontrolled
experiment. Use safe observation points and stop if an acceptance or safety
condition fails.

Fictional test card M-T gives a defined stable load and requires temperature
below its supplied80°C limit. A rising83°C result is not accepted because the
machine now moves. Record the condition, safely stop and investigate. A changed
case with temperature75°C but an abnormal new leak also fails readiness unless
the actual plan expressly establishes and accepts that condition. Meeting one
metric does not erase another defect.

**Check:** Who is told before a machine is released? **Answer:** The responsible
operating/maintenance roles identified by the procedure, with actual condition,
limits and unresolved items. **Remediation:** conduct a mock release briefing
where the receiver explains the state back.

## M10 — Close the repair with evidence and a prevention decision

Record the original symptom, diagnostic findings, measurements, procedure and
parts used, settings/clearances, test results, remaining restrictions and the
people who performed and accepted the work. Distinguish the repair performed
from the cause established. "Replaced seal" describes an action; "seal damaged
by verified misalignment" requires supporting evidence. Avoid claiming a root
cause merely because the symptom disappeared during a brief run.

Identify follow-up monitoring or specialist work required by the repair plan.
If repeat failures suggest a system problem, preserve samples, trends and failed
parts so a qualified review can examine installation, operation and material
compatibility. Repeated replacement without a feedback loop can normalize a
hazardous condition. Do not unilaterally change the engineered design to prevent
a failure without the appropriate review and authorization.

**Worked case:** three seals failed in a short period and no alignment record
exists. Record the repeat pattern and request a system review rather than
declaring the fourth seal an assured solution. **Changed case:** a verified
cause has been corrected but the planned proving period is incomplete. State
the conditional status and required follow-up instead of signing an unconditional
release.

**Check:** What makes a repair record useful to the next mechanic? **Answer:**
Traceable evidence and actual decisions, not just a list of purchased parts.
**Remediation:** rewrite a vague service ticket into symptom, evidence, action,
test, limits and follow-up sections.
