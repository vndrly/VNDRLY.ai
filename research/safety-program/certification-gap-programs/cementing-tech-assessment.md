# Cementing Technician — delivery and assessment packet

Development status: assessment material, not an issued qualification. Use with
`cementing-tech-instruction.md` and the referenced existing catalog courses.
Controlled written forms and marking guidance are separate instructor-only
files, not learner catalog lessons.

## Delivery sequence and instructor preparation

The instructor must have documented cementing execution experience appropriate
to the equipment and tasks being taught. The assessor must be capable of
recognizing unsafe omissions in those tasks, not merely reading a rubric.
The responsible employer supplies permission, safe equipment, applicable OEM
procedures and an approved training/job plan. Do not use an operating well as
a convenient substitute for a controlled training environment.

1. Record the learner's requested scope, existing shared-course completions and
   equipment experience. Diagnose unit conversion, diagram reading and ability
   to explain a pressure boundary. Provide remediation for gaps.
2. Review the referenced shared-course evidence. Reuse unchanged completed
   courses; do not create copies or demand repeat completion solely because
   they are now constituents of another certification.
3. Teach Course A, C01–C04, using a complete fictional job dossier. Allow roughly
   four instructional hours plus calculations and feedback; extend as required
   for demonstrated understanding rather than awarding credit for seat time.
4. Teach Course B, C05–C12, with a unit diagram, sample records and staged
   changes. Allow roughly six instructional hours plus practice and feedback.
5. Complete the six guided applications below. Learners attempt decisions
   before the instructor explains the expected solution.
6. Conduct the controlled written examination and the practical evaluation.
   Schedule practical work only after safety readiness, typically at least a
   separate session. There is no time bonus for bypassing a control.
7. Review all evidence, remediation and scope limitations before any award.

These durations are instructional planning estimates, not an externally
recognized training-hour claim or an alternative to required supervised practice.

## Safe equipment and documents

Prepare a complete approved fictional job package with well/string identifiers,
revision, sequence, material list, dimensions, recipe, instrument units,
pressure-test procedure, safe-state actions, limits and named authorities.
Supply a piping diagram and deenergized training assembly, clearly defective
sample components that cannot enter service, labeled sample containers,
calibrated/verified measurement equipment appropriate to the exercises, mock
instrument traces, quantity records and safety data sheets for any actual
materials present. Use benign training liquids where practical. No actual
cement mixing or pressure test without the separate facility risk assessment,
competent supervision and approved procedure.

An operational endorsement requires observation on the actual equipment family
under an authorized safe plan. Paper or deenergized demonstrations are recorded
as such and cannot be represented as live pumping competence. The instructor
can teach theory without a live well; the full equipment-specific award remains
conditional until the required operating performance is observed.

## Guided applications and explained results

### A1 — Job and material release (C01, C02)

Give the learner a Well A program, Well B delivery ticket and a tote with the
correct product family but unverified lot. Ask for a release decision, a
discrepancy report and the minimum missing evidence. The correct result is
hold, identify both mismatches, isolate the questionable material and request
the appropriate document/material dispositions. Do not accept "ask supervisor"
alone: the report must say what differs and which part of the job is held.
Change the case to matching identifiers with a revised recipe and require a
fresh comparison. Retain both attempts and explanations.

### A2 — Quantity calculation and sensitivity (C03, C04)

Use the 8.5/5.5-inch, 1,000-ft teaching case. Require labeled geometry,
40.8005 bbl geometric volume, 48.9606 bbl with the supplied 20% allowance,
and approximately 232.961 sacks at 1.18 ft³/sack. Whole-sack availability is
233, subject to an approved actual batch plan. At 9.0-inch outer diameter,
the corresponding values are 49.3006, 59.1607 and 281.4945; availability 282.
Accept reasonable rounding if intermediate precision is preserved and the
decision is unchanged. Do not accept substituting casing ID into the annulus.

Then supply 1,200 strokes at 0.100 bbl/stroke and a 116 bbl tank result.
Expected count volume is 120 bbl and discrepancy 4 bbl. The learner must
identify uncertainty and boundaries rather than diagnose formation loss.
Remediate unit/model mistakes with a changed geometry and new stroke factor.

### A3 — Configuration and test readiness (C05, C06)

Place an unidentified connection on the deenergized display and show an
unverified branch on its diagram. The learner must reject the unidentified
part for service and flag the branch before release. Present the fictional
2,000 → 1,940 → 1,890 psi test trace against its supplied stable criterion.
Expected decision: failed acceptance evidence, hold, safe-state procedure and
qualified investigation. Do not physically reproduce a leak. Change the case
to a stable trace with an expired gauge verification; readiness still fails.

### A4 — Slurry sample and transition control (C07, C08)

Provide the fictional 15.7–15.9 lb/gal band and a traceable 15.3 result.
The learner reports the deviation and applies the provided stop/divert/hold
instruction without improvising a recipe correction. Have them label and
handover a benign sample identifying stage, time, location and recipe.
Then provide 120 bbl at the specified well reference and 2 bbl upstream fill.
Expected source quantity is 122 bbl only when the 120 excludes that fill.
Change the wording so 120 already includes fill: adding another 2 is wrong.

### A5 — Time, returns and emergency communication (C09, C10)

Supply 30 bbl delivery, 21 bbl returns in a common defined window, and no
confirmed explanation. The learner reports 9 bbl unexplained difference,
actual measurements and prescribed actions without fabricating a cause.
Provide the 180-minute fictional planning window, 110 planned work, 30 reserved
and 50 delay. Total190 exceeds180 by10; technical review is required.
Change the delay to20: total160 fits arithmetically, but the learner must still
state that material/system assumptions and approved contingencies govern.

### A6 — Isolation, closeout and handover (C11, C12)

Provide a zero pump gauge with an unverified closed branch, a proposed wash-water
destination not on the plan, and a job summary claiming "barrier verified"
without acceptance evidence. The learner keeps disconnection held, obtains
approved waste handling and corrects the unsupported acceptance claim while
preserving the original record. Change the case to a fully documented boundary
and approved disposal destination but an unresolved component defect. Equipment
readiness remains restricted until qualified disposition; cleanup is not repair.

## Individual practical stations

Use the following criteria as direct observations, not learner self-certification.
For each station score five dimensions 0–2: preparation/evidence, technical
accuracy, safe sequence, communication, and traceable record. Two means correct
and independent with a defensible explanation; one safe but incomplete or
prompted; zero missing, incorrect or unsafe. Pass each station at least8/10,
no zero in any dimension, and no critical unsafe action. A high average does
not offset a failed station. Record coaching and reassess independently on a
changed case; never erase the original result.

| Station | Required observable performance | Critical failure examples |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Program/materials | Correct well/revision; reconcile recipe, lots and water; record mismatch and release owner | Releases wrong/unknown material or wrong-well program |
| P2 Calculations/measurement | Label geometry; preserve units; calculate quantities; reconcile count/tank; identify uncertainty | Alters input to conceal discrepancy; treats calculation as field authorization |
| P3 Rig-up/test | Trace all pressure spaces; verify compatibility/condition; explain approved test; reject invalid result | Uses unidentified component; approaches pressurized suspected leak; bypasses hold |
| P4 Mixing/stages | Verify recipe and instrument; obtain representative sample; record actual stage quantity/time; use correct transition | Improvises recipe; mislabels sample knowingly; continues beyond given stop limit |
| P5 Deviation response | Recognize injected trigger; use prescribed safe state; report facts/unknowns; obtain reviewed restart | Ignores trigger; invents well-control action outside authority; conceals delay |
| P6 Closeout/handover | Verify each isolated space; state disposal route; preserve defects; hand over acceptance limits | Opens unverified boundary; unauthorized discharge; claims unproved barrier acceptance |

Each station must be observed individually even when a crew practices together.
Use tabletop injects during safe pauses; do not introduce actual unsafe pressure,
contamination, connection defects or unexpected equipment movement.

## Equipment-specific operational route

An authorized assessor must observe an integrated sequence on the named mixing/
pumping equipment family under the approved training/operating plan: readiness
review, equipment checks, configuration verification, instrument confirmation,
briefing, approved startup, controlled fluid processing/pumping as applicable,
stage tracking, safe stop, verified closeout and record handover. Use a safe
training circuit or appropriately authorized operation; the assessor decides
whether it genuinely demonstrates the claimed equipment scope.

The learner identifies controls and limits before use. The assessor records
the exact machine, procedure revision, medium, permissible conditions and
observed actions. A simulated abnormal event must be labeled simulated. Do not
claim a real emergency intervention was demonstrated from verbal answers alone.
If an essential task cannot be safely observed, leave that scope unawarded.
This is a normal delivery condition, not permission to mark an incomplete
assessment complete.

Operational pass requires every listed stage safely and independently completed,
all P-station standards met and no critical failure. The assessor can stop the
evaluation immediately. After instruction and safe practice, the learner repeats
the failed stage and enough of the sequence to show the corrected skill works
in context. A second assessor reviews any disputed critical failure before
an award decision; schedule pressure must not change the standard.

## Controlled written examination

Use separate Form A or B with twelve cases aligned to C01–C12. Each case has
three decisions scored0–2: correct supported reasoning2, safe partial reasoning1,
incorrect/missing/unsafe0. Maximum72; pass at least58/72, at least4/6 in every
case, and no unresolved critical unsafe decision. This per-domain floor prevents
strong arithmetic from compensating for unsafe release decisions.

Allow120minutes, ordinary calculator and the permitted formula/reference sheet.
No answer key, coursebook, outside assistance or collaboration. Accommodations
may change presentation/time, not the skill being assessed. The assessor records
the form/version, scores, reasoning and accommodations. Corrective instruction
is followed by changed Form B (or an independently prepared equivalent changed
form after both have been used); do not repeat a memorized answer key as a retake.

## Award and renewal rules

The proposed VNDRLY/U Cementing Technician credential requires the referenced
shared-course evidence, all corrected C checks, six applications, six practical
stations, the scoped operational route and a passing controlled final. Record
the equipment family and task scope explicitly. A learner with theory only
receives a study record, not the operational credential. A signature cannot
substitute for absent evidence.

This award does not confer another issuer's certification, authorize slurry
engineering, accept a well barrier, qualify the learner for every cementing
technology, or replace employer/operator approval. Remedial squeeze operations,
foamed/nitrified systems, offshore-specific interfaces and other specialized
routes require their own demonstrated scope before those claims are made.

Require reassessment when equipment/process changes materially, performance
shows a deficiency, or the employer's authorized task scope changes. A yearly
VNDRLY evidence-review reminder is an administrative review, not a claimed
regulatory expiry. Record actual currency decisions and the basis for them.
